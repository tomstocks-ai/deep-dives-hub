# QBTS — D-Wave Quantum Inc.

D-Wave built and sold the world's first commercial quantum computer, and since January 2026 it is the only publicly traded pure-play offering both annealing and gate-model systems. The order book has genuinely turned — first-half 2026 bookings of \$35.5M were up 1,120% year over year and remaining performance obligations hit \$40.7M — but almost none of it has reached the income statement. Q2 2026 revenue was \$3.08M, flat year over year and 24% below consensus, trailing-twelve-month revenue is roughly \$12.4M, and the adjusted EBITDA loss more than doubled to \$69.9M in the first half. Against a ~\$6.2B market cap that is several hundred times sales, richer than either IonQ or Rigetti. A September 8, 2026 definitive agreement for up to \$100M of CHIPS and Science Act funding and a \$546.2M cash pile buy roughly three years of runway; two plaintiff-firm investigations opened in September over the Q2 miss. This is a **HOLD / SPEC.** — the backlog has to convert into recognized revenue before the multiple is defensible.

## Company Overview

D-Wave Quantum Inc. (NASDAQ: QBTS) has been selling quantum computers commercially longer than anyone. Founded in Burnaby, British Columbia, it went public via SPAC in 2022 and is relocating its corporate headquarters from Palo Alto to Boca Raton, Florida by the end of 2026. Alan Baratz, a former JPMorgan and Avaya executive with a PhD in computer science, has been CEO since 2020; John Markovich is CFO.

**The dual-platform structure.** D-Wave's original and still dominant product is *quantum annealing* — a special-purpose architecture that finds low-energy states of an optimization problem rather than running arbitrary quantum circuits. The current flagship, Advantage2, carries roughly 4,500 qubits and is the system behind D-Wave's March 2024 quantum-supremacy claim on a materials-simulation problem, a result the company says has not been successfully challenged. Annealing is narrower than gate-model computing but it works today, which is why D-Wave has revenue while most of the cohort has science projects.

In January 2026 D-Wave closed the acquisition of **Quantum Circuits, Inc.**, a Yale-derived developer of error-corrected superconducting gate-model systems, for roughly \$550M (about \$252.8M of that in net cash). The strategic logic is the dual-rail qubit: built-in erasure detection catches around 90% of errors that occur, delivering gate fidelities above 99.9% at an observed 0.5% erasure rate. That lets D-Wave claim logical qubits using roughly an order of magnitude fewer physical qubits than architectures without erasure detection. Combined with D-Wave's own on-chip cryogenic control — multiplexed DACs that can address tens of thousands of qubits with about 200 control wires — management argues D-Wave is the only company holding all three ingredients for scaled, error-corrected superconducting gate-model machines. The two-qubit entangling gate result was published in *Nature* in 2026.

**Revenue model.** Three lines: on-premises and hosted **system sales** (episodic, eight-figure, very high margin), **QCaaS** subscriptions through the Leap cloud service (recurring, ~99.9% uptime), and **professional services** via the D-Wave Launch program. FY2025's mix was \$16.2M systems, \$5.5M QCaaS and \$2.7M professional services — which is the whole problem in one line: two-thirds of revenue came from lumpy hardware deals. In the first half of 2026 that flipped hard: system sales were \$0.3M versus \$13.7M a year earlier, and total revenue fell 67% to \$5.9M even as the subscription base grew.

**Customer base.** Over 100 customers recognized revenue in H1 2026, more than half commercial enterprises. Forbes Global 2000 companies were 48.5% of first-half revenue, up from 7.5% a year earlier, and commercial customers were 67.7% of revenue versus 16.0%. Named engagements include AT&T, Nasdaq Verafin, Unisys, Oki Electric, Shionogi, LG CNS, Sharp, CINECA and the Lombardy regional quantum facility in Italy. On the defense side, D-Wave works with **Anduril and Davidson Technologies** on U.S. air-and-missile-defense planning, where a proof-of-concept showed the Stride hybrid solver delivering at least 10x faster time-to-solution, a 9-12% improvement in threat mitigation and 45-60 additional intercepts in a 500-missile simulation. A dedicated U.S. government business unit was formed in late 2025.

**Moat.** Two decades of annealing know-how, a large patent estate, the only fielded systems with a peer-reviewed supremacy claim, and now the erasure-detection gate-model IP. D-Wave was one of only two vendors named a Leader in the IDC MarketScape: Worldwide Quantum Computing 2026 Vendor Assessment. The moat is real; the question is whether it is monetizable at this decade's pace.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (the last full year) was a record that flattered the run-rate.** Revenue of \$24.6M was up 179% from \$8.8M, but \$13.7M of it came from a single first-ever annealing system sale recognized in the first half. GAAP gross profit rose 265% to \$20.3M and GAAP gross margin jumped to 82.6% from 63.0% — almost entirely the mix effect of that high-margin hardware deal. GAAP operating expenses rose 46% to \$120.7M. The headline net loss of \$355.1M, or \$(1.11) per share, is misleading: \$270.5M of it was non-cash warrant remeasurement and warrant-exercise losses driven by the share price appreciating during the 2025 quantum rally. Adjusted net loss was \$84.5M, or \$(0.26) per share, and adjusted EBITDA loss was \$71.8M. All publicly traded warrants were redeemed in November 2025, so that distortion is gone going forward.

**H1 2026 is where the thesis gets tested.** Revenue fell 67% to \$5.9M because the comparable period contained that system sale. Q2 2026 revenue of \$3.08M was essentially flat against \$3.095M in Q2 2025 and missed the ~\$4.03M consensus by about 24%; EPS of \$(0.13) GAAP (reported as \$(0.10) on some adjusted bases) missed as well. GAAP gross margin compressed to 55.4% in Q2 from 63.8%, and to 59.4% for the half from 87.6%, as services and personnel costs grew against a services-weighted mix.

**Operating expenses are the real story.** H1 2026 GAAP opex of \$111.5M was up 108% year over year, including \$9.3M of non-recurring Quantum Circuits deal costs, \$19.0M more in personnel (73% of it sales, marketing and R&D), and \$16.4M more in stock compensation plus depreciation and amortization. R&D alone more than doubled to \$54.0M for the half. Adjusted EBITDA loss widened from \$26.1M to \$69.9M. Operating cash outflow was \$73.5M against \$34.6M, and with \$5.9M of capex and internal software spend, free cash outflow was roughly \$79M for six months — about \$40M per quarter versus the \$16-20M quarterly range that prevailed through 2024 and 2025.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable investment securities were \$546.2M at June 30, 2026, down from \$884.5M at year-end 2025 and \$819.3M a year earlier; management attributes over 90% of the decline to the Quantum Circuits cash consideration. Total debt is modest at roughly \$35M (an equipment financing term loan and a Canadian SIF loan). Interest income of \$10.8M in the half meaningfully offsets burn. The acquisition added \$342.6M of goodwill and \$211.8M of intangibles, which is why D&A jumped from \$0.7M to \$8.5M in six months. Shares outstanding were 372.0M at June 30, 2026 versus 358.7M at December 31, 2025. At roughly \$40M per quarter of burn, the \$546.2M balance plus the up-to-\$100M CHIPS award is about three years of runway — comfortable by pure-play quantum standards.

**The forward-looking metrics.** Bookings for H1 2026 were \$35.5M, up 1,120% from \$2.9M, and remaining performance obligations were \$40.7M at June 30, up 668% from \$5.3M, with 57% expected to be recognized within twelve months and 72% within two years. QCaaS revenue from *production* applications (as opposed to experimentation) reached \$1.3M, or 37.3% of QCaaS revenue, versus 9.8% a year earlier — the single most encouraging datapoint in the release. The caveats: H1 bookings leaned on one \$20M Florida Atlantic University system sale, \$2.3M of the total came in with Quantum Circuits, and Q2 standalone bookings were only \$2.1M.

## Valuation

At \$16.78 (September 28, 2026) on roughly 372M shares, market cap is about \$6.2B. Netting \$546.2M of cash against ~\$35M of debt gives an enterprise value near \$5.7B.

| Basis | Revenue | EV/Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 reported | \$24.6M | ~233x |
| Trailing twelve months to 2026-06-30 | ~\$12.4M | ~460x |
| Total RPO (contracted, not yet recognized) | \$40.7M | ~141x |

Those are the numbers that matter. For context, IonQ trades near 68x EV/revenue on \$130M of FY2025 revenue and Rigetti — the cohort's other extreme — sits on \$7.1M. D-Wave's multiple is closer to Rigetti's than IonQ's while its revenue has been flat for two years. A discounted cash flow is not meaningful for a company whose free cash flow is \$(160M) annualized with no guided path to breakeven, so scenario analysis is the honest tool.

**Bull case: \$34–\$45.** The \$40.7M RPO converts on schedule, the Florida Atlantic Advantage2 lands by year-end 2026, and FY2027 revenue reaches \$45-60M. The CHIPS money funds the roadmap without dilution, the gate-model milestones land, and the cohort re-rates. That range roughly matches the Street's ~\$35-36 average target and \$43-45 high, and implies about 100-170% upside — plausible only if revenue recognition finally follows bookings.

**Base case: \$16–\$22.** Bookings stay lumpy, conversion is slower than the 57%-in-12-months guide, FY2027 revenue lands at \$30-45M off a ~\$12.4M trailing base, and gross margin normalizes in the 55-70% band. Adjusted EBITDA loss stays near \$140M annualized; the balance sheet plus CHIPS funds roughly three years. The multiple stays compressed while investors wait, and the stock trades around current levels. This is where the weight of probability sits.

**Bear case: \$7–\$11.** Revenue is flat for a third year, the market stops paying several hundred times sales, burn forces a raise at depressed prices, and the plaintiff-firm investigations harden into a class action. The 52-week low is already \$12.75; this range assumes the de-rating goes further, which in a cohort that has round-tripped from \$46.75 in twelve months is not a stretch.

Technically the stock is below both its 50-day (\$18.21) and 200-day (\$21.00) moving averages, down 33.7% over a year and about 64% from the \$46.75 high. The \$12.75 low is the obvious support; \$18-21 is the moving-average resistance band that has to be reclaimed before any trend change is credible.

## Growth Catalysts

**Revenue conversion is the whole near-term story.** The \$20M Florida Atlantic University Advantage2 purchase is scheduled to deploy by end-2026 and would be the largest revenue event in company history. Together with the \$10M two-year Fortune 100 QCaaS license and the €10M Lombardy capacity booking, that is the backlog that has to show up in Q3 and Q4 prints.

**The CHIPS award.** The September 8, 2026 definitive agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce gives D-Wave access to up to \$100M under the CHIPS and Science Act, with Commerce taking a minority, non-controlling equity stake as a condition. Rigetti, Quantinuum and PsiQuantum signed comparable \$100M agreements the same day. The stated targets are a 100,000-qubit annealing system and a 10,000-qubit gate-model machine supporting 100 logical qubits at over one million operations. Non-dilutive federal capital plus explicit policy endorsement is the most valuable thing that has happened to this cohort in 2026, though a government equity position is also a new overhang.

**The annealing roadmap.** Multi-chip fabrics using superconducting interconnects and packaging take Advantage3 to a 20,000-qubit intermediate system in 2029 and ultimately 100,000 qubits by 2031. The scalable I/O prototype is the enabling piece: reaching 100,000 qubits with no more than a 20% increase in I/O lines over the 4,500-qubit Advantage2.

**The gate-model roadmap.** Announced August 2026, with dated milestones: a 17-physical-qubit system in 2026 at logical error rates 2x below physical; 49 qubits in 2027 at a 20-fold error reduction factor; 181 qubits in 2028 at 2,000-fold, described as the blueprint for fault tolerance; 10 logical qubits in 2030 for the first fault-tolerant algorithms; 100 logical qubits in 2032 capable of over a million operations, targeting quantum chemistry and quantum AI. A first-of-its-kind error-aware gate-model simulator is coming, bundled with system access for developers.

**Government and defense.** The new U.S. government business unit, the \$1.57M NSF NQVL/ERASE grant, second-year NORDTECH SQFab funding from the Department of War, and the Anduril/Davidson missile-defense work all point at a customer set with budget authority and tolerance for long procurement cycles.

**Production workloads.** QCaaS production revenue moving from 9.8% to 37.3% of QCaaS revenue is the quiet signal that customers are moving from pilots to operations. If that mix keeps shifting, the recurring line gets a lot more valuable than the hardware line.

**Events.** Qubits Asia 2026 in Seoul on October 28, 2026 targets the Korean and Japanese base (LG CNS, Sharp, Oki, Shionogi) and historically has been a venue for customer announcements.

## Risk Factors

**Revenue has not grown in two years.** Q2 2026 revenue of \$3.08M is statistically identical to Q2 2025's \$3.095M, and trailing-twelve-month revenue of roughly \$12.4M is *below* FY2025's \$24.6M because the base period contained a one-off system sale. Two consecutive quarterly misses against a low bar is a pattern, not noise.

**The valuation has no margin for error.** Several hundred times trailing sales on a company burning \$40M a quarter means the equity is a pure option on 2029-2032 milestones. Small changes in the discount rate or the sector narrative move this stock 30%.

**Lumpy, concentrated bookings.** Q2 2026 standalone bookings were \$2.1M. Take the FAU deal out of H1 and the picture is far less impressive. Because revenue recognition follows delivery on multi-million-dollar systems, a single slipped installation can erase a quarter.

**Accelerating burn.** Free cash outflow roughly doubled to ~\$40M per quarter. Management frames this as investment in the dual-platform roadmap and go-to-market, which is defensible, but it shortens runway and raises the probability of a raise if the stock stays weak.

**Legal overhang.** Pomerantz LLP opened an investigation on September 10, 2026 and Kessler Topaz Meltzer and Check followed in late September, both centred on the August 6 Q2 disclosure. These are investigations, not filed complaints, and no wrongdoing has been established — but they consume management time and cap sentiment.

**Dilution history.** D-Wave raised \$536.7M through at-the-market offerings and \$99.3M from warrant exercises in H1 2025 alone, plus \$37.8M via Lincoln Park. Share count rose from 358.7M to 372.0M in six months on compensation alone. The company has never hesitated to sell equity into strength.

**Acquisition risk.** \$342.6M of goodwill and \$211.8M of intangibles rest on the gate-model roadmap being delivered. A slip past 2028 makes impairment a live question, and the \$8.5M of six-month D&A already drags reported margins.

**Technology-path risk.** Annealing addresses a narrower problem class than universal gate-model computing. If fault tolerance arrives at IBM, Google, IonQ or Quantinuum first, D-Wave's commercial head start in optimization may not transfer, and the gate-model business it bought is starting from behind.

**Classical competition.** Every quantum optimization win has to beat a GPU-accelerated classical heuristic that is itself improving fast. The supremacy claim was on a materials-simulation problem, not on the commercial optimization workloads that pay the bills.

## Recommendation

**Rating: HOLD / SPEC.** Position for a backlog-conversion proof point rather than for the story.

The reason this is not a SPEC. BUY alongside IonQ and Rigetti is arithmetic. D-Wave is the cohort's most commercially mature vendor by history and customer count, yet its trailing revenue is \$12.4M against a \$6.2B market cap — several hundred times sales — while IonQ trades at roughly 68x on \$130M. The bookings inflection is real and the balance sheet plus CHIPS money removes near-term financing risk for about three years. But the market has now watched two quarters where a 1,120% bookings increase produced zero revenue growth, and it is repricing accordingly. The Q3 print in early November is the event that resolves this in one direction or the other.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a speculative sleeve position: 0.5-1.5% of a diversified portfolio, or 1-2% for an investor deliberately building basket exposure across QBTS, IONQ, RGTI and INFQ rather than picking a winner. Do not size this as a core technology holding.

**Entry strategy.** At \$16.78 the stock sits between the \$12.75 52-week low and the \$18.21 fifty-day average, below the \$21.00 two-hundred-day. Scaling in near \$13-15 offers a better risk/reward than adding here; a decisive reclaim of \$21 on a Q3 beat with recognized revenue above \$8M would be the confirmation signal for a fuller position.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$12.50 breaks the 52-week low and invalidates the base case; for a thesis-driven holder the fundamental stop is two more quarters of flat revenue with RPO not converting.

**What would change the rating.** Upgrade to SPEC. BUY on Q3/Q4 2026 revenue recognition above \$8-10M per quarter with RPO holding or growing, or on a second eight-figure system booking outside the U.S. government channel. Downgrade further on an equity raise below \$15, a filed securities class action, or a slip in the 2026 17-qubit gate-model delivery.

**Catalyst calendar.** Qubits Asia, Seoul — October 28, 2026. Q3 2026 earnings — early November 2026. Florida Atlantic Advantage2 deployment and 17-qubit gate-model delivery — by end of 2026. Headquarters move to Boca Raton — end of 2026. 49-qubit gate-model system — 2027. CHIPS milestone drawdowns — ongoing.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: mixed, deteriorating through Q3.** The technical and policy news flow in 2026 has been genuinely strong — a *Nature* publication, IDC Leader designation, the \$100M CHIPS agreement, two published roadmaps. The financial coverage has been the opposite. Headlines from Zacks, Yahoo Finance and TikR in September 2026 ran along the lines of "D-Wave Quantum's revenue hasn't moved in two years" and "profitability remains under pressure." The Q2 slide deck highlighted 37.6% commercial-revenue growth and 50% QCaaS growth; the market looked past both to the \$960K revenue shortfall.

**Analyst sentiment: constructive but stale.** Sixteen to seventeen analysts cover the name. MarketBeat shows 13 Buy, 2 Hold and 1 Sell — a Moderate Buy consensus with a \$36.27 average target, \$44 high and \$22 low. StockAnalysis reports a Strong Buy consensus from S&P Global's panel with a \$35.24 average. Even the lowest target on the Street implies roughly 30% upside from \$16.78, which tells you targets have not been fully marked down since the August print. Treat the consensus as a lagging indicator here, not a signal.

**Price action and technicals.** Down 33.7% over twelve months, about 64% below the \$46.75 high, and roughly 32% above the \$12.75 low. Below both the 50-day (\$18.21) and 200-day (\$21.00) averages, with the shorter average below the longer — a downtrend structure. InvestingPro publicly flagged the stock as overvalued ahead of a 45.9% decline, hitting its fair-value estimate of \$16.99 in August 2026. The September 17 pop of 9.2% on nothing more than a user-conference announcement, and a separate 9% jump on the CHIPS news, show how thin the float's conviction is in both directions.

**Retail and social.** QBTS remains one of the most heavily traded retail quantum names alongside RGTI and IONQ, and Motley Fool has been running comparison pieces ("Which quantum stock has the best shot at survival?", "D-Wave vs QuantumScape") throughout September — a reliable marker of retail attention. The tone in that coverage has shifted from roadmap enthusiasm toward survival and cash-runway questions. The plaintiff-firm press releases circulating since September 10 add a distinctly negative retail overlay.

**Short interest and options.** Elevated short interest and heavy option volume are characteristic of this cohort and contribute to the violent single-day moves noted above. Specific current short-interest and options-flow figures were not verified for this writeup, so no numbers are asserted.

**Composite sentiment score: 4/10.** Bullish long-term narrative, strong government validation, and a supportive analyst consensus, offset by a broken price trend, two revenue misses, accelerating burn, and an active legal overhang. Sentiment is negative on the tape and positive on paper; the tape has been right for twelve months.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: D-Wave sells quantum computers, and unlike most of its competitors it has been selling them to paying customers for years. It has two kinds now — the original "annealing" machines that are very good at one thing (finding the best option among an enormous number of possibilities, like scheduling or routing) and, since buying Quantum Circuits in January 2026, a second line of more general-purpose "gate-model" machines with clever built-in error detection.

The good news is that orders are pouring in. In the first six months of 2026 D-Wave signed \$35.5M of new business, twelve times the prior year, and it has \$40.7M of signed contracts waiting to be delivered. The U.S. government just committed up to \$100M and took a small equity stake. The company has \$546M in the bank, enough for roughly three years at the current spending rate.

The bad news is that signed orders are not the same as revenue. D-Wave only records revenue when it delivers, and it has not delivered much: sales were \$3.1M in the most recent quarter, exactly the same as a year earlier, and below what analysts expected. Meanwhile spending has doubled. So investors are paying about \$6.2B for a company with roughly \$12M of annual sales — a price that only makes sense if the order book converts and keeps growing.

The next three months decide a lot. A \$20M computer is supposed to be installed at Florida Atlantic University by the end of 2026, and the November earnings report will show whether the backlog is finally turning into sales. Until then this is a small, speculative position, not a core holding — a bet on quantum computing becoming a real industry this decade, held in a size you can afford to be wrong about.
