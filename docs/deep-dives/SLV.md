# SLV — iShares Silver Trust

SLV is the largest physically-backed silver ETP: 493,984,760 ounces of bullion (15,364.6 tonnes, ~\$30.28B of net assets) split across 546.75M shares, so each share carries roughly 0.9034 oz of silver less a 0.50% annual sponsor fee. The physical story has not changed — the Silver Institute projects a sixth consecutive annual deficit of 46.3M oz in 2026 on top of 762.1M oz of cumulative above-ground drawdown since 2021 — but the *price* has, violently. Silver peaked at \$121.62/oz on 29 January 2026 and trades near \$61 today, a roughly 50% drawdown driven almost entirely by real interest rates. SLV closed at \$54.95 on 2026-09-28 after a 5.5% single-day drop. That leaves a structurally bullish asset in a cyclically hostile regime, which is why this refresh moves the rating from BUY to **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

SLV is not a company. It is a grantor trust sponsored by BlackRock, launched 21 April 2006, that holds allocated silver bars in London vaults and issues shares representing an undivided interest in that metal. There is no management team making capital-allocation decisions, no revenue, and no earnings — the share price is the silver price, scaled by ounces per share and dragged down slowly by the fee.

**Mechanics that matter.** Shares are created and redeemed only in baskets (currently ~45,156 shares) by authorised participants, which is what keeps the market price pinned to NAV — the median spread of 0.02% and the small 0.79% discount on a violent down day show the arbitrage working. The fee is paid in kind: the trust sells silver daily, so ounces per share decline over time. Two decades of that compounding is real money — roughly \$950 of metal eroded on a \$10,000 position held 20 years.

**Competitive position.** SLV's moat is liquidity, not cost. At ~15.5M shares of average daily volume and a 0.02% spread it is the default institutional and options-market vehicle for silver exposure. Cheaper physical alternatives exist — abrdn's SIVR charges 0.30% versus SLV's 0.50% — and buyers who care more about basis points than depth should know that. US taxable investors should also note that gains on physically-backed metal trusts are taxed as collectibles, at rates up to 28%, rather than at the 20% long-term capital-gains rate.

## Financial Analysis

There is no income statement here, so the correct analogues are assets, flows, cost and tracking.

**Assets and AUM.** \$30.28B of net assets on 493.98M ounces. Because both the metal price and the ounce count move, AUM is a poor proxy for flows on its own: silver is down ~50% from its January record, so the fact that the trust still carries \$30B of assets tells you the ounce base has held up far better than the price. At the 29 January 2026 record of \$121.62/oz, a comparable ounce count would have implied roughly \$60B of net assets — the AUM halving is a price effect, not evidence of a mass exodus.

**Cost structure.** One line: a 0.50% annual sponsor's fee, settled by selling trust silver. That is the entire "expense base." It also means SLV structurally underperforms spot silver by ~0.5%/year before any tracking noise — visible in the fund's own numbers, where the trailing 1-year total return of 62.63% sits just under the LBMA benchmark.

**Returns.** Calendar 2025 was extraordinary: +147.86% total return. 2026 has given a large share of it back — the fund reported a -18.54% year-to-date total return through 31 August 2026, and the September selloff extended that. Trailing 1-year total return was still +62.63%, and the Yahoo Finance quote puts the 1-year price return at +29.3% as of 2026-09-28, the difference being which window you measure across the January spike. Longer-dated numbers remain strong: +157.8% over three years, +204.7% over ten.

**Tracking and liquidity quality.** Price closed 0.79% below NAV on 2026-09-28 — a discount, as you would expect on a day of forced selling — and the trust spent slightly more days at a discount than a premium in 2025 (110 versus 137 premium days is roughly balanced). A 0.02% median spread and 22.6M shares traded on 2026-09-28 versus a 15.5M 30-day average confirm the vehicle absorbed the volume without dislocating.

**Spot silver — the actual fundamental.** Silver traded \$60.68–61.86/oz on 28–29 September 2026 after falling 5.4% on Monday 28 September to about \$60.81. Gold settled near \$4,127/oz the same day, putting the gold/silver ratio at roughly 68:1, inside the 67–70 band that has held since mid-July.

**Supply and demand.** The Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 puts the 2025 deficit at 40.3M oz and forecasts 46.3M oz for 2026, the sixth consecutive shortfall, with cumulative above-ground drawdown since 2021 of 762.1M oz. Industrial fabrication hit a record 680.5M oz in 2024, about 59% of total demand, with solar PV alone at 17% of demand versus 5.6% in 2015. Mine supply cannot respond quickly because most silver is a byproduct of copper, zinc, lead and gold mining and therefore follows those metals' price signals, not silver's.

## Valuation

Silver has no cash flows, so "valuation" means the balance between real interest rates (which set the paper price) and the physical deficit (which sets the floor). The gap between them is the entire 2026 story: the deficit widened and the price halved.

Targets below are SLV share prices. Convert at ~0.9034 oz per share, so SLV ≈ spot silver × 0.90.

| Scenario | SLV target | Implied spot silver | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | \$72–81 | ~\$80–90/oz | 10-year TIPS real yield falls back below 2.0%; UBS path (\$70/oz Dec 2026, \$75/oz mid-2027, \$80/oz Sep 2027) is met or beaten; gold/silver ratio compresses toward 55:1; ETP creations rebuild the ounce base. |
| **Base** | \$60–67 | ~\$66–74/oz | At most one more Fed hike, policy rate topping near 4.1%; real yields hold 2.2–2.6%; industrial fabrication near records as AI and EV electrical demand offsets ~5% solar thrifting; ratio stays 62–70. |
| **Bear** | \$41–47 | ~\$45–52/oz | Real yields push past 2.6% on a hiking path toward 4.6%; crowded spec longs capitulate into thin shorts; oil shock tips industrial demand into recession; retest of the \$41.66 52-week low. |

Against \$54.95, that is roughly +9% to +22% in the base case, +31% to +47% in the bull case, and -14% to -25% in the bear case. The skew is positive but the near-term tape is not: SLV sits below its 50-day moving average (\$57.55) and well below its 200-day (\$65.95).

**The valuation anchor worth naming.** Sell-side consensus is more constructive than the price action. UBS expects \$70/oz by December 2026, \$75/oz in March and June 2027, and \$80/oz by September 2027. That is a forecast, not a company guide — there is no company to guide — and it depends on the same real-yield variable that has gone the wrong way all year.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Real yields rolling over.** The single highest-leverage catalyst. The 10-year TIPS yield rose ~20bp from mid-August to 2.55%, and that move explains most of the gap between gold's ~23% drawdown and silver's ~48–50%. A reversal works in the other direction with the same force.
- **A softer Fed path.** The FOMC hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%, its first increase since 2023, under Chair Kevin Warsh. Futures price roughly 4.1% by December 2026 and 4.6% by September 2027. Any dot-plot or CPI evidence that the path is shorter than priced unwinds a lot of the 2026 compression.
- **The sixth straight deficit.** 46.3M oz forecast for 2026 into a market that has already drained 762.1M oz of buffer since 2021. Physical tightness shows up in premiums and delivery queues before it shows up in the futures price; the two cannot diverge indefinitely.
- **Industrial demand mix shift.** Solar loadings per module are forecast to fall ~5% year over year, but AI data-centre electrical build-out, EV electrification and broader electronics — up 51% in silver usage since 2016 — have been offsetting it. Net industrial trajectory is still positive.
- **Monetary-demand overlay.** Investor and sovereign rotation out of fixed income toward hard assets, including reported Chinese and Indian silver import behaviour, adds a demand layer silver historically lacked and raises both the floor and the ceiling.
- **Gold/silver ratio compression.** At ~68:1, silver is cheap against gold relative to its long-run average. Silver has historically outperformed sharply in the late stage of precious-metals bull runs as the ratio closes.

## Risk Factors

- **Real yields, first and foremost.** At 2.55% on the 10-year TIPS, the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding metal is the highest it has been this cycle. If the 10-year Treasury holds near 4.97% and real yields extend, nothing in the physical story stops the paper price from grinding lower.
- **Higher beta than gold, both ways.** Silver fell roughly twice as far as gold from the January record. Any allocator sizing SLV like a gold position is mis-sizing the volatility, which the fund itself reports at a 44.35% three-year standard deviation.
- **Crowded positioning with no cushion.** Speculative net length rebuilt to 25.2% of COMEX open interest as of 8 September, the 73rd percentile of the past 60 weeks, while gross shorts stayed thin. Thin shorts mean no short-covering bid to catch a decline, and the rebuilt longs are the exposed position.
- **Industrial cyclicality.** Roughly 59% of silver demand is industrial fabrication. An oil-shock-driven slowdown — crude above \$100 after a Saudi pipeline attack, and further escalation after US-Iran talks collapsed on 28 September — hits that demand directly while simultaneously pushing the Fed to stay tight.
- **Solar thrifting.** Manufacturers keep reducing silver per cell. If that accelerates beyond the forecast ~5%, the fastest-growing demand line stalls even as installations rise.
- **Structural drags specific to the wrapper.** The 0.50% sponsor fee erodes ounces per share every day; US collectibles tax treatment can reach 28% on gains; there is no yield; and holders have no claim on specific bars and cannot redeem for metal below basket size.
- **SLV is the liquidity valve.** In risk-off unwinds, the most liquid metals instrument is what gets sold, which can amplify drawdowns beyond what spot alone would imply.

## Recommendation

- **Rating:** **HOLD** (downgraded from BUY on 2026-06-07)
- **Why the downgrade:** the physical thesis is unchanged and arguably stronger, but the mechanism setting the price has turned hostile. A Fed that is hiking, a 2.55% real yield, price below both the 50-day (\$57.55) and 200-day (\$65.95) moving averages, and speculative longs rebuilt rather than washed out is not a configuration to add aggressively into. The asymmetry is fine; the timing is not.
- **Position sizing:** 3–5% of a portfolio as a precious-metals sleeve, sized for 40%+ annualised volatility, not gold-like volatility. Existing holders: hold.
- **Entry strategy:** scale in on evidence, not on price alone. The trigger worth waiting for is the 10-year TIPS yield turning down through ~2.3%, or a reclaim of the 50-day moving average near \$57.55 on volume. Absent that, tranche buys in the \$47–52 area, where the bear-case zone and the January-to-September downtrend converge.
- **Stop loss / invalidation:** a weekly close below the \$41.66 52-week low breaks the structure and implies the market is repricing the deficit narrative itself, not just discounting rates.
- **Technicals in brief:** 52-week range \$41.66–109.83. Price \$54.95, 50-day \$57.55, 200-day \$65.95 — a clear downtrend with the 50-day below the 200-day. First resistance is the 50-day; \$60–62 is the next shelf. Support is the September lows, then \$47, then the 52-week low. The 5.5% drop on 2026-09-28 came on 22.6M shares against a 15.5M average, which is capitulation-flavoured volume rather than quiet drift.
- **Catalyst calendar:**
  - 14 October 2026 — September US CPI
  - 27–28 October 2026 — FOMC meeting
  - Early November 2026 — World Gold Council Q3 central bank demand data
  - November 2026 — Silver Institute Interim Silver Market Review

## Sentiment Analysis

**Overall: negative near-term momentum inside a constructive medium-term narrative. Score ~4.5/10.**

- **News tone.** Uniformly risk-off on the latest tape. Coverage of 28 September described gold and silver losing about \$1 trillion of combined market value in a day, with silver down 5.4% and gold down 3.7%, attributed to rising Treasury yields, a firmer dollar, an oil spike after US-Iran talks collapsed, and renewed rate-hike expectations. Headlines are about drawdowns and support levels, not upside.
- **Analyst and institutional view.** Constructive and notably above spot. UBS targets \$70/oz by December 2026 rising to \$80/oz by September 2027, and UBS's Giovanni Staunovo has publicly attributed the weakness to oil and rate-hike pricing rather than a change in fundamentals. Specialist silver research remains focused on the six-year deficit. The divergence between price and forecasts is the defining sentiment feature right now.
- **Positioning / options.** CFTC data show speculative net length at 25.2% of COMEX silver open interest as of 8 September, up from 19.7% on 18 August and in the 73rd percentile of the last 60 weeks. Gross shorts have stayed thin. Options activity in gold and silver has reportedly stayed elevated even as spot went quiet — traders are paying for movement, not conviction in direction.
- **Retail / social.** Silver retail communities remain highly engaged — the metal did put in a \$121.62 record eight months ago and finished 2025 up nearly 148% — but the tone has shifted from euphoria to defensiveness and dip-buying debate. Sentiment around the January top is now a liability: a lot of retail size was added above \$90/oz equivalent.
- **Flows.** SLV still holds 493.98M oz and \$30.28B of assets. The ounce base has been far more resilient than the price, which argues that holders have not panicked wholesale. The trust traded at a 0.79% discount on the selloff day — mild, and consistent with orderly selling rather than a dislocation.

**Read-through:** sentiment is stretched negative on a two-month horizon while the institutional forecast distribution sits 15–30% above spot. That combination historically resolves to the upside once the macro driver turns, which is exactly why this is a HOLD rather than a SELL.

## Readability Pass

Plain-English version of the thesis:

SLV is a big pile of silver bars in a London vault, chopped into shares. Buy a share and you own about nine-tenths of an ounce of silver, minus half a percent a year that BlackRock takes out in metal for running the thing.

The world is genuinely using more silver than it digs up — six years running now — because solar panels, EVs, electronics and data centres all need it, and most silver comes out of the ground as a leftover from copper and zinc mines, so miners cannot just make more when the price rises.

Despite that, silver has fallen by about half this year, from \$121.62/oz in January to about \$61 now. The reason is interest rates. Silver pays you nothing to hold it. When safe government bonds pay a high return *after* inflation — the "real yield," currently ~2.55% — holding a metal that pays nothing looks expensive, and investors sell. The Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023, which made that worse. Silver falls harder than gold in these moments because it is smaller, more industrial, and more traded by speculators.

So the two forces point in opposite directions. Physical scarcity says higher. Interest rates say lower. Right now interest rates are winning, and the chart is in a downtrend. The rating is HOLD: own some, do not chase it, and wait for real yields to turn before adding.

Jargon decoder:

- **ETP / ETF:** an exchange-traded product; here, a trust holding physical metal whose shares trade like a stock.
- **NAV:** net asset value — what the silver inside is actually worth per share. SLV can trade slightly above (premium) or below (discount) it.
- **Spot price:** the price for immediate delivery of one ounce of silver.
- **Real yield:** the bond interest rate after subtracting inflation. The key driver of precious-metal prices.
- **Gold/silver ratio:** how many ounces of silver buy one ounce of gold. High means silver is cheap versus gold. It is ~68 now.
- **Supply deficit:** demand exceeding mine plus recycled supply, filled by drawing down existing stockpiles.
- **Thrifting:** engineering out expensive material, for example using less silver per solar cell.
- **COT report:** the CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders, showing how speculators and commercial hedgers are positioned in futures.
- **Sponsor fee:** the annual charge, paid by selling trust silver, so ounces per share shrink over time.
- **Collectibles tax rate:** the US tax treatment applied to gains on physical metal trusts, up to 28% rather than the 20% top long-term capital-gains rate.
