## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Western Digital (now WD) is the world's largest manufacturer of hard disk drives (HDDs), serving cloud, enterprise, and consumer storage markets. Following the February 2026 spinoff of SanDisk (NAND flash), WD is a pure-play HDD company focused on nearline capacity drives for hyperscaler data centers and enterprise storage.

**Revenue Segment Breakdown:**

| Segment | % of Revenue | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Cloud & Data Center** | ~70% | Nearline HDDs for hyperscaler and enterprise data centers |
| **Client & Consumer** | ~30% | Desktop, notebook, and external storage drives |

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

**Competitive Advantages:**

- Duopoly with Seagate in the HDD market — together they control >95% of global production
- Technology leadership in ePMR and HAMR high-capacity drives (24TB+ platforms)
- Deep hyperscaler relationships for AI data lake and cloud storage architectures
- Post-SanDisk separation allows focused R&D and capital allocation on HDD

**Competitive Moat Assessment:** STRONG — duopoly economics provide structural pricing power

### Management Team Track Record

**Key Executives:**

- **David Goeckeler:** CEO (since 2020); led the SanDisk separation and HDD-focused strategy
- **Kris Sennesael:** CFO; strong execution on margin expansion

## 4. GROWTH CATALYSTS

1. **AI data lake demand** — hyperscaler AI training creates massive cold/warm storage needs that favor high-capacity HDDs
2. **HAMR technology ramp** — heat-assisted magnetic recording enables 30TB+ drives with higher ASPs
3. **Post-separation focus** — pure-play HDD story attracts dedicated storage investors
4. **Capacity pricing power** — duopoly dynamics with Seagate support supply discipline
5. **Q4 FY2026 momentum** — $3.75B revenue, 54.4% gross margin, $1.3B FCF in a single quarter

## 5. RISKS

1. **HDD cyclicality** — demand can decline 20–30% in a downturn; elevated margins may be near-peak
2. **SSD substitution** — falling NAND prices could accelerate HDD-to-SSD transition in some workloads
3. **Single-segment concentration** — no diversification buffer post-SanDisk separation
4. **Customer concentration** — hyperscaler ordering patterns are lumpy and can shift quickly
5. **Stock has rallied ~540% from 52-week lows** — substantial upside already captured
