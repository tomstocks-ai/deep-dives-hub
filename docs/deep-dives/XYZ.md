# XYZ — Block, Inc.

Block is the two-sided fintech behind Square (merchant commerce) and Cash App (consumer finance), and it is in the middle of the most aggressive margin reset in its history. Gross profit grew 25% year over year in Q2 2026 to \$3.17B while adjusted operating margin hit an all-time-high 27%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to \$12.51B of gross profit (+21%) and \$4.02 of adjusted diluted EPS (+70%). At \$73.31 the stock trades at roughly 18x guided adjusted EPS despite ~\$3.9B of trailing free cash flow — a discount that reflects three live doubts: whether a workforce cut of roughly 40% can be absorbed without damaging product velocity, whether Cash App's fast-growing lending book survives a consumer credit downturn, and how much GAAP earnings volatility the bitcoin balance sheet will keep importing.

## Company Overview

Block, Inc. was founded in 2009 as Square, renamed Block in 2021, and changed its ticker from SQ to XYZ in January 2025 while remaining NYSE-listed. It was added to the S&P 500 effective July 23, 2025. Market capitalisation is ~\$44.7B at \$73.31 on roughly 610M diluted shares.

The business is two ecosystems reported through three revenue lines:

- **Square** — point-of-sale hardware, payment processing, payroll, banking and software for sellers. Square gross payment volume was \$72.85B in Q2 2026, up 13.4% year over year.
- **Cash App** — a consumer financial app spanning peer-to-peer transfers, the Cash App Card, direct deposit, investing, bitcoin, and a rapidly scaling lending stack (Cash App Borrow plus the Afterpay buy-now-pay-later book acquired in 2022). Cash App generated \$1.97B of gross profit in Q2 2026, up 31%.
- **Reporting segments** — Commerce Enablement (\$3.34B Q2 revenue, +15.3%), Financial Solutions (\$1.38B, +40.3%), and Bitcoin Ecosystem (\$1.89B, -12.8%). Bitcoin is a large revenue line but a trivial profit line: \$72M of gross profit in Q2 2026, down 31%.

The moat is the combination of a merchant network and a consumer network that Block can cross-connect — Cash App users discovering Square merchants, Square sellers underwritten off Block's own payment data. Underwriting is the sharpest edge: Block sees the cash flows of the businesses and consumers it lends to, which is why Financial Solutions gross profit is compounding above 40% while the payments business grows in the teens.

Management is Jack Dorsey as Block Head (CEO) with Amrita Ahuja as COO and CFO. In February 2026 Dorsey announced an AI-driven restructuring that eliminated roughly 4,000 roles, targeting a workforce below 6,000 from a little over 10,000 — one of the largest single-day AI-attributed reductions in tech. Block's internal agent framework, "goose", is the stated mechanism. Secondary coverage has cited a net savings target in the \$800–900M range; I could not confirm that figure in a company filing, so treat it as an estimate rather than company-guided.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025) was the inflection year:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | \$24.19B | \$24.12B |
| Revenue growth | +0.3% | +10.1% |
| Gross profit | \$10.36B company-reported (+17%) | \$8.96B |
| Gross margin | ~43% | ~37% |
| Operating income | \$1.76B | \$1.04B |
| Net income | \$1.31B | \$2.90B |
| Diluted EPS | \$2.10 | \$4.56 |
| Free cash flow | \$2.43B | \$1.55B |

The revenue line is close to useless as a growth signal because bitcoin resale revenue — \$8.50B in FY2025, falling — is booked gross at near-zero margin. Gross profit is the metric management guides and the market watches, and it accelerated through the year: +18% in Q3 2025, +24% in Q4 2025 to \$2.87B, +27% in Q1 2026 to \$2.91B, +25% in Q2 2026 to \$3.17B. FY2024's higher net income and EPS are not a deterioration story — they were inflated by a large one-time tax valuation-allowance release.

Q2 2026 (reported 5 August 2026) is the latest quarter: revenue \$6.62B (+9.3%, ahead of the ~\$6.54B consensus), gross profit \$3.17B (+24.8%), adjusted operating margin 27% (a record), adjusted diluted EPS \$1.02 (+65%) against a ~\$0.48 consensus. Commerce Enablement gross profit rose 18% to \$1.81B and Financial Solutions gross profit rose 42.9% to \$1.29B.

The balance sheet and cash flow are the reassuring part. Cash and investments were \$6.74B at 30 June 2026 against \$7.17B of total debt — roughly net-debt-neutral (~\$0.4B net debt). Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow was \$4.06B and free cash flow \$3.88B on \$176M of capex, a 15.5% FCF margin. Block also holds 9,032 BTC, worth ~\$703M in September 2026.

The caution is the gap between adjusted and GAAP profitability. TTM GAAP net income through June 2026 was only \$357M (Q2 2026 GAAP net income was ~\$89M) versus \$1.31B for FY2025, because bitcoin is now carried at fair value and 2026's crypto drawdown flows straight through the income statement. Trailing GAAP P/E is therefore ~125x while forward adjusted P/E is ~16–18x. Both numbers are real; the adjusted series is the better read on operations, and the FCF line supports it.

## Valuation

At \$73.31 the setup is:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Market cap | ~\$44.7B |
| Enterprise value | ~\$45B (near-zero net debt) |
| EV / FY2026E gross profit (\$12.51B) | ~3.6x |
| Price / FY2026E adjusted EPS (\$4.02) | ~18x |
| Forward P/E (next twelve months) | ~16x |
| Price / TTM FCF (\$3.88B) | ~12x |
| Price / sales (TTM) | ~1.8x |

Roughly 12x free cash flow for a business compounding gross profit at 21% with an expanding margin is not a demanding price. The market is applying a discount for execution and credit risk, not for growth doubt. Street consensus sits well above the tape: 42–46 covering analysts, consensus "Moderate Buy" to "Buy", average twelve-month target \$95–\$98, high \$125, low \$55, with no sell ratings. BTIG reaffirmed Buy with a \$100 target on 28 September 2026, Cantor Fitzgerald Overweight at \$95 on 25 September, Evercore \$96.

**Scenarios (twelve months):**

- **Bull: \$105–\$120.** Gross profit growth holds near 20% into FY2027, the restructuring savings land and adjusted operating margin pushes past 30%, Cash App lending scales without a credit break, and FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~\$5.50. At 20x that is ~\$110.
- **Base: \$88–\$96.** FY2026 guidance is met, FY2027 gross profit grows mid-to-high teens, FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near \$5.20, and the multiple holds at 17–18x. This is roughly where the sell-side consensus sits.
- **Bear: \$50–\$58.** Consumer credit deteriorates and loan-loss provisions absorb the margin gains, the headcount reduction visibly slows product velocity, Square GPV growth decelerates into single digits, and the multiple compresses to 11–12x on flat ~\$4.30 adjusted EPS. The \$48.21 fifty-two-week low is the reference floor.

Technically the stock is caught between its moving averages — below the 50-day (~\$80.02) and above the 200-day (~\$69.93) — after a 4.1% drop on 28 September. The 52-week range is \$48.21 to \$86.92 and the one-year return is -2.8%, even though the stock is up double digits year to date from a weak start.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Cash App lending is the profit engine.** Financial Solutions gross profit is growing above 40% against high-teens growth in payments. Borrow and Afterpay underwritten off Block's own transaction data is the highest-return capital in the company, and it is still early in its penetration of the Cash App base.
- **The AI cost reset.** If the February 2026 restructuring holds, Block carries a structurally lower cost base into FY2027. Management has already delivered a 27% record adjusted operating margin and guides to 28% for the full year; the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide of \$4.02 implies ~70% growth largely from operating leverage rather than revenue.
- **Square and Cash App cross-pollination.** Local merchant discovery inside Cash App and Cash App as a tender type at Square sellers turn two separate networks into one flywheel — the single largest untapped strategic asset.
- **Guidance credibility.** Q3 2026 is guided to 18% gross profit growth with adjusted diluted EPS up ~89%. Block has beaten its own gross profit guide for several consecutive quarters; another beat on 5 November would reset the narrative.
- **Banking and lending expansion.** Square Financial Services, Block's own bank charter, lets the company hold more of its lending economics rather than renting balance sheet.
- **Bitcoin optionality.** Bitcoin is currently a drag, but the 9,032 BTC treasury, Cash App bitcoin distribution, and Block's mining-hardware and self-custody efforts are free call options on a crypto recovery that nothing in the current price pays for.

## Risk Factors

- **Consumer credit.** This is the main one. Block has deliberately levered its profit growth to lending into subprime-adjacent consumer segments. A rise in unemployment or delinquencies would hit the fastest-growing, highest-margin part of the company first, and provisions arrive faster than growth decelerates.
- **Restructuring execution.** Cutting roughly 40% of headcount and betting the difference on AI agents is unproven at this scale. Attrition of key engineers, slower shipping, or degraded customer support would show up in gross profit growth two to four quarters from now, not immediately.
- **GAAP earnings volatility.** Fair-value accounting on the bitcoin treasury means reported net income swings with crypto prices, independent of operations. TTM GAAP net income of \$357M against \$1.31B in FY2025 is the demonstration.
- **Square competitive pressure.** Toast, Shopify, Clover, Stripe and Adyen all attack parts of the seller stack. Square gross profit growth of ~9–18% is healthy but not insulated, and hardware-plus-software bundles are increasingly commoditised.
- **Regulatory and compliance overhang.** Block has a history of federal and state scrutiny of its anti-money-laundering and consumer-compliance programmes, and third-party coverage in 2026 continues to reference open federal inquiries. I could not verify the current status of any specific matter, so size this as an unquantified tail risk rather than a known liability.
- **Bitcoin revenue decline.** Bitcoin Ecosystem revenue fell 12.8% and its gross profit fell 31% in Q2 2026. The profit contribution is small, but the revenue optics depress headline growth and complicate the story for generalist investors.
- **Insider selling and sentiment.** A director sold 18,000 Class A shares between 24 and 28 September 2026 under a pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plan; the stock fell 4.1% on the final day. Mechanically benign, but it is what the tape is currently reacting to.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Bull \$105–\$120 / Base \$88–\$96 / Bear \$50–\$58.

The core argument is that Block is being priced as a story stock while it behaves like a cash machine: ~12x trailing free cash flow, ~18x guided adjusted EPS, 21% gross profit growth, record margins, and effectively no net debt. Consensus sits 30% above the current price and no covering analyst rates it a sell.

- **Position sizing** — a standard full position for a diversified fintech sleeve, not an oversized one. The credit-cycle exposure and the restructuring bet mean this can lose a third of its value in a consumer downturn without the thesis being wrong on product.
- **Entry** — \$73 is already an attractive entry, sitting just above the 200-day (~\$69.93) and well below the 50-day (~\$80.02). Scaling in works better than a single fill: start here, add on a hold of the 200-day, and add again on a reclaim of \$80 with the 50-day turning up. A break below \$69.93 on volume argues for waiting rather than averaging down.
- **Stop loss** — a close below ~\$62 (roughly 15% below the current price and decisively through the 200-day) invalidates the technical setup. A thesis stop matters more: two consecutive quarters of rising Cash App lending loss rates or a gross profit guide cut.
- **Catalyst calendar** — Q3 2026 results expected 5 November 2026; Q4 and FY2026 results with initial FY2027 guidance in February 2027; monthly consumer credit and delinquency data throughout; bitcoin price as a continuous swing factor on reported GAAP results.
- **What would change the rating** — an upgrade to a higher-conviction stance needs one clean quarter showing the cost savings sticking with gross profit growth intact. A downgrade needs credit deterioration or evidence the headcount cut is damaging product velocity.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Overall: moderately positive on fundamentals, negative on near-term price action.**

- **Analysts** — the most bullish constituency. 4 strong buy, 29 buy, 9 hold, 0 sell; average target \$95.42 across 42 analysts on one tally and \$98.15 across 46 on another; high \$125, low \$55. BTIG reaffirmed Buy at \$100 on 28 September and Cantor reaffirmed Overweight at \$95 on 25 September, both after the stock had already sold off — targets are not being cut into weakness.
- **News tone** — constructive. Q2 2026 coverage led with the record 27% margin, the 25% gross profit growth and the raised guide. The dominant 2026 narrative is the "AI reset": Dorsey's headcount cut and the goose agent framework have been covered widely and largely as a strategic bet rather than a distress signal, though the tone is wait-and-see rather than celebratory.
- **Retail and social** — mixed and impatient. The bull case is well understood and repeated (cheap on FCF, lending compounding, Cash App network reignited); the bear case leans on the DOJ and credit-risk overhang and on the observation that the stock has gone nowhere in a year. Independent newsletters covering the name near \$79 called it "high conviction, high tolerance only", which is a fair summary of the retail mood.
- **Options flow** — currently tilted bearish. A recent options-sentiment read scored the name bearish with a modest conviction reading. Earlier in 2026 a large-trade sweep showed 35% bullish versus 57% bearish positioning by trade count, though call premium outweighed put premium by roughly five to one — bearish in count, bullish in dollars.
- **Positioning** — short interest was 12.85M shares at 31 August 2026, just 2.41% of float and down ~6% from the prior report. There is no crowded short here; the pressure is from disappointed longs, not new bears.
- **Insider activity** — one director sold 18,000 shares in three tranches from 24 to 28 September 2026 under a 10b5-1 plan adopted in March 2026. Pre-scheduled, so low information content, but it was the proximate trigger for the 4.1% drop on 28 September.
- **Score: 6.5/10.** Fundamental sentiment is clearly positive and analyst support is unanimous in direction; short-term flow, insider optics and a year of flat performance keep enthusiasm capped.

## Readability Pass

Block runs two big businesses: Square, which sells card readers and software to shops and restaurants, and Cash App, which is a money app used by tens of millions of consumers. Ignore Block's headline revenue — it is distorted by bitcoin that Block buys and resells at almost no profit. Watch gross profit instead. That grew 25% last quarter, and Block now keeps 27 cents of operating profit from every dollar of gross profit, the best it has ever managed.

The reason profits are jumping faster than sales is twofold. Block is lending more — to consumers through Cash App Borrow and Afterpay, and to merchants through its own bank — which is the most profitable thing it does. And in February 2026 it cut roughly 4,000 jobs, about 40% of its people, replacing a lot of internal work with AI agents. Management says FY2026 adjusted earnings will be \$4.02 per share, up about 70%.

The stock at \$73 costs about 18 times those earnings and about 12 times the cash the business actually generates, which is cheap for something growing this fast. Wall Street's average target is around \$95.

Two things could go wrong. If the economy weakens and borrowers stop paying, the lending profits reverse quickly — that is the real risk. And cutting 40% of staff might quietly slow down the product work that drives future growth; nobody will know for another year. Rating: BUY, with \$105–\$120 if it works, \$88–\$96 if things go as planned, and \$50–\$58 if credit turns.
