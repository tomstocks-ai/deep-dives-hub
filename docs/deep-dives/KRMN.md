# KRMN — Karman Holdings Inc.

Karman Space and Defense is a sole-source or limited-source supplier of the structural and propulsive hardware that surrounds a missile or a rocket: payload protection systems, hydro/aerodynamic interstage systems, and propulsion and launch systems. Operationally it is one of the best-positioned suppliers in the fastest-growing corners of the U.S. defense budget — Q2 FY2026 revenue rose 58.2% to a record \$182.1 million with 24.4% organic growth, adjusted EBITDA margin of 30.0%, and record backlog of \$1.3 billion. Yet the stock closed at \$33.68 on 2026-09-28, down ~53% over twelve months and ~72% from its 52-week high of \$118.38. The gap between the operating results and the share price is the whole story: an abrupt CFO transition, an auditor change, a short-seller report on internal controls, and an unproven ability to convert 30% EBITDA margins into cash have made investors unwilling to pay for the growth until the books are clean. Rated **SPEC. BUY** — the franchise is real, the discount is large, and the Q3 report on 5 November 2026 is the binary event.

## Company Overview

Karman designs and manufactures mission-critical hardware for programs where failure is not recoverable, which is why its content tends to be qualified onto a program and stay there for its life. The product families are:

- **Payload Protection Systems** — fairings, shrouds, nose cones and radomes that protect a warhead or satellite through boost and reentry.
- **Hydro/Aerodynamic Interstage Systems** — the structural transitions between rocket stages, and, since the Seemann Composites and MSC acquisitions, the composite and resin structures used on submarine programs.
- **Propulsion and Launch Systems** — solid rocket motor cases, nozzles, pressure vessels and launch structures.

The business is organised around four end markets. On the FY2025 full year, Hypersonics and Strategic Missile Defense contributed \$150.0 million, Space and Launch \$149.8 million, and Tactical Missiles and Integrated Defense Systems \$171.7 million, on \$471.5 million of total revenue. Maritime Defense Systems was broken out as a fourth end market in 2026 following Seemann/MSC and contributed \$33.6 million in Q2 FY2026 alone. In that same quarter Tactical Missiles was the growth leader at +55.4% year over year, Hypersonics grew 24.2%, and Space and Launch grew only 6.3% as launch schedules slipped — a reminder that the "space" label understates how defense-weighted the revenue really is.

The moat is qualification and switching cost rather than technology per se. Karman serves more than 150 prime contractors and programs, which limits single-program concentration, and its content sits on THAAD, PAC-3, NGI, GMLRS, hypersonic test beds, classified programs, liquid-fuelled engine content for legacy and emerging launch providers, and Columbia and Virginia class submarines. The company cites nearly 50 years of operating history and is headquartered in Huntington Beach, California.

Ownership and management are where the story gets complicated. Karman IPO'd in February 2025 raising \$581 million and completed a \$1.2 billion non-dilutive secondary in 2025, meaning the sponsor has been selling into a short public history. Jon Rambeau became CEO during 2026, succeeding Tony Koblinski. CFO Mike Willis' departure was announced on 26 August 2026 with Chris Boynton taking over effective 14 September 2026 — described by the company as deliberate succession planning, though no such process had been disclosed previously. RBC has separately flagged an auditor change. Three senior-level changes plus an auditor change inside eighteen months of an IPO is the single biggest reason this stock trades where it does.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was \$471.5 million, up 36.6% from \$345.3 million, with gross profit of \$190.0 million (40.3% gross margin, up from 38.3%). GAAP net income was \$17.4 million, or \$0.13 per diluted share, held down by \$44.6 million of interest expense and \$12.7 million of transaction-related costs. Adjusted EBITDA was \$145.3 million, a 30.8% margin.

FY2026 has accelerated sharply. H1 revenue was \$333.3 million, up 54.9%, with gross profit of \$142.1 million (42.6% margin) and adjusted EBITDA of \$99.4 million (29.8% margin). Q2 alone delivered \$182.1 million of revenue (+58.2% total, +24.4% organic), \$78.2 million gross profit, \$34.8 million operating income, \$14.0 million net income, \$0.11 GAAP EPS and \$0.14 adjusted EPS. Management raised FY2026 guidance to \$730–745 million of revenue and \$215.0–222.5 million of adjusted EBITDA, implying roughly 56% revenue growth and ~50% adjusted EBITDA growth at the midpoints.

The margin structure is stable and genuinely good for a structures supplier: adjusted EBITDA margin has sat in a tight 29.8%–31.2% band across FY2024, FY2025 and H1 FY2026, and RBC notes the post-IPO acquisitions have been broadly consistent with that ~30% profile. Interest expense is the main drag between EBITDA and EPS, and a completed debt repricing is expected to cut annual interest cost by about \$4 million.

**Balance sheet.** At 30 June 2026: cash \$51.7 million, total assets \$1,444.0 million, total liabilities \$1,022.9 million, stockholders' equity \$421.1 million. Debt is \$756.9 million of term note (\$5.6 million current plus \$751.3 million long term) plus \$102.1 million of finance lease liabilities and \$16.2 million of operating leases. Net funded debt is therefore ~\$705 million, or ~3.2x the midpoint of FY2026E adjusted EBITDA; including finance leases it is ~\$807 million, or ~3.7x. Goodwill and intangibles total \$824.9 million against \$421.1 million of equity — a direct consequence of the acquisition programme and a reason any impairment or purchase-accounting question would hit hard.

**Cash flow — the weak link.** Karman did not present a cash flow statement in either the FY2025 or Q2 FY2026 earnings releases I reviewed, so I am not putting a free cash flow figure in this write-up. What is visible on the balance sheet is that contract assets rose from \$107.2 million (end-2024) to \$156.3 million (end-2025) to \$187.1 million (30 June 2026), and receivables from \$55.2 million to \$78.7 million to \$115.0 million, while revenue roughly doubled. Working capital is absorbing cash as the company scales. RBC describes the free cash flow outlook as "murky" and notes management guides to positive FCF in H2 FY2026. Until that is on paper, EBITDA quality is an open question, and that is exactly the gap the short thesis exploits.

Share count is stable at 132.5 million basic and diluted, so none of the growth has come from equity dilution since the IPO.

## Valuation

At \$33.68 on 132.5 million shares the equity is worth roughly \$4.5 billion. Adding ~\$705 million of net funded debt gives an enterprise value of about \$5.2 billion; including finance leases, ~\$5.3 billion.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA (\$218.75M midpoint) | ~24x |
| EV / FY2026E revenue (\$737.5M midpoint) | ~7.0x |
| P / FY2026E adjusted EPS (consensus ~\$0.59) | ~57x |
| P / FY2027E adjusted EPS (consensus ~\$0.95) | ~35x |
| Net funded debt / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA | ~3.2x |
| FY2025 GAAP P/E (\$0.13 EPS) | ~259x |

Two things follow. First, the de-rating has been violent but the stock is not statistically cheap — 24x forward EV/EBITDA is still roughly a 60–80% premium to mature primes like General Dynamics and L3Harris at 13–15x. J Capital made exactly this argument, citing an EV/EBITDA near 28x when the shares were higher. Second, the premium is not absurd for a business compounding revenue above 50% with 30% margins and a backlog up 65% year to date; the question is whether that growth is organic, cash-generative and auditable.

On my own FY2027 build — high-teens organic growth plus Walker, implying roughly \$265 million of adjusted EBITDA, which is my estimate and not company-guided — today's price is about 19–20x FY2027E EV/EBITDA. That is the multiple a clean, high-growth defense supplier can defend; it is not a multiple that survives a restatement.

**Scenario targets**

- **Bull: \$60–\$75.** The 5 November print clears the air, H2 free cash flow comes in positive, the three pending long-term agreements above \$1 billion combined are signed, and the shares re-rate to ~22–24x FY2027E EBITDA. This is still below the ~\$88–90 sell-side average target, which I regard as stale relative to the governance risk.
- **Base: \$40–\$50.** FY2026 lands inside guidance, organic growth moderates toward the high teens, free cash flow turns modestly positive but thin, leverage stays near 3.5x, and the multiple recovers only partway to ~20–22x forward EBITDA as the accounting questions fade without a clean resolution.
- **Bear: \$20–\$26.** An internal-controls finding or restatement emerges, cash conversion stays negative, prime contractors second-source Karman content, and the multiple compresses toward the 13–15x that mature primes command.

## Growth Catalysts

**End-market demand is the strongest part of the story.** Management points to more than \$90 billion of recent prime contractor awards for THAAD and PAC-3 interceptors and over \$76 billion for new Columbia and Virginia class submarines. Karman has qualified content on both families. Interceptor and munitions restocking is a multi-year, appropriations-backed cycle, not a sentiment trade.

**Bookings and backlog.** Q2 FY2026 bookings were nearly \$500 million across all end markets, including a large multi-year contract with a space and launch customer. Backlog reached \$1.3 billion at 30 June 2026, up 65% from the end of FY2025 — roughly 1.8x FY2026E revenue, which is unusually good visibility for a components supplier. Management is actively negotiating three further long-term defense agreements with combined potential value above \$1 billion.

**The pivot to multi-year prime procurement.** The U.S. government's shift toward multi-year prime contracts converts what used to be annual purchase orders into long-term agreements. For a qualified sole-source supplier that structurally improves revenue visibility and capacity-planning economics.

**Maritime as a fourth leg.** Seemann Composites and MSC, acquired in January 2026, took Karman from "deep space" into "deep sea" and delivered \$33.6 million of Maritime Defense Systems revenue in Q2 FY2026 alone off a zero base. Submarine composite and resin content is a long-cycle, budget-protected franchise.

**Europe via Walker Precision Engineering.** The agreement to acquire Walker for approximately \$94 million opens the European defense supply chain at a moment when NATO members are raising procurement budgets. It is small relative to the base but strategically the first non-U.S. platform.

**Alternative-supplier wins.** Management describes "going on offense" — winning second-source positions on programs where it was not previously qualified, and new content areas. This is the organic growth engine that has to work for the bull case, because it is the answer to the second-sourcing bear case.

**Capacity and cost.** Gross property, plant and equipment rose from \$134.8 million to \$173.0 million in six months as Karman scales to existing program requirements. The completed debt repricing should save about \$4 million of annual interest.

## Risk Factors

**Governance and accounting — the dominant risk.** Three items compound: the CFO transition announced 26 August 2026 without a previously disclosed succession process; an auditor change flagged by RBC; and the J Capital short report published in early September 2026 on its Substack "The Equity Dispatch," which argued Karman disclosed acquisitions as "not material" despite paying notably high prices and that "ongoing weak internal controls at Karman, given difficult accounting challenges for defense products, increase our concern." Karman had not formally responded as of the Motley Fool's 4 September write-up, though RBC's 27 September note says the company has since gone on the offensive with disclosures on margins, growth, acquisitions and working-capital efficiency. Short sellers have an economic interest in the outcome and should be discounted accordingly — but a material weakness or restatement is the one outcome that breaks the thesis rather than delaying it.

**Cash conversion.** Revenue nearly doubled while contract assets grew to \$187.1 million and receivables to \$115.0 million. If cash generation does not inflect in H2 FY2026 as guided, the company funds growth with debt at 3.2–3.7x leverage or with equity at a depressed price.

**Leverage and acquisition dependence.** \$756.9 million of term debt plus ~\$102 million of finance leases, and \$824.9 million of goodwill and intangibles against \$421.1 million of equity. Reported growth of 58% versus 24% organic means roughly two-thirds of the top-line increase came from deals — Seemann/MSC, Five Axis, MTI, ISP, now Walker. Each adds integration risk and purchase-accounting judgement.

**Valuation.** ~24x FY2026E EV/EBITDA leaves no margin for error even after a ~72% drawdown. Multiple compression alone accounts for most of the bear case.

**Customer, program and second-sourcing risk.** Karman sells to prime contractors who have both the incentive and, increasingly, the urgency to qualify second sources and negotiate price. RBC argues these concerns are overdone given the need to raise output, but the risk is structural. Space and Launch revenue grew only 6.3% in Q2 as launch schedules shifted, and SLS revenue has been declining — a reminder that program timing cuts both ways.

**Budget and political risk.** Revenue depends on U.S. defense appropriations. RBC explicitly ties recent weakness to investor caution ahead of the U.S. midterm elections. A continuing resolution or a delayed FY2027 appropriation pushes awards right.

**Market structure.** Short interest was 6.94% of float as of 15 June 2026. The shares set repeated 52-week lows through September 2026 and sit well below the 50-day (~\$46) and 200-day (~\$70) moving averages. There is no technical support in evidence yet.

## Recommendation

**SPEC. BUY.** The rating reflects a genuinely good business trading at a governance discount, with a dated catalyst that resolves the central question.

The case for owning it: Karman is, in RBC's words, the best end-market-positioned supplier in missiles, missile defense, space, hypersonics and maritime. Backlog is up 65% year to date to \$1.3 billion, organic growth is 24%, adjusted EBITDA margin is a stable 30%, guidance has been raised twice in 2026, and the demand backdrop is appropriations-backed rather than narrative-driven. Consensus sits at roughly \$88–90 against a \$33.68 price, and even the lowest published sell-side target of \$37 is above the market.

The case for caution: none of that matters if the numbers are wrong. A CFO exit, an auditor change, and a short report on internal controls arriving inside six weeks is a pattern, not noise, and the absence of a disclosed free cash flow figure makes the EBITDA hard to verify independently. That is why this is SPEC. BUY and not BUY.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a half-size or smaller speculative position — 1–2% of a portfolio, not a core defense holding. Investors who require audited comfort should simply wait for 5 November; the cost of waiting is the risk of a gap up, which is an acceptable price for avoiding a restatement.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is in a confirmed downtrend: price \$33.68 against a 50-day moving average near \$46 and a 200-day near \$70, with a 52-week range of \$32.20–\$118.38 and successive new lows through September. Scale in rather than taking a single position. A first tranche in the low \$30s near the \$32.20 low is defensible for investors sizing small; adding on a reclaim of the 50-day moving average after a clean Q3 gives up some upside in exchange for confirmation. Momentum indicators have been persistently weak — this is a falling-knife chart, and the discipline is tranches, not conviction sizing.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$28 on volume — roughly 17% below the current price and decisively through the 52-week low — invalidates the "de-rated quality" read and puts the bear case of \$20–\$26 in play. Any 8-K disclosing a material weakness, restatement or SEC inquiry should be treated as an immediate exit regardless of price.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: negative and deteriorating.** The 2026 news flow reads as an unbroken sequence of setbacks — repeated 52-week lows on 9, 17, 22 and 28 September; headlines such as "Why Karman Holdings Stock Was Sliding This Week" (28 August, CFO exit) and "Why Karman Holdings Stock Was Wilting Again This Week" (4 September, short report). Shares fell 7.2% on 9 September, 3.7% on 17 September and 6.3% on 28 September. The one positive note in the period is framing rather than fact: a Yahoo Finance piece arguing the CEO transition strengthens the long-term bull case.

**Sell side: constructive but retreating.** Consensus is "Moderate Buy" per MarketBeat with an average target of \$88.09 across 12 analysts; stockanalysis.com reports "Strong Buy" and \$89.70 across 11 analysts polled by S&P Global; TipRanks shows a lower \$77.86 average. The range is wide — \$135 high, \$37 low — which is itself a sentiment signal: analysts disagree about whether this is a franchise or an accounting problem. The direction of travel is down: Citigroup raised to \$78 on 10 August, then Weiss cut its grade on 10 September, then RBC cut from \$85 to \$70 on 27 September while keeping Outperform. Targets are being marked toward the price rather than the price rising toward targets.

**Bear positioning.** Short interest was 8.66 million shares, 6.94% of public float, as of 15 June 2026 — elevated for a large-cap industrial but not a crowded short. J Capital's report is public and unrebutted in detail as of this writing. The activist-short overhang is the primary reason a 24x multiple has not found buyers.

**Retail and social.** I did not find quantified retail or social sentiment data for KRMN in this session. The qualitative signal available is that the Motley Fool disclosed a house position in and recommendation of Karman while publishing critically on it, and that its "Moneyball Superscore" shows 70/100. Ticker Nerd's systematic factor model ranks KRMN 16 of 100 as of 21 September 2026 — weakest quartile — which is a momentum-driven score, consistent with the chart rather than the fundamentals.

**Options flow.** Not available in this session; I make no claim about positioning.

**Net sentiment score: 3/10.** Price action, news tone and factor rankings are all negative; sell-side ratings and the backlog are the only positives, and the sell side is cutting. Sentiment this poor on fundamentals this good is the setup — but it is also a warning that the market may know something the press releases do not.

## Readability Pass

Karman makes the hard parts that go around missiles and rockets — the nose cones, the interstage structures, the motor cases — and more recently composite parts for submarines. Its customers are the big U.S. defense primes, and once its parts are certified onto a program they are very hard to replace.

Business performance is excellent. Revenue grew 37% in 2025 to \$471.5 million and is growing 55%+ in 2026, with a record \$1.3 billion order backlog and profit margins near 30%. Management raised the forecast for this year twice.

The stock has still fallen by half in a year, and by about 72% from its high. That is not because the business deteriorated. It is because investors stopped trusting the reporting: the CFO left abruptly in August, the auditor was changed, a short-selling firm published a report in September questioning the company's internal controls and how it accounts for its many acquisitions, and Karman has not yet published a free cash flow number proving its profits turn into cash. When a company has been public for barely eighteen months and all of that happens at once, investors sell first and ask questions later.

So the situation is simple to state. If the 5 November quarterly report is clean and shows cash coming in, the stock is very cheap relative to what analysts think it is worth (roughly \$88 on average, versus \$34 today). If it is not clean, this gets worse. My scenarios are \$60–\$75 if it clears, \$40–\$50 if it muddles through, and \$20–\$26 if the accounting concerns prove real.

Verdict: worth owning small, as a speculation on a good business with a bookkeeping cloud over it. Not worth owning large until the cloud lifts. Anyone who needs certainty should wait for 5 November.
