# SATL — Satellogic

Satellogic is a vertically integrated Earth observation (EO) company that designs, builds, launches, and operates its own satellites, and it is increasingly positioning itself as the infrastructure layer for what it calls **Persistent Global Intelligence (PGI)** — AI-driven, always-on geospatial intelligence for defense and government customers. With 20+ satellites in orbit, ~72% gross margins, first-ever positive operating cash flow in Q1 2026, and the AI-first **Merlin** constellation set to launch around October 2026, SATL is a high-conviction-if-it-executes story wrapped around one binary catalyst. This report refreshes the thesis as of July 28, 2026, at a share price of \$3.54.

---

## Company Overview

**Business model.** Satellogic controls the entire EO stack — satellite design, manufacturing, launch procurement, on-orbit operations, and data delivery. Most revenue comes from selling imagery, analytics, and Constellation-as-a-Service (CaaS) capacity rather than from one-off hardware sales, which is why gross margins run near 72% — closer to a software business than a traditional satellite manufacturer.

**The PGI repositioning.** Over Q2 2026 the company sharpened its identity from "cheap high-resolution imagery" to being the infrastructure company for **Persistent Global Intelligence**: continuous, AI-processed monitoring delivered as decision-ready intelligence rather than raw pixels. Two partnerships anchor this pivot — **SynMax** (announced June 23, 2026) and **SpaceKnow** (announced June 30, 2026) — both bringing AI-powered, planetary-scale analytics aimed squarely at defense and government end markets.

**Sovereign / technology-transfer model.** A differentiated go-to-market: Satellogic sells satellites plus technology transfer to nations building their own space programs, with existing relationships spanning Portugal, Malaysia, Albania, and Australia. This creates large, lumpy contracts and recurring downstream data relationships.

**Management.** Co-founder **Emiliano Kargieman** remains CEO. The board added **Lt. Gen. (Ret.) Michael E. Williamson**, meaningfully strengthening defense credibility as the company leans into government demand. Offsetting that, **CFO Rick Dunn is stepping down** after a transition period — Roth characterized it as an "orderly management transition," but a CFO change during a scaling phase is a governance risk worth flagging (see Risk Factors). Dual-class share structure keeps founders in voting control.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (reported May 12, 2026) — the inflection quarter.**

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Detail |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Revenue | \$6.1M | +80% YoY; beat \$5.27M consensus |
| Adjusted EBITDA loss | Improved ~32% YoY | Operating leverage showing up |
| Operating cash flow | Positive | **First-ever positive operating cash flow** |
| GAAP EPS | -\$0.84 | Driven by a ~\$113M **non-cash** change in fair value of financial instruments — not operational |

The single most important takeaway: the large GAAP loss is an accounting artifact of marking warrants and convertible instruments to fair value, **not** a cash operating loss. Underneath it, the core business generated cash for the first time.

**Trailing and full-year figures.**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| FY2025 revenue | \$17.71M (+37.6% YoY) |
| FY2025 net loss | -\$4.78M |
| TTM revenue | ~\$20.4M (+58% YoY) |

**Reading the -\$90.5M TTM net loss.** This looks alarming next to a -\$4.78M FY2025 loss, but the swing is dominated by non-cash fair-value remeasurement of financial instruments (the same line that produced the -\$0.84 Q1 GAAP EPS). Investors should anchor on revenue growth, gross margin, adjusted EBITDA trend, and — now — operating cash flow, rather than GAAP net income.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** The company entered 2026 with a strengthened balance sheet after prior raises and now benefits from positive operating cash flow, which reduces (but does not eliminate) the odds of a near-term dilutive raise. A **10M-share block trade priced at \$9.94** during the year was a secondary/liquidity event rather than a primary capital raise, but it underscores how quickly supply can hit a small float.

## Valuation

At \$3.54 with ~148.2M shares outstanding, market cap is ~\$525M against TTM revenue of ~\$20.4M — roughly **26x TTM sales**. That is rich on today's revenue but typical for a high-growth, pre-scale space-tech name with 72% gross margins and a binary hardware catalyst pending.

**Analyst view.** Consensus is **Strong Buy** with an average 12-month target of ~\$11.28 — Roth \$15, Northland \$11, Cantor \$10, Freedom \$10.40. The Street's targets cluster around the bull case, which reflects both genuine optimism about Merlin/PGI and the thin-coverage optimism bias common to small-cap growth names. Our base case is deliberately more conservative than the sell-side average.

**Comparable context.** Planet Labs (PL) trades as the scale leader with ~200 satellites and ~56% gross margins; Satellogic's edge is unit economics (~72% gross margin) and the sovereign/technology-transfer model, at a fraction of the revenue base.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Merlin AI-first constellation launch (~October 2026)** — the key binary catalyst. Onboard AI processing enables 1-meter daily global remapping with real-time detection and alerting, a capability defense/intelligence customers pay premium prices for. Success reframes the entire valuation; failure or delay is the central bear risk.
- **\$18M one-year international defense contract (won May 26, 2026)** — the customer moved from trial to full-scale deployment in under six months, validating both the product and the land-and-expand motion. Renewal and expansion are near-term proof points.
- **SpaceKnow (June 30, 2026) and SynMax (June 23, 2026) partnerships** — AI-powered / planetary-scale geospatial intelligence for defense and government, cementing the **Persistent Global Intelligence** positioning and opening analytics-layer revenue on top of raw imagery.
- **Sovereign / technology-transfer replication** — Portugal, Malaysia, Albania, and Australia relationships form a template; each additional nation-level program is a large, high-visibility contract.
- **Board and defense credibility** — the addition of Lt. Gen. (Ret.) Michael E. Williamson supports pursuit of larger US and allied government programs.
- **Path to profitability** — first-ever positive operating cash flow in Q1 2026 plus a narrowing adjusted EBITDA loss point toward operational break-even in the 2027-2028 window if growth holds.

## Risk Factors

- **Binary Merlin launch (~Oct 2026).** A launch failure or material delay would damage credibility, likely stall the defense pipeline, and could force dilutive financing. This single event dominates the risk profile.
- **Customer concentration.** A small number of large defense and sovereign customers drive the majority of revenue and backlog; the loss or non-renewal of one materially dents the growth story.
- **CFO transition / governance.** Rick Dunn is stepping down after a transition period. Even an "orderly" CFO change during a scaling phase raises execution and continuity risk until a permanent successor is named and settled.
- **GAAP optics and dilution.** The -\$90.5M TTM net loss (mostly non-cash fair-value charges) will keep GAAP headlines ugly, and warrant/note conversions plus secondary supply (e.g. the 10M-share block at \$9.94) can dilute or overhang the small float.
- **Competition.** Planet Labs' 200+ satellite constellation and Maxar's entrenched government relationships compete for the same defense and commercial budgets.
- **Small-cap liquidity and volatility.** A 52-week range of \$1.26–\$12.00 and beta of 1.27 mean sharp moves in both directions on news or macro risk-off.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** Satellogic offers an asymmetric, catalyst-driven setup: 72% gross margins, +58% TTM revenue growth, a broadening defense/PGI pipeline, and — new this quarter — positive operating cash flow, all ahead of a single make-or-break Merlin launch. The reward (\$10–12 bull) meaningfully exceeds the risk (\$1.50–2 bear) from \$3.54, but the outcome is genuinely binary, so this is a speculative-sizing position only.

**Position sizing.** Speculative bucket — roughly 0.5–2% depending on risk tolerance. Do not size it as a core holding.

**Entry strategy.** Scaling in is prudent given the binary October catalyst: build a partial position now, keep reserve to add on a confirmed successful Merlin launch, and avoid chasing pre-launch spikes.

**Technical context.** Shares trade at \$3.54, well off the \$12.00 high and roughly 2.8x the \$1.26 low. With beta 1.27 and RSI/EMA visible on the widget above, expect elevated volatility into the August earnings print and the October launch. A break and hold above the mid-single digits would likely require Merlin de-risking; a slide toward the \$2 handle would signal the bear scenario gaining traction.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: Satellogic builds and flies its own camera satellites and sells the pictures — and, increasingly, AI-processed answers ("what changed here and when") to militaries and governments. It is small (~\$20M of revenue) but keeps about 72 cents of every sales dollar as gross profit, which is unusually good for a hardware company. This quarter it generated cash from operations for the first time and beat expectations with 80% revenue growth, even though the official "net loss" looks huge — that loss is mostly a paper accounting adjustment, not real cash going out the door.

The whole story hinges on one event: launching the AI-powered **Merlin** satellites around October 2026. If that works, the defense pipeline (a new \$18M contract plus the SynMax and SpaceKnow deals) could compound and the stock could run to \$10–12. If it fails or slips, the stock could fall to \$1.50–2. That is why the rating is **speculative buy**: the upside is larger than the downside from \$3.54, but it is a binary bet — size it small.
