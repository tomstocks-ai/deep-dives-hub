# TE — T1 Energy Inc.

T1 Energy Inc. (NYSE: TE), formerly FREYR Battery, is a U.S. solar manufacturer building the country's first fully integrated silicon solar supply chain. Its G1_Dallas module plant (~5 GW) is operational and drove a step-change in FY2025 revenue to \$755.3M, while the ~2.1 GW G2_Austin cell fab is under construction and targeted online by Q4 2026. A pending ~\$32M acquisition of KORE Power pushes TE into battery energy storage and data center infrastructure. At \$4.90 (2026-07-28), this remains a high-beta, policy-sensitive speculative story.

---

## Company Overview

**Business model.** T1 Energy is executing a vertical-integration strategy across the U.S. crystalline-silicon solar value chain (polysilicon → wafers → cells → modules). The company rebranded from FREYR Battery to T1 Energy after pivoting away from its original battery-cell roadmap and acquiring U.S. solar manufacturing assets.

- **G1_Dallas (operational):** ~5 GW annual TOPCon module capacity in Wilmer, Texas. This facility is the engine behind the FY2025 revenue ramp and received an "A" bankability grade from Intertek CEA in June 2026 — an important commercial signal for utility-scale buyers underwriting long-life projects.
- **G2_Austin (under construction):** ~2.1 GW solar cell fab in Milam County, Texas, targeted online around Q4 2026. Once running, it closes the loop on domestic cell supply and reduces dependence on imported cells.
- **KORE Power (pending, ~\$32M, announced 2026-06-03):** extends TE into battery energy storage systems (BESS) and data center infrastructure — a deliberate diversification beyond pure solar-module manufacturing and a bridge back toward the company's battery heritage.

**Revenue segments.** Today revenue is dominated by utility-scale module sales from G1_Dallas, supplemented by Section 45X advanced-manufacturing tax credits. Cell sales (post-G2) and BESS/data center offerings (post-KORE) are the intended future legs.

**Moat.** The differentiator is being among the only fully integrated U.S. silicon solar manufacturers. Domestic content lets developers capture IRA bonus credits, and FEOC/tariff dynamics on foreign polysilicon and cells structurally favor U.S.-made supply. The moat is policy-reinforced rather than purely technological.

**Management.** CEO Daniel Barcelo leads the company through the FREYR-to-T1 transition, the G2 build-out, and the KORE Power expansion.

---

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was \$755.3M, up from just \$2.94M in FY2024 — a near-total transformation reflecting the G1_Dallas ramp rather than organic growth off a real base. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is ~\$879.5M. Profitability, however, remains deeply negative: FY2025 net loss was -\$380.8M with diluted EPS of -\$1.88, and TTM net income is -\$385.1M. Encouragingly, Q1 2026 (reported 2026-05-12) delivered record quarterly adjusted EBITDA and record gross margin, evidence that operating leverage is starting to work as volumes scale.

**Balance sheet.** Leverage is the central financial risk. Debt/equity is ~178% (1.78x) — high, but materially improved from the prior ~269%. The current ratio of ~1.25 is thin but positive. The April 2026 pricing of \$125M of upsized convertible senior notes due 2031 eased near-term balance-sheet stress and helps fund G2_Austin.

**Cash flow.** Free cash flow is negative, driven by the capital intensity of the G2_Austin build. Section 45X credits are a critical cash and margin lever, and management has targeted a comprehensive financing package around Q2/Q3 2026 to fully fund the remaining build-out. Additional capital raises remain a realistic possibility.

## Valuation

At \$4.90 the market cap is ~\$1.37B on ~279.3M shares, against ~\$755M FY2025 revenue and ~\$879.5M TTM revenue — roughly 1.6–1.8x sales for a fast-scaling but loss-making manufacturer. With negative earnings, the stock trades on a forward EBITDA/optionality narrative (G2 completion, 45X monetization, KORE diversification) rather than trailing multiples. First Solar remains the profitable, well-capitalized benchmark the bull case points to for multiple expansion.

**Scenarios (must match the JSON):**

- **Bull — \$14–16:** G2_Austin comes online near Q4 2026 and completes the integrated supply chain; KORE Power opens BESS and data center markets and re-rates the multiple; 45X credits are monetized, supporting record margins; comprehensive G2 financing closes on favorable terms; domestic-content and tariff dynamics favor U.S. modules.
- **Base — \$9–10:** G1_Dallas sustains ~5 GW run rate; G2 financing closes and production starts near Q4 2026 with some slippage; KORE closes and contributes modest 2027 revenue; 45X credits partially monetized; one further raise likely. This aligns with the ~\$10.07 consensus 12-month target.
- **Bear — \$3–4:** balance-sheet stress forces further dilutive financing; policy weakens 45X; G2 or KORE stumble; Chinese oversupply crushes module pricing; the short thesis and high beta drive a de-rating.

## Growth Catalysts

- **G2_Austin completion (~Q4 2026):** adds ~2.1 GW of domestic cell capacity and completes vertical integration, structurally improving cost and IRA-qualification positioning.
- **KORE Power acquisition (~\$32M, closing H2 2026):** diversifies TE into BESS and data center infrastructure — arguably the highest-optionality catalyst, tying the solar business to surging grid-storage and data center power demand.
- **Section 45X monetization:** continued credit sales convert manufacturing scale directly into cash and margin, a repeatable lever as volumes grow.
- **Comprehensive G2 financing (Q2/Q3 2026):** closing a full financing package would remove the single biggest overhang on the equity.
- **Bankability momentum:** the June 2026 Intertek CEA "A" grade for G1_Dallas lowers the barrier for large utility-scale offtake.
- **Policy tailwinds:** FEOC rules, Section 232 polysilicon dynamics, and a domestic-manufacturing-friendly policy backdrop all favor integrated U.S. producers.

---

## Risk Factors

- **Balance sheet and liquidity:** even at ~178% D/E with a ~1.25 current ratio, the capital intensity of G2 and the KORE deal means further dilutive financing is a live risk despite the \$125M convertible raise.
- **Profitability unproven:** FY2025 net loss of -\$380.8M and TTM -\$385.1M; record Q1 2026 EBITDA is encouraging but the durable path to net profit is not yet demonstrated.
- **Policy dependency:** the thesis leans heavily on Section 45X credits and domestic-content rules; adverse changes would hit margins and demand.
- **Execution and integration:** G2_Austin timing/financing and KORE Power integration both carry meaningful execution risk.
- **Commodity/competition:** Chinese oversupply can crush module prices; First Solar competes from a position of profitability and scale.
- **Short-seller and volatility risk:** Fuzzy Panda Research published a short report on 2026-05-19 (Roth Capital and others defended the company); with a beta of 2.17 and a 52-week range of \$1.15–\$12.49, drawdowns can be severe.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** T1 Energy offers a differentiated, policy-advantaged bet on integrated U.S. solar manufacturing with genuine diversification optionality via KORE Power — but it is unprofitable, highly leveraged, and subject to short-seller pressure and high volatility. Size positions accordingly: this belongs in the speculative sleeve of a portfolio, not as a core holding.

- **Position sizing:** keep it small (a low single-digit percent of portfolio) given beta 2.17 and binary financing/policy outcomes.
- **Entry strategy:** at \$4.90 the stock sits well below the ~\$10.07 consensus target; scaling in on weakness rather than chasing rallies is prudent given the wide \$1.15–\$12.49 range.
- **Catalyst calendar:** Q2 2026 earnings on 2026-08-19; KORE Power close in H2 2026; comprehensive G2 financing in Q2/Q3 2026; G2_Austin production start ~Q4 2026.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: T1 Energy makes solar panels in Texas and is one of the only companies trying to build the entire silicon solar chain inside the U.S. Its big Dallas factory is running and just produced its first real revenue year (\$755M, up from almost nothing), but the company still loses a lot of money and carries heavy debt. It is building a second Texas plant (Austin, for solar cells) due around the end of 2026, and it is buying KORE Power to get into batteries and data center power. Government manufacturing credits (Section 45X) are central to the story. The stock is cheap versus where analysts think it could go (~\$10 average target vs \$4.90 today), but it is risky: it needs more funding, depends on policy, and has been targeted by a short seller. It swings hard, so treat it as a small, speculative bet — attractive if the Austin plant and financing come together, dangerous if they slip. Bull \$14–16, Base \$9–10, Bear \$3–4.
