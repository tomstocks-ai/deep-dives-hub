# IMSR — Terrestrial Energy Inc.

Terrestrial Energy (NASDAQ:IMSR) is a development-stage advanced nuclear company building the Generation IV **Integral Molten Salt Reactor (IMSR)**, a small modular reactor (SMR) that runs on widely available Standard-Assay Low Enriched Uranium (SALEU) rather than scarce HALEU fuel. At \$5.11 (2026-07-28) the stock trades near its 52-week low, down 80%+ from post-SPAC highs, giving the company a ~\$541M market cap. A ~\$292M October 2025 Nasdaq raise left ~\$280M of cash on the balance sheet — multi-year runway — while a May 2026 NRC Safety Evaluation Report, a Texas A&M RELLIS site lease, and a Riot Platforms data-center collaboration have all advanced the thesis. It remains a high-risk, high-reward speculative bet: no revenue is expected until ~2030 and the closely watched TETRA pilot criticality is still pending and unconfirmed.

---

## Company Overview

**Business model.** Terrestrial Energy designs and intends to commercialize the IMSR, a Generation IV molten salt SMR that delivers zero-carbon electricity and high-temperature industrial heat. The reactor operates at ~585°C — far hotter than conventional light-water reactors (below 300°C) — enabling industrial-heat, hydrogen, and data-center applications, with load-following and black-start capability to complement renewables.

**Key differentiator.** Unlike many advanced-reactor rivals that depend on High-Assay Low Enriched Uranium (HALEU), the IMSR uses SALEU — the same class of fuel that has powered conventional reactors for decades. That choice sidesteps the HALEU supply-chain bottleneck and reduces geopolitical and regulatory uncertainty.

**Revenue model (pre-revenue today).** Management envisions a razor-and-blades lifecycle model once plants are operating in the early 2030s: pre-construction services, construction and component supply, recurring Core-unit replacements roughly every seven years, and ongoing fuel-salt supply and O&M. Over a multi-decade plant life the recurring core and fuel revenues are expected to dominate, giving the model high customer retention.

**Regulatory moat.** The IMSR was the first molten salt reactor to complete the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC) Vendor Design Review and the first MSR to secure meaningful NRC engagement. Terrestrial has since deepened that lead — see Financial Analysis and Growth Catalysts.

**Management.** Founder Simon Irish continues as CEO, backed by a team with substantial nuclear, regulatory, and energy experience, including former NRC and CNSC staff. In 2026 the company appointed **Tyler Gronbach** (formerly of Wolfspeed) as VP of Investor Relations and Public Relations, sharpening its public-markets communication after the SPAC listing.

## Growth Catalysts

**Regulatory de-risking.** On 2026-05-12 the NRC issued a **Safety Evaluation Report approving Terrestrial's Postulated Initiating Events topical report**, a concrete step that narrows licensing uncertainty; the company has also submitted additional safety analysis to the NRC. Further topical-report approvals through 2026 would continue building the licensing foundation.

**First commercial site.** On 2026-06-17 Terrestrial signed **ground-lease and R&D agreements with The Texas A&M University System at the RELLIS campus**, securing exclusive access to ~77 acres for its first planned IMSR commercial deployment and for site characterization work.

**AI / data-center power demand.** On 2026-05-06 Terrestrial launched a **collaboration with Riot Platforms** to develop nuclear-powered large-scale data center projects, directly linking the IMSR to surging AI and data-center electricity demand — a narrative that has re-rated several nuclear names.

**TETRA pilot reactor.** The TETRA pilot was targeted for criticality in the near term under a DOE fast-track program. There is **no public confirmation that criticality has been achieved**, so it should be treated as a pending near-term catalyst, not an accomplished fact. A successful, on-schedule criticality would be the single most powerful proof point for the technology.

**Longer-term.** A first **Construction Permit application (targeted 2028)** would mark the transition from design and licensing toward buildout and eventual revenue.

**TAM.** The IMSR targets zero-carbon baseload electricity and industrial heat across OECD markets — a multi-hundred-billion-dollar opportunity spanning grid power, industrial process heat, hydrogen, coal-plant repowering, and 24/7 data-center supply.

## Risk Factors

**Business and execution.** The NRC has never licensed a non-water-cooled commercial reactor, so licensing timelines are inherently uncertain. The IMSR is a first-of-a-kind design, and deployment carries technical and schedule execution risk. Competitors including NuScale, Oklo, Kairos, and X-energy are pursuing the same SMR opportunity.

**TETRA milestone risk.** Because TETRA criticality is pending and unconfirmed, any slippage or technical setback would likely pressure sentiment given how central the milestone is to the near-term narrative.

**Financial.** The company is pre-revenue with no meaningful revenue expected until ~2030. While ~\$280M in cash provides multi-year runway, commercialization will ultimately require additional capital, creating longer-dated dilution risk.

**Macro and sector.** Cheap natural gas and renewables-plus-storage compete for the same load; shifts in nuclear policy, uranium markets, or public perception could slow permitting and deployment.

**Market signal.** The stock is down 80%+ from SPAC highs and sits near its 52-week low (\$4.38–\$31.50 range) — a clear sign of market skepticism that raises volatility around every catalyst.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** Terrestrial Energy suits investors with a high risk tolerance and a multi-year horizon who want venture-style exposure to the nuclear/SMR renaissance. The improved balance sheet (~\$280M cash, multi-year runway), concrete regulatory progress (May 2026 NRC SER), a secured first-deployment site at Texas A&M RELLIS, and a Riot Platforms data-center tie-in have all strengthened the thesis versus prior coverage, while valuation near 52-week lows offers asymmetric upside if catalysts land.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a small, venture-style allocation (roughly 1–3% for most, more only for dedicated nuclear bulls) sized so that a total loss would not impair the portfolio.

Technically the shares are basing near multi-month support; watch the 52-week low around \$4.38 as the key downside reference and prior resistance in the \$8–10 zone (the base-case target band) as the first upside checkpoint. ## Readability Pass

In plain English: Terrestrial Energy is trying to build a new kind of small nuclear reactor that uses liquid (molten) salt instead of water. Its big advantage is that it runs on ordinary reactor-grade uranium (SALEU) that is easy to buy, rather than the scarce, expensive enriched fuel (HALEU) many rivals need. The company doesn't sell anything yet and won't earn real revenue until roughly 2030.

The good news in 2026: it raised a large amount of cash (ending 2025 near \$298M, about \$280M now), so it can fund years of work without scrambling for money. Regulators (the NRC) approved an important safety document in May 2026, it locked up land at Texas A&M for its first plant, and it teamed up with Riot Platforms to explore powering data centers — a hot area because of AI's electricity appetite.

The catch: this is still a bet on the future. The pilot reactor (TETRA) was supposed to "turn on" (reach criticality) soon, but there's no confirmation it has, so don't assume it. If licensing drags, the reactor hits snags, or rivals win first, the stock could fall toward \$3–4. If the milestones land, it could run to \$14–18. That's why it's rated a speculative buy — high risk, high potential reward, small position size.
