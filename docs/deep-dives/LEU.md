# LEU — Centrus Energy Corp.

Centrus Energy is the only NRC-licensed producer of High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium (HALEU) in the United States and the only publicly traded pure-play enricher in the West. At \$140.37 (September 28, 2026) the stock has fallen ~70% from its \$464.25 52-week high and ~55% over one year, even as the company signed a \$900M DOE HALEU enrichment award, grew total backlog to \$4.5B, and added commercial HALEU offtakers. The de-rating reflects three real problems: a ~\$500M equity-and-warrant raise in September 2026 that materially dilutes a 20.45M-share base, a free cash flow burn that is accelerating as the multi-billion-dollar Piketon and Oak Ridge buildout ramps, and a DOE FY2027 budget that no longer funds the legacy HALEU Operation Contract. The asset is genuinely strategic; the equity is a financing story until the centrifuges spin.

## Company Overview

Centrus Energy Corp. (NYSE: LEU) supplies nuclear fuel components to utilities in the U.S., Japan, the Netherlands and elsewhere, and is restoring domestic uranium enrichment capability at scale. It has supplied more than 1,850 reactor-years of fuel since 1998 and runs with roughly 467 employees.

**Business segments (Q2 2026 revenue mix):**

- **Low-Enriched Uranium (LEU) — \$153.4M, 87% of revenue.** Sells separative work units (SWU), natural uranium hexafluoride, uranium concentrates and conversion, and enriched uranium products to nuclear utilities under medium and long-term contracts. Within Q2, SWU revenue was \$100.0M and uranium revenue was \$53.4M.
- **Technical Solutions — \$22.7M, 13% of revenue.** Technical, manufacturing and engineering services, historically dominated by the DOE HALEU Operation Contract at Piketon, Ohio, which is billed cost-plus-incentive-fee.

**Competitive position and moat:**

- Sole NRC-licensed HALEU production facility in the United States, with close to three years of production history and roughly one metric ton delivered to DOE as of mid-2025. HALEU (5–20% U-235 enrichment) is required by most advanced reactor and SMR designs.
- Only publicly traded, operating enricher in the Western market. Peers are state-backed (Orano, Urenco) or pre-revenue startups (General Matter, Global Laser Enrichment).
- American Centrifuge technology is U.S.-origin and "unobligated," which makes the output usable for national security and space missions that foreign-origin fuel cannot serve.
- Total backlog of \$4.5B as of June 30, 2026, extending to 2040.

**Management:** President and CEO Amir Vexler leads the expansion program. Insider ownership is minimal at ~0.22%; institutions hold ~85% of shares.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **\$900M DOE HALEU enrichment award, signed July 2026.** Finalized terms of a task order to deploy commercial-scale HALEU capacity at the American Centrifuge Plant in Piketon. Includes options for up to \$170M of HALEU purchases for DOE missions, taking total contract value with all options to ~\$1.07B. The company intends to transition the demonstration cascade to commercial operation.

2. **January 1, 2028 Russian import waiver cliff.** Waivers permitting Russian LEU imports terminate no later than January 1, 2028 under the Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act, and DOE has stated there is no plan or statutory mechanism to extend them. U.S. utilities must source replacement enrichment. This is the single largest structural demand driver for domestic SWU pricing.

3. **Commercial HALEU offtake contracts.** In Q2 Centrus signed a first-of-a-kind large-scale commercial HALEU supply agreement that potentially includes prepayments. On September 17, 2026 it signed a definitive multi-year HALEU supply contract with Antares Nuclear covering compact microreactors for the U.S. Army, Air Force and Space Force, with deliveries before the end of the decade and prepayments that help fund expansion.

4. **Backlog conversion.** Total backlog of \$4.5B extends to 2040. The LEU segment holds ~\$3.7B, including ~\$3.0B of contingent LEU and HALEU commitments, ~\$2.4B of which sit under definitive agreements tied to construction of Piketon capacity. Converting contingent to firm is the key value-unlock mechanic.

5. **Construction execution.** Geiger Brothers was selected as construction contractor for the major enrichment plant expansion. Centrus has locked larger commitments with suppliers deemed critical to insulate the project from price swings. The first new Oak Ridge centrifuge is targeted for completion by year-end 2026 — a hard, near-dated, verifiable milestone.

6. **SWU pricing.** Management reports healthy demand with persistently constrained supply putting upward pressure on SWU prices; average SWU selling price rose 3% year over year in Q2 despite lower volumes.

7. **Warrant capital.** The September offering includes common warrants over up to 6,992,382 shares across four series. Exercises would bring in substantial non-dilutive-to-thesis growth capital if the stock recovers — the flip side of the dilution risk.

## Risk Factors

### Business risks

- **Well-funded competition on the same DOE dollar.** DOE awarded \$900M each to Centrus, Peter Thiel-backed General Matter, and French state-owned Orano in January 2026, plus \$28M to Global Laser Enrichment for SILEX development. Per Politico reporting, General Matter offered 355 tonnes of HALEU annually for its \$900M against Centrus's initial 12 tonnes per year scaling up after 2030 — a roughly 30x gap in stated ambition for identical funding. If General Matter clears NRC licensing anywhere near that target, the scarcity premium in LEU's valuation erodes.
- **DOE FY2027 defunding of the HALEU Operation Contract.** The proposed DOE FY2027 budget does not include further funding for operating the HALEU cascade, and DOE has communicated it does not currently intend to exercise further options. That contract represents approximately \$0.8B of Technical Solutions backlog. Technical Solutions was already loss-making at the gross line in Q2.
- **Execution risk on a multi-billion-dollar buildout.** Commercial-scale production is targeted for 2029. Centrifuge manufacturing scale-up, permitting, and a 1,000-job construction program all have to land roughly on schedule.
- **Customer concentration.** Revenue depends on a small number of utilities and government counterparties, plus sales backlog that is heavily contingent.

### Financial risks

- **Dilution.** The September 11, 2026 offering priced ~\$500M at a combined \$199.64 per share and warrant: 500,000 shares of Class A common, pre-funded warrants for 2,005,513 shares, and common warrants over up to 6,992,382 shares. Against ~20.45M shares outstanding, full warrant exercise is a large increase in the share count. Shares outstanding are already up ~31% year over year.
- **Negative free cash flow.** Trailing-twelve-month FCF is ~-\$164M and FY2026 capital deployment guidance is \$350–\$500M. Operating cash flow turned negative in H1 2026.
- **Weak GAAP earnings quality.** Operating income is ~\$0.5M on a trailing basis; reported profit leans on investment income from the cash balance. Altman Z-Score of ~2.67 and Piotroski F-Score of 3 are not comfortable readings.
- **Lumpy revenue.** LEU contracts carry annual, not quarterly, purchase commitments, so quarterly results swing on delivery timing and contract vintage pricing.

### Macro and sector risks

- **SWU and uranium price sensitivity.** The entire margin structure rests on enrichment pricing staying firm through the buildout. Q2 already showed unit costs rising faster than prices.
- **Policy dependence.** Appropriations, government shutdowns, NRC licensing, and DOE contracting posture all drive outcomes Centrus does not control.
- **Geopolitics cuts both ways.** The domestic enrichment push is a response to Russian supply risk. Any easing of that risk reduces urgency; escalation raises it.
- **Volatility.** The stock has had 85 moves greater than 5% in the past year. Beta ~1.33 understates the realized whipsaw.

## Recommendation

**Rating:** SPEC. BUY

**Rationale:** The strategic case is stronger than it was three months ago — the \$900M DOE contract is signed rather than proposed, backlog grew to \$4.5B, and Antares is a real commercial HALEU offtaker with prepayments. The stock is nonetheless 70% off its high because the near-term equity math got worse: a ~\$500M dilutive raise, negative free cash flow, and DOE walking away from the legacy cascade contract. At \$140 with ~\$33.78 per share of net cash and a monopoly license, the downside is bounded by assets while the upside depends on 2029 production. That combination is a speculative buy, not a core holding, and it earns the "SPEC." qualifier on funding risk rather than on business quality.

## Readability Pass

**What Centrus actually does:** Uranium straight out of the ground is mostly the wrong isotope. Centrus runs centrifuges that spin uranium gas until the useful isotope concentrates. The unit they sell is a "separative work unit," or SWU — literally the amount of spinning work performed. Utilities buy SWU to make reactor fuel.

**Why HALEU matters:** Today's reactors run on uranium enriched below 5%. Most next-generation designs, including small modular reactors, need 5–20% — that is HALEU. Centrus holds the only U.S. license to make it, and its output is "unobligated," meaning it carries no foreign restrictions and can be used for defense and space missions.

**The 2028 deadline:** U.S. utilities have been allowed to keep importing Russian enriched uranium under temporary waivers. Those waivers end January 1, 2028 and the law has no extension option. Somebody has to fill that gap, which is why Washington handed out \$2.7B in enrichment awards.

**Why the stock fell 70% anyway:** Building enrichment plants costs billions, and Centrus does not produce at commercial scale until roughly 2029. In September it sold about \$500M of new stock and warrants to fund construction, which shrinks the ownership slice of existing shareholders. Meanwhile the government's proposed FY2027 budget stops paying for the old HALEU demonstration contract — roughly \$0.8B of expected future revenue that may not arrive.

**What "contingent backlog" means:** Of the \$4.5B backlog, about \$3.0B only becomes real if Centrus actually builds the plants. It is a promise of orders conditional on delivery, not money in the bank.

**Short interest at 26.5%:** More than one in four shares are borrowed and sold by traders betting on a decline. If a milestone lands well, those traders must buy back — which can move the price violently upward. The reverse is also true.

**Net cash of \$33.78 per share:** Cash minus debt works out to about a quarter of the share price. It cushions the downside, but it is committed to construction, so it is not returnable value.
