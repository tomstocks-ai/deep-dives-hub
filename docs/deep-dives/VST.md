# VST — Vistra Corp.

Vistra Corp. (NYSE: VST) is the largest competitive integrated retail electricity and power generation company in the United States, with roughly 44,000 MW of generation capacity and ~5 million retail customers. Its diversified fleet — natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar, and battery storage — sits at the center of the AI-driven power demand boom, supplying baseload and flexible power into the tightest U.S. markets, especially Texas (ERCOT) and PJM. Q2 2026 ongoing operations adjusted EBITDA surged 31% year-over-year to \$1.77B, and management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of \$6.8B–\$7.6B while pointing to a 2027 midpoint opportunity of \$7.4B–\$7.8B. At \$137.09 (2026-08-30), the stock trades near the bottom of a wide 52-week range and at just ~13.2x forward earnings, while the analyst consensus mean target of \$217 implies roughly 58% upside. We rate Vistra a **BUY** for investors who can tolerate high utility-sector leverage and regulatory noise.

## Company Overview

Vistra is a vertically integrated power company: it owns and operates generation assets, sells electricity into wholesale markets, and serves retail customers through brands such as TXU Energy and Dynegy Energy Services. Headquartered in Irving, Texas, the company employs approximately 6,390 people and is organized into Retail, Texas, East, West, and Asset Closure segments.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---|---|
| **Retail** | Sells electricity and related products to ~5 million residential, commercial, and industrial customers across multiple states. Provides stable cash flows and a natural hedge to generation output. |
| **Texas** | Wholesale generation in ERCOT, including the state's largest merchant nuclear fleet (Comanche Peak) and substantial natural-gas capacity. |
| **East** | PJM, ISO-NE, and NYISO generation assets, with large baseload and peaking gas plants plus nuclear exposure. |
| **West** | Generation in California and other Western markets, including solar and battery storage. |
| **Asset Closure** | Management of retired or retiring coal and other legacy assets; typically a cost center rather than a profit driver. |

Vistra's generation stack spans natural gas (the largest share), nuclear, coal, solar, and battery storage. That fuel diversity is a strategic asset in an era of volatile gas prices and intermittent renewable growth: the fleet can capture high scarcity pricing in tight markets while nuclear units provide zero-carbon baseload eligible for production tax credits.

### Competitive Moat

Vistra's moat rests on **scale, geographic concentration in tight markets, and retail-wholesale integration**:

- **Scale:** ~44,000 MW of capacity makes Vistra one of the largest competitive power producers in the U.S., giving it procurement, operational, and hedging advantages.
- **Market position:** A leading share of ERCOT generation and a meaningful presence in PJM, the two markets most exposed to data-center load growth.
- **Retail backbone:** ~5 million customers generate recurring margins and provide a natural demand hedge.
- **Nuclear PTC downside protection:** The Inflation Reduction Act's nuclear production tax credits support cash flows from the zero-carbon fleet.
- **Balance-sheet optionality:** Strong projected free cash flow supports buybacks, debt reduction, and selective growth.

The moat is not unassailable — new entrants, subsidized renewables, and tech-owned generation projects are real threats — but Vistra's incumbent position in constrained markets is hard to replicate quickly.

### Management

CEO Jim Burke and the executive team have focused on three priorities since the 2021 restructuring: (1) stabilizing and growing the retail platform, (2) returning excess capital via buybacks, and (3) positioning the generation fleet for a power-demand renaissance driven by data centers and electrification. Since November 2021, Vistra has repurchased ~\$6.5B of stock and reduced its share count by roughly 30%. Management has expressed openness to adding more buyback authorization as cash flow materializes.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **AI data center power demand.** Vistra is one of the few public companies with a multi-gigawatt, multi-fuel fleet positioned to serve hyperscaler data centers in ERCOT and PJM. Load growth from AI and onshoring is tightening reserve margins and lifting forward power prices.

2. **Helix Digital Infrastructure.** Announced in August 2026, Helix is a partnership with KKR, NVIDIA, and the Kuwait Investment Authority. Vistra has committed up to \$1.0B to provide a one-stop power-plus-data-center infrastructure solution, directly monetizing the AI buildout.

3. **Long-term power purchase agreements.** Vistra has signed multi-year PPAs with Meta and other customers, locking in attractive returns and de-risking future cash flows.

4. **Cogentrix acquisition.** Pending acquisition of gas-generation assets is expected to add EBITDA and strengthen Vistra's position as a flexible power provider for data center load.

5. **Nuclear production tax credits.** IRA nuclear PTCs provide a floor under nuclear cash flows and help protect against downside power price scenarios.

6. **Share repurchases.** ~\$6.5B bought back since November 2021 has reduced share count by ~30%. ~\$1.2B remains on the authorization and is expected to be completed by end-2027, with the board open to adding more.

7. **Hedging visibility.** As of August 3, 2026, ~100% of expected 2026 generation was hedged, ~94% of 2027, and ~72% of 2028, giving investors multi-year cash-flow visibility.

## Risk Factors

### Business and Operational Risks

- **ERCOT data center interconnection queue audit.** Texas has launched an audit of a queue that management says is inflated with 400+ GW of speculative projects. A delayed or throttled queue could slow data center load growth in Vistra's most important market, though management views the audit as a positive long-term step to clear out unrealistic projects.
- **PJM regulatory uncertainty.** The Incremental Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) proposal, capacity market changes, and ongoing RBP/appeals processes create uncertainty for new-build and existing-asset economics in Vistra's East segment.
- **Fuel price and generation risk.** Coal and gas price volatility, forced outages, and transmission constraints can hurt margins.
- **Competition.** Tech companies are exploring owned generation, and subsidized renewables plus battery storage continue to pressure dispatch margins in some hours.

### Financial Risks

- **High leverage.** D/E ~373% and negative tangible book value mean the equity is highly leveraged. A sustained rise in rates or a credit-spread widening could pressure the stock regardless of operations.
- **GAAP noise from hedges.** Future-settling hedges create unrealized mark-to-market gains/losses that obscure underlying profitability quarter to quarter.
- **Refinancing needs.** While most debt is long-dated, refinancing at higher rates would increase interest burden.
- **Capital intensity.** Utility/IPPs require continuous capex for maintenance, environmental compliance, and growth; misallocation can destroy value.

### Macro and Sector Risks

- **Recession / power demand slowdown.** A material economic slowdown would reduce industrial and commercial load and pressure power prices.
- **Natural gas price collapse.** Lower gas prices reduce the marginal price of electricity in many markets and can compress energy margins.
- **Political and regulatory shifts.** Changes in environmental rules, nuclear policy, or state-level energy regulation can affect asset values.
- **AI capex cycle risk.** If data center buildouts slow or shift toward self-generation, Vistra's growth narrative could cool.

## Readability Pass

**What Vistra does, in plain English:** Vistra is a big U.S. power company. It owns power plants (mostly natural gas and nuclear) and sells electricity into wholesale markets, while also selling electricity directly to homes and businesses through retail brands like TXU Energy. In simple terms, it makes and sells power.

**Why it matters for the stock:** The explosion of AI data centers is creating enormous new demand for electricity. Data centers need reliable, around-the-clock power, and Vistra's fleet is one of the largest in the country. As power markets get tighter, electricity prices tend to rise, and Vistra's profits can grow. The company also signed deals to provide power directly to big tech companies like Meta.

**The catch:** Vistra borrowed a lot of money to build and buy its fleet, and it has spent billions buying back its own stock. That makes the company more financially risky than a typical utility. If power prices fall, interest rates spike, or regulators make life harder in Texas or the PJM market, the stock could fall hard. The recent pullback shows investors are worried about exactly those risks.

**Bottom line:** Vistra is a leveraged bet on the AI-driven boom in U.S. power demand. At ~\$137, the stock looks cheap relative to expected earnings and analyst price targets, but the high debt load means it is not a safe widows-and-orphans utility. It is a BUY for investors who understand and can tolerate that leverage.
