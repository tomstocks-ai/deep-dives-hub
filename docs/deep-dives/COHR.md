# COHR — Coherent Corp.

Coherent Corp. (NYSE:COHR) is a scaled, vertically integrated photonics leader and one of the primary beneficiaries of the AI datacenter interconnect boom. It supplies 800G and emerging 1.6T optical transceivers, indium phosphide lasers, and engineered materials — including silicon carbide — to hyperscalers and network OEMs. At \$288.14 (as of 2026-08-04) and a \$56.4B market cap, the AI datacom thesis is intact and analyst targets sit above the current price, but the stock trades at roughly 120x trailing GAAP earnings after a ~3x run off its 52-week low. With a ~2.0 beta and real co-packaged-optics and capex-cyclicality overhangs, the risk/reward is balanced — hence a **HOLD** rating.

---

## 1. Company Overview

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Coherent Corp. is a global leader in materials, networking, and laser technology, formed from the 2022 combination of II-VI and the legacy Coherent business. It is one of the few optical vendors that is vertically integrated from crystal growth and wafer fabrication through to finished photonic modules, which underpins its cost position and supply security in the AI datacom market.

The business reports across three segments:

| Segment | Approx. share of revenue | Description |
|---------|--------------------------|-------------|
| **Networking** | ~50% | The primary growth engine — 800G and emerging 1.6T optical transceivers, indium phosphide lasers, and datacom/telecom components sold to hyperscalers and network OEMs. |
| **Materials** | ~30% | Engineered materials including silicon carbide (SiC) substrates, optics, and compound-semiconductor materials for power, EV, industrial, and aerospace/defense end markets. |
| **Lasers** | ~20% | Industrial, aerospace/defense, and life-sciences laser systems and components — a more cyclical, slower-growth business. |

The Networking segment is compounding rapidly on AI datacom demand, while Materials and Lasers provide diversification and a margin-expansion story as the company divests non-core assets and pays down debt.

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

Coherent is a top-tier optical transceiver supplier competing against Lumentum, Marvell, InnoLight, and Fabrinet, and it sits ahead of smaller players such as Applied Optoelectronics. Its moat rests on:

- **Vertical integration:** in-house indium phosphide laser fabrication and materials capabilities that few peers match at scale, giving supply security when transceiver demand outstrips industry capacity.
- **Scale and breadth:** a broad product portfolio spanning 100G through 1.6T, with the manufacturing footprint to serve multiple hyperscalers simultaneously.
- **Qualification switching costs:** hyperscaler transceiver qualification is long and difficult, creating stickiness once designed in.

**Competitive moat assessment:** MODERATE-to-STRONG in datacom optics; more commoditized in Lasers.

### Management Team Track Record

CEO Jim Anderson, who joined in 2025 from Coherent's semiconductor peers, has prioritized portfolio simplification, deleveraging, and margin expansion. The management agenda centers on divesting non-core assets, paying down the debt taken on in the II-VI merger, and concentrating capital on the high-growth datacom franchise.

## 3. Valuation

At \$288.14, Coherent trades at approximately 120x trailing GAAP earnings — a rich multiple that prices in continued AI datacom compounding. The valuation is the central debate: the growth is real, but so is the multiple risk.

### Scenario-Based Valuation

| Scenario | Price Target | Key Assumptions |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Bull Case** | \$430–500 | 800G ramps to full scale and 1.6T transceivers reach volume across multiple hyperscalers; datacom pricing stays healthy with demand exceeding supply through 2027; silicon carbide and engineered-materials recovery adds a second growth engine; continued deleveraging and divestitures re-rate the multiple. |
| **Base Case** | \$300–360 | Networking keeps compounding on 800G volume plus early 1.6T while Materials and Lasers stabilize; consolidated revenue grows mid-teens toward ~\$7.5B in FY2027 with non-GAAP gross margin drifting toward 40% and continued debt paydown; the stock holds a premium AI-datacom multiple and broadly tracks the ~\$390 analyst consensus target over 12 months. |
| **Bear Case** | \$150–200 | Hyperscaler AI capex cycle peaks and datacom demand decelerates sharply; co-packaged optics and linear pluggable optics erode the pluggable TAM faster than expected; the multiple compresses from ~120x toward peer/historical levels; Materials and Lasers stay weak. |

The base case of \$300–360 straddles the current price, which is consistent with a HOLD: the ~\$390 consensus implies modest upside, but the bear case highlights how far a multiple compression could take the shares.

## 4. Growth Catalysts

1. **800G ramp to full scale:** 800G datacom transceivers are the near-term revenue driver, with demand continuing to exceed supply across multiple hyperscalers.
2. **1.6T transceiver ramp (2027):** volume shipments of 1.6T optics represent the next major TAM expansion as AI clusters scale interconnect bandwidth.
3. **Silicon carbide / engineered-materials recovery:** a rebound in EV and power end markets would turn the Materials segment into a genuine second growth engine.
4. **Deleveraging and divestitures:** continued debt paydown and non-core divestitures can drive a multiple re-rate as margins and FCF expand.
5. **CHIPS Act funding:** execution on the up-to-\$50M letter of intent for the Sherman, Texas facility expansion supports domestic capacity and margins.

## 5. Risk Factors

### Business Risks
- **Customer concentration:** revenue skews to a small set of hyperscalers and network OEMs; loss of share at any one would be material.
- **Co-packaged optics (CPO) and linear pluggable optics:** these architectures could disrupt the pluggable transceiver franchise over the medium term.
- **Cyclical Materials and Lasers:** EV/SiC and industrial end markets remain a drag if they stay soft.

### Financial Risks
- **Stretched valuation:** ~120x trailing GAAP earnings after a ~3x run off the 52-week low leaves little room for disappointment.
- **Leverage:** meaningful post-merger debt makes the equity more sensitive to a demand or margin miss.

### Macro / Sector Risks
- **AI capex cyclicality:** a peak in hyperscaler AI spending could reverse datacom demand abruptly.
- **High beta (~2.0):** extreme share-price volatility tied to the broader AI trade amplifies drawdowns.

## 6. Recommendation

**Rating: HOLD**

Coherent is executing well on the most important part of its business — AI datacom optics — and the ~\$390 analyst consensus sits above the current price. But at \$288.14 the shares already carry a premium AI-datacom multiple (~120x trailing GAAP) after tripling off the 52-week low, and the base case of \$300–360 straddles today's price. That balance of intact thesis against stretched valuation and a ~2.0 beta argues for patience rather than chasing.

## 8. Readability Pass

- **Optical transceiver:** a device that converts electrical signals into light and back, moving data across fiber inside and between data centers. 800G and 1.6T describe how much data one link carries per second — the higher the number, the more AI traffic a single fiber handles.
- **Vertical integration:** Coherent makes many of its own building blocks (laser chips, materials) instead of buying them, which lowers cost and secures supply when demand outstrips the industry.
- **Co-packaged optics (CPO):** a newer design that puts the optics next to the switch chip; if it takes off faster than expected, it could shrink demand for the standalone pluggable transceivers Coherent sells today.
- **The thesis in one line:** Coherent is a genuine winner of the AI interconnect buildout, but the stock has already re-rated to ~120x earnings, so the current price mostly reflects that good news — a HOLD until a better entry or a fresh catalyst.
