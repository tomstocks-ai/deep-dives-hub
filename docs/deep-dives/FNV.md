# FNV — Franco-Nevada Corporation

Franco-Nevada is the largest gold-focused royalty and streaming company in the world, and it is having the best stretch of financial results in its history. FY2025 revenue rose 64% to \$1.82B and adjusted net income rose 74% to \$1.08B (\$5.58/share); H1 2026 then set fresh half-year records with \$1,231.6M of revenue (+67%), \$1,121.6M of adjusted EBITDA (a 91.1% margin) and \$807.5M of adjusted net income (\$4.19/share). The company carries no debt, holds \$4.3B of available capital, and is tracking toward the upper half of its 2026 guidance of 510,000–570,000 gold equivalent ounces (GEOs). The offset is valuation and commodity beta: at \$245.81 the stock trades at roughly 32x trailing earnings, and gold has corrected to around \$4,300/oz from 2026 record highs, below the \$4,500/oz assumption embedded in company guidance.

## Company Overview

Franco-Nevada does not operate mines. It provides upfront capital to mining companies in exchange for a share of future output, structured either as a royalty (a percentage of revenue or net profit from a defined area) or a stream (the right to buy a percentage of production at a fixed, below-market price). The result is a business with mine-level revenue exposure but essentially no exposure to operating cost inflation, capital overruns, or labour disputes. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 91.2% in Q2 2026.

**Commodity mix (Q2 2026 revenue):** Precious metals were 86% of revenue — 70% gold, 14% silver, 2% PGM — with the remaining 14% from Diversified assets (iron ore royalties and U.S./Canadian energy royalties). Geographically, 88% of revenue came from the Americas: 40% South America, 25% Canada, 16% U.S. and 7% Central America and Mexico.

**Key assets:** Antapaccay and Antamina (Peru, Glencore/BHP-operated streams), Candelaria (Chile, Lundin), Detour Lake and Canadian Malartic (Canada, royalties on Agnico Eagle mines), Côté Gold (IAMGOLD), Greenstone and Valentine (Equinox Gold), Guadalupe-Palmarejo (Coeur), Tocantinzinho (G Mining), Stillwater and Western Limb (Sibanye-Stillwater PGM), Vale iron ore royalty, Weyburn and SCOOP/STACK energy interests, and the Cobre Panamá precious-metals stream (Panama, First Quantum) which has been suspended since November 2023.

**Management:** Paul Brink is President and CEO, and was appointed Chair of the World Gold Council during 2026. The company has a long record of deploying capital at scale without leverage — 2024 and 2025 were its two largest capital-deployment years — and it remains debt-free.

**Moat:** Diversification across more than 100 producing and development properties, a cost structure that does not scale with mine inflation, first-look access to deal flow from decades of operator relationships, and a balance sheet (\$1.01B cash, \$4.3B available capital, zero debt) that lets it write large cheques through commodity downturns when miners are capital-constrained.

### Cash flow

- H1 2026 operating cash flow: \$1,002.9M (+39%), a half-year record; FY2025 was \$1.49B (+80%)
- Capital expenditure is immaterial (\$0.3M of property and equipment in H1 2026), so operating cash flow is effectively free cash flow
- H1 2026 acquisitions of royalty, stream and working interests: \$529.7M, funded entirely from cash on hand
- H1 2026 dividends paid: \$161.1M, roughly 16% of operating cash flow

The takeaway is structural: because Franco-Nevada does not mine, essentially every incremental dollar of revenue from higher metal prices drops to cash flow. That is the entire reason the stock carries a premium multiple to the miners whose output it is exposed to.

## Growth Catalysts

- **H2-weighted production.** Management stated on August 11, 2026 that it is tracking toward the upper half of its 510,000–570,000 GEO guidance range. Candelaria (higher-grade Phase 12 ore plus underground insourcing), Tocantinzinho, Côté Gold (second cone crusher commissioned, plant at near full capacity in June), Greenstone (69% of days above 27,000 tpd versus 51% the prior quarter) and Valentine (113% of nameplate) all ramp into H2.
- **Cobre Panamá stockpile deliveries.** Panama authorised removal, processing and export of on-site stockpiled ore on April 7, 2026; the first processing train was commissioned in May 2026 and first copper concentrate produced. First Quantum estimates 30,000–40,000 tonnes of copper in 2026 and ~70,000 tonnes in total, implying ~23,100 gold ounces and ~265,000 silver ounces to Franco-Nevada. Deliveries were expected to start in Q3 2026, with one-third landing in H2 2026.
- **Cobre Panamá restart optionality.** Panama established a high-level ministerial commission (Commerce and Industries, Economy and Finance, Environment) during Q2 2026 to evaluate the mine's future, informed by the SGS Global integral audit published June 19, 2026 showing 87.7% overall compliance. Franco-Nevada suspended its ICSID arbitration in 2025 to make room for negotiation. A restart would restore an asset that once delivered ~15% of quarterly revenue and is not in current estimates.
- **Deal pipeline funded by \$4.3B of available capital.** Recent additions: a A\$200M follow-on package with Minerals 260 announced September 13, 2026 (A\$170M / ~\$122M royalty lifting Bullabulling to a 3.90% gross royalty, plus a A\$30M / ~\$22M equity subscription); a Comet Vale 2.0% gold royalty in Western Australia (July 15, 2026, \$8.4M); a 1.0% NSR on Youanmi (May 2026, \$32.9M); the \$40.0M Victoria Gold royalty portfolio including a 6.0% NSR on Banyan's AurMac (April 2026); and added Greenstone NPI/NSR coverage (June 2026).
- **Organic resource and expansion growth at no cost to Franco-Nevada.** Côté/Gosselin consolidated Measured and Indicated resources of 20.3Moz gold with a 40,000 tpd expansion study due Q4 2026; Bullabulling's July 2026 pre-feasibility outlines 150,000 oz/yr over 19 years from Q4 2028; Séguéla's approved 30% expansion; Valentine Phase 2 sanctioned in August 2026; Hemlo Interlake resources up 18% year over year; Discovery's Kidd acquisition potentially doubling Timmins output to 500,000 oz/yr.
- **Energy revenue leverage.** H1 2026 oil revenue of \$78.8M rose 20% year over year. The company quantifies the sensitivity: a \$10 move above the \$70/bbl WTI guidance assumption lifts oil revenue by roughly 12%.
- **Dividend growth.** The quarterly dividend was raised 16% to \$0.44 in January 2026, extending a streak of annual increases. At ~16% of operating cash flow the payout leaves ample room.

## Risk Factors

### Business risks

- **Cobre Panamá is unresolved and politically driven.** The mine has been in Preservation and Safe Management since November 2023. The arbitration Franco-Nevada filed in June 2024 under the Canada-Panama FTA was voluntarily suspended in 2025 to enable negotiation, which means the legal recovery path is currently dormant rather than pending. A ministerial commission is deliberating with no announced deadline, and Panama has signalled it will insist on resource-ownership terms in any restart. Outcomes range from a full restart to permanent closure, and the stockpile programme is finite (finishing in 2027).
- **Operator dependence.** Franco-Nevada earns what third parties produce and has no operational control. Q2 2026 already showed the mechanism working against it at Candelaria (lower grades), Hemlo (delayed stope access) and Guadalupe-Palmarejo, and Q3 2026 Antamina silver deliveries were guided lower. Subsequent events at Caserones (13 days of storm-related disruption) and Canadian Malartic (Barnat pit wall movement, outside the royalty area) illustrate the constant drip of third-party operating risk.
- **Acquisition discipline.** Growth requires continuously redeploying cash into new streams and royalties. In a high gold price environment, competition for deals is fierce and the risk of overpaying rises — a mispriced stream permanently impairs returns with no operational lever to fix it.

### Financial risks

- **Gold price sensitivity is the dominant variable.** Roughly 70% of revenue is gold and 86% is precious metals. Gold's correction to ~\$4,300/oz already puts spot below the \$4,500/oz guidance assumption; a move toward \$3,500/oz would take a meaningful bite out of both earnings and the multiple at the same time.
- **Premium multiple compression.** At ~32x trailing earnings, a de-rating toward the low 20s would cost more than a third of the share price even with flat earnings. Rising long bond yields — the 30-year Treasury reached multi-decade highs during September 2026 — are the classic trigger for both a lower gold price and a lower multiple on long-duration cash flows.
- **Consensus misses.** Q2 2026 missed on both revenue and EPS despite record absolute results. With expectations already elevated, further timing-driven misses are a live risk into the November print.
- **Tax exposure.** The company has disclosed ongoing Canada Revenue Agency audit exposure and flags potential changes in Canadian tax treatment of offshore streams, as well as global minimum tax adoption, as unresolved risks.

### Macro and sector risks

- **Jurisdictional concentration in Latin America.** 40% of revenue comes from South America and 7% from Central America and Mexico. Cobre Panamá is the case study in how quickly a permitted, operating, multi-billion-dollar asset can be shut down by courts and street protest.
- **Currency and commodity breadth.** Revenue is exposed to the Canadian and Australian dollars, Brazilian real and Mexican peso, and to iron ore, copper, oil and gas prices in the Diversified segment.
- **Silver below plan.** Guidance assumes \$75/oz silver; silver has corrected well below that level, which is a direct drag on the 14% of revenue that silver represents.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY**

The thesis is that Franco-Nevada offers the cleanest available exposure to precious metals without operating, cost-inflation, or balance-sheet risk, at a price that discounts roughly spot gold and gives nothing for Cobre Panamá or for the deal pipeline that \$4.3B of dry powder will fund. ## Readability Pass

**What Franco-Nevada actually does.** Think landlord, not tenant. Newmont and Agnico Eagle are tenants — they dig, hire, buy fuel, and absorb permitting costs. Franco-Nevada is the landlord that owns a slice of the property and collects a share of whatever comes out of the ground, forever, without ever operating a shovel. When mining costs rise, the tenant absorbs it. When metal prices rise, the landlord captures most of the benefit.

**What a royalty is versus a stream.** A royalty pays Franco-Nevada a percentage of the revenue (or net profit) from a defined piece of ground. A stream lets Franco-Nevada buy a percentage of the metal at a fixed low price and resell it at market — the spread is the profit. Both are contracts, not equity, and both survive changes in mine ownership.

**What GEOs are.** Gold Equivalent Ounces express everything the company receives — silver, PGM, iron ore, oil, gas — as if it were gold, so total production can be tracked with one number. Note a 2026 methodology change: the company now converts using fixed ratios based on its guidance prices (\$4,500/oz gold, \$75/oz silver), rather than prevailing market prices. That makes GEO counts comparable year to year but means the GEO figure no longer moves with spot prices; revenue does.

**Why zero debt matters.** Most companies borrow to grow. Franco-Nevada does not, and holds \$1.0B of cash plus \$4.3B of available capital. It cannot be forced into a bad decision by a lender or a refinancing window, and it can write large cheques precisely when miners are desperate for capital — which is when the best deals get done.

**The bear argument in one sentence.** You are paying about 32 times earnings for a business whose revenue is 70% gold, at a moment when gold has just fallen from a record high and long-term interest rates are at multi-decade highs, while one of the portfolio's largest historical assets sits idle in a country that has not decided whether to let it restart.
