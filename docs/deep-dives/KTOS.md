# KTOS — Kratos Defense & Security Solutions, Inc.

Kratos builds the affordable, mass-producible hardware the Pentagon says it needs: jet-powered target and tactical drones, hypersonic vehicles and solid rocket motors, small turbofan engines, microwave electronics for missiles and radars, and virtualized satellite ground software. The operating story is strong — second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 30.5% to \$458.8 million with 19.1% organic growth, trailing book-to-bill is 1.3 to 1.0, backlog is \$2.084 billion and the bid pipeline is \$15.0 billion — yet the stock at \$44.07 sits ~67% below its January 2026 high of \$134. That gap is the whole debate: the business is compounding, but a 15x-sales multiple has reset, ~\$1.35 billion of equity issuance diluted holders ~21% year over year, and free cash flow is guided to a use of \$85–105 million this year. Rated **SPEC. BUY** on a 2027 production-ramp thesis, with the caveat that GAAP earnings remain close to zero until that ramp lands.

## Company Overview

Kratos operates two reporting segments. **Kratos Government Solutions (KGS)** is the larger, at \$379.7 million of revenue in Q2 2026 (83% of the total) and \$1.055 billion in FY2025. It spans defense rocket support and hypersonics, turbine technologies (small jet engines), microwave electronic products for missiles, radars, air defense and counter-UAS, C5ISR mobility systems, training systems, and the Space, Training and Cyber business whose OpenSpace product is a first-to-market virtualized, software-defined satellite command-and-control and telemetry ground system. **Kratos Unmanned Systems (KUS)** contributed \$79.1 million in Q2 2026 and \$292.0 million in FY2025, covering full-scale aerial targets (BQM-167, BQM-177), the Firejet family, and the XQ-58A Valkyrie uncrewed tactical aircraft.

The moat is process rather than patent. CEO Eric DeMarco's stated philosophy is that "affordability is a technology" — Kratos deliberately uses proven rather than bleeding-edge technology, designs for high-volume low-cost manufacture up front, and makes internally funded capital and R&D investments ahead of government commitment so it can be first to market. That makes Kratos a preferred partner for the large primes when it does not prime the work itself: the USMC MUX TACAIR Collaborative Combat Aircraft award announced in 2026 pairs the Kratos Valkyrie airframe with Northrop Grumman mission systems and autonomy software. The company pays no dividend and does not buy back stock, reinvesting everything into capacity.

Management is unusually explicit about the investment phase. FY2026 forecast investments total \$250–275 million, comprising \$125–135 million of capex (hypersonics and engine manufacturing, a payload integration facility for MACH-TB, BladeWorks turbofan test cells, expanded microwave facilities in Israel and the US, a second lot of 12 Valkyries), \$75–85 million of working-capital investment (Zeus and Oriole rocket motor inventory, small jet engine inventory, drone materials), and \$50–55 million funding the Prometheus joint venture. Kratos now has over 700 personnel in Israel following the Orbit acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 (ended December 28, 2025) delivered revenue of \$1.347 billion, up 18.5% total and 16.6% organic from \$1.136 billion, with gross profit of \$307.9 million (22.9% margin), operating income of \$25.6 million, net income of \$22.0 million, diluted EPS of \$0.13 and adjusted EPS of \$0.55. Adjusted EBITDA was \$119.9 million, an 8.9% margin. The gap between \$25.6 million of operating income and \$119.9 million of adjusted EBITDA is mostly non-cash: \$37.5 million of depreciation, \$35.5 million of stock compensation and \$9.6 million of intangible amortization.

FY2026 is accelerating. Q1 revenue was \$371.0 million with \$38.7 million of adjusted EBITDA and a 1.6-to-1 book-to-bill; Q2 revenue was \$458.8 million (up \$107.3 million year over year) with \$38.2 million of adjusted EBITDA and a small operating loss of \$1.6 million. First-half revenue of \$829.8 million produced \$16.3 million of net income, \$0.09 of diluted EPS, \$0.40 of adjusted EPS and \$76.9 million of adjusted EBITDA (9.3% margin). Inside KGS, Q2 organic growth was led by Defense Rocket Systems at 50.2%, Turbine Technologies at 43.3% and Microwave Products at 29.5%. KUS grew only 8.1% organically, though management now expects ~10% for the full year on recent Valkyrie-related awards.

The balance sheet is the strongest asset. Cash stood at \$1.4376 billion at June 28, 2026, up from \$560.6 million at year-end 2025, after \$1.3484 billion of net equity issuance in the first half funded \$346.8 million of acquisitions and the investment program. Long-term debt is zero; the only structured obligations are \$139.9 million of finance leases and \$53.7 million of operating leases. Net interest income of \$10.2 million in Q2 alone now exceeds segment operating income, which flatters net income relative to operations. Total equity is \$3.427 billion against \$871.2 million of goodwill and \$220.8 million of intangibles.

Cash flow is where the thesis is tested. FY2025 operating cash flow was negative \$42.1 million and free cash flow was a use of \$125.4 million after \$95.3 million of capex. First-half 2026 operating cash flow was negative \$38.4 million, driven by a \$104.3 million build in unbilled receivables and a \$24.8 million inventory build. Full-year 2026 guidance is \$30–50 million of operating cash flow against \$125–135 million of capex, for a free cash flow use of \$85–105 million. Management says this investment continues at least into fiscal 2027. Dilution is the other cost: diluted shares went from 157.4 million in Q2 2025 to 190.1 million in Q2 2026, and FY2026 stock compensation is guided at \$60–64 million.

## Valuation

At \$44.07 the market capitalization is roughly \$8.5 billion. Backing out \$1.44 billion of cash and adding ~\$140 million of finance leases gives an enterprise value near \$7.2 billion, or ~4.0x the \$1.78 billion midpoint of FY2026 revenue guidance and ~41x the \$174.5 million midpoint of adjusted EBITDA guidance. On trailing GAAP earnings the multiple is meaningless — around 260–285x a \$0.17 trailing EPS. Pushing to FY2027 at guided 18–23% organic growth and another ~100bps of margin gives ~\$2.1–2.2 billion of revenue and ~\$230 million of adjusted EBITDA, so ~3.3x sales and ~31x EBITDA one year out. There is no meaningful DCF support at these cash flows; a discounted-cash-flow case only works if you underwrite the 2028 inflection when capex normalizes and the engine, rocket-motor and Valkyrie lines run at rate.

The de-rating is the context. At the January 2026 peak the stock traded above 15x sales, a software multiple for a hardware maker running low-single-digit operating margins; that has compressed to roughly 5x trailing sales. Sell-side has not followed the price down — the MarketBeat consensus is a "Moderate Buy" with an average target near \$98, and a TIKR model published in September carried a \$106 target. That ~120% gap between price and consensus is itself a signal to treat consensus targets with caution rather than as a floor.

**Bull case: \$75–\$95.** FY2027 revenue of ~\$2.1–2.2 billion with ~10.8% EBITDA margins, hypersonics scaling from ~\$200 million in 2025 toward \$700 million-plus in 2027, the ~\$1 billion sole-source hypersonic award landing, Valkyrie becoming a program of record and the GEK engine line reaching rate. That combination supports roughly 6–7x forward sales on a self-funded balance sheet — still below the January peak and below consensus.

**Base case: \$50–\$62.** FY2026 lands inside guidance, FY2027 grows ~20% with modest margin expansion, and free cash flow stays negative through 2027 before inflecting. The market pays 4–5x forward sales and ~40x forward EBITDA for backlog conversion without a growth premium.

**Bear case: \$28–\$36.** Cash burn persists past 2027, awards slip or go to primes and non-traditional competitors, dilution continues, and the multiple compresses toward ~2.3x forward sales as investors demand cash before capacity.

## Growth Catalysts

The near-term event is Q3 2026 earnings, expected in early November 2026, which management has said will carry initial full-year 2027 guidance — the first framed view of 2027 margins and cash flow. Q3 revenue is guided to \$460–480 million with \$40–45 million of adjusted EBITDA and 19–25% organic growth, and Q4 organic growth is guided at 19–31%.

Hypersonics is the largest driver. Kratos supports MACH-TB, which carries roughly \$7 billion of funding over five years, received approximately \$400 million of Department of War funding for hypersonic and other national security programs, and was selected to develop a next-generation highly maneuverable hypersonic missile. Management has publicly said it hopes to receive an additional ~\$1 billion hypersonic opportunity by the end of 2026 that it believes would be sole source to Kratos as prime. Supporting this, 120 Zeus and Oriole solid rocket motors are on order with deliveries beginning in the second half of 2026 and ramping into 2027.

Propulsion is the second leg. The GEK800 Serial Number 1 turbofan achieved first ignition on September 21, 2026 in partnership with GE Aerospace, and Kratos is investing working capital to enable production of 3,000 small jet engines in 2027 to address cruise-missile demand.

Unmanned systems is the optionality. The Northrop-teamed USMC MUX TACAIR CCA award uses a missionized Valkyrie; Kratos has received another tactical drone program-of-record award and believes it is sole source on two more opportunities; and the plan is to reach ~40 Valkyries per year beginning by the start of 2028. Elsewhere, recent wins include a ~\$156 million sole-source counter-UAS IDIQ from the Department of Energy Office of Secure Transportation, a ~\$100 million sole-source space domain awareness production award, a ~\$35 million military-grade hardware production award, a US Army next-generation Javelin seeker development contract, and a directed energy contract worth about \$160 million. The backlog grew to \$2.084 billion with \$1.572 billion funded, and the pipeline expanded from \$14.3 billion to \$15.0 billion in a single quarter.

## Risk Factors

**Cash flow is the central business risk.** Kratos has now guided to negative free cash flow in both 2025 (a \$125.4 million use) and 2026 (an \$85–105 million use), with investment continuing at least into 2027. Working capital is absorbing cash faster than revenue is adding it — unbilled receivables rose \$104.3 million in the first half alone. Much of the spend, including rocket motor inventory and the second Valkyrie lot, is ahead of definitized customer delivery schedules.

**Earnings quality is thin.** FY2026 guided operating income of \$16–22 million on ~\$1.78 billion of revenue is roughly a 1% operating margin; Q2 posted an outright operating loss. Net income is increasingly supported by interest income on the raise rather than operations. Stock compensation of \$60–64 million annually exceeds guided operating income.

**Dilution has been material and may continue.** Two large equity raises (\$555.9 million in H1 2025, \$1.3484 billion in H1 2026) took diluted shares from 157.4 million to 190.1 million year over year. Management's stated preference for self-funding investment ahead of contract means further issuance is plausible if the pipeline grows again.

**Competitive and program risk.** The Air Force's main CCA airframe selections went largely to rival primes, leaving some Kratos work limited to smaller propulsion design awards, and DeMarco is openly benchmarking Kratos against privately held Anduril. Products Kratos develops may never become programs of record, and competitor bid protests can delay or cancel awards.

**Macro and sector risk.** The late-2025 and early-2026 federal shutdown plus an extended continuing resolution delayed short-turn, higher-margin awards and depressed Q1 2026 — a repeat is possible. Shekel strength is actively hurting profitability at the Israeli microwave operations, fixed-price contracts expose Kratos to input inflation and tariffs, and \$871.2 million of goodwill plus \$220.8 million of intangibles from Nomad and Orbit carry impairment risk if integration disappoints.

## Recommendation

**SPEC. BUY.** The fundamental case is real: 19.1% organic growth, raised guidance, 1.3-to-1 trailing book-to-bill, a \$2.084 billion backlog with \$1.572 billion funded, a \$15.0 billion pipeline, and \$1.44 billion of cash against no funded debt. The reason this is speculative rather than a clean BUY is that none of it has produced cash or GAAP earnings yet, and it is not scheduled to until 2028.

**Position sizing:** treat this as a high-volatility satellite position, not a core defense holding — 1–2% of a portfolio, with the prime contractors carrying the sector's defensive weight.

**Entry strategy:** the stock closed at \$44.07 on 2026-09-28, essentially on its 52-week low of \$43.09, and trades well below both its 50-day (\$51.56) and 200-day (\$69.62) moving averages, with the 50-day far under the 200-day. That is a downtrend, not a base. Scaling in across the \$40–45 zone is defensible for investors sizing small, but waiting for the Q3 print and initial FY2027 guidance in early November before committing full size is the higher-probability approach. The \$43 area is the only visible support; below it there is no reference until the pre-breakout \$27 level from late 2025. Overhead resistance sits at the 50-day near \$51 and then the \$60 shelf.

**Stop loss:** a weekly close below \$38 breaks the 52-week low decisively and invalidates the base case; treat that as the exit for trading positions. Investors holding for the 2028 ramp should instead track a fundamental stop — two consecutive quarters of free cash flow worse than guidance, or a slip in the Valkyrie or engine production schedule.

**Catalyst calendar:** Q3 2026 results plus initial FY2027 guidance in early November 2026; the hoped-for ~\$1 billion hypersonic award by year-end 2026; Zeus and Oriole rocket motor deliveries ramping through H2 2026 into 2027; the GEK800 engine line scaling toward 3,000 units in 2027; Valkyrie reaching ~40 aircraft per year from the start of 2028.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: constructive on operations, skeptical on price.** Coverage since the August 4 print has been consistently positive on execution — the Q2 beat (\$0.21 adjusted EPS versus \$0.13–0.14 consensus, revenue \$458.8 million versus ~\$410 million expected), the raised guidance, the GE Aerospace GEK800 ignition on September 21, the 43rd ARAV-B ballistic target flight during Pacific Dragon 2026 — while simultaneously questioning the multiple. Headlines have run to "Can New Defense Awards Spark a Rebound?" and "Why Is Kratos Defense Stock Down 63% From Its All-Time High."

**Sell-side: bullish and far above spot.** MarketBeat shows a "Moderate Buy" consensus with an average target near \$98.06 as of late September; a TIKR model published September 8 carried a \$106 target. Seeking Alpha is openly split, with pieces titled "Buy Even At A Double Premium" alongside "Strong Momentum, But The Valuation Keeps Me Cautious" published the same week. A consensus roughly 120% above the last close usually means estimates have not been marked to the de-rating, so treat those targets as aspirational.

**Retail and flow: capitulation, not accumulation.** The name ran from ~\$27 to \$134 between late 2025 and January 2026 on loyal-wingman, hypersonic and CCA enthusiasm, then unwound in a near-continuous slide — down 16.3% in February, 18.2% in March, 10.6% in April, 10.9% in May, and still grinding lower into September. Reporting attributes part of the pressure to insider and institutional selling after the January peak, with large growth funds trimming. The one-year return is -50.0% and the stock is making 52-week lows while the broader Zacks Aerospace-Defense Equipment group is down only ~4.5% year to date, so this is idiosyncratic de-rating rather than sector weakness.

**Options flow:** not verified for this writeup — no reliable positioning data was available in the sources consulted, so no inference is drawn.

**Net sentiment score: 4/10.** Fundamental momentum and analyst targets are positive, but price action, flow and the cash-flow profile are firmly negative, and the market is currently trading the cash burn rather than the backlog.

## Readability Pass

Kratos makes cheap, buildable-at-scale military hardware — target drones, the Valkyrie loyal-wingman jet, hypersonic test vehicles, rocket motors, small jet engines and satellite ground software. Business is booming: revenue grew 30% last quarter, orders are coming in faster than revenue is going out, and the order book stands at \$2.1 billion with a \$15 billion list of things it is bidding on.

The problem is that growth costs money. Kratos is spending \$250–275 million this year on factories, rocket motors and aircraft it is building before customers have signed firm delivery schedules, so it will burn \$85–105 million of cash in 2026 and keep spending into 2027. It raised about \$1.35 billion selling stock to fund that, which means each existing share now owns ~21% less of the company than a year ago. Accounting profit is nearly zero — guided operating income is about 1% of revenue.

The stock has fallen from \$134 in January to \$44 now. That fall was mostly justified: at the top it was priced like a software company. At \$44 it is priced at about 4x next year's sales and 41x next year's EBITDA — no longer absurd, not yet cheap. Analysts think it is worth around \$98, which says more about stale models than about value.

The trade: buy small if you believe 2028 is the year the factories fill up and the cash turns positive. The chart is still falling, the 52-week low is right here at \$43, and the November earnings report with 2027 guidance is the honest decision point. Bull \$75–95, base \$50–62, bear \$28–36.
