# AVAV — AeroVironment, Inc.

AeroVironment (NASDAQ: AVAV) is the U.S. leader in small unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) and Switchblade loitering munitions, and following the transformational \$4B-plus BlueHalo acquisition (closed May 1, 2025) it has reinvented itself as a diversified next-generation defense technology company spanning air, land, sea, space, and cyber. FY2026 (ended April 30, 2026) revenue surged 141% to a record \$1.98B, with a \$1.2B funded backlog and \$2.7B of bookings, though heavy purchase-accounting amortization, a \$241M goodwill impairment, and a near-doubling of the share count produced a GAAP net loss of \$265M (adjusted EPS \$3.31). With global demand for drones and loitering munitions accelerating, the growth thesis is compelling — but integration execution, negative free cash flow, and a premium valuation on adjusted earnings keep this a **SPEC. BUY** at ~\$159.

---

## 1. Company Overview

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

AeroVironment ("AV") designs, develops, and manufactures unmanned and autonomous systems, precision loitering munitions, counter-UAS technology, directed-energy weapons, space systems, and cyber and electronic-warfare capabilities. The overwhelming majority of revenue comes from the U.S. Department of Defense and allied governments, split between product sales (drones and munitions hardware) and higher-touch contract services. After the BlueHalo combination, AV reports in two segments:

| Segment | % of Revenue | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Autonomous Systems (AxS)** | ~75% | Switchblade loitering munitions, Puma, JUMP 20 and P550 small UAS, unmanned ground vehicles, and related ground-control and support services. |
| **Space, Cyber and Directed Energy (SCDE)** | ~25% | Legacy BlueHalo portfolio — space systems and laser communications, counter-UAS, directed-energy (LOCUST), electronic warfare, and cyber and mission solutions. |

In FY2026, AxS generated the bulk of revenue (\$492M of the \$642M Q4 total), while SCDE — created by the BlueHalo deal — added a fast-growing, technology-rich second engine. The model blends recurring, program-of-record production (Switchblade, Puma) with sticky sustainment services and a rising pipeline of emerging-technology programs.

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

**Competitive Advantages:**

- **Loitering-munition leadership:** Switchblade 300 and 600 are the most combat-proven loitering munitions in the U.S. arsenal, with battlefield validation in Ukraine driving global demand.
- **Small UAS installed base:** Puma and Raven have long been standard tactical drones for the U.S. Army and dozens of allied militaries, creating a durable sustainment and upgrade franchise.
- **Breadth after BlueHalo:** The acquisition added counter-UAS, directed energy, space, and cyber, positioning AV to sell integrated "detect-decide-act" capability rather than point products.
- **Speed and manufacturing scale:** AV is investing aggressively in domestic capacity to deliver at the pace modern conflict demands — a differentiator versus slower primes.

**Competitive Moat Assessment:** MODERATE — strong in loitering munitions and small UAS, but facing rising low-cost drone competition and integration risk across the newly combined portfolio.

### Management Team Track Record

Chairman, President and CEO **Wahid Nawabi** has led AV's pivot from a niche small-drone maker into a broad defense-technology platform, culminating in the BlueHalo combination — the largest acquisition in company history. **Sean T. Woodward** serves as Executive Vice President and CFO, overseeing the balance sheet through the post-acquisition capacity buildout. Management has prioritized scaling production, integrating BlueHalo, and diversifying into the customer's highest-priority mission areas.

## 2. Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Revenue** | \$820.6M | \$1.98B | **+141%** |
| **Gross Margin** | \$318.6M (39%) | \$500.6M (~25%) | Mix and amortization pressure |
| **Adj. EBITDA** | \$146.4M | \$286.1M | **+95%** |
| **Net Income (Loss)** | \$43.6M | −\$265.1M | Goodwill impairment and amortization |
| **EPS (Diluted)** | \$1.55 | −\$5.40 | GAAP loss |
| **Non-GAAP EPS** | \$3.28 | \$3.31 | +1% |

FY2026 revenue rose 141% to a record \$1.98B, with the combined BlueHalo and Empirical Systems Aerospace acquisitions contributing the bulk of the growth and legacy revenue also expanding. GAAP gross margin fell to ~25% from 39%, reflecting a higher mix of lower-margin service revenue and \$265M of depreciation and amortization (including acquired-intangible amortization). The GAAP net loss of \$265.1M was driven primarily by a \$240.7M goodwill impairment and heavy purchase-accounting charges; excluding those items, non-GAAP EPS was \$3.31 and adjusted EBITDA reached \$286.1M.

### Cash Flow

| Metric | FY2026 | Analysis |
|--------|--------|----------|

Cash generation is the current soft spot: AV consumed cash in FY2026 as it built working capital and manufacturing capacity to serve surging demand. Liquidity is ample (~\$632M in cash and short-term investments), but the market will want to see free cash flow turn positive as backlog converts.

At ~\$159, AVAV trades around 48x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance (\$3.02–\$3.34) and roughly 4x sales — a clear premium to traditional defense primes that reflects its high revenue growth, drone and loitering-munition leadership, and next-generation technology exposure. The stock has already corrected hard from a 52-week high near \$418 to the mid-\$130s low, so much of the froth has come out, but the valuation still demands strong execution.

## 4. Growth Catalysts

- **Switchblade and loitering munitions:** Combat validation in Ukraine and adoption under U.S. Army LMAMS and the Pentagon's Replicator initiative are driving multi-year demand for Switchblade 300 and 600, with allied foreign military sales layering on top.
- **Small UAS franchise:** Puma, the new JUMP 20 group-2/3 VTOL platform, and the P550 next-generation system position AV for large recurring procurement and sustainment awards.
- **BlueHalo cross-sell:** Counter-UAS, directed energy (LOCUST laser systems), space and laser communications, and cyber open new high-priority budget lines and integrated-capability bids.
- **Capacity expansion:** Domestic manufacturing investment aims to convert the \$1.2B funded backlog and \$2.7B of bookings into rapid deliveries.
- **Budget tailwinds:** Elevated U.S. and allied defense spending, drone-warfare prioritization, and counter-UAS demand support a multi-year growth runway.

## 5. Risk Factors

1. **Integration risk:** BlueHalo is the largest acquisition in AV's history; failure to realize synergies — or further goodwill impairments after the \$241M FY2026 charge — would undermine the thesis.

2. **Government funding dependence:** With revenue concentrated in U.S. and allied government programs, continuing resolutions, budget caps, or reprioritization can delay Switchblade and UAS procurement.

3. **Cash flow and leverage:** Negative free cash flow, ~\$729M of long-term debt, and convertible notes raise the stakes on converting backlog to cash.

4. **Dilution:** The diluted share count roughly doubled to ~50M to fund the BlueHalo deal, and additional issuance would further dilute holders.

5. **Valuation and competition:** A premium multiple on adjusted earnings leaves little room for error, while a proliferation of low-cost drones and loitering munitions could pressure pricing and share.

## 8. Readability Pass

In plain English: AeroVironment makes small military drones (Puma, JUMP 20) and "kamikaze" loitering munitions (Switchblade) that have become famous in Ukraine, and in 2025 it bought a company called BlueHalo to add lasers, counter-drone systems, space tech, and cyber. That deal more than doubled its revenue to a record \$1.98B in the year to April 2026, and it is sitting on \$1.2B of funded orders. The catch: the acquisition created big accounting charges and a paper loss, the company burned cash to build factories and stock up on parts, and it issued a lot of new shares to pay for the deal. The stock has crashed from about \$418 to \$159, so a lot of hype is gone, but it is still not cheap on adjusted profits. Demand for drones is booming and analysts see it heading back toward \$225, but there is real risk in pulling the BlueHalo integration off. On balance that makes it a **SPEC. BUY** — attractive for its growth, best bought on dips toward \$135–\$150, with targets of \$185–\$225 (base), \$250–\$290 (bull), and \$110–\$140 (bear).
