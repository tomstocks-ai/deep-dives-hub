# AMZN — Amazon.com, Inc.

Amazon is running the largest capital-spending programme in corporate history in order to defend and extend the lead of AWS in AI infrastructure. In Q2 2026 the company posted its first \$200B quarter (net sales \$200.6B, +20% year-over-year) and AWS grew 36.7% to \$42.2B — the fastest cloud growth in 18 quarters — with a \$496B contracted backlog behind it. The cost is visible: roughly \$220B of 2026 cash capex has pushed trailing twelve-month free cash flow to an outflow of \$7.6B, and consensus FY2027 EPS now sits *below* FY2026. At \$246.15 the stock trades ~14% below its 52-week high and ~25% below the \$321–331 consensus target, which is the essence of the debate: the demand is contracted, the returns are deferred.

## Company Overview

Amazon operates three reported segments:

| Segment | Q2 2026 net sales | Y/Y | Q2 2026 operating income | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America | \$116.2B | +16% | \$9.1B | 7.9% |
| International | \$42.2B | +15% | \$1.7B | 4.1% |
| AWS | \$42.2B | +37% | \$16.6B | 39.4% |
| **Consolidated** | **\$200.6B** | **+20%** | **\$27.5B** | **13.7%** |

AWS is 21% of sales but contributed 61% of Q2 2026 segment operating income. Within the retail businesses the profit mix is increasingly advertising: advertising services reached \$19.8B in Q2 2026, +26% year-over-year, and subscription services (mostly Prime) added \$13.7B, +12%. Third-party seller services were \$46.8B (+16%), with sellers accounting for 61% of paid units.

The moat has three sources. First, scale in fulfilment — 1.6 million employees, a private logistics network, and record same-day and overnight delivery speeds for Prime members. Second, the switching costs and data gravity of AWS, which now spans compute, storage, managed databases, and an AI stack (Bedrock, AgentCore, SageMaker). Third, custom silicon: Trainium and Graviton. Amazon disclosed that its chips business passed a \$25B annualised run rate growing triple digits, Graviton is used by 98% of the top 1,000 EC2 customers, and Graviton5 reached general availability delivering up to 25% better compute performance than Graviton4. Anthropic and OpenAI have both made multi-year, multi-gigawatt Trainium commitments — a structural advantage in cost per token that rented GPUs cannot match.

Management is stable and owner-oriented: Andy Jassy as President and CEO, Brian Olsavsky as CFO, Jeff Bezos as Executive Chair. Capital allocation is entirely reinvestment — no dividend, and no meaningful buyback in the current cycle. Since October 2025 Amazon has cut more than 30,000 corporate roles, including a 14,000-person round and subsequent reductions inside the AGI organisation, while headcount overall rose 3% year-over-year to 1.595 million as fulfilment capacity expanded.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025).** Net sales \$716.9B, +12% year-over-year. Gross margin 50.3%, up from 42.0% in FY2021 — a structural shift driven by advertising, third-party services, and AWS. Operating income \$80.0B (11.2% margin). Net income \$77.7B and diluted EPS \$7.17. Operating cash flow \$139.5B; capex net of proceeds \$128.3B; free cash flow \$11.2B, already down from \$25.9B on a trailing basis in Q1 2025.

**Trailing twelve months to 30 June 2026.** Net sales \$775.7B (+16%). Operating income \$93.7B (12.1% margin). Net income \$135.3B and diluted EPS \$12.44 — but that figure is not clean. Q2 2026 net income of \$62.6B (\$5.75 diluted) includes \$53.4B of non-operating pre-tax gains, primarily marks on the Anthropic investments. Strip that out and comparable Q2 EPS was roughly \$1.88 against a ~\$1.83 consensus. The operating business beat, modestly; the headline beat by 3x for accounting reasons.

**The cash flow story is the real story.** Trailing free cash flow has gone \$25.9B → \$18.2B → \$14.8B → \$11.2B → \$1.2B → **negative \$7.6B** across the last six quarters as capex net of proceeds climbed to \$169.0B TTM, +64% year-over-year. Q2 2026 alone carried \$54.2B of gross capex against \$45.4B of operating cash flow. Management raised 2026 cash capex guidance to approximately \$220B from \$200B, attributing the increase to higher memory prices and greater capacity requirements, and still expects capacity to trail demand.

**Balance sheet at 30 June 2026.** Cash and equivalents \$78.2B plus marketable securities \$44.8B. Property and equipment net has grown to \$446.0B from \$357.0B at year-end 2025. Long-term debt nearly doubled to \$128.9B from \$65.6B, with \$67.0B of long-term debt issued in H1 2026 — the build is now partly debt-funded. Total assets crossed \$1.10 trillion and equity is \$551.6B, helped by \$66.3B of accumulated other comprehensive income that itself reflects unrealised investment gains. Cash interest paid remains trivial at \$1.7B TTM, so leverage is not a solvency question; it is a signal about how much of the capex the operating business can no longer self-fund.

**Q3 2026 guidance.** Net sales \$197.0B–\$202.0B (+9% to +12%; nearly 400bp higher excluding the Prime Day shift in both years), below the ~\$203.9B analysts had modelled. Operating income \$22.5B–\$26.5B versus \$17.4B in Q3 2025.

## Valuation

At \$246.15 and 10,783 million shares outstanding, the market capitalisation is ~\$2.65 trillion.

Multiples depend heavily on which earnings number you use, and this is where the argument lives:

- FY2026 consensus EPS of ~\$12.88 puts the stock at ~19x — but that consensus is contaminated by the Anthropic mark.
- FY2027 consensus EPS of ~\$10.47 puts it at ~24x. A forward estimate that *falls* year-over-year is the tell: depreciation on \$220B of 2026 capex begins landing in the income statement in 2027 and 2028.
- On trailing free cash flow the stock has no multiple at all, because trailing free cash flow is negative.

A sum-of-the-parts frame is more useful. AWS at \$148.4B TTM revenue growing 37% with a 36.8% TTM operating margin, compared against cloud and AI infrastructure peers, plausibly supports \$1.6–2.0 trillion on its own. Advertising at a ~\$79B annualised run rate growing 26% is worth a further several hundred billion at digital-advertising multiples. That leaves the retail and logistics business — \$627B of TTM sales at a ~6% blended segment margin — carried at a modest valuation, which is roughly how the market has always treated it.

The bridge from here to the scenarios rests on three variables: whether AWS growth holds above 30%, what the depreciation schedule does to reported EPS in 2027–28, and when free cash flow inflects back positive.

**Bull case — \$340–380.** AWS sustains 33%+ growth as the \$496B backlog converts, AI and chips run rates compound off \$25B each, capex peaks in 2027, and free cash flow inflects sharply positive in 2028 as depreciation is absorbed by a far larger revenue base. Advertising stays above 20%. This is roughly 28–32x a normalised FY2028 EPS of ~\$12 and implies the consensus \$321–331 target proves conservative rather than aggressive.

**Base case — \$285–315.** AWS decelerates gradually to the high-20s, consolidated operating margin holds near 13%, 2027 capex plateaus rather than falling, and free cash flow returns to modestly positive in 2028. Reported EPS is flat to down in 2027 before resuming growth. This lands near the low end of the analyst consensus range and implies ~16–28% upside over twelve months.

**Bear case — \$185–215.** AI capacity demand digests, AWS growth slows toward 20% while capex commitments are already sunk, depreciation compresses operating margin back toward 10%, the Anthropic mark partially reverses and turns the reported EPS comparison ugly, and the market re-rates Amazon as a capital-intensive infrastructure business rather than an asset-light compounder. The 52-week low of \$196.00 sits inside this band, so it is not a hypothetical level.

## Growth Catalysts

**AWS backlog conversion.** The \$496B of contracted future revenue is the single most important number in the story. Management has stated that 2027 capacity is already reserved. Each quarter of conversion at 30%+ growth is direct evidence that the capex is pre-sold rather than speculative.

**Custom silicon economics.** Trainium at a \$25B+ run rate with decade-scale commitments from Anthropic and multi-gigawatt commitments from OpenAI gives AWS a cost structure its competitors rent from Nvidia. Graviton5 at general availability, with revenue commitments up nearly 3x quarter-over-quarter, extends the same logic to general-purpose compute.

**Agentic AI monetisation.** Bedrock now hosts 10+ additional managed foundation models including GPT-5.6, Claude Opus 5, Gemma 4, and Grok 4.3; customers spent more in Q2 2026 than in all prior quarters combined. AgentCore added payments, web search, and Harness. Amazon also committed \$1B to AWS Forward Deployed Engineering to shorten enterprise agent deployments from months to days.

**Advertising and retail media.** 26% growth on a \$19.8B quarterly base, now augmented by Alexa for Shopping — active users close to doubling and interactions up more than 5x year-over-year, with Alexa shoppers spending 40%+ more per order.

**Optionality outside the core.** Amazon Leo reached nearly 400 satellites in orbit with initial satellite internet service expected to begin this year. Zoox received the first NHTSA Part 555 exemption allowing a purpose-built robotaxi to charge for rides. Amazon Supply Chain Services opens the logistics network to third parties (P and G, 3M, Lands' End, American Eagle as first customers). Amazon Business reached \$60B in annualised gross sales. None of these is in consensus estimates in a meaningful way.

**The Anthropic stake.** Amazon committed up to an additional \$25B in April 2026 alongside the Trainium commitment. The Q2 mark of \$53.4B pre-tax shows the scale; press reports in September 2026 have speculated the stake could be worth several hundred billion dollars at an eventual Anthropic IPO. Treat any specific figure as speculation, not guidance — but the direction of the optionality is real, and so is the concentration risk it creates.

## Risk Factors

**Capital intensity and the depreciation wave.** This is the dominant risk. Roughly \$220B of 2026 cash capex, TTM free cash flow of negative \$7.6B, and \$446B of net property and equipment mean years of rising depreciation. Management's defence is that servers break even in under three years, useful lives run five to six years, and data centres monetise for thirty-plus. If any of those assumptions is optimistic — through faster accelerator obsolescence or lower utilisation — the free cash flow gap is a permanent transfer from shareholders to capacity rather than an investment.

**Falling forward estimates.** Consensus FY2027 EPS of ~\$10.47 is below FY2026's ~\$12.88. Buying a ~24x multiple on an estimate that is declining is a narrower trade than the 56-buy rating sheet implies.

**Anthropic mark-to-market volatility.** \$53.4B of pre-tax gains flowed through Q2 2026 other income, and \$42.0B of unrealised investment gains ran through other comprehensive income. These marks are non-cash, unrealised, and reversible. Reported EPS is now materially a function of a private company's valuation.

**AWS competitive and demand risk.** In FY2025 AWS grew 19.7% while Google Cloud grew 35.8% and Microsoft's Intelligent Cloud grew 29.7%; AWS margin fell to 35.4% that year before recovering. The 2026 acceleration reverses that gap, but it also raises the bar — a single decelerating quarter with capex still climbing is exactly the evidence bears are waiting for.

**Input cost inflation.** The capex increase from \$200B to \$220B was explicitly attributed in part to memory prices. Amazon is a price-taker on DRAM and HBM in a tight market, and the guidance language flags memory chip supply volatility as a named risk factor.

**Regulatory and legal.** The expanded FTC settlement over Prime billing and cancellation practices begins paying automatic refunds to additional customers on 1 October 2026. Broader antitrust scrutiny of marketplace practices continues, and Amazon has reportedly blocked at least one rival agentic shopping bot from its store — a pattern likely to attract attention as agentic commerce grows.

**Consumer and macro exposure.** Roughly 79% of sales still come from retail segments exposed to tariffs, discretionary spending, and currency. Q3 guidance already embeds an ~80bp unfavourable FX impact.

**Leverage.** Long-term debt roughly doubled to \$128.9B in six months. Interest cover remains enormous, but the funding mix has changed and the option to keep spending at this rate now depends partly on credit markets.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Entry is attractive relative to the fundamental trajectory, with the explicit acknowledgement that the free cash flow trough and the 2027 EPS air pocket are both still ahead.

The case is that a business compounding revenue at 16–20% with its highest-margin segment accelerating to 37% growth, sitting on \$496B of contracted backlog, trading 25% below consensus and 14% below its own 52-week high, is being penalised for spending that is already sold. The counter-case — that reported earnings fall in 2027 and free cash flow stays negative — is true and is the reason this is a BUY rather than a high-conviction one.

**Position sizing.** A core position, 3–5% of a diversified portfolio. Amazon is not a speculative holding, but it is currently a capital-cycle holding, and the payoff is 2028-weighted.

**Entry strategy.** Scale in. The stock sits below its 50-day moving average (~\$256) and just above its 200-day (~\$241), which is a weak technical posture inside a broadly intact uptrend. A reasonable approach is a first tranche near \$240–250, a second on any retest of the \$225–235 zone, and a third only on confirmation — meaning an AWS growth print at or above 33% in the Q3 report. RSI has been unremarkable through September; there is no oversold signal to lean on.

**Stop loss and invalidation.** For a trading position, a stop below the 52-week low at \$196 is the technical line. For an investment position the thesis invalidation is fundamental rather than price-based: AWS growth decelerating below 25% for two consecutive quarters while capex guidance is maintained or raised. That combination would mean the backlog is not converting on schedule and the capacity is not pre-sold.

**What would make this a HOLD.** A Q3 print showing AWS below 30% with unchanged 2026 or 2027 capex, or any indication that server useful lives are being shortened.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Analyst sentiment: strongly positive, with a notable dissent.** 56 of 59 analysts rate Amazon a Buy with three Holds, producing a Moderate Buy consensus and an average twelve-month target of \$321.19 on MarketBeat; S and P Global shows a Strong Buy consensus and a \$328.22 average across 60 analysts; TipRanks shows \$331.16 with a \$400 high and \$230 low. Zacks Research recently downgraded from Strong Buy to Hold. As 24/7 Wall St. observed on 27 September, the two or three Holds setting the bottom of the target range matter more than the count of bulls if the dissenters are right about utilisation and depreciation.

**Price action: weak versus the narrative.** The stock is down ~4.5% month-to-date and ~14% from the 52-week high of \$287.20, has returned 10.8% over one year against a far stronger AI-complex tape, and sits below its 50-day moving average while holding its 200-day. That is the market voting on capex, not on AWS.

**News tone: bullish on AWS, sceptical on cash.** Coverage since the July release splits cleanly. The AWS acceleration, \$496B backlog, Trainium wins, and \$25B AI and chips run rates drive the positive coverage. The negative coverage is uniformly about the same three things: negative free cash flow, the \$53.4B Anthropic gain inflating headline EPS, and FY2027 estimates falling below FY2026.

**Institutional and insider flow: mildly negative.** Q2 2026 13F filings show trimming at Van Eck, BNP Paribas, ICONIQ, Senator Investment Group, Strategic Advisors, and Rational Advisors, with some funds cutting positions 20–34%; Arthedge and several advisers added. Insiders sold 71,589 shares worth ~\$18.6 million over the trailing 90 days, including SVP David Zapolsky and CFO Brian Olsavsky — all under pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plans, which limits the signal value.

**Retail and social: constructive but less enthusiastic than in 2025.** Amazon is no longer the default AI long in retail discussion; the "capex without cash flow" framing has taken hold, and Ticker Nerd's systematic factor model ranked AMZN 49 of 100 as of 18 September, in the lower half of its universe. Reliable options-flow data was not available for this session, so no claim is made about positioning.

**Composite sentiment score: 6.5 / 10.** Institutional analyst conviction is high and fundamental momentum in AWS is excellent, but price action, insider and institutional flow, the estimate trajectory, and factor-model rankings are all neutral-to-negative. The gap between analyst targets and price is unusually wide for a mega-cap, which tends to resolve through either estimate cuts or a re-rating — the Q3 AWS number decides which.

## Readability Pass

Amazon makes money three ways: selling and shipping things, renting computing power through AWS, and selling advertising. AWS is only about a fifth of revenue but roughly three-fifths of the profit, so AWS is what the stock trades on.

Right now AWS is doing very well. It grew 37% last quarter, its fastest in more than four years, and customers have signed contracts worth \$496 billion of future revenue. Amazon has also built its own AI chips, which is cheaper than renting them from Nvidia, and both Anthropic and OpenAI have committed to using them for years.

The problem is what it costs. Amazon will spend about \$220 billion on data centres and equipment this year. That is more cash than the business generates, so free cash flow — the money left over after building things — has gone negative for the first time in years, and Amazon has borrowed to cover the difference. All that new equipment also gets written off over time, which will push reported profits *down* in 2027 before they go back up. Analysts expect earnings per share of about \$10.47 next year versus about \$12.88 this year.

One more thing to know: last quarter's headline profit of \$5.75 per share was mostly an accounting gain from Amazon's stake in Anthropic going up in value. Excluding that, the real number was about \$1.88. The stake could be very valuable, but it is a paper gain that could reverse.

So the bet is simple. If the \$496 billion of signed contracts turns into revenue on schedule and the data centres earn their cost, today's price of \$246 is cheap against a Wall Street consensus around \$321–331, and our base case of \$285–315 is conservative. If AI demand cools while the spending is already committed, the bear case of \$185–215 is where this goes. We rate it BUY, sized as a core position, bought in pieces rather than all at once, with the next quarterly AWS growth number as the thing to watch.
