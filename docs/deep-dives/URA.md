# URA — Global X Uranium ETF

The Global X Uranium ETF is the largest and most liquid listed vehicle for uranium and nuclear-fuel-cycle equity exposure, with roughly \$5.74B of net assets across 62 holdings and a 0.69% expense ratio. The striking feature of 2026 is a dislocation: URA trades at \$40.13, some 36% below its 52-week high of \$62.28 and down ~17.6% over twelve months, while the underlying commodity went the other way — spot U₃O₈ is around \$89.50/lb (up ~7% year-on-year) and the UxC long-term contract price hit an all-time high of ~\$96/lb in August 2026, surpassing the 2007 cycle peak. The equity drawdown is a de-rating of nuclear-adjacent share prices, not a deterioration in the uranium market. That is the setup for a BUY, tempered by the fact that URA's index has been absorbing a growing sleeve of pre-revenue reactor developers whose valuations, not uranium, now drive a meaningful share of the fund's volatility.

## Company Overview

URA is a passive, market-cap-weighted exchange-traded fund launched on 4 November 2010 by Global X, listed on NYSE Arca. It tracks the Solactive Global Uranium and Nuclear Components Total Return Index, which spans the nuclear value chain rather than uranium mining alone: producers, developers, physical-uranium vehicles, enrichment and conversion companies, and reactor-component and small-modular-reactor firms.

Two structural points matter more than any single weight. First, Cameco at 21.81% means URA is substantially a leveraged Cameco position with a diversified tail — a single-name concentration most investors underestimate. Second, the reactor-technology sleeve (Oklo, NuScale, and newer index entrants including Lightbridge, Eagle Nuclear, Terra Innovatum and NANO Nuclear) is largely pre-revenue. Global X's index provider added Lightbridge in July 2026 and Eagle Nuclear and Terra Innovatum in early August 2026, so this sleeve is getting broader, not narrower.

**What this means:** URA is not a bet on the uranium price. It is a bet on uranium producers *and* on the equity market's willingness to pay up for nuclear technology stories. In 2026 those two things moved in opposite directions.

## Financial Analysis

An ETF has no income statement, so the equivalents are assets, costs, flows, distributions and what the index actually owns.

**Assets and flows.** AUM stands at ~\$5.74B on ~140.05M shares outstanding. A Global X index press release described the fund as holding "over \$4 billion in net assets" in August 2025. Since the share price fell ~17.6% over the past twelve months, AUM growing by roughly 40% from that base can only have come from net creations — investors added money into the drawdown. Precise monthly flow figures were not obtainable from the sources available in this session, so treat the ~40% AUM growth as an estimate derived from the AUM and price data rather than a reported flow number. Directionally, though, the conclusion is robust: URA is not suffering a redemption spiral.

**Cost.** The 0.69% expense ratio is the cheapest of the three main uranium/nuclear funds (URNM 0.75%, NLR ~0.62% for a utility-heavy mandate). For a thematic fund holding illiquid foreign listings, that is competitive but not cheap in absolute terms — 0.69% compounds to roughly 3.4% of capital over five years.

**Distributions.** URA paid \$2.08433 per share ex-dividend 30 December 2025 (paid 7 January 2026), against \$0.7414 the prior year and \$1.6816 in December 2023. The TTM yield is ~5.19%. These are lumpy annual distributions largely reflecting realised gains and pass-through income, not a sustainable dividend stream — the payout ratio screens at ~169%, which is the arithmetic of a capital-gains distribution, not a warning about coverage.

**Performance.** Total return was -13.07% over the trailing year including dividends. Since inception the average annual return is -3.14%, a reminder that this fund spent a decade in a uranium bear market before the 2023–2025 boom.

**Portfolio valuation.** The reported portfolio P/E of ~35.4 is the number to sit with. Cameco and Kazatomprom trade on producer multiples; the aggregate is dragged up by the developer sleeve, some of which has no earnings at all. URA is therefore not a "cheap commodity equity" basket at these levels.

**The underlying commodity, which is the actual earnings driver.** Spot U₃O₈ closed around \$89.45–89.55/lb in the week to 25 September 2026 and briefly moved above \$90 after August month-end. The UxC long-term price reached an all-time high of ~\$96/lb in August 2026 (Cameco's published month-end average was \$96.50/lb), above the previous cycle high set nearly two decades ago. Spot remains well below the current-cycle peak of \$107/lb and the 2007 record of \$136/lb. Producer results confirm the pricing environment: Uranium Energy Corp reported fiscal 2026 results on 29 September 2026 with a realised price of \$93.13/lb, \$753M of liquid assets and no debt.

**What this means:** the fund's cost structure and flows are healthy, the commodity underpinning its constituents' earnings is at multi-decade highs on a contract basis, and the share price is down a third. The disconnect is a valuation event in the equities, concentrated in the non-producing holdings.

## Valuation

URA has no intrinsic value of its own — it is worth the sum of its holdings. Valuation therefore means mapping uranium price and nuclear-equity sentiment onto the basket.

| Scenario | Uranium spot / term | Nuclear equity sentiment | URA target |
|----------|---------------------|--------------------------|------------|
| **Bull** | Spot \$110–130/lb, term above \$105/lb | Contracting cycle delivers large RFPs; developer sleeve re-rates on first firm orders | \$58–70 |
| **Base** | Spot \$90–105/lb, term holds \$95–100/lb | Producers re-rate on contract economics; developers stay range-bound | \$46–54 |
| **Bear** | Spot back to \$70–80/lb | Developer sleeve de-rates further; thematic outflows; index dilution bites | \$30–36 |

The base case of \$46–54 implies roughly 15% to 35% upside from \$40.13 and corresponds to URA reclaiming its 200-day moving average of \$48.30 as producer earnings catch up with record term prices. The bull case of \$58–70 requires the seasonal contracting cycle to convert into signed multi-year contracts at rising floors and ceilings, which is precisely what Sprott identified as the market's next catalyst. The bear case of \$30–36 does not require the uranium thesis to break — it only requires the speculative half of the portfolio to keep compressing while spot drifts back toward \$80.

Relative valuation matters here. If the thesis is purely a uranium spot recovery, URNM is the higher-beta instrument (Cameco, NexGen and physical uranium at close to half the fund). If the thesis is AI power demand rather than uranium, NLR's nuclear utilities capture power purchase agreements without any uranium price sensitivity. URA sits in the middle: broader, cheaper, more liquid, and less pure. Investors who want the commodity should know they are buying reactor developers too.

## Growth Catalysts

- **The contracting cycle.** This is the single most important catalyst. Maximum European uranium contract coverage falls from 100% in 2030 to 36% by 2034, and US coverage falls from roughly 60% to just 9% between 2030 and 2033. Because uranium must be contracted and processed years ahead of delivery, utilities must address that gap well before the delivery years. The 2026 World Nuclear Symposium in September pointed to material requests for proposals from US and European utilities following the conference.
- **Record term prices feeding producer economics.** At ~\$96/lb the long-term price sets higher floors and ceilings in contracts, giving miners downside protection and the revenue visibility needed to fund restarts and greenfield projects. This flows into the earnings of URA's producer holdings with a lag.
- **US strategic demand.** The National Nuclear Security Administration issued a request for information in August 2026 for uranium purchases beginning as early as 2030, with long-term requirements of roughly four million pounds of US-origin U₃O₈ per year over ten years — approximately 100% of current US mine production. Imported material generally cannot satisfy it. Several projects have also entered the FAST-41 federal permitting programme.
- **Supply that cannot respond quickly.** Since 2020 uranium prices roughly tripled while annual mine production rose only ~50%, and most of that came from restarts rather than new mines. The 2026 IAEA Red Book puts typical project timelines at 15 to 20 years from identification to production. Kazatomprom has warned of declining Kazakh output after 2026–2027 on deposit depletion, while holding 2026 guidance at 27,500–29,000 tU on a 100% basis.
- **Data-centre and AI electricity demand.** The Department of Energy projects data centres could reach up to 12% of US electrical demand by 2028. Nuclear is the dispatchable carbon-free option utilities can offer a hyperscaler, and uranium demand is relatively insensitive to price because fuel is a small share of reactor operating cost.
- **Policy scale.** Thirty-nine countries now back the pledge to at least triple global nuclear capacity by 2050, which the World Nuclear Association estimates would take ~\$6 trillion of investment and lift capacity from ~440 GWe toward ~1,457 GWe.
- **Inventory exhaustion.** Excess commercial inventories are no longer a meaningful supply source; remaining holdings are strategic, and China has accounted for nearly all recent utility inventory growth.

## Risk Factors

- **Portfolio composition risk, the most under-appreciated.** Roughly a tenth of the fund sits in pre-revenue reactor developers (Oklo 5.75%, NuScale 2.92%, plus X-energy, Lightbridge, NANO Nuclear, Eagle Nuclear and Terra Innovatum in smaller weights). These names moved 5–13% on single sell-side actions during September 2026 — Piper Sandler's sector split on 10 September knocked Oklo, NuScale and X-energy down ~5% each. A ~35x portfolio P/E means URA carries equity-valuation risk that is independent of the uranium price.
- **Index dilution.** Solactive kept adding small speculative nuclear names through July and August 2026. Each addition lowers the fund's average holding quality and raises its correlation with retail risk appetite rather than with U₃O₈.
- **Single-name concentration.** Cameco at 21.81% means a Cameco-specific problem — an operational issue at Cigar Lake or McArthur River, or a guidance miss — hits the whole fund. A July 2026 sulfuric acid outage already suspended Cigar Lake mining temporarily.
- **Spot price stall.** Utility contracting paused temporarily in September 2026 and spot has been drifting sideways near \$89–90/lb. Spot is still 16% below the current-cycle peak of \$107/lb.
- **Momentum and trend.** At \$40.13 URA sits below both its 50-day (\$43.28) and 200-day (\$48.30) moving averages, with the 52-week low of \$37.18 only ~7% away. The downtrend is intact until the fund reclaims the 50-day.
- **Jurisdictional and political risk.** Kazakhstan supplies roughly a fifth of global primary production; Canada has floated export taxes on uranium in response to US tariffs. Kazatomprom's discretionary decision to cut 2026 nominal output by close to 10% shows how much the market depends on one state producer's choices.
- **Tail risk of a nuclear incident.** A serious accident anywhere would freeze reactor development globally and crush this basket regardless of supply fundamentals.
- **Structural fund drags.** A 0.69% fee, foreign withholding on non-US dividends, and lumpy annual capital-gains distributions that create tax friction in taxable accounts. Since-inception average annual return is -3.14%, which is what a non-diversified thematic fund looks like across a full cycle.

## Recommendation

- **Rating:** **BUY**
- **Current price:** \$40.13 (28 September 2026)
- **Targets:** Bull \$58–70 · Base \$46–54 · Bear \$30–36
- **Position sizing:** 2–4% of portfolio as a commodity/energy-transition allocation. Size it as a volatile satellite, not a core holding — beta is 1.16 against the market but effective beta to uranium equities is far higher.
- **Entry strategy:** Scale in rather than committing at once. The fund is below both major moving averages, so add on strength through \$43.28 (50-day) for trend confirmation, or average into weakness toward the \$37.18 52-week low for value. Avoid a single lump entry in a confirmed downtrend.
- **Stop loss:** A weekly close below \$36 invalidates the dislocation thesis and implies the equity de-rating has become something worse. That is ~10% below spot.
- **Time horizon:** 18–36 months. The contracting cycle, not the next month of spot prints, is the thesis.
- **Technical context:** price \$40.13 versus 50-day \$43.28 and 200-day \$48.30; 52-week range \$37.18–62.28. The 50-day is below the 200-day, a bearish configuration. RSI has been cycling through oversold territory during the August and September selloffs without a durable reversal, so treat oversold readings as a reason to scale rather than to load up.
- **Better alternatives if your view is narrower:** URNM for maximum uranium spot beta; NLR for the AI power-demand angle without commodity exposure; Cameco directly if you want the producer without the developer sleeve.
- **Catalyst calendar:**
  - October 2026 — seasonal post-symposium utility contracting; watch for the RFPs flagged at the World Nuclear Symposium
  - Late October 2026 — Kazatomprom Q3 operations and trading update
  - Monthly — UxC and TradeTech long-term price prints; the key question is whether term holds above \$96/lb
  - November 2026 — Cameco Q3 results (21.81% of the fund)
  - December 2026 — Solactive index rebalance and URA's annual distribution ex-date
  - 2027 — NNSA domestic uranium procurement progressing from request for information toward contracts

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: mixed, improving at the commodity level and still fragile at the equity level.** Sprott's 24 September 2026 report is unambiguously constructive, framing record term prices as "a new phase" and the post-symposium period as the start of an active contracting cycle. The Wall Street Journal ran "Data Centers Light Fire Under Uranium Prices" in early September. Against that, the equity headlines through August and September were a run of drawdown stories: "Uranium ETFs Just Crashed 30 Percent While AI Power Demand Keeps Breaking Records" (18 August), "Nuclear Stocks Slide as Piper Sandler Splits the Sector" (10 September), "NuScale Power Drops 7% as Nuclear Stocks Hand Back the Vote Rally" (18 September).

**Sell-side positioning.** Piper Sandler's September differentiation of the sector — separating names it favours from those it does not — is a sign the market has stopped trading nuclear as one undifferentiated basket. That is negative for a market-cap-weighted index fund that owns all of them, and it is a reason URA may lag the best individual names in a recovery.

**Retail and social.** Uranium remains a well-followed retail theme, but the tone has shifted from euphoria to attrition after a ~36% drawdown. Benzinga's late-September note on the leveraged Direxion uranium fund described a persistent downtrend with prices back below the 50-day moving average, while conceding "bears may now be tired" — a fair summary of the mood. The presence of Oklo and NuScale in the top ten keeps URA tied to retail risk appetite for speculative nuclear names.

**Flows, the most useful signal.** AUM of \$5.74B against a share price down 17.6% over twelve months implies net creations into the decline. Investors have been buying this drawdown, not abandoning it. That is the opposite of what a broken thematic trade normally looks like.

**Producer commentary.** Kazatomprom's choice to cut 2026 nominal output by roughly 10% rather than sell into prevailing prices, and Sprott's observation that producers remain focused on pricing discipline over market share, both point to a supply side unwilling to chase volume. That is bullish and it is management behaviour rather than analyst opinion.

**Composite read:** moderately bullish, roughly 6.5 out of 10. The commodity and policy signals are strong, the flow data is supportive, and the technical and sell-side picture is still weak. That combination favours accumulation over a single decisive entry.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: URA is a fund that holds shares in 62 companies connected to nuclear fuel — the companies that dig uranium out of the ground, the ones that process it, and the ones building the next generation of reactors. You buy one thing and get the whole industry.

The price of uranium itself is doing well. The price utilities pay under long multi-year contracts just reached an all-time high of about \$96 a pound, higher than at any point since records began. But URA's share price has fallen about 36% from its high. That gap exists because roughly a tenth of the fund is invested in companies that are designing reactors but do not yet sell anything, and the stock market has become much less willing to pay high prices for promising-but-unprofitable businesses. The uranium story did not break; the enthusiasm for nuclear technology shares did.

Why this could work out well: electricity demand is rising fast, largely because of AI data centres, and nuclear is the only carbon-free power source that runs reliably around the clock. Utilities have far less uranium locked in for the 2030s than they need — American utilities have only about 9% of their requirements contracted for 2033 — and new mines take 15 to 20 years to build. When buyers must have something that takes two decades to produce, the price tends to rise.

Why it might not: the speculative part of the fund can keep falling on its own, independent of uranium. About a fifth of the fund is a single company, Cameco, so one company's bad news hurts the whole thing. And the fund is currently in a downtrend, trading below its average price over both the last 50 and the last 200 trading days.

Jargon decoded:

- **ETF:** a fund that trades on an exchange like a single stock. Buying one share gets you a slice of everything the fund owns.
- **U₃O₈ (yellowcake):** the form uranium is traded in. Prices are quoted per pound.
- **Spot price vs term price:** spot is what uranium costs for immediate delivery (~\$89.50/lb). Term is what utilities agree to pay under contracts running years into the future (~\$96/lb, a record). Term matters more because most uranium is sold that way.
- **Expense ratio:** the annual fee, here 0.69%, taken automatically from the fund's assets. On a \$10,000 position that is \$69 a year.
- **AUM:** assets under management — the total value of everything the fund holds, here ~\$5.74B.
- **Market-cap weighted:** larger companies get bigger weights automatically, which is why Cameco is 21.81% of the fund.
- **Contract coverage:** the share of a utility's future uranium needs already locked in. Low coverage in the 2030s is the core bullish argument.
- **SMR:** small modular reactor — a smaller, factory-built reactor design. Several SMR developers are in this fund.
- **Baseload power:** electricity available 24/7, unlike solar or wind. Nuclear is one of very few carbon-free baseload sources.
- **Moving average:** the average price over a recent period. Trading below the 50-day and 200-day averages, as URA is, signals a downtrend.
