# FN — Fabrinet

Fabrinet is the contract manufacturer that physically builds a large share of the world's high-speed optical transceivers, and fiscal 2026 was its best year ever: revenue rose 36% to \$4.64B, non-GAAP EPS rose 39% to \$14.09, and data-center revenue hit \$669M in the June quarter — 51% of the total, up 68% year over year. Despite that, the stock closed at \$409.96 on 2026-09-28, roughly 45% below its 52-week high of \$748.89 and below both its 50-day (~\$454) and 200-day (~\$533) moving averages. The de-rating is not about missed numbers; it is about the composition of growth (NVIDIA-driven datacom normalising as NVIDIA dual-sources 800G), a gross margin stuck near 12%, and free cash flow that collapsed to \$4M in FY2026 as capex more than doubled. At ~22x consensus FY2027 EPS with ~\$875M of net cash and no debt, the market is paying a modest multiple for a business still compounding earnings above 30%.

## Company Overview

Fabrinet is an outsourced manufacturer, not a product company. It provides advanced optical packaging and precision optical, electro-mechanical and electronic manufacturing services to OEMs of complex products — optical communication components, modules and subsystems, plus automotive components, medical devices, industrial lasers and sensors. Its core plants are in Chonburi and Pinehurst, Thailand, with additional operations in Nava Nakorn (Thailand), the United States, China and Israel. It employed ~21,521 people as of the latest disclosure and has been listed on the NYSE since June 2010.

The business model is "any mix, any volume" high-complexity manufacturing. Fabrinet does process design and engineering, supply chain management, PCB assembly, advanced packaging, integration, final assembly and test. Customers hand over designs that are hard to build — hermetically sealed optical packages, silicon-photonics assemblies, co-packaged optics — and Fabrinet's accumulated process know-how, low-cost Thai labour base and clean-room scale make it very hard to second-source quickly.

Revenue mix in the fiscal fourth quarter of 2026 (ended 2026-06-26) was \$669M of data-center revenue (51% of total, +68% year over year, +13% sequentially), \$413M of communications-infrastructure revenue (+40% year over year), and the ~\$234M balance in automotive, industrial, medical and other markets. Within data center, the fastest-growing lines are data-center interconnect (400ZR and 800ZR coherent pluggables, which crossed a \$1B annualised run rate by the end of FY2026) and high-performance-compute PCBAs for a major hyperscaler's silicon platform.

The moat is real but narrow: switching an optical program out of Fabrinet means requalifying a manufacturing line, which takes quarters. The flip side is customer concentration. In FY2026 Cisco was 20% of revenue, NVIDIA 16%, Nokia 11% and Amazon 11% — roughly 58% in four accounts. Seamus Grady has been CEO since 2017 and the company has run the same disciplined, low-leverage playbook throughout: no debt, no dividend, modest buybacks, and capacity added only against visible customer commitments.

## Financial Analysis

Fiscal 2026 ended 2026-06-26. Revenue was \$4,641.1M, up 35.7% from \$3,419.3M in FY2025. GAAP gross profit was \$556.5M (12.0% margin) and non-GAAP gross profit \$568.0M (12.2%). GAAP operating income was \$462.9M (10.0% margin) versus \$324.4M (9.5%) a year earlier; non-GAAP operating margin was 10.8% versus 10.5%. GAAP net income was \$473.0M and diluted EPS \$13.05, against \$332.5M and \$9.17 in FY2025. Non-GAAP net income was \$510.9M and non-GAAP diluted EPS \$14.09, up 39% from \$10.17.

The fiscal fourth quarter was the peak: revenue \$1,315.8M (+45% year over year), GAAP diluted EPS \$3.83, non-GAAP diluted EPS \$4.10 — both above the guidance range and above the ~\$1.27B / \$3.81 consensus.

Two large one-off items roughly offset inside the FY2026 GAAP result: a \$56.7M gain on revaluation of non-marketable equity securities, and a \$57.4M tax provision arising from the OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax framework. Both are excluded from the non-GAAP figures, and Pillar Two continues into FY2027 guidance at \$0.40 per share in the September quarter — a structural, not one-time, tax headwind.

Margin is the honest weak spot. Non-GAAP gross margin was 12.2% in FY2026 versus 12.4% in FY2025, and 12.2% in Q4 FY2026 versus 12.5% a year earlier. Revenue grew 45% in the quarter and gross margin still went backwards 30 basis points. Operating leverage is coming from SG&A discipline (SG&A rose only 7% on 36% revenue growth), not from the factory floor. This is what an EMS business looks like: earnings scale with volume, not with price.

The balance sheet is pristine. At fiscal year end Fabrinet held \$346.7M of cash and equivalents plus \$528.3M of short-term investments — ~\$875M, or roughly \$24 per share — against zero borrowings and total liabilities of \$1,456.7M that are almost entirely trade payables. Shareholders' equity was \$2,454.3M. Shares outstanding were 35.83M with 36.25M diluted, essentially flat year over year.

Cash flow is where the AI ramp shows up as pain. FY2026 operating cash flow was \$256.7M, down from \$328.4M, because working capital absorbed cash: inventories rose \$442.9M (to \$1,021.2M) and receivables rose \$259.3M (to \$1,017.9M), only partly offset by a \$370.8M increase in payables. Capex was \$252.5M versus \$121.1M, so non-GAAP free cash flow was \$4.2M for the year against \$207.3M in FY2025, and negative \$36.9M in the fourth quarter alone on \$91.9M of quarterly capex. That is a deliberate choice — Fabrinet is pre-building capacity — but it means the company is currently converting almost none of its record earnings into distributable cash.

Guidance for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (quarter ended 2026-09-25, results due 2026-11-02) is revenue of \$1.375B to \$1.425B, GAAP diluted EPS of \$3.39 to \$3.54 and non-GAAP diluted EPS of \$4.10 to \$4.25 on ~36.3M diluted shares. The midpoint of \$1.40B was well above the ~\$1.32B consensus standing before the print.

## Valuation

At \$409.96 the market capitalisation is ~\$14.7B. Net of ~\$875M of cash and investments and with no debt, enterprise value is ~\$13.8B, or ~3.0x FY2026 revenue and ~27x FY2026 non-GAAP operating profit of \$500.1M.

On earnings: 29.1x trailing FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of \$14.09, 31.4x trailing GAAP EPS of \$13.05, and ~22x the ~\$18.56 FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS compiled by Zacks (which implies ~32% growth and is consistent with the ~22.5x forward P/E shown by data aggregators). Street models carried by MarketBeat then step FY2028 to ~\$21.27, putting the stock at ~19x two years out. Those are consensus estimates, not company guidance — Fabrinet guides one quarter at a time.

Cash-flow multiples tell the opposite story: Zacks puts FN on ~56x price/cash flow versus ~21x for its sector, precisely because FY2026 free cash flow was \$4M. Whether 22x forward earnings or 56x cash flow is the right lens depends entirely on whether the current capex and inventory build converts into FY2027–FY2028 revenue.

Analyst consensus is constructive and far above the tape: MarketBeat shows a Moderate Buy from ten analysts with an average 12-month target of \$649.50 (range \$452 to \$800); S&P Global's poll of nine analysts (via stockanalysis.com) shows Buy with a \$734.11 average; TipRanks shows \$726 average, \$598 low, \$850 high. Zacks rates the shares a Rank 2 (Buy) while flagging a Value Score of C. In other words, sell-side targets sit 55% to 80% above spot — a gap that usually resolves through target cuts, a price recovery, or both.

**Scenario targets.** **Bull: \$700–820.** **Base: \$480–560.** **Bear: \$280–340.**

The bull case needs datacom to re-accelerate as merchant transceiver makers and hyperscalers replace NVIDIA's earlier growth, Building 10 to fill quickly, and mix shift into co-packaged and near-package optics to finally lift gross margin above the 12–13% band; \$23–25 of FY2028 EPS at ~30x gets there. The base case assumes FY2027 unfolds roughly as consensus expects (~\$18.50 of non-GAAP EPS, gross margin flat at 12.0–12.5%) and the stock re-rates to a 26–30x multiple as free cash flow turns positive again — \$480–560. The bear case assumes hyperscale AI capex digestion in calendar 2027, further NVIDIA share loss, and a multiple compressed to 18–20x on flat \$15–16 EPS.

## Growth Catalysts

**Capacity as a forward order book.** Building 10 at the Chonburi campus adds ~2 million square feet and, on management's own numbers, ~\$3.0–3.5B of annual revenue capacity, taking total capacity toward \$8.5–9.3B. Adding Nava Nakorn, the newly acquired ~130,000 square foot Santa Clara new-product-introduction facility and two further potential Chonburi factories, the planned footprint could ultimately support ~\$12.5–14B of annualised revenue — roughly 2.7x to 3.0x FY2026. Fabrinet does not build speculatively, so the footprint plan is itself a statement about customer visibility.

**Data-center interconnect.** 400ZR and 800ZR coherent pluggables passed a \$1B annualised run rate by the end of FY2026 and management expects multi-year growth. The driver is structural: hyperscalers are power-constrained at individual sites, so they are splitting training clusters across campuses and buying coherent optics to stitch them together.

**High-performance compute PCBAs.** Multiple programs with a major hyperscaler ramped through Q4 FY2026, and Fabrinet is installing capacity for that customer's next-generation silicon platform. This is the diversification away from transceivers that the bear case says cannot happen.

**Co-packaged and near-package optics.** Management has repeatedly flagged NPO/CPO and optical circuit switching as higher-margin opportunities where its packaging expertise matters more than its labour cost. Citi has publicly sized the optical switching market at ~\$11B. iPronics announced a silicon-photonics packaging and assembly line for next-generation AI optical circuit switches at Fabrinet in March 2026.

**Telecom and space.** Communications-infrastructure revenue grew 40% year over year in Q4 FY2026, helped by telecom systems and satellite communications. Fabrinet has two major low-earth-orbit satellite customers and describes the segment as significant and growing quickly.

**Datacom inflection.** Management expects datacom to return to sequential growth in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The 2026-11-02 print is the first direct test of that claim.

## Risk Factors

**Customer concentration.** Cisco 20%, NVIDIA 16%, Nokia 11%, Amazon 11% of FY2026 revenue. Any one of those insourcing, dual-sourcing further, or cutting builds moves the P&L immediately — which is exactly what happened with NVIDIA's 800G transceivers after Fabrinet had been sole manufacturer of the 400G and early 800G generations.

**Structurally thin margins.** At 12% gross and ~11% non-GAAP operating margin, a 100 basis point cost shock erases roughly a tenth of gross profit. Fabrinet has no pricing power over its OEM customers and little ability to offset wage inflation, Thai baht strength or component cost spikes.

**Free cash flow is currently negligible.** \$4.2M for FY2026, negative \$36.9M in Q4. Inventories nearly doubled to \$1.02B. If end demand slows before that inventory ships, Fabrinet takes both a write-down risk and a cash-conversion problem while carrying a much larger fixed-asset base.

**Capacity risk cuts both ways.** A \$12.5–14B footprint plan against \$4.6B of revenue is a bet. If AI optics demand plateaus, the depreciation and overhead land in a lower-margin revenue base.

**Geographic and tax exposure.** The great majority of manufacturing is in Thailand, with additional operations in China and Israel — concentration risk in flood-prone Ayutthaya-region industrial estates, plus tariff, export-control and geopolitical exposure. The OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax is a permanent step-up in the effective tax rate: \$57.4M in FY2026 and \$0.40 per share guided in Q1 FY2027.

**Competition.** Fabrinet competes with large diversified EMS players (Jabil, Flex, TTM Technologies), with customers' own in-house lines, and increasingly with Chinese optical module manufacturers on cost.

**Sector beta.** The AI optics complex trades as one block. Fabrinet fell 6% on 2026-09-14 when Corning announced a \$2B at-the-market equity offering, and the group slid again on 2026-09-28 with no company-specific news. Position sizing has to respect that.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Targets: **Bull \$700–820 · Base \$480–560 · Bear \$280–340.**

The thesis is simple. Fabrinet is growing revenue 36% and non-GAAP EPS 39%, guiding the September quarter to \$1.375–1.425B and \$4.10–4.25, carries ~\$875M of net cash and no debt, and trades at ~22x consensus FY2027 earnings after a 45% drawdown. The drawdown was driven by mix anxiety (NVIDIA normalising) and cash-flow optics (capex), not by deteriorating demand. Base case fair value of \$480–560 implies ~17% to ~37% upside from \$409.96; the sell-side average sits at \$650–734, which we treat as too optimistic.

**Position sizing.** Treat this as a cyclical AI-infrastructure position, not a compounder: 2–3% of a diversified portfolio. The 12% gross margin and four-customer concentration mean the downside distribution has a real left tail.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is in a confirmed downtrend — below the 50-day (~\$453.76) and 200-day (~\$532.95) moving averages, with the 50-day below the 200-day. Do not buy the full position into that. Stage it: a third near current levels (\$400–420), a third on either a retest of the 52-week low near \$355.50 or a reclaim of the 50-day on volume, and the final third after the 2026-11-02 print confirms datacom sequential growth. Momentum indicators have been persistently weak since mid-August; wait for RSI to turn up from oversold rather than catching the knife.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$340 breaks the 52-week low structure and invalidates the base case — exit there. That is ~17% below spot, which is why the position should be small.

**Catalyst calendar.** Q1 FY2027 results 2026-11-02 (the datacom sequential-growth test). Q2 FY2027 results early February 2027. OFC 2027 in March, where Fabrinet historically hosts investor sessions and discusses CPO/NPO and 1.6T. Q3 FY2027 results in May 2027. Building 10 revenue contribution through fiscal 2027.

**What would change the rating.** A downgrade to HOLD if Q1 FY2027 shows datacom flat again or gross margin below 12.0%; an upgrade in conviction if gross margin breaks above 12.5% while revenue holds a \$1.4B-plus quarterly pace.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: Fabrinet builds other companies' optical parts. When an AI data centre needs hundreds of thousands of high-speed optical plugs to connect its chips, someone has to physically assemble those plugs to very tight tolerances. Fabrinet is the biggest independent company doing that, mostly out of factories in Thailand.

Business is booming. Sales grew 36% last fiscal year to \$4.6 billion, profits grew faster, and the June quarter alone did \$1.3 billion with data centres over half of it. The company told investors the current quarter should do \$1.375 to \$1.425 billion — more than Wall Street expected.

The stock fell anyway, and has kept falling — it is down about 45% from its high. Three reasons. First, NVIDIA used to buy its optical plugs almost exclusively from Fabrinet and now buys from several suppliers, so that particular growth engine has cooled and other customers have to take over. Second, Fabrinet is a contract manufacturer, so it only keeps about 12 cents of gross profit per dollar of sales, and that number is not improving even as sales soar. Third, the company is spending heavily on new factories and stockpiling parts, so almost none of last year's record profit turned into spare cash.

The counter-argument is that you are now paying about 22 times next year's expected earnings for a debt-free company with \$875 million in the bank that is still growing earnings above 30%, and every analyst covering it thinks the shares are worth \$600 to \$800. We think \$480 to \$560 is the honest base case, which is still meaningfully above today's \$409.96.

So: buy it, but buy it small and buy it in pieces. The chart is still falling, the customer list is short, and the profit margin is thin. If the November 2nd results show the data-centre business growing again quarter over quarter, the story works. If they do not, this is a \$300 stock.
