# USAR — USA Rare Earth, Inc.

USA Rare Earth is trying to do in three years what China spent three decades building: a complete rare earth value chain — mine, oxide separation, metal and alloy, finished sintered magnet — entirely outside Asia. As of September 2026 it owns the pieces. The Pela Ema heavy rare earth mine in Goiás, Brazil came with the Serra Verde acquisition that closed on 3 September 2026. Less Common Metals (LCM) in Cheshire, UK makes rare earth metals and alloys at commercial scale, with a sister plant planned at Lacq, France. Stillwater, Oklahoma is commissioning sintered NdFeB magnet lines. Wheat Ridge, Colorado runs hydrometallurgical separation and magnet-swarf recycling. Round Top in Hudspeth County, Texas — now 100% owned after the Texas Mineral Resources acquisition closed in August 2026 — is the long-dated heavy rare earth resource. Washington has committed up to \$1.6B under the CHIPS Act and capitalised a \$1.55B offtake vehicle for Pela Ema. What the company does not yet have is commercial economics: trailing revenue is roughly \$13M, gross margin is negative, and the two assets that matter most do not generate meaningful cash before 2027 and 2028. At \$14.41 — 67% below the 52-week high of \$43.98 — the market is charging a fraction of the peak for the same asset base, which is what makes this interesting and dangerous at the same time.

## Company Overview

The business today spans four operating nodes and one development project:

- **Pela Ema mine, Brazil (Serra Verde)** — an ionic clay deposit and the only large-scale producer outside Asia of all four magnetic rare earths (neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium, terbium). Production began in January 2024 but commissioning and optimisation are unfinished. Stage 1 targets a run-rate of roughly 4,000 metric tons per annum of total rare earth oxide (TREO) by the end of 2026; Stage 2 construction targets average annual output of 6,400 tpa, with commissioning expected to begin within twelve months.
- **LCM, Cheshire, UK** — a long-established producer of rare earth and critical mineral metals and alloys, acquired in November 2025. LCM supplies USAR's own magnet lines and third parties, and produced the first commercial pour of 2N–2N5 purity yttrium metal outside China in April 2026. Metal and alloy capacity is being expanded toward 3,000 MTPA, with a planned 3,750 MTPA plant at Lacq, France co-located with Carester's Caremag facility.
- **Stillwater, Oklahoma** — sintered NdFeB permanent magnets. Phase 1a was commissioned in March 2026 and is guided to a 600 MTPA run-rate by Q4 2026; Phase 1b takes the site to 1,200 MTPA in Q1 2027.
- **Wheat Ridge, Colorado** — a hydrometallurgical demonstration facility commissioned in June 2026, running three flowsheets in parallel: Round Top ore, third-party mixed rare earth carbonate, and magnet swarf recycling. In July 2026 it produced commercial-grade dysprosium and NdPr oxide from recycled swarf, a capability management expects to cover up to 30% of future magnetic oxide feedstock needs.
- **Round Top, Texas** — the long-dated resource, rich in heavy rare earths and other critical minerals, with Fluor and WSP as EPCM partners. The Definitive Feasibility Study is due to complete in Q4 2026 and publish in Q1 2027; commercial production is targeted for late 2028.

A second U.S. metals and magnet plant is being built at Blacksburg, South Carolina — 6,400 tpa of NdFeB magnets and 5,000 tpa of strip-cast metal and alloy, ground broken on 9 September 2026, commissioning targeted from 2028. Combined with Stillwater expansion, management's 2030 ambition is 10,000 tpa of magnets and 10,000 tpa of heavy rare earth metal and alloy. USAR also holds a ~13.6% stake in French separation specialist Carester alongside InfraVia, with rights to purchase Caremag oxide output and access Carester's process IP.

The moat, such as it is, is not geological — it is the combination of a producing non-China heavy rare earth mine, downstream metal-making know-how that very few Western firms possess, and a government sponsor willing to fund the gap between the two. Management changed at the top on 1 October 2026: Barbara Humpton retired and Thras Moraitis, Serra Verde's CEO, took over the combined company. That is a mine operator replacing an industrial executive, which fits the next phase but resets the leadership track record.

Three things stand out. First, the headline net loss of only \$10.3M flatters the quarter — a \$33.8M fair-value gain on warrants did the work, and the adjusted loss of \$0.15 per share missed the ~\$0.07 consensus. Second, revenue of \$5.82M missed consensus by roughly 25%, and the business is selling below cost: higher heavy rare earth input costs produced a gross loss, which management is addressing with price increases expected to show up in later quarters. Third, the spending curve is steep — H1 2026 operating cash outflow of \$75.3M plus \$108.4M of capex is \$183.7M of negative free cash flow in six months.

Post-quarter, the capital structure changed materially. Serra Verde closed on 3 September 2026 for \$300M cash plus approximately 126.8 million shares, and brought roughly \$325M of principal under Serra Verde's U.S. International Development Finance Corporation loan (a \$100M tranche was extinguished at closing when related warrants were exercised; pro forma carrying value about \$304.1M, priced at an assumed Term SOFR plus 4%). Pro forma cash at 30 June 2026 was \$1.392B. Serra Verde itself is not yet an earnings contributor: in H1 2026 it recorded \$588K of revenue, a \$4.7M gross loss and a \$39.8M operating loss, and remains development-stage for accounting purposes with commercial operations expected in 2027. On top of that, the company filed a \$1.25B mixed securities shelf and registered 126.48 million shares for resale by selling stockholders in early September.

Non-dilutive funding partially offsets this. The Department of Commerce definitive agreements unlock up to \$1.6B — \$277M of federal funding and up to \$1.3B of senior secured loan capacity under the CHIPS programme, disbursed against milestones. A \$14.2M Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant and up to \$19.3M from the Department of Energy add smaller amounts. The Pela Ema Phase 1 offtake sits with a special purpose vehicle capitalised at \$1.55B by the U.S. government and private investors, with escalating annual price floors.

## Valuation

Conventional multiples are meaningless here. Trailing revenue is about \$13.2M against an equity value of roughly \$5.1B once the Serra Verde shares are counted (~353M shares at \$14.41; several data providers still show ~\$3.5B on a stale 244.7M share count). There is no earnings, no EBITDA, and no positive gross profit to capitalise. Valuation is therefore an asset-and-milestone exercise: what is a producing ex-China heavy rare earth mine plus Western magnet capacity plus \$1.39B of pro forma cash worth against a build programme that consumes several hundred million dollars a year?

Sell-side consensus is unusually wide and unusually bullish relative to the tape: MarketBeat shows 11 analysts with an average 12-month target of \$33.71 (high \$45, low \$21, Moderate Buy); stockanalysis.com shows 9 analysts at \$35.56 with a Strong Buy consensus. Jefferies initiated in early September 2026 at Buy with a \$21 target — the low end, and notably the most recent. Several firms trimmed targets through August and September. Treat the ~\$33–36 average as an aspiration built on 2028–2030 capacity, not a near-term fair value.

**Bull case: \$28–38.** Pela Ema hits its Stage 1 run-rate, declares commercial operations in 2027 and starts delivering against a price-floored offtake; Stillwater passes customer qualification and ramps to 1,200 MTPA; CHIPS money funds Blacksburg and Round Top without new equity; non-China premiums persist. In that world the stock re-rates toward the analyst range.

**Base case: \$17–22.** Revenue scales from ~\$13M TTM into the tens of millions through 2027 as LCM alloy sales, first Stillwater magnet deliveries and Pela Ema concentrate ramp, but gross margin hovers around breakeven while input costs and commissioning inefficiencies persist. Serra Verde commercial operations slip into mid-2027, CHIPS milestones are met with modest delays, cash burn runs \$400M+ a year against ~\$1.39B pro forma liquidity plus government loans, and the equity recovers toward its 50-day (~\$16.85) and 200-day (~\$19.36) moving averages.

**Bear case: \$8–11.** A durable U.S.-China rare earth detente compresses the non-China premium, Pela Ema commissioning slips again while DFC debt service starts, Stillwater ramps slowly, and the company returns to the equity market off the \$1.25B shelf. Below the 52-week low of \$11.45, this becomes a cash-and-optionality valuation.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Serra Verde ramp.** Stage 1 to ~4,000 tpa TREO by end-2026, commercial operations in 2027, Stage 2 toward 6,400 tpa average. This is the single largest swing factor because it converts USAR from a builder into a producer of the heavy rare earths that magnets actually need.
- **Government-backed offtake.** The \$1.55B SPV takes 100% of Pela Ema Phase 1 output with escalating price floors, with Serra Verde keeping 70% of economics above the floor. Contracted volume with downside protection is rare in this sector.
- **Stillwater and Blacksburg magnet capacity.** 600 MTPA run-rate in Q4 2026, 1,200 MTPA in Q1 2027, then 6,400 tpa of magnets and 5,000 tpa of metal and alloy at Blacksburg from 2028 — a path toward the 10,000 tpa magnet / 10,000 tpa metal ambition for 2030, with about 490 jobs in South Carolina.
- **CHIPS Act drawdowns.** \$277M of grants and up to \$1.3B of senior secured loans released on milestones, plus \$14.2M from Texas and up to \$19.3M from the DOE.
- **Round Top DFS.** Completion in Q4 2026 and publication in Q1 2027 puts real project economics on a deposit that has been a story for a decade, ahead of a late-2028 production target and more than \$1.4B of planned West Texas investment.
- **Swarf recycling and third-party feedstock.** Wheat Ridge has already produced commercial-grade Dy and NdPr oxide from magnet scrap; management sees swarf covering up to 30% of future oxide feedstock, which shortens the dependence on Round Top.
- **Price spreads.** As of August 2026 the company cited non-China dysprosium oxide trading at roughly 9.2x Chinese prices and yttrium oxide at a 208x differential. Western buyers paying strategic premiums is the commercial foundation of the whole plan.
- **Process technology partnerships.** The September 2026 tie-up with Pasqal and Riven Systems applies quantum machine learning to separation chemistry — early-stage, but a signal of how much of the cost problem sits in processing.

## Risk Factors

- **Nothing is commercial yet.** TTM revenue of ~\$13.2M comes with a gross loss. Serra Verde is still development-stage for accounting purposes. Round Top does not produce until late 2028 at the earliest.
- **Burn versus build.** H1 2026 alone consumed \$183.7M of free cash flow, and the programme ahead spans Stillwater, Blacksburg, Lacq and Round Top simultaneously. Liquidity is strong today; it is not unlimited.
- **Dilution.** Approximately 126.8M shares issued for Serra Verde, a 126.48M-share resale registration creating overhang, a fresh \$1.25B mixed shelf, and outstanding warrants and earnouts. The share count has moved a long way in a short time.
- **Inherited leverage and covenants.** Roughly \$325M of DFC principal at an assumed Term SOFR plus 4%, with restrictive covenants, and debt service that begins before mature operating cash flow.
- **The offtake cuts both ways.** The SPV takes 100% of Phase 1 volumes and 30% of the upside above price floors, potentially for 20 years after commercial operations. Downside protection is paid for with capped upside and reduced flexibility.
- **Geopolitics is the thesis.** The equity trades on the non-China premium. Rare earth names sold off hard around the September 2026 Trump-Xi summit in Washington on detente hopes, even though U.S. officials noted Chinese rare earth deliveries were still falling short. A genuine supply normalisation would undercut the entire re-rating.
- **Policy and process risk.** CHIPS disbursements are milestone-contingent. Bloomberg reporting in September 2026 on the role of Commerce Secretary Lutnick's former firm in raising the \$1.5B PIPE the week before the \$1.6B federal commitment adds headline and political risk to the funding path.
- **China export-control designation.** The company has been named on a Chinese export control list, which has constrained and is expected to continue constraining its ability to source certain raw materials and supplies from China.
- **Execution under a new CEO.** Ramping Pela Ema, qualifying magnet customers, building Blacksburg and financing Round Top at once, with leadership that changed on 1 October 2026.
- **Volatility.** Beta of 2.61, and 30.78M shares (15.18% of float) sold short as of 31 August 2026, up 8.3% from the prior report.

## Recommendation

**SPEC. BUY** — a position for capital that can tolerate a 50% drawdown and a three-year holding period.

Position sizing: treat this as a venture-style allocation, 1–2% of a diversified portfolio, and no more than a third of total critical-minerals exposure. Pairing with MP Materials diversifies the execution risk without leaving the theme.

Entry strategy: the stock closed at \$14.41 on 28 September 2026, below both the 50-day (\$16.85) and 200-day (\$19.36) moving averages, having fallen 5% that session with 13.7M shares traded. The 52-week range is \$11.45 to \$43.98, and the one-year return is -17.8%. Scale in rather than buying a single clip: a first tranche here, a second in the \$11.50–12.50 area if the \$11.45 low is retested, and a third only on confirmation — either Pela Ema hitting its Stage 1 run-rate or the first quarter of positive gross margin. Momentum is clearly negative and there is no technical support to lean on above the 52-week low.

Stop loss: for a thesis-driven position, use fundamental rather than price stops — a failed CHIPS milestone, a Pela Ema commissioning delay pushing commercial operations past 2027, or an equity raise off the shelf at a discount. If you prefer a hard stop, a weekly close below \$11 breaks the range and invalidates the "priced for the assets" argument.

Catalyst calendar: CEO transition on 1 October 2026; Q3 2026 results around 5 November 2026 (first quarter with Serra Verde consolidated); Stillwater 600 MTPA and Pela Ema ~4,000 tpa run-rates plus Round Top DFS completion in Q4 2026; DFS publication and Stillwater Phase 1b in Q1 2027; Serra Verde commercial operations during 2027; Blacksburg commissioning and Round Top production in 2028.

The stock still fell, because the macro narrative (U.S.-China detente around the September Washington summit) overwhelmed company-specific progress, and because Q2 missed on both revenue and adjusted loss. Institutionally, Insider Monkey counted 34 hedge funds holding the stock at the end of Q2 2026, down from 39 three months earlier. **Options flow:** no clean read available in this session; treat the 2.61 beta and the short interest as the better volatility signals.

**Composite sentiment score: 5/10.** Bullish fundamentals and bullish analysts against negative price momentum, a heavy short base, dilution overhang and a macro narrative that currently works against the theme.

## Readability Pass

In plain terms: rare earth magnets are in almost everything that moves or computes — motors, drones, jets, robots, hard drives, medical scanners — and China controls most of the world's supply, especially the heavy elements like dysprosium and terbium that make magnets survive heat. USA Rare Earth is building a complete Western alternative: it now owns a mine in Brazil that produces those heavy elements, metal and alloy plants in the UK and France, a magnet factory in Oklahoma with a second being built in South Carolina, a recycling and separation facility in Colorado, and a big undeveloped deposit in Texas. The U.S. government is paying a large share of the bill — up to \$1.6B in CHIPS Act grants and loans — and has helped set up a \$1.55B vehicle that agrees to buy the Brazilian mine's output at guaranteed minimum prices.

The problem is timing. The company sold about \$13M of product over the past year and lost money on every unit. The Brazilian mine is not formally commercial until 2027. The Texas mine will not produce until 2028. Meanwhile it is spending a few hundred million dollars a year, it issued about 127 million new shares to buy the Brazilian business, and it took on roughly \$325M of debt with the acquisition.

The stock has fallen from \$43.98 to \$14.41 — partly because results disappointed, partly because a thaw in U.S.-China relations makes investors doubt that Western buyers will keep paying premium prices. So you are buying a genuinely strategic set of assets, largely funded, at about a third of the peak price, in exchange for accepting that nothing proves out for at least another year. Analysts think it is worth roughly \$33–36; the newest analyst to cover it says \$21. Our view: worth owning small, in pieces, with the expectation of a rough ride.
