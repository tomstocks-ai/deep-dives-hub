# TEM — Tempus AI, Inc.

Tempus AI is a Chicago-based precision-medicine platform that runs a large genomic diagnostics lab and then monetises the resulting multimodal clinical dataset by licensing it — and AI models built on it — to pharmaceutical companies. Q2 2026 produced \$382.5M of revenue (+22% YoY), the company's first GAAP profitable quarter and \$8.0M of adjusted EBITDA, and management raised FY2026 guidance to \$1.595–1.605B with ~\$65M of adjusted EBITDA. The business is executing; the share price is the problem. After a 54% one-month advance driven by reimbursement clarity on the xT and xF assays, TEM trades at ~10x forward sales and roughly 24% above the \$68.56 sell-side consensus target, with a 3.7 beta and GAAP profitability still dependent on mark-to-market securities gains. Rated HOLD at \$85.08.

## Company Overview

Tempus operates two reported segments.

**Diagnostics** (\$289.3M in Q2 2026, 76% of revenue, +20% YoY) is the genomic sequencing lab. It spans oncology tissue and liquid-biopsy profiling (xT, xF), hereditary cancer and germline testing (largely the acquired Ambry Genetics franchise), molecular residual disease (MRD) monitoring, and a growing cardiology AI line delivered as FDA-cleared ECG algorithms. Oncology test volume grew 31% YoY in Q2 2026, up from 28% in Q1. MRD volume reached 9,000 tests in the quarter, up from 6,500 in Q1 and ~4,700 in Q4 2025 — a steep ramp off a small base. Hereditary was the weak spot at +5% revenue growth.

**Data and Applications** (\$93.2M in Q2 2026, 24% of revenue, +28% YoY) licenses de-identified multimodal records and, increasingly, trained models to pharma and biotech. The Insights data-licensing sub-line grew 36% YoY. Tempus signed ~\$200M of new licences in Q2 alone, with named counterparties including AstraZeneca, GSK, Bristol-Myers Squibb, Merck, BioNTech, Daiichi Sankyo, Level Set Bio and Incyte. It closed FY2025 with over \$1.1B of remaining total contract value and 126% net revenue retention. In September 2026 it extended its Recursion Pharmaceuticals licence and added a new agreement covering Recursion's RNA foundation model.

The moat is the flywheel between the two: clinical sequencing volume generates proprietary longitudinal data, that data funds high-margin licensing, and licensing revenue subsidises lab economics that competitors must fund from testing alone. Data and Applications gross margin ran 70.2% in Q2 2026 versus 62.6% for Diagnostics, so mix shift is also a margin story. Tempus has reinforced the data asset with acquisitions — Ambry Genetics, Paige AI (digital pathology), Deep 6 AI (trial matching), OneOme — and in September 2026 launched an initiative to assemble 100,000 whole genomes linked to longitudinal clinical outcomes.

Founder-CEO Eric Lefkofsky (previously Groupon) controls Class B shares and remains the dominant voice; Jim Rogers is CFO. Founder control cuts both ways: it has enabled aggressive M&A and reinvestment, but it also concentrates strategic risk and, as of Q2 2026, produced \$54.1M of quarterly stock-based compensation — roughly 14% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 (the last completed fiscal year) revenue was \$1.272B, up 83.4%, though that headline is inflated by the Ambry acquisition; organic growth was ~33%. Gross profit was \$797.9M for a 62.7% gross margin, up from 55.0% in FY2024. GAAP net loss was \$245.0M (-\$1.41 diluted) and adjusted EBITDA was -\$7.4M, a \$97.3M year-over-year improvement. Free cash flow was roughly -\$245M (operating cash flow -\$218.1M, \$21.0M capex, \$6.2M capitalised software).

FY2026 is the inflection year. H1 2026 revenue was \$730.6M (+28% YoY) with 64.1% gross margin. Q2 specifics:

| Data and Applications revenue | \$93.2M | \$72.8M | +28% |
| Gross profit | \$246.5M | \$195.0M | +26% |

Two caveats on the "first GAAP profit" headline. First, it was manufactured below the operating line: the \$5.6M of net income includes \$98.5M of unrealised gains on marketable equity securities, against a \$75.9M operating loss. Non-GAAP net loss was -\$7.7M (-\$0.04 per share). Second, it is not cash. H1 2026 operating cash flow was -\$80.8M, worse than the -\$61.5M of H1 2025, driven by a \$50.6M receivables build as revenue scaled.

The balance sheet is adequate but levered. At 30-Jun-2026 Tempus held \$599.6M of cash, \$216.4M of marketable equity securities (\$820.7M combined) against \$1.173B of convertible senior notes and a \$188M convertible promissory note. During Q2 it issued \$460M of 0.0% converts due 2032 and used the proceeds to retire \$207.7M of term debt and a \$100M revolver draw, taking a \$11.6M extinguishment loss and cutting quarterly interest expense from \$21.6M to \$10.3M. Accumulated deficit stands at \$2.52B against \$444.9M of equity.

TTM figures per S&P Global data: revenue \$1.43B (+50%), net income -\$254.4M, EPS -\$1.43.

## Valuation

At \$85.08 the market cap is ~\$15.35B on 180.4M shares. Adding ~\$1.36B of converts and notes and deducting \$820.7M of cash and securities gives roughly \$15.9B of enterprise value.

| EV / FY2026E adj. EBITDA | ~\$65M (company guide) | ~245x |
| P/E | negative GAAP earnings | n/a |

There is no earnings anchor, so this is a revenue-multiple story. Roughly 10x forward sales is a software-like multiple on a business that is three-quarters clinical lab services — defensible only if the data segment keeps compounding near 30% and mix shift keeps lifting gross margin.

Sell-side consensus is a "Buy"/"Moderate Buy" rating with an average 12-month target of \$68.56 (20 analysts per S&P Global; MarketBeat shows \$69.63 across 22), high \$100, low \$35. The consensus target sits ~19–24% **below** the market price, which is unusual and reflects how fast the stock moved in September. Recent actions are clustered near the price rather than above it: Piper Sandler upgraded to Overweight with a \$76 target (1-Sep), H.C. Wainwright raised to \$66 (11-Sep), Goldman Sachs initiated Neutral at \$75, UBS initiated Neutral at \$70, Rothschild and Co Redburn initiated Neutral at \$67. Forward FY2027 EPS estimates have swung from -\$0.11 to roughly breakeven over 90 days with eight upward revisions in 30 days, so targets will likely drift higher — but the starting point is below spot.

**Scenario framework**

- **Bull: \$130–\$155.** FY2028 revenue reaches ~\$2.6B as the full ~\$400M ADLT/xF uplift lands and Personalis MRD scales; 10–12x EV/sales on that base implies \$130–\$155 per share on ~195M shares.
- **Base: \$85–\$100.** FY2026 lands inside guidance, FY2027 approaches the ~\$1.97B consensus, and the stock holds 8–9x forward EV/sales while sell-side targets migrate up to meet the price.
- **Bear: \$45–\$58.** Reimbursement timing slips, Personalis disappoints or momentum simply unwinds; a 3.7-beta name retraces to the \$60 50-day and \$56 200-day moving averages, with 4–5x forward sales implying the low end.

## Growth Catalysts

**Reimbursement repricing is the biggest single lever.** The FDA approved the tumour-only xT CDx assay in Q2 2026, making Tempus the first lab holding companion-diagnostic approval for both tumour-only and tumour-normal comprehensive genomic profiling. That approval migrates tissue testing to Advanced Diagnostic Laboratory Test (ADLT) pricing. On the Q2 call and at the Morgan Stanley healthcare conference on 15-Sep, Lefkofsky sized xT pricing at \$80–100M of incremental annualised revenue and xF approval plus pricing at a further \$250–300M, consistent with prepared remarks guiding to ~\$400M of 2028 revenue uplift across the two approvals. This is the September re-rating in one sentence: the same volumes, repriced.

**MRD via Personalis.** The \$1.5B enterprise-value acquisition announced 20-Jul-2026 (\$16.25 per PSNL share, up to 50% fundable in cash) folds the ultrasensitive NeXT Personal MRD assay into Tempus' commercial infrastructure. Management sizes the MRD market at \$20B+. Tempus already distributed NeXT Personal under a collaboration, so integration risk is lower than a cold acquisition, and Personalis secured UK regulatory clearance for NeXT Personal Dx for clinical-trial use. Close is guided to late Q4 2026 or early 2027 and is excluded from current guidance.

**Data licensing and foundation models.** ~\$200M of Q2 bookings, 126% net revenue retention, and the first delivery of a Tempus oncology foundation model to AstraZeneca. The September Recursion agreement — licensing Recursion's RNA foundation model — shows the relationship running in both directions. A \$1.1B+ remaining contract value backlog underpins the segment.

**Cardiology and non-oncology expansion.** FDA 510(k) clearances for Tempus ECG-PH (pulmonary hypertension, August 2026) and ECG-MR (mitral regurgitation, 28-Sep-2026) extend the AI product line beyond oncology into screening algorithms that run on standard ECGs. Up to \$9.5M of ARPA-H ADVOCATE funding (announced 9-Sep) supports an autonomous AI agent for heart-failure care.

**Health-system land grab.** Multi-year collaborations with the Keck School of Medicine of USC (1.5M+ annual patient visits), NYU Langone, Northwestern Medicine, Yale New Haven and Memorial Sloan Kettering (digital-pathology consortium) each feed both segments.

## Risk Factors

**Valuation is the primary risk.** ~\$15.4B of market value on ~\$1.6B of 2026 revenue leaves no margin for a missed quarter, and the consensus target is below spot. A 3.7 beta means drawdowns in biotech or high-multiple equities land amplified here. The 52-week range of \$40.77–\$104.32 is a fair description of how wide the distribution is.

**Profitability is not yet real.** The Q2 GAAP profit came from \$98.5M of unrealised securities gains against a \$75.9M operating loss, and H1 operating cash flow was -\$80.8M. Adjusted EBITDA of \$8.0M on \$382.5M of revenue is a 2% margin. Stock-based compensation of \$54.1M in the quarter flatters every adjusted metric and dilutes holders.

**Reimbursement risk cuts both ways.** The bull case rests on ADLT pricing and xF approval — decisions made by the FDA and CMS, not by Tempus. Slippage of a few quarters materially changes the 2027–2028 revenue bridge that the current multiple assumes.

**Integration and M&A risk.** Ambry, Paige AI, Deep 6 AI, OneOme and now Personalis in under three years. Goodwill and intangibles total \$783M against \$444.9M of equity. Hereditary revenue growing only 5% in Q2 2026 is an early signal that Ambry's contribution is maturing as the acquisition laps.

**Leverage.** ~\$1.36B of converts and notes against \$820.7M of cash and securities. The 2032 converts carry a 0.0% coupon, so cash interest is manageable, but conversion is dilution and refinancing depends on the equity staying elevated.

**Competition.** Natera and Guardant Health in liquid biopsy and MRD, Foundation Medicine (Roche) and Caris in comprehensive genomic profiling, Exact Sciences in screening, Illumina upstream. Several are better capitalised.

**Governance and insider behaviour.** Founder control via Class B shares. Into the September rally, CEO Lefkofsky sold ~\$16.1M of stock (17-Sep, \$80.00–\$81.26), co-CEO Ryan Fukushima and EVP Andrew Polovin also sold. These are plausibly scheduled sales, but the timing is not a vote of confidence at these levels.

## Recommendation

**HOLD at \$85.08.** The operating story has genuinely improved — 22% revenue growth accelerating in oncology volumes, 240bp of gross-margin expansion, positive adjusted EBITDA, a credible \$330–400M reimbursement uplift and a sensible MRD acquisition. None of that is in dispute. What is in dispute is paying ~10x forward sales for it after a 54% one-month move, with the average analyst target 19–24% below spot and GAAP profitability still borrowed from mark-to-market gains.

**Scenario targets: Bull \$130–\$155 · Base \$85–\$100 · Bear \$45–\$58.** Base case brackets the current price, which is the definition of a hold.

**Position sizing.** Small — 1–2% of a growth sleeve for new money, given the 3.7 beta. Existing holders sitting on the September move have a reasonable case for trimming back to a starter position and letting the Q3 print set the next entry.

**Entry strategy.** Do not chase. The stock is extended: \$85.08 sits 42% above the \$60.02 50-day moving average and 51% above the \$56.30 200-day, a gap that historically resolves through consolidation rather than continuation. Better risk/reward exists in the \$65–\$72 zone, which is where the bulk of the September analyst targets cluster and where the pre-breakout base formed. A retest of \$60 (50-day) would be a strong accumulation area if the fundamental trajectory is intact.

**Stop loss.** For momentum positions, \$68 — below that the September reimbursement-driven move is largely given back and the \$56 200-day becomes the next reference. Longer-horizon holders should frame risk against the \$40.77 52-week low rather than a tight stop, because a 3.7-beta name will trigger any stop placed close.

**Catalyst calendar.** Q3 2026 results 3-Nov-2026 (oncology volumes, MRD test count, whether FY2026 guidance moves again); Personalis close late Q4 2026 or early 2027 including funding mix; xF FDA approval and ADLT pricing during 2027; Q4/FY2026 results and first FY2027 guidance around February 2027, the first real test of the "25%+ multiyear growth" claim.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: strongly positive, arguably saturated.** September 2026 produced an uninterrupted run of favourable headlines — ARPA-H funding (9-Sep), the 100,000-genome initiative (11-Sep), the Morgan Stanley conference reimbursement commentary (15-Sep), the Recursion foundation-model licence (22-Sep) and the ECG-MR FDA clearance (28-Sep). Coverage framing shifted from "healthcare AI stock down 30–65% year-to-date" in May to "is more upside ahead?" by mid-September. That inversion of tone inside four months is itself a caution signal.

**Sell-side: constructive on fundamentals, neutral on the stock.** Ratings split roughly 11 buy / 5–8 hold / 2 sell, producing a "Moderate Buy" consensus with an average target of \$68.56–\$69.63 — below spot. The three initiations in September (Goldman \$75 Neutral, UBS \$70 Neutral, Rothschild and Co Redburn \$67 Neutral) all chose Neutral. Piper Sandler's upgrade to Overweight at \$76 (1-Sep) is the clearest bullish action, and even that target is below the current price. Goldman's framing — "highly visible near-term growth in diagnostics but mid-term questions" — captures the house view well.

**Retail and social: hot.** TEM topped the daily US volume chart on 15-Sep with ~\$550M traded and gained 10.7% that day, then 13.8–14.5% on 17-Sep. Six-month gain of ~99%. The stock has a persistent retail following (it periodically appears in congressional-trading and "AI stock" screens), which adds upside velocity and downside gap risk in equal measure.

**Insider flow: negative.** CEO Lefkofsky sold ~\$16.1M on 17-Sep at \$80.00–\$81.26 — the single day the stock rose 14%. Co-CEO Ryan Fukushima and EVP Andrew Polovin also sold in the same window. No insider buying disclosed.

**Options and volatility.** A 3.7 beta and a 52-week range spanning \$40.77–\$104.32 imply expensive premium; the practical read is that implied volatility is elevated into the 3-Nov print, so covered calls against existing stock are a more attractive way to express caution than outright selling. Detailed options-flow data was not available for this session, so no directional skew claim is made.

**Composite sentiment score: 6.5/10.** Fundamental momentum and news flow are strongly positive; price extension, insider selling and a consensus target below spot pull the score back toward neutral.

## Readability Pass

Tempus AI does two things. It runs a big cancer-genomics lab that sequences patients' tumours and tells oncologists which treatments should work. And because it has done that for years, it owns one of the largest libraries of linked genetic and clinical outcome data anywhere — which it rents to drug companies at 70% gross margins. The lab creates the data; the data pays better than the lab.

The business is working. Revenue grew 22% last quarter to \$382.5M, the data side grew 28%, cancer test volumes grew 31%, and the company turned its first quarterly profit — though that profit only happened because of a \$98.5M paper gain on some shares it owns, not because operations made money.

The exciting part is pricing. Tempus just won FDA approval that lets it charge a higher regulated rate for the tests it already performs. Management thinks that repricing is worth \$330–400M a year once a second approval lands. Same tests, more revenue. It is also buying Personalis for \$1.5B to own the best technology for detecting cancer that has come back after treatment.

The problem is that the market figured this out in September. The stock rose 54% in a month to \$85. That is about ten times next year's revenue for a company that still loses money on a GAAP basis, and the average Wall Street price target is \$68.56 — below where it trades. Insiders, including the CEO, sold into the rally.

So: good company, stretched price. Wait for a pullback toward \$65–\$72, or into the \$60 area, before buying. If you already own it, the September gain is a reasonable place to trim.
