# MTZ — MasTec, Inc.

MasTec is one of North America's largest specialty infrastructure contractors, and it is having an operationally excellent year that the stock has completely refused to reward. Q2 2026 set company records on revenue, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS and backlog; guidance went up, not down; and the company closed the largest acquisition in its history to push deeper into electrical work for AI data centers. Yet at \$204.09 (2026-09-28) the shares sit ~54% below the 52-week high of \$441.43, well under the 50-day (\$263) and 200-day (\$306) moving averages, with RSI at 32. The de-rating traces to two things: a \$400M cut to the 2026 Communications outlook disclosed on 2026-07-31, and a sector-wide AI-capex confidence shock in mid-September. Neither dented the backlog. That gap between operating results and price is the trade.

## Company Overview

**What it does.** MasTec designs, builds and maintains large-scale infrastructure across four reporting segments. It is a labour- and equipment-intensive project business: MasTec bids work, self-performs a high share of it with ~36,000 employees, and recognises revenue as the work progresses. Profitability is a function of project selection, crew productivity and how well fixed-price risk is managed.

**Segments (Q2 2026 revenue and adjusted EBITDA margin):**

| Segment | Q2 2026 revenue | YoY | Adj. EBITDA margin | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean Energy and Infrastructure | \$1.62B | +43% | 7.9% | Solar and wind (~60–65% solar), heavy civil, water, industrial, general buildings, turnkey data centers |
| Power Delivery | \$1.25B | +19% | 9.1% | Transmission lines, substations, distribution, grid hardening — now including Superior Group |
| Communications | \$0.89B | +6% | 8.2% | Wireless site work, wireline and fiber, install-to-the-home, data center interconnect |
| Pipeline Infrastructure | \$0.64B | +19% | 18.4% | Natural gas pipeline construction and integrity work |

**The Superior Group acquisition.** On 2026-07-07 MasTec agreed to buy Electrical Specialists, Inc., trading as The Superior Group, for approximately \$1.65B — \$1.175B cash, \$475M in MasTec shares, plus a performance earn-out. The deal closed 2026-07-20. Superior is a full-service electrical contractor specialising in inside-the-fence work at mission-critical facilities and hyperscale data centers: ~3,000 skilled team members, ~\$1.4B of 18-month backlog, and enough margin to lift Power Delivery into the low double digits. Management sized the contribution at ~\$800M of revenue and ~\$100M of EBITDA in the second half of 2026 alone. This is the single most important strategic change at MasTec in years — it converts the company from a supplier of grid capacity *around* data centers into a builder of the electrical systems *inside* them.

**Moat.** Scale, self-perform capability and relationships. MasTec is a top-three player in most of its markets, and many customer engagements run through long-term master service agreements that create repeat revenue. The differentiated asset is the ability to put a single construction-management wrapper around civil, power, telecom and maintenance scopes — which is exactly what hyperscalers buying a turnkey campus want.

**Management and alignment.** CEO Jose Ramon Mas, son of founder Jorge Mas, has run the company since 2007. CFO Paul DiMarco. Insiders hold ~20.7% of shares, which is unusually high for a company this size and keeps the family's interests tied to long-term value rather than quarterly optics.

FY2025 figures above are implied by the year-over-year growth rates management quoted alongside FY2026 guidance rather than taken from a filing directly, so treat them as approximations.

**What this means.** The operating business is compounding fast and margins are widening, not narrowing. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded ~100bps year over year in Q2. The one blemish was flow-through: adjusted EPS of \$2.22 landed 0.6% below consensus because Communications execution problems plus higher indirect fuel and equipment costs ate profit that revenue should have delivered. That miss — on an otherwise record quarter with raised guidance — is what triggered an 18.5% one-day drop.

### Backlog

- 18-month backlog: **\$21.4B** at Q2 2026, up \$4.9B (+30%) year over year and up \$1.1B sequentially
- Consolidated book-to-bill: **1.2x**, led by Pipeline (1.7x) and Clean Energy (1.3x)
- Segment records: Power Delivery ~\$6.3B, Clean Energy ~\$7.8B, Pipeline ~\$1.8B (highest since 2020)
- Superior adds ~\$1.4B on a comparable 18-month basis, not yet in the reported figure

The critical detail management stressed: of the ~\$2.5B of backlog added in the first half of 2026, **very little converts to 2026 revenue** — the majority benefits 2027. The market priced the 2026 Communications shortfall and ignored the 2027 setup.

### Balance sheet

- Cash and equivalents: **\$316M**
- Total debt: **\$3.24B**; net debt ~**\$2.92B**
- Book equity: \$3.60B; book value per share \$44.54
- Net leverage: **1.8x** reported at Q2, **2.2x** pro forma for Superior; management targets below 2.0x by year-end
- Current ratio 1.40; interest coverage 4.69x; Altman Z-score 3.92

**What this means.** Leverage went up to buy Superior and is now the main thing that can go wrong on the financial side. At 2.2x pro forma it is not dangerous for a company generating \$1.6B of EBITDA, but it removes the cushion. The year-end sub-2.0x target depends entirely on Q4 cash generation landing as promised.

### Cash flow

- TTM operating cash flow: **\$582M**; capex \$337M; **free cash flow \$245M**
- Q2 2026 operating cash flow: essentially flat — working capital investment absorbed all of the earnings growth
- FY2026 guide: **>\$1B** operating cash flow, with the majority expected in Q4

**What this means.** This is the weakest part of the story and deserves scepticism. Free cash flow of \$245M against \$16.1B of revenue is a 1.5% margin. Growth in a contracting business consumes receivables and unbilled work faster than it produces cash, and MasTec has now guided a very large cash number into a single quarter. If Q4 disappoints, both the leverage target and the quality-of-earnings narrative take damage at the same time.

### Scenario analysis

Assumptions: FY2026 revenue \$18.2B and adjusted EBITDA \$1.6B per guidance; FY2027 consensus EPS ~\$12.4 (MarketBeat/S&P Global polled estimates, not company-guided); ~79M diluted shares plus modest dilution from the Superior stock consideration.

| Scenario | FY2027 EPS | Multiple | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | \$12.40+ | 30–35x forward | \$380–\$430 |
| **Base** | \$11.50–\$12.40 | 22–24x forward | \$260–\$300 |
| **Bear** | \$8.50–\$10.00 | 16–18x forward (7–8x EV/EBITDA) | \$150–\$180 |

**Bull case (\$380–\$430).** Mission-critical awards convert, Superior outperforms and lifts Power Delivery margins, Communications inflects in 2027 as spectrum equipment ships, and the multiple recovers toward where the market valued this business six months ago. This range brackets the \$414 average analyst target.

**Base case (\$260–\$300).** Guidance is met, Q4 cash lands, leverage drops below 2.0x, FY2027 grows mid-to-high teens on backlog plus Superior, and the stock re-rates only partially because AI-capex uncertainty keeps a discount on the name. From \$204 that is +27% to +47%.

**Bear case (\$150–\$180).** Data-center awards slip to 2028, Communications stays broken into 2027, the Q4 cash target is missed, and the market treats MasTec as a late-cycle E&C name at 7–8x EV/EBITDA.

**Calibration.** Sell-side consensus is materially above all of this: 21 analysts, "Strong Buy", average target \$413.80 with a range of \$321 to \$581. Those targets have not been fully marked down for September's sentiment shift, which is why the base case here sits well below them.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **Mission-critical / data-center scale-up.** Management was explicit that exposure to mission critical "at scale is still in its early stages" and smaller as a share of revenue than peers. Superior plus the turnkey data-center award booked in Q4 2025 changes that. Several large pursuits are live with awards expected before year-end 2026.
2. **Superior Group cross-selling.** ~3,000 electricians, ~\$1.4B backlog, low-double-digit Power Delivery margins, and — per management — customer conversations that have gone "unbelievably well" since announcement. The 2028 organic targets set at the May 2026 Investor Day exclude Superior entirely.
3. **Grid modernisation and load growth.** Power Delivery backlog hit a record ~\$6.3B on transmission, hardening and reliability spend driven by aging infrastructure and data-center load. Utilities are being pushed toward larger, more integrated delivery models, which favours scale contractors.
4. **Natural gas pipeline cycle.** Pipeline margins reached 18.4% in Q2 with book-to-bill of 1.7x and backlog at post-2020 highs. Management says reported backlog understates visibility and that customers committing to future gas deliveries for power generation will drive multi-year investment through 2028–2029.
5. **Communications recovery in 2027.** The 2026 shortfall is timing, not demand destruction: carriers bought spectrum but the build equipment ships next year, so they are batching the work. MasTec is separately pursuing "billions of dollars" of hyperscaler connectivity opportunities that land in 2027.
6. **Cash flow and deleveraging.** Delivering >\$1B of operating cash flow would validate the model and take leverage back below 2.0x, removing the main balance-sheet objection.

## Risk Factors

### Business risks
- **AI capex sentiment and actual project cancellations.** Mid-September 2026 saw AI infrastructure stocks sell off hard on slowdown warnings from industry leaders, alongside reporting on cancelled and paused data-center projects and state-level moratoria. MasTec told investors the state-ban concern is "a little bit overblown," but the award timing risk is real.
- **Communications segment.** FY2026 segment revenue was cut ~\$400M to ~\$3.25B, with margins tracking ~100bps lower year over year. Roughly half the cut is wireless (spectrum equipment timing), half wireline (deferred project starts as prior programmes roll off).
- **Fixed-price execution.** Q2's profit flow-through missed on project execution plus fuel and equipment costs. Unapproved change orders stood just above \$200M — management calls this ordinary-course timing, but it is a number worth tracking each quarter.
- **Integration risk.** Superior is the largest acquisition MasTec has ever done. Management describes it as a largely standalone entity needing limited integration, which reduces but does not eliminate the risk.

### Financial risks
- **Leverage.** \$3.24B gross debt and 2.2x pro forma net leverage after a cash-heavy acquisition, in an environment where the policy rate sits near 4%.
- **Cash conversion.** \$245M TTM free cash flow, flat Q2 operating cash flow, and a >\$1B annual target concentrated in Q4. Working capital is the swing factor.
- **No dividend, thin buyback.** Shareholder yield is 0.68%; there is no income support under the stock during a de-rating.

### Macro and sector risks
- **Hyperscaler capex discipline.** MasTec's growth case leans on continued heavy AI-related build. Independent estimates have questioned whether AI revenue can justify current spending levels, and any capex reset flows straight to backlog.
- **Rates and utility budgets.** Higher rates raise debt service and can slow utility and developer capital plans.
- **Tariffs and input costs.** Steel, aluminium and equipment costs hit fixed-price work directly.
- **High beta.** At a 5-year beta of 1.82, MTZ amplifies both sides of infrastructure sentiment.

## Recommendation

- **Rating:** BUY
- **Position sizing:** 2–3% of a diversified portfolio; this is a high-beta cyclical, not a core holding

A sensible approach is a first tranche near current levels (\$195–\$215), a second on either a confirmed reclaim of the 50-day or a flush toward the 52-week low of \$182, and a final tranche after Q3 earnings resolve the Communications margin question.

What keeps this from being a higher-conviction call is cash: \$245M of trailing free cash flow and a Q4-loaded \$1B guide mean the balance sheet story is unproven until February.

## Readability Pass

**In plain English:** MasTec builds the physical stuff that electricity, gas and data move through — power lines, substations, solar farms, pipelines, fiber, and now the electrical guts of AI data centers. It is having its best year ever. Revenue is up 23%, profits up 40%, and the pile of signed-but-unfinished work hit a record \$21.4B. Management raised its forecast for the year. The stock still fell by more than half.

Two reasons. First, its smallest-growing division (Communications, which does cell towers and fiber) will bring in about \$400M less than expected this year because phone carriers bought new radio spectrum but the equipment to install it does not ship until 2027, so they are waiting to do the work all at once. Second, and bigger: in mid-September the whole market got nervous about whether AI data-center spending is sustainable, and everything connected to that theme got sold.

**The terms that matter:**

**Backlog** — signed contracts not yet completed. MasTec's \$21.4B is roughly 14 months of work already booked. Crucially, most of what was added recently is for 2027, not this year, which is why good backlog news did not rescue 2026 numbers.

**Adjusted EBITDA** — cash profit before interest, tax and accounting charges for wear-and-tear. MasTec's 8.8% margin means ~8.8 cents of cash profit per dollar of revenue. Thin by tech standards, normal for construction.

**Book-to-bill** — new work won divided by work billed. Above 1.0x means the backlog is growing. MasTec's 1.2x means it is winning faster than it is burning.

**Net leverage** — net debt divided by annual EBITDA. At 2.2x pro forma, MasTec owes a bit over two years of cash profit. Manageable, but it is why the Q4 cash flow number matters so much.

**Forward P/E of 18.8x** — you are paying about 19 times next year's expected earnings. For a company whose earnings are forecast to grow ~36% a year over three years, that is inexpensive. The catch is free cash flow: only \$245M actually arrived as spendable cash over the last year, so the earnings are real but slow to turn into money.

**Bottom line:** a good business at a cheap price with a broken chart and one legitimately unproven metric (cash conversion). Buy in pieces, not all at once.
