## 1. Executive Summary

Alphatec Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: ATEC) is a medical technology company focused on the surgical treatment of spinal disorders, selling implants, instruments, imaging, neuromonitoring, and navigation/robotics systems through independent sales agents and direct representatives. The stock trades at \$9.55, near the lower end of its 52-week range and roughly 60% below its 2026 highs, after a Q1 2026 revenue miss and a full-year guidance cut driven primarily by underperformance in the EOS imaging segment.

We rate ATEC **SPEC. BUY**. The core surgical implant business remains healthy: Q1 2026 surgical revenue grew at a double-digit pace, surgeon adoption and procedural volume expanded more than 20%, and adjusted EBITDA guidance was reaffirmed despite the top-line cut. The bull case rests on a turnaround in EOS, continued adoption of the Valence intraoperative navigation/robotics platform, and operating leverage that drives free cash flow positive in 2026. The bear case is heavy debt (~\$466M net debt), convertible note overhang, ongoing dilution, and a competitive spine market dominated by Medtronic, Globus Medical, Stryker, and Zimmer Biomet.

**Price Targets:**

- **Bull:** \$18–\$22

## 2. Business Overview

Alphatec is headquartered in Carlsbad, California, and develops a comprehensive ecosystem for spine surgery centered on the Alpha InformatiX platform. The strategy is to capture more revenue per procedure by integrating preoperative planning, intraoperative imaging, neuromonitoring, navigation, and robotics.

### Product Platform

| Product / System | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| **Alpha InformatiX** | umbrella platform tying together imaging, monitoring, navigation, and robotic capabilities |
| **EOS imaging** | full-body, weight-bearing 2D/3D imaging system for surgical planning and alignment assessment |
| **SafeOp Neural InformatiX** | automated EMG/SSEP/MEP neural monitoring to reduce nerve injury risk during surgery |
| **Valence intraoperative system** | navigation and robotics integration into spine procedures |
| **Sigma Prone TransPsoas (PTP) Access** | minimally invasive lateral positioning and access systems |

### Revenue Model

- **Surgical revenue:** spine implants, instruments, and biologics sold through direct reps and independent distributors; the core growth engine.
- **EOS revenue:** capital-equipment sales and related service of full-body imaging systems; currently the weakest segment.
- **Geographic focus:** primarily United States, with limited international presence.

### Competitive Landscape

The spine market is concentrated and highly competitive. Medtronic holds the largest implant share (~28%), followed by Globus Medical, Stryker, and Zimmer Biomet. Alphatec competes as a mid-sized, innovation-focused player with a differentiated information-driven platform, but it lacks the scale, balance sheet, and hospital contracting leverage of the large multinationals.

## 4. Investment Thesis

### Why ATEC?

1. **Surgical business momentum:** Q1 2026 case volume and surgeon growth exceeded 20%, suggesting Alphatec continues to take share in a competitive implant market. Piper Sandler has cited ATEC as a share-gainer versus Globus, Stryker, and Zimmer.

2. **Integrated platform strategy:** Alpha InformatiX bundles implants, imaging (EOS), neuromonitoring (SafeOp), and navigation/robotics (Valence) into a higher-capture, stickier ecosystem. As hospitals seek to standardize vendors, an integrated platform can win larger contracts.

3. **Valence robotics / navigation launch:** The Valence intraoperative system is a key new product. Successful adoption would lift ASPs per case, differentiate Alphatec from pure implant competitors, and support margin expansion.

4. **EOS turnaround optionality:** EOS imaging is currently a drag, but a fix in sales execution, service reliability, or capital purchasing environment could turn a headwind into a modest tailwind without requiring new product invention.

5. **Operating leverage:** Adjusted EBITDA guidance was reaffirmed at ~\$134M despite a lower revenue outlook. The 35% drop-through rate on lost revenue implies significant cost absorption and scalability.

6. **Free cash flow inflection:** Targeting at least \$20M of free cash flow in 2026 would mark a transition from a cash-consuming growth story to a self-funding one, improving balance-sheet flexibility.

7. **Refinancing benefits:** The new \$300M facility reduces interest expense by more than \$6M per year and pushes maturities to 2031, reducing near-term liquidity risk.

### Valuation Commentary

- **Base Case (\$12–\$15):** Assumes surgical growth continues at a mid-teens pace, EOS stabilizes near guided levels, adjusted EBITDA reaches ~\$134M, and the refinancing improves sentiment. Implies a 2026 EV/EBITDA of ~18–22x and modest multiple expansion as free cash flow materializes.
- **Bull Case (\$18–\$22):** Requires EOS to recover above guidance, Valence adoption to accelerate, surgical revenue growth to reaccelerate toward 20%+, and the company to deliver at least \$20M of free cash flow. At the high end, the stock would approach analyst consensus targets.
- **Bear Case (\$7–\$9):** Reflects further EOS deterioration, competitive share loss, a miss on EBITDA or free cash flow guidance, or renewed balance-sheet stress. The current price already embeds significant pessimism, but downside remains if the surgical business also slows.

## 9. Readability Pass / Plain-English Summary

Alphatec makes spine-surgery products: implants, imaging machines, nerve monitors, and now a navigation/robotics system. Its implant business is growing quickly, but its EOS imaging division has disappointed, so the company cut its 2026 revenue forecast. Even so, it kept its profit and cash-flow targets, which suggests it can absorb the revenue miss and still make money. The stock is down a lot, analysts still see 100%+ upside, and the company just refinanced its debt to lower interest payments. The main risks are the heavy debt load, competition from bigger medical-device companies, and whether EOS and the new Valence robot can gain traction. This is a speculative buy: the upside is large if execution improves, but the balance sheet and competitive risks are real.
