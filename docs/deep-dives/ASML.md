# ASML — ASML Holding N.V.

ASML is the world's sole supplier of extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) lithography — the single most critical and un-substitutable machine for manufacturing the leading-edge logic and memory chips that power AI. It is a true monopoly sitting at the very base of the entire AI hardware stack: no EUV, no advanced chips. FY2025 was a record year (net sales €32.7B, +15.6%, net income €9.6B), and momentum has accelerated into 2026 — after a strong Q2 2026 (€9.3B sales, €2.9B net income), management raised its 2026 outlook to €43–45B on a step-up in customers' medium-term AI capacity plans, backed by a €38.8B order backlog. The NASDAQ ADR has pulled back roughly 21% from its ~\$2,000 high to ~\$1,583, leaving a ~32x forward multiple on a 30%-plus grower with an unassailable moat. For a franchise of this quality that is arguably the cleanest picks-and-shovels bet in AI, that is an attractive setup — a **BUY**, with cyclicality, China export controls, and a rich absolute valuation as the risks to respect.

## Company Overview

**ASML Holding N.V.** (NASDAQ / Euronext Amsterdam: ASML), founded in 1984 and headquartered in Veldhoven, the Netherlands, is the dominant supplier of photolithography systems to the semiconductor industry. Lithography is the step that prints the intricate circuit patterns onto silicon wafers; it is the most complex and expensive equipment in a modern fab, and ASML is the only company on earth that makes EUV machines. With more than 44,000 employees, ASML supplies every leading-edge chipmaker in the world.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

| Segment | Description | Role |
|---------|-------------|------|
| **EUV lithography** | Extreme-ultraviolet systems (Low-NA NXE and High-NA EXE) for the most advanced logic and DRAM nodes — ASML is the sole global supplier | Core growth engine and the AI beneficiary |
| **DUV lithography** | Deep-ultraviolet immersion and dry systems for mature and trailing-edge nodes | Large, more cyclical volume base |
| **Metrology and inspection** | Systems that measure and verify patterning accuracy (YieldStar, HMI e-beam) | Complements lithography, raises yield |
| **Installed Base Management** | Service, field options, and upgrades on the ~6,000-system installed base (~€8.2B in FY2025) | High-margin, recurring, and growing |

ASML sold 300 new lithography systems in FY2025. The strategic value is concentrated in EUV: each Low-NA EUV tool costs on the order of \$150M–\$200M, and next-generation High-NA (EXE-series) systems run around \$350M+. Every leading-edge transistor — the AI accelerators from NVIDIA and AMD, the custom silicon from the hyperscalers, and the HBM/DRAM stacked alongside them — is patterned on ASML machines.

### Competitive Moat

- **EUV monopoly:** ASML is the only company that produces EUV lithography systems, the product of decades of R&D and a uniquely deep supply chain (including the exclusive light source from Cymer and optics from ZEISS). There is no substitute and no credible competitor at the leading edge.
- **Un-substitutable in AI:** Advanced AI chips cannot be manufactured without EUV. This puts ASML at a chokepoint of the AI buildout — arguably a more durable position than any single chip designer.
- **Installed base flywheel:** A large and growing installed base generates high-margin service and upgrade revenue (Installed Base Management), smoothing the equipment cycle and compounding over time.
- **Technology roadmap:** High-NA EUV extends ASML's lead for the next decade, and its 2024 Investor Day 2030 model targets €44–60B revenue at 56–60% gross margin.

### Management

ASML is led by President and CEO **Christophe Fouquet** and CFO **Roger Dassen**. Management has a strong track record of executing on a difficult technology roadmap and returning cash: for 2025 ASML declared a dividend of €7.50 per share (up 17%) and, having completed its 2022–2025 buyback (€7.6B repurchased), launched a new share buyback program of up to €12B to be executed through December 31, 2028.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **AI-driven EUV demand (the core driver):** Customers have raised their medium-term capacity plans on more robust expectations for the sustainability of AI demand, translating into record order intake and the raised €43–45B FY2026 guidance. Every advanced AI chip and its HBM/DRAM requires more EUV layers.

2. **High-NA EUV (EXE-series) ramp:** The next-generation High-NA platform extends ASML's technology lead for sub-2nm logic and advanced DRAM. Q4 2025 already recognized revenue on two High-NA systems; volume adoption raises ASML's average selling price and served market.

3. **€43–45B FY2026 outlook and €38.8B backlog:** The raised 2026 guidance (up from an initial €34–39B) and a record backlog give unusually strong forward visibility for a capital-equipment company.

4. **Installed Base Management growth:** A ~6,000-system installed base drives high-margin, recurring service and upgrade revenue (~€8.2B in FY2025) that compounds and cushions the equipment cycle.

5. **2030 model and capital return:** ASML's 2024 Investor Day framed a 2030 opportunity of €44–60B revenue at 56–60% gross margin, supported by a growing dividend and a €12B buyback running through 2028.

## Risk Factors

### Business Risks

- **Cyclicality (the defining risk):** Semiconductor capital equipment is historically boom-bust. 2026 is a strong EUV year; a 2027 digestion phase could pressure orders and earnings after a peak.
- **Customer concentration:** A handful of leading-edge customers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron) drive EUV demand. Order timing is lumpy and any single customer's capex pause is material.
- **High-NA adoption:** The EXE-series is extremely expensive, and its adoption pace and economics at volume are not yet proven.

### Financial Risks

- **Valuation risk:** At ~50x trailing and ~32x forward earnings, near record highs, the stock embeds continued strong execution. Multiple compression is the dominant downside driver.
- **FX and lumpy cash flow:** ASML reports in euros while the ADR trades in dollars, so EUR/USD swings affect ADR returns; free cash flow is lumpy on prepayment and shipment timing.

### Macro and Sector Risks

- **China exposure and export controls:** US and Dutch restrictions limit advanced-tool sales to China, and elevated China DUV revenue is expected to normalize — both can cut sales and bookings.
- **Geopolitics and tariffs:** Trade tensions, tariffs, and the concentration of leading-edge manufacturing in Taiwan and Korea add tail risk.
- **AI-trade de-rating:** A broad AI-capex or semiconductor de-rating would hit a high-beta (~1.4) name still up more than 2x off its 52-week low.

## Recommendation

**Rating: <span class="rating-buy">BUY</span>**

ASML is, in my view, the single highest-quality franchise in the entire AI buildout. It holds a genuine monopoly on EUV lithography — the one tool that cannot be designed around and without which no leading-edge AI chip can be made. That is a more durable competitive position than almost any chip designer enjoys, and it sits at a chokepoint that captures value from the whole industry regardless of which chipmaker wins. FY2025 was a record, Q2 2026 beat guidance, management raised the 2026 outlook to €43–45B, and a €38.8B backlog gives rare forward visibility.

The reason this is a BUY rather than a HOLD is that quality plus a reasonable forward multiple now line up. After a ~21% pullback from the ~\$2,000 high to ~\$1,582.95, the ADR trades at roughly 32x forward earnings on a business growing 30%-plus with 50%-plus gross margins and a strong balance sheet — and the analyst consensus (~\$1,970–\$2,120) implies ~25–34% upside. That is not cheap in absolute terms, but it is a fair-to-attractive price for a monopoly of this caliber. The honest caveats are cyclicality (2026 is a strong EUV year and 2027 could digest), China export-control risk, and the fact that the stock is still near record highs.

## Readability Pass

**What ASML actually does:** ASML makes the machines that print the tiny circuit patterns onto computer chips. Its most advanced machines use "extreme-ultraviolet" (EUV) light, and ASML is the only company in the world that makes them. Every cutting-edge chip — the AI processors from NVIDIA and AMD, and the fast memory stacked next to them — has to be made using ASML's machines. In other words, there is no advanced AI hardware without ASML.

**Why the stock is attractive:** The AI boom means chipmakers are racing to build more leading-edge capacity, and that means buying more ASML machines. Sales hit a record €32.7B in 2025 and are guided to €43–45B in 2026, with a €38.8B order backlog that shows the demand is real and booked. The stock recently fell about 21% from its high, so you can buy this one-of-a-kind business at about 32 times next year's expected earnings — expensive-sounding, but reasonable for a monopoly growing this fast.

**What could go wrong:** Chip-equipment demand moves in cycles. 2026 looks strong, but 2027 could cool off if customers pause after a big build. Governments also restrict what ASML can sell to China, which can cut into sales. And because the stock is near record highs, any disappointment could hurt.

**Key terms explained:**

- **EUV / DUV lithography:** The machines that print chip circuits; EUV is the most advanced (ASML-only), DUV handles older chips.
- **High-NA EUV:** ASML's newest, most powerful (and most expensive, ~\$350M+) lithography system for future chips.
- **Backlog:** Orders already booked but not yet delivered — €38.8B here, signaling strong future revenue.
- **Installed Base Management:** Ongoing service and upgrades on machines already sold — steady, high-margin, recurring revenue.
- **Cyclicality:** Chip-equipment sales rise and fall in waves; a strong year can be followed by a digestion year.
