# SNPS — Synopsys, Inc.

Synopsys is one half of the electronic design automation duopoly that every advanced chip must pass through, and since closing the Ansys acquisition in July 2025 it has extended that position from silicon design into system-level simulation. Q3 FY2026 revenue grew 42% to \$2.477B, non-GAAP EPS of \$3.91 beat the high end of guidance, the troubled Design IP segment returned to year-over-year growth, and management raised full-year FY2026 guidance to ~\$9.715B of revenue and ~\$15.07 of non-GAAP EPS. Yet the stock is down ~13% over the past year at \$417.61 — the market is paying ~28x guided FY2026 non-GAAP earnings for a business the Street targets near \$575, because it is not sure whether AI expands Synopsys' toll booth or eventually replaces it.

## Company Overview

Synopsys sells the software, silicon IP and simulation tools that semiconductor and systems companies use to design, verify and manufacture chips. It reports two segments:

**Design Automation** — \$2,003.0M in Q3 FY2026, 80.9% of revenue, up from \$1,312.1M a year earlier. This houses the core EDA franchise (Fusion Compiler for synthesis and place-and-route, VCS for logic simulation, PrimeTime for timing signoff, IC Validator for physical verification, TestMAX for test), the hardware-assisted verification products (ZeBu emulation, HAPS prototyping), and now the entire Ansys multiphysics portfolio (HFSS electromagnetics, Icepak thermal, structural and fluids simulation). Adjusted segment operating margin was 45.2% in the quarter.

**Design IP** — \$473.8M in Q3 FY2026, 19.1% of revenue, up from \$427.6M. Pre-verified building blocks that customers license rather than design themselves: logic libraries, embedded memories, PCIe/DDR/USB/MIPI interface IP, and security IP. Adjusted segment operating margin was 26.5%, recovering from 20.1% a year earlier. This segment declined year over year for much of FY2026 before returning to growth in Q3, and management is reallocating resources within it toward higher-growth opportunities — it divested the Processor IP Solutions business during the year.

The moat is structural rather than contractual. Foundry process design kits are co-developed with Synopsys and Cadence, design teams are trained on specific toolchains, verification methodologies are embedded in customers' sign-off flows, and a tape-out error at 3nm or below costs tens of millions of dollars. Switching costs are measured in engineering-years. Revenue is roughly 40% time-based (recurring subscription), with upfront product revenue and maintenance making up the rest; deferred revenue stood at \$2.72B including the long-term portion.

Ansys changes the shape of the company. It contributed ~\$711M in Q3 and management expects ~\$2.98B in FY2026 — close to a third of total revenue. The strategic logic is that chip design and system design are converging: a data center GPU module is simultaneously a silicon problem, a thermal problem, a power-delivery problem and an electromagnetic problem. Synopsys is the only vendor that now owns tooling across all four. Management targets \$400M of cumulative annual synergies by the fourth year after close.

Sassine Ghazi is president and CEO; Shelagh Glaser is CFO. Two notable outside parties have taken positions: Nvidia bought \$2.0B of Synopsys common stock in December 2025 alongside an expanded multi-year partnership on AI-assisted design, and Elliott Management built a multi-billion-dollar stake disclosed in March 2026.

Non-GAAP EPS of \$3.91 beat the \$3.67 consensus and exceeded the high end of management's own guidance. Revenue of \$2.48B beat the ~\$2.44B consensus.

**Nine months FY2026:** revenue \$7,161.6M versus \$4,799.3M; gross margin \$5,215.0M or 72.8%; GAAP operating income \$681.0M versus \$793.5M; GAAP diluted EPS \$3.27 versus \$5.61; non-GAAP diluted EPS \$11.02 versus \$10.08. GAAP earnings fell despite revenue growing ~49% because the Ansys purchase accounting brought \$1,210.3M of intangible amortization in nine months (versus \$99.2M a year earlier) and \$236.3M of restructuring charges.

That gap deserves attention. FY2026 guidance implies GAAP EPS of \$3.84–\$4.08 against non-GAAP EPS of \$15.04–\$15.10. The bridge is ~\$1.61B of intangible amortization, ~\$950M of stock-based compensation, ~\$380M–\$410M of restructuring, and tax effects. Amortization is genuinely non-cash and will roll off; stock compensation is a real economic cost that the non-GAAP number ignores. A conservative view would haircut non-GAAP EPS by the ~\$4.90 per share of stock compensation, landing near \$10 of "cash-plus-dilution" earnings, or ~42x the current price.

**Cash flow and balance sheet (as of 2026-07-31):** nine-month operating cash flow was \$2,298.6M against \$156.1M of capex, so ~\$2.14B of free cash flow; full-year guidance is ~\$2.8B operating cash flow and ~\$2.6B free cash flow on ~\$225M of capex. Cash and short-term investments were \$3.608B. Total debt was \$10.04B (\$1.02B short-term, \$9.02B long-term), down from \$13.48B at October 31, 2025 — Synopsys repaid \$3.46B in nine months, helped by \$2.0B of stock issued to Nvidia and \$440M of divestiture proceeds. Net debt is therefore ~\$6.4B against ~\$2.6B of annual free cash flow, a manageable ~2.5x. Goodwill is \$26.8B and net acquired intangibles \$11.5B against \$31.2B of equity: nearly all of the book value is acquisition accounting, which is why an Ansys impairment is a real (if not imminent) tail risk.

## Valuation

At \$417.61 with ~191.6M shares outstanding, market capitalization is ~\$80.0B. Adding \$10.04B of debt and subtracting \$3.61B of cash gives an enterprise value of ~\$86.4B.

The ~\$17.7 FY2027 figure is an inference, not a company-guided number: HSBC's published \$20.01 FY2027 estimate was described as 13% above the Wall Street average, which implies a consensus near \$17.7. Treat it as an estimate.

Sell-side targets cluster well above spot. MarketBeat's tally of 21 analysts shows an average twelve-month target of \$574.61 with a \$700 high and a \$420 low, and a "Moderate Buy" consensus (12 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell as of late August 2026). A separate MarketBeat snapshot on 2026-09-25 put the consensus at \$561.00. The dispersion is the story: a \$420 low target from BNP Paribas — which nonetheless upgraded from Underperform to Neutral — sitting alongside a \$700 high tells you the debate is about the durability of the model, not this year's numbers.

**Scenario targets**

- **Bull: \$560–\$640.** EDA compounds at a double-digit rate as AI, multi-die packaging and physical-AI designs raise tool intensity per chip; Ansys cross-sell beats the \$400M synergy plan; Design IP growth sustains; agentic AI is priced as a Synopsys tailwind. That is ~32–36x the implied ~\$17.7 FY2027 Street number, or ~30x an above-Street \$20.
- **Base: \$470–\$520.** FY2026 lands near guided midpoints, FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches roughly \$17.7, and the multiple holds at 27–29x forward earnings. This sits deliberately below the ~\$575 consensus average.
- **Bear: \$320–\$370.** AI agents compress seat counts and pricing, Ansys synergies slip, or China and Intel headwinds re-escalate, and the stock trades at 18–21x forward earnings — the multiple a decelerating software franchise earns.

Base-case midpoint of ~\$495 is ~18% above spot; the bear case is ~15% below the 52-week low of \$362.55, so it requires genuine thesis damage rather than just multiple compression.

## Growth Catalysts

**AI raises the complexity tax.** Every generation of AI accelerator adds transistors, power-delivery difficulty, thermal difficulty and verification cycles. Design Automation revenue grew ~53% year over year in Q3 and management guided to double-digit EDA growth — this is the part of the thesis that is already visible in reported numbers rather than promised.

**Ansys cross-sell.** The combined portfolio lets Synopsys sell a chip customer thermal, electromagnetic and structural simulation it previously bought elsewhere, and lets it sell Ansys' aerospace, automotive and industrial base a silicon-adjacent workflow. Management targets \$400M of cumulative annual synergies by year four post-close (FY2029). Morgan Stanley's upgrade was explicitly built on integration progress.

**Agentic AI in the toolchain.** Synopsys is building autonomous design workflows with Nvidia, Microsoft and AMD. Early evaluations reportedly cut design cycle times ~25–40%. If Synopsys can price on outcomes rather than seats, AI becomes a revenue expander instead of a seat-count threat — this is the single largest swing factor in the multiple.

**Design IP inflection.** The segment returned to year-over-year growth in Q3 FY2026 with adjusted operating margin recovering to 26.5% from 20.1%. Continued growth removes the most-cited bear argument and would likely be the proximate trigger for a re-rating.

**Deleveraging and capital return.** \$3.46B of debt repaid in nine months, a \$2.0B repurchase authorization replenished in February 2026, \$300M of treasury stock purchased year to date, and ~\$2.6B of guided free cash flow. As gross debt falls, interest expense (\$429M in nine months) converts into earnings.

**Strategic validation.** Nvidia owning \$2.0B of stock and Elliott holding a multi-billion-dollar activist stake both raise the floor on strategic urgency and capital-allocation discipline.

## Risk Factors

**AI disruption of the licensing model.** This is the reason the stock de-rated in 2026 and it is not a trivial worry. Synopsys charges largely per seat per year for tools operated by expensive human engineers. If AI agents let a team of ten do the work of thirty, the customer's willingness to pay for thirty seats erodes. Synopsys' answer is to own the agents and reprice, but no one — including management — can say with confidence how that negotiation lands.

**Ansys integration.** \$26.8B of goodwill and \$11.5B of net acquired intangibles rest on the assumption that the combination works. Synergy slippage, sales-force disruption, or churn in the simulation installed base would hit both earnings and the balance sheet. Restructuring charges of \$380M–\$410M in FY2026 are the visible cost of forcing two organizations together.

**China and export controls.** FY2026 guidance explicitly assumes no further changes to export control or Entity List restrictions — which is another way of saying the guidance is exposed if policy moves. EDA has been a repeated target of US restrictions, and Chinese domestic EDA vendors are being funded specifically to displace Synopsys.

**Customer concentration, including Intel.** A small number of large semiconductor and hyperscaler customers drive a disproportionate share of revenue. Intel's foundry and product roadmap problems were one of the named headwinds in 2026; a further slip pressures a major account.

**GAAP economics.** Guided FY2026 GAAP EPS of \$3.84–\$4.08 versus non-GAAP EPS of \$15.04–\$15.10 is a large gap. Roughly \$950M of annual stock-based compensation is a genuine cost to shareholders that the non-GAAP figure excludes. Diluted shares rose from 161.7M to 192.3M year over year, mostly from the Ansys stock consideration and the Nvidia placement.

**Leverage.** \$10.04B of total debt with ~\$1.0B current and ~\$429M of nine-month interest expense. Comfortable at ~2.5x net debt to free cash flow, but it removes the balance-sheet optionality Synopsys had before 2025.

**Design IP volatility.** One quarter of year-over-year growth after a run of declines is an encouraging data point, not a trend. Nine-month Design IP revenue of \$1,335.0M was still slightly below the prior year's \$1,344.7M, and nine-month segment operating margin fell to 22.6% from 27.0%.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Position sizing: a core position, 2–4% of a growth-oriented portfolio. This is a large-cap, cash-generative franchise, not a speculative holding — but the AI-disruption debate is genuinely unresolved, which argues against making it an outsized position.

**Why BUY at \$417.61.** The company beat the high end of its own guidance, raised full-year revenue, margin, EPS and cash flow targets, and saw its weakest segment turn positive — and the stock is still ~13% lower than a year ago and ~23% below its \$539.48 52-week high. Three houses upgraded into that gap (Morgan Stanley on integration, HSBC to Buy on the AI growth outlook, BNP Paribas off Underperform). Consensus sits near \$575, roughly 35% above spot. You are buying a duopoly asset at ~28x guided earnings with a ~3.3% free cash flow yield while the market debates a risk that has so far shown up in the multiple and not in the results.

**Entry strategy.** Scale in rather than buying a full position at once. The shares trade above the 50-day moving average (\$401.37) but below the 200-day (\$445.05) — a stock that has stopped falling but has not confirmed a trend. The \$395–\$405 zone, where the 50-day sits just above the recent base, is the more attractive add. A close back above \$445 would confirm the intermediate-term reversal and is a reasonable trigger for completing the position. Support sits at \$362.55 (52-week low); resistance at \$445 then \$539.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$355 — beneath the 52-week low — invalidates the base and suggests the AI-disruption narrative is winning. Consider that a thesis review, not just a technical stop.

**Catalyst to watch.** The Q4 and FY2026 report, estimated for ~December 2, 2026, will carry the first FY2027 guidance. That guide is the real event: it is the first year where growth is organic rather than acquisition-driven, and where investors can judge whether double-digit EDA growth and Ansys synergies are structural.

**What would change the rating.** Upgrade conviction on a second consecutive quarter of Design IP growth plus FY2027 guidance above the implied ~\$17.7 Street non-GAAP EPS. Downgrade to HOLD on evidence of seat-count compression at major customers, Ansys synergy deferral, or renewed China restrictions.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Sell-side: constructive and improving.** Consensus is "Moderate Buy" — 12 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell across 18 analysts as of 2026-08-31, with an average target of ~\$561–\$575 depending on the snapshot and a \$420–\$700 range. The direction of travel matters more than the level: Morgan Stanley upgraded on Ansys integration, HSBC upgraded to Buy on the AI growth outlook with a Street-high \$20.01 FY2027 EPS estimate, and BNP Paribas lifted the stock off Underperform to Neutral in late September 2026 with a \$420 target, citing moderating AI-disruption concerns and improving Intel and China conditions. When the most bearish house capitulates to neutral, the marginal seller is usually gone.

**News tone: net positive, recovering from negative.** Coverage through mid-2026 was dominated by the stock's decline, Ansys margin dilution and the "is AI coming for EDA" question — headlines like "Synopsys Fell 11% This Year" framed the narrative. Since the Q3 beat on 2026-08-26 the tone has shifted to upgrades and the gap between price and analyst targets.

**Institutional and activist positioning: stable with two strong hands.** Hedge fund holders rose marginally to 85 in Q2 2026 from 84 in Q1. AQR increased its stake 973% to ~\$672M while Alkeon cut 20% to ~\$567M and Farallon cut 32% to ~\$370M — churn rather than exodus. The two positions that matter are Nvidia's \$2.0B stake and Elliott Management's multi-billion-dollar activist position, both established in the last twelve months. Elliott's presence typically means pressure on margin discipline and capital returns.

**Short interest: modest but rising.** ~5.71M shares short as of 2026-08-31, up from ~4.36M at 2026-07-31, or ~2.98% of shares outstanding. The increase signals real bearish conviction on the AI thesis; the absolute level is too low to set up a squeeze.

**Retail and social: muted.** SNPS is not a retail favorite. Discussion is dominated by professional and semi-professional investors debating the AI-disruption question rather than momentum chasing. No options-flow data was available in this session, so no read is offered there.

**Sentiment score: 6.5/10** — positive and improving, held back from higher by an unresolved structural debate and a stock still below its 200-day moving average.

## Readability Pass

In plain terms: almost no advanced computer chip gets designed without software from either Synopsys or its rival Cadence. That is a very good business — customers cannot easily switch, and every harder chip design means more software needed. Last year Synopsys bought Ansys, which makes simulation software for heat, electricity and physical structures, so it now covers both the chip and the system the chip lives in.

The business is performing well. Last quarter revenue grew 42%, profits beat expectations, and management raised its forecast for the year. The part of the business that had been shrinking started growing again.

But the stock is down about 13% over the past year. Investors are worried about one big question: if artificial intelligence makes chip engineers much more productive, companies will need fewer engineers, and Synopsys charges by the engineer. Its own tools could reduce demand for its own tools. Synopsys' answer is to build the AI itself and charge for results instead of seats — but nobody knows yet how that turns out.

The trade: you are buying a business with a near-unbreakable market position at about 28 times this year's adjusted earnings, roughly 35% below where analysts think it should trade, while the market worries about a risk that has not yet shown up in any actual number. If Synopsys keeps beating and its IP business keeps growing, the stock likely closes the gap toward \$470–\$520. If AI really does shrink engineering headcount, it could fall to \$320–\$370. The upside case needs AI to become a Synopsys product; the downside case needs AI to become a Synopsys substitute. Watch the December report — it comes with the first forecast for next year, which is the first clean read on the combined company.
