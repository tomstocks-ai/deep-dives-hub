# ASPI — ASP Isotopes Inc.

**Price:** \$2.60 (2026-09-28) | **Market Cap:** ~\$399M | **Exchange:** NasdaqCM | **Sector:** Materials | **Rating:** HOLD / SPEC.

ASP Isotopes is an advanced-materials group building isotope enrichment, helium/LNG, radiopharmacy and nuclear-fuel businesses, with operating assets concentrated in South Africa. The equity has lost roughly three-quarters of its value in twelve months — from \$10.35 a year ago and a 52-week high of \$14.49 to \$2.60 — as repeated commercialisation delays, a heavy loss run-rate and persistent share issuance eroded the premium the market once paid for optionality. The assets are real and the helium market backdrop is extraordinarily tight, but the pattern of slipped timelines is now the dominant fact in the story, so this refresh downgrades the rating from SPEC. BUY to HOLD / SPEC.

---

## Company Overview

ASP Isotopes Inc. (Dallas, Texas; incorporated 2021; IPO November 2022; 271 employees) produces and sells enriched isotopes and, since the Renergen acquisition, helium and LNG. Reported results are organised into two segments: **Nuclear Fuels** and **Specialist Isotopes and Related Services**.

Operationally the group runs as four distinct businesses:

- **PET Labs / medical isotopes** — four radiopharmacies globally, delivering tens of thousands of radiopharmaceutical doses in 1H 2026. Organic revenue growth exceeded 50% in 1H 2026; management forecasts FY 2026 revenue of roughly \$14M against \$6M in 2025. This is the only division with a genuine, growing commercial revenue base.
- **Renergen / Tetra4 (Virginia Gas Project, Free State, South Africa)** — onshore natural gas production, liquefaction and helium extraction. Renergen is 94.5% owner of Tetra4. Phase 1 nameplate capacity is planned at 2,500 GJ/day of LNG and 250 kg/day of liquid helium.
- **Electronic gases** — Silicon-28 and carbon-based materials for next-generation semiconductors and quantum computing, enriched using the proprietary Aerodynamic Separation Process (ASP) at Pretoria facilities.
- **Quantum Leap Energy (QLE)** — development-stage nuclear fuels: uranium conversion, HALEU/LEU+ enrichment, Lithium-6/7 separation, waste treatment. Partnerships or commercial initiatives with TerraPower, Fermi America and the South African Nuclear Energy Corporation (Necsa).
- **Alpa Theranostics** — wholly owned biotech developing nanobody-conjugated radionuclide therapeutics (Terbium-161, Actinium-225), first clinical candidate targeted to enter human trials within twelve months.

**Strategy — sum of the parts via listed subsidiaries.** Management's stated medium-term plan is to hold majority stakes in separately listed subsidiaries. Two listings are in progress: Noble Africa (the Renergen holding company) via a reverse merger with ENDRA Life Sciences Inc. (NASDAQ: NDRA), expected to trade as "NOBA" with ASPI retaining ~89%; and QLE as a standalone listing with a possible later tax-efficient distribution of QLE stock to ASPI holders.

**Moat.** Proprietary ASP and Quantum Enrichment (QE) technologies; an operating onshore helium resource in a geopolitically neutral jurisdiction, which is scarce; vertical integration from enrichment through radiopharmacy dose delivery. The counterweight is that the enrichment plants have repeatedly failed to reach commercial output, and management attributes this to legacy OEM equipment rather than the core technology.

**Management.** Executive Chairman and CEO Paul Mann has been unusually explicit about the delays, writing in the August 2026 shareholder letter that "the delays in our stable isotope division have been frustrating" and are "attributable primarily to performance issues with certain equipment provided by our former OEM suppliers." That candour is welcome; the credibility gap it acknowledges is the central governance issue for the stock.

### Cash flow

Free cash flow is materially negative. Operating losses plus capital spending on Renergen Phase 1 tie-ins, compressor rebuilds at the Silicon-28 plant and radiopharmacy expansion consume cash every quarter. Against a market capitalisation of ~\$399M and Q2 cash of ~\$255M, the enterprise value ascribed to all five businesses is modest — but the same arithmetic means the cash pile is the main thing holding the equity up, and at the current burn it funds roughly six to seven quarters before Phase 2 capital needs are even considered.

**Phase 2 funding is not secured.** Management expects approximately \$750M of senior debt from the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation and Standard Bank of South Africa, explicitly "subject to entering into binding definitive agreements." Phase 2 construction is guided at roughly 44 months.

## Growth Catalysts

- **First helium production at Renergen.** Tetra4 commenced commissioning of the Phase 1 liquid helium plant on 20 August 2026 after engineering optimisation, and the August shareholder letter committed to commencing production "prior to September 30, 2026." As of 29 September 2026 no production announcement has been issued — the single most important near-term item to watch.
- **Phase 1 drilling complete.** The drilling programme has reached the cumulative flow rate needed to run the process plant at nameplate once tie-ins are finished, with recent wells flowing more than ten times the rate of some earlier wells. Remaining work is engineering and pipeline construction rather than subsurface risk.
- **Helium offtake contracts.** A five-year take-or-pay with an Asian industrial gases company covers ~15% of Phase 1 capacity at an initial base price above \$600/Mcf plant-gate. Management targets 5–15 year contracts covering 50–75% of Phase 1 volumes and says further negotiations are advanced.
- **Helium market dislocation.** Roughly half of global helium supply is estimated to be offline; Qatari production is damaged, Russia has tightened exports, China has restricted re-exports, and the U.S. federal reserve is in legal receivership. Management reports spot prints moving from ~\$400/Mcf to over \$2,000/Mcf in some cases, on very small volumes. Salt-cavern storage has absorbed the shock so far and is expected to be largely drawn down.
- **Silicon-28 commercial shipment.** The plant now runs 48 stages in cascade and has enriched material to 98% versus 92% natural abundance; the target is a first 99%-enrichment batch during 2H 2026. Three commercial contracts with U.S. customers are already signed. Reaching 99.995% for quantum computing requires further compressor upgrades and additional stages.
- **Noble Africa listing.** Reverse merger with ENDRA Life Sciences plus a ~\$50M private placement (~\$20M from ASPI, ~\$30M from others including \$750k from ASPI directors and management), targeted to close in 2H 2026, creating what would be the only Nasdaq-listed pure-play helium company with ASPI at ~89%.
- **QLE listing and HALEU.** QLE holds South African civilian enrichment authorisation for cold commissioning of a uranium enrichment test bench — the first such authorisation in South Africa in decades — a Pre-Implementation Services Contract with Necsa, a Texas A and M Engineering Experiment Station research agreement on uranium hexafluoride conversion, and a University of Bristol collaboration on lithium enrichment. The DOE estimates U.S. HALEU need at 50 tonnes per year by 2035 rising to 500 tonnes per year by 2050.
- **Other near-term items.** Ytterbium-176 continuous processing vessel installation in 2H 2026; Carbon-14 feedstock arrival enabling commercial enrichment; first Carbon-12 samples in 2H 2026; updated Tetra4 geological resource estimate in Q4 2026; Alpa Theranostics first-in-human dosing within twelve months.

## Risk Factors

### Business risks

- **Chronic schedule slippage is the base rate, not the exception.** Silicon-28 samples shipped in August 2025 and commercial product is still guided to 2H 2026. The QLE spin-off targeted for H1 2026 has not happened. A Ytterbium-176 laser failure in 2H 2025 cost months. Any new timeline deserves a discount.
- **Equipment dependency.** The Silicon-28 bottleneck is compressors ordered by Klydon eight years before ASPI's acquisition that are not helium-tight; over 200 OEM compressors have had to be rebuilt in-house, with more to go.
- **Single-asset concentration.** The near-term revenue and EBITDA story rests overwhelmingly on one gas field in the Free State province of South Africa.
- **Execution bandwidth.** Five businesses, two separate listings, a reverse merger and a clinical programme are being run simultaneously by a small team.

### Financial risks

- **Burn versus cash.** Roughly \$35M consumed in Q2 2026 against ~\$255M of cash. TTM EBITDA of about -\$69.1M.
- **Dilution.** Share count has climbed to 153.31M. A ~\$210M raise earlier in 2026 was priced far above today's level, and a July 2026 registration covers a further 23.16M shares. Any raise at \$2.60 or below would be heavily dilutive.
- **Phase 2 is unfunded.** The ~\$750M DFC and Standard Bank package has no binding definitive agreement, and Phase 2 is a 44-month build.
- **QLE note overhang.** \$79.1M of third-party QLE convertible principal converts into QLE equity on a listing, diluting ASPI's stake in that subsidiary.

### Governance and legal risks

- On 28 September 2026, investor rights firm Halper Sadeh LLC announced it is investigating whether certain officers and directors of ASP Isotopes breached their fiduciary duties to shareholders. Such announcements are common and frequently amount to nothing, but the timing alongside a fresh 52-week low is an overhang that has to be disclosed.
- Insider selling has been visible, though the September COO sale of 8,438 shares was disclosed as tax withholding on vested equity.

### Macro and sector risks

- **South Africa exposure** — power reliability, permitting, currency and regulatory risk around both the gas project and the enrichment facilities.
- **Helium price normalisation.** The current dislocation is a geopolitical artefact. If Qatari capacity returns faster than expected, the \$600/Mcf contract assumption weakens.
- **Nuclear licensing.** HALEU work requires approvals in multiple jurisdictions; ASPI has not applied its enrichment technology to U-235 beyond the Necsa services contract.
- **Competition** — established enrichment and industrial-gas incumbents plus national laboratories have far greater scale.

## Recommendation

**Rating:** HOLD / SPEC. — downgraded from SPEC. BUY.

**Rationale.** The sum-of-the-parts arithmetic is genuinely attractive: ~\$399M of market value against ~\$255M of Q2 cash implies the market is paying very little for an operating gas field with a Phase 2 plan sized at 5–7% of world helium supply, a radiopharmacy business growing over 50% organically, three signed Silicon-28 contracts, and a nuclear-fuels subsidiary with a first-in-decades South African enrichment authorisation. Against that, the company has missed nearly every self-imposed deadline in the past eighteen months, the burn is roughly \$35M a quarter, Phase 2 depends on \$750M of debt that has not been documented, and as of 29 September the "helium production before September 30" commitment has not been confirmed. Price is below both the 50-day (~\$3.80) and 200-day (~\$5.31) averages at a 52-week low with a beta of 3.60. Buying a falling knife one day before a self-imposed deadline expires is not a risk/reward worth paying for; waiting for confirmation costs relatively little given how far the stock has already fallen.

**Position sizing.** Speculative only — 1% of portfolio maximum, sized to be written off entirely.

**Entry strategy.** Do not add at \$2.60. Wait for one of two confirmations: (a) a press release confirming liquid helium production at Tetra4, or (b) a confirmed first commercial Silicon-28 shipment. Either would justify re-rating to SPEC. BUY. Existing holders can hold a reduced position into those events. A second, cheaper entry would be a capitulation flush toward the \$1.75–\$2.00 area with cash intact and no equity raise announced.

**Stop loss.** \$2.00 — roughly 23% below the current price and below the round-number support that a dilutive raise would likely test.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: ASP Isotopes is trying to build five businesses at once. The one that matters most right now is a gas field in South Africa that produces helium — a gas the world is desperately short of because war damage, export restrictions and a legal dispute have knocked out roughly half of global supply. If that plant starts producing, ASPI earns real revenue at very high prices under long contracts. Management said production would start before the end of September 2026 and, as of 29 September, has not confirmed it.

The second business is a chain of pharmacies that make radioactive medicines for cancer scanning and treatment. It is small but genuinely growing, around \$14M of revenue this year versus \$6M last year.

The other three — enriched silicon for computer chips and quantum computers, nuclear reactor fuel, and an early-stage cancer drug programme — are promising but have slipped their deadlines repeatedly. The silicon plant shipped its first samples in August 2025 and still has not sold a commercial batch, because eight-year-old compressors bought with an earlier acquisition leak helium and have had to be rebuilt by hand.

The company has roughly \$255M of cash and burns about \$35M a quarter. Its whole stock market value is about \$399M, so investors are paying only about \$145M above the cash for everything else — cheap if the plants work, fair if they do not. The shares have fallen about 75% in a year because investors stopped believing the deadlines.

Jargon decoded:

- **Isotope enrichment** — sorting atoms of the same element by weight, because some weights are more useful for medicine, chips or reactor fuel.
- **HALEU** — high-assay low-enriched uranium, the fuel most next-generation small reactors need.
- **Take-or-pay contract** — the customer pays for the agreed volume whether or not they take delivery, which makes future revenue predictable.
- **Mcf** — thousand cubic feet, the unit helium is priced in. A price above \$600 per Mcf is historically very high.
- **EBITDA** — a rough measure of operating cash profit before interest, tax and depreciation.
- **Reverse merger** — going public by merging into an already-listed shell company instead of running an IPO.
- **Sum of the parts** — valuing each business separately and adding them up, used when a group's divisions are too different to value together.
