# ARM — Arm Holdings plc

Arm Holdings designs the CPU architecture and processor IP that powers nearly every smartphone on Earth and an accelerating share of data-center, automotive, and AI silicon. It monetizes through upfront license fees plus per-chip royalties — an asset-light, ~97% gross-margin model. FY2026 (ended March 2026) revenue grew ~23% to \$4.92B, and Q1 FYE27 revenue hit a record \$1.29B (+22%) as data-center royalties more than doubled. The long-term thesis rests on Armv9 royalty-rate expansion, Compute Subsystems (CSS) attach, and a pivot toward selling Arm's own chips, with management targeting roughly \$25B of revenue by fiscal 2031. At ~\$239.06 (2026-08-04) the stock trades at ~244x trailing and ~100x forward earnings — a valuation that prices in flawless execution. Rated **HOLD**: a premier franchise, but the risk/reward is balanced until there is a better entry or clearer proof of the silicon strategy.

## Company Overview

**Arm Holdings plc** (NASDAQ: ARM) is the world's most widely licensed CPU architecture company. Headquartered in Cambridge, England, and majority-owned by SoftBank Group, Arm designs the instruction-set architecture (ISA) and processor IP — Cortex CPUs, Mali GPUs, and increasingly full Compute Subsystems — that chip designers license to build their own silicon. Arm does not (historically) manufacture chips; it sells the blueprints and collects royalties on every chip shipped that uses its designs. More than 300 billion Arm-based chips have shipped to date, and Arm's architecture underpins ~99% of the world's smartphones.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Arm's revenue has two engines that reinforce each other: licensing seeds future royalties, and royalties compound for a decade or more as licensed chips ship in volume.

| Segment | % of FY2026 Revenue | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Royalty** | ~53% (\$2.61B) | Per-chip fees on every Arm-based chip shipped; driven by unit volumes, Armv9 mix, and CSS adoption |
| **License and Other** | ~47% (\$2.31B) | Upfront and subscription access to Arm IP (Cortex, Mali, CSS); leading indicator of future royalties |

Royalty revenue is annuity-like and rises as newer, higher-value architectures (Armv9) and CSS designs displace older ones — each carrying a higher royalty rate. Licensing is lumpier but signals the future royalty pipeline. In Q1 FYE27, royalty revenue rose 22% to \$715M (with data-center royalties more than doubling) and licensing rose 23% to \$574M.

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

**Competitive Advantages:**

- **Ubiquity and ecosystem lock-in:** Arm's ISA is the default for mobile and embedded, with a vast software, tools, and developer ecosystem that is extraordinarily costly to replicate or migrate away from.
- **Power efficiency leadership:** Arm's performance-per-watt advantage is the reason it is expanding from phones into laptops, cars, and — critically — AI data centers, where energy is the binding constraint.
- **Rising monetization per chip:** Armv9 and CSS let Arm capture more value per device, lifting royalty rates even when unit growth is modest.

**Competitive Moat Assessment:** STRONG (architecture standard with deep ecosystem lock-in), though facing a long-dated challenge from the open-source RISC-V ISA.

### Management Team Track Record

- **Rene Haas:** CEO (since February 2022), a long-time Arm and semiconductor-industry executive driving the push into data center, AI, and first-party silicon.
- **Jason Child:** CFO, focused on the licensing-to-royalty monetization flywheel and margin discipline.
- **SoftBank Group:** Controlling shareholder following Arm's September 2023 IPO; retains roughly 90% of shares, which concentrates control and limits public float.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

FY2026 (fiscal year ended March 31, 2026) was a record year, and momentum carried into Q1 FYE27.

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Revenue** | \$4.01B | \$4.92B | **+23%** |
| **Gross Margin** | 97.0% | 97.5% | +56 bps |
| **Operating Income (GAAP)** | \$831M | \$900M | +8% |
| **Net Income (GAAP)** | \$792M | \$904M | +14% |
| **EPS (Diluted, GAAP)** | \$0.75 | \$0.85 | +13% |

**Q1 FYE27 (quarter ended June 30, 2026):** revenue of \$1.29B, up 22% YoY and a first-quarter record, with GAAP net income of \$270M (\$0.25/sh) and non-GAAP EPS of \$0.45 — ahead of the guide. Management guided Q2 FYE27 to \$1.33B–\$1.43B revenue and \$0.43–\$0.51 adjusted EPS.

**Quality of earnings:** The gap between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings is large and driven mainly by heavy stock-based compensation (~\$1.05B in FY2026). This is the single biggest caveat on Arm's reported profitability — dilution is real and must be weighed against the headline non-GAAP figures.

### Balance Sheet

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Cash and Investments** | ~\$3.6B | Rose from ~\$2.8B a year earlier |
| **Total Debt** | ~\$432M | Modest; net cash ~\$3.2B |
| **Net Cash** | ~\$3.2B | ~\$3.0 per share |

The balance sheet is solid: a comfortable net-cash position funds R&D, the CSS roadmap, and the early stages of a first-party silicon push without reliance on debt.

### Cash Flow

Arm's asset-light model converts profits to cash well once SBC is normalized. FY2026 operating cash flow was ~\$1.52B and free cash flow was ~\$979M (a ~20% FCF margin), up sharply from a depressed FY2025. Capex has risen (to ~\$545M in FY2026) as Arm invests in engineering infrastructure for CSS and its chip ambitions, but the model remains far less capital-intensive than a fab-owning peer.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **Data-center royalty inflection.** Arm-based server CPUs — Nvidia Grace, AWS Graviton, Microsoft Cobalt, Google Axion — are scaling, and data-center royalties more than doubled YoY in Q1 FYE27. Management targets \$1B of server-CPU revenue across FY2027–FY2028.

2. **Armv9 and Compute Subsystems (CSS).** Each newer architecture and pre-integrated subsystem carries a higher royalty rate, lifting revenue per chip even without unit growth. Rising Armv9/CSS mix is the core royalty-expansion lever.

3. **First-party silicon strategy.** Arm's move toward designing and selling its own chips could dramatically expand its addressable revenue per design, and is the single biggest driver behind the ~\$25B fiscal-2031 revenue ambition.

4. **AI at the edge and in the data center.** AI inference is spreading to phones, PCs, and embedded devices — all Arm strongholds — broadening the licensing pipeline.

5. **Automotive and IoT expansion.** Software-defined vehicles and edge compute add long-duration, higher-value licensing and royalty streams beyond mobile.

## Risk Factors

### Business Risks

- **RISC-V (critical, long-dated):** The open-source RISC-V ISA is a structural threat to Arm's licensing moat, especially in cost-sensitive and custom silicon where licensees may prefer a royalty-free architecture.
- **Channel conflict from the chip pivot:** Selling Arm-branded chips risks competing with the very licensees that pay Arm royalties, potentially straining key relationships.
- **Customer and end-market concentration:** Smartphone royalties remain a large share of the base; mobile-unit cyclicality directly affects results.

### Financial Risks

- **Extreme valuation:** At ~244x trailing / ~100x forward earnings, any deceleration or margin surprise can trigger a sharp multiple reset.
- **Stock-based compensation and dilution:** SBC of ~\$1.05B in FY2026 flatters non-GAAP metrics and dilutes shareholders.
- **Governance and float:** SoftBank's ~90% stake concentrates control, limits liquidity, and amplifies volatility around any secondary offerings.

### Macro and Sector Risks

- **AI capex cycle:** Part of the growth premium depends on continued AI infrastructure spending; a pullback would hit sentiment and data-center royalties.
- **China exposure:** A meaningful portion of royalties ties to China; geopolitics, export controls, and the semi-independent Arm China entity add uncertainty.
- **Semiconductor cyclicality:** Licensing timing is lumpy and unit demand is cyclical, making quarter-to-quarter results volatile.

## Recommendation

**Rating: HOLD**

Arm is a premier franchise — a near-monopoly CPU architecture with ~97% gross margins, ~23% revenue growth, an annuity-like royalty base, and genuine optionality from data center, AI, and a first-party silicon pivot. The problem is price, not quality. At ~244x trailing and ~100x forward earnings, the market already capitalizes years of flawless execution, leaving thin margin for error and outsized downside on any stumble.

**Position sizing:** For investors who must own it, a small 1–2% position is prudent given the valuation and volatility. Treat additions as opportunistic rather than urgent.

**Entry strategy:** Accumulate on meaningful weakness — the \$180–210 zone (toward the upper bear / lower base range) offers a more defensible risk/reward. Avoid chasing post-earnings spikes.

**Stop loss:** A sustained close below ~\$150 (the bottom of the bear range) would signal the growth-and-pivot narrative is breaking and warrant reassessment.

**Key levels (via the TradingView widget above):** Watch the 50-day and 200-day EMAs for trend; the stock has been volatile around earnings. RSI spikes into prints have historically preceded sharp mean-reversion given the rich multiple.

**Catalyst calendar:**

- **Q2 FYE27 earnings (November 2026):** guide of \$1.33B–\$1.43B revenue and \$0.43–\$0.51 adjusted EPS; watch data-center royalty momentum.
- **CSS and Armv9 ramp (FY2027):** royalty-rate expansion and progress toward the \$1B server-CPU target.
- **Q3 FYE27 earnings (February 2027):** updated full-year outlook and licensing pipeline.
- **First-party silicon milestones (2026–2027):** any formal Arm-branded chip launch would reshape the model.

## Readability Pass

**What Arm actually does:** Almost every smartphone, and a growing number of data-center and car chips, are built on Arm's design "recipes" for the processor brain. Arm doesn't make the chips — it licenses the recipes and then collects a small fee (a royalty) for every chip built with them. Because those recipes sit inside billions of devices, the fees keep flowing for years.

**What the numbers say:** Arm sold \$4.92B of licenses and royalties in its last fiscal year, up about 23%, and keeps roughly 97 cents of gross profit on every revenue dollar — extraordinary economics. It has more cash than debt and generated about \$979M of spare cash.

**What the ~244x P/E means:** Investors are paying about \$244 today for every \$1 Arm earned over the past year. That is one of the richest valuations among large tech companies and only pays off if Arm keeps growing fast for many years — including successfully selling its own chips.

**The main risk in plain terms:** The price already assumes near-perfect execution. If growth slows, or if a free alternative (RISC-V) chips away at Arm's business, the stock could fall a long way. That balance of a great company at a demanding price is why the rating is HOLD.
