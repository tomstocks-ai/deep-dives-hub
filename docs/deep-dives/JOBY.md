# JOBY — Joby Aviation, Inc.

Joby Aviation is the best-capitalized pure-play in electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) air taxis. At \$7.27 (2026-07-29) the stock trades near its 52-week low of \$6.89, down roughly 60% from its one-year high of \$20.95, even as the company stacks up commercialization milestones: a ~\$2.5B cash and short-term investment war chest, a deepening Toyota manufacturing joint venture, a finalized Virgin Atlantic partnership to bring air-taxi service to the UK, the Blade passenger-mobility acquisition, historic point-to-point demonstration flights in New York City, and a planned commercial launch in Dubai. The disconnect between the news flow and the tape reflects the market's core anxiety — Joby is still pre-commercial, burns roughly \$500M a year, and its revenue is gated on the binary timing of FAA type certification. This is a high-conviction, high-uncertainty speculative name where the balance sheet buys time that most eVTOL competitors do not have.

## Company Overview

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Joby Aviation, Inc. is an air-mobility company founded in 2009 and headquartered in Santa Cruz, California. It designs, tests, manufactures, and intends to operate a piloted, all-electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft — the S4 — built to carry a pilot plus four passengers at speeds up to ~200 mph with near-silent, zero-emission operation. The long-term model is a vertically integrated "build and operate" air-taxi service: Joby manufactures the aircraft and runs the passenger network itself, rather than only selling airframes.

Today Joby is effectively pre-commercial from an eVTOL-revenue standpoint. Reported revenue jumped to \$53.43M in FY2025 (from just \$136K the prior year), driven almost entirely by the acquisition of **Blade's passenger mobility business**, which brings existing helicopter and short-haul air-transport operations and a customer base that Joby intends to transition onto eVTOL aircraft as they enter service.

**Revenue and Activity Breakdown (approximate):**

| Segment | Role | Description |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **Blade passenger mobility** | Near-term revenue | Acquired helicopter and short-haul passenger operations providing current cash revenue |
| **Government and flight services** | Small revenue | Contracts with the U.S. Department of Defense (Agility Prime) and demonstration/engineering services |
| **eVTOL air taxi** | Future revenue | The core S4 commercial passenger service, pending FAA type certification |

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

- **Best-funded eVTOL pure-play:** ~\$2.5B in cash and short-term investments (as of Q1 2026) dwarfs most competitors' balance sheets and funds a multi-year path through certification and scale-up.
- **Certification lead:** Joby is among the furthest along in the FAA type-certification process, having progressed through flight testing of conforming aircraft — a hard-won regulatory moat in a field where certification is the gating event.
- **Toyota alliance:** Toyota is both a major investor (a \$500M strategic commitment) and manufacturing partner; the two launched a joint venture to industrialize S4 production, giving Joby automotive-grade manufacturing expertise.
- **Global partnerships:** a finalized Virgin Atlantic deal for the UK, agreements in Japan and Korea via Toyota and SK Telecom, and a Dubai launch partnership with the Road and Transport Authority create first-mover positions in multiple markets.
- **Vertical integration:** in-house electric powertrain, software, and aircraft design create IP depth, though it also raises capital intensity.

**Moat Assessment:** MODERATE — the balance sheet, certification progress, and Toyota relationship are real advantages, but no eVTOL company has yet proven commercial-scale demand or unit economics, so the moat is prospective rather than earned.

The large short-term investment portfolio also generates interest income that cushions the reported net loss.

## Risk Factors

**Regulatory and Execution Risks:**

- **Binary certification risk:** commercial revenue is gated on FAA type certification, whose timing is uncertain and outside Joby's full control; any delay pushes out the entire revenue thesis.
- **Manufacturing scale-up:** transitioning from hand-built prototypes to serial production under the Toyota JV carries meaningful execution and supply-chain risk.
- **Commercialization timeline:** eVTOL has a history of ambitious timelines slipping; demand, pricing, and unit economics remain unproven at commercial scale.

## Readability Pass

**Plain-English thesis:** Joby is building small, quiet, all-electric aircraft that take off and land like helicopters and are meant to work as air taxis — short hops over congested cities. Of all the companies chasing this idea, Joby has the most money in the bank (about \$2.5 billion), is furthest along with U.S. safety regulators, and has Toyota as both an investor and a manufacturing partner, plus deals with Virgin Atlantic and Dubai.

The catch is that Joby is not yet allowed to fly paying passengers — it needs the FAA to certify the aircraft first, and nobody knows exactly when that will happen. Meanwhile it loses about \$500 million a year and keeps issuing new shares to fund itself, which dilutes existing owners. That is why, despite all the good headlines, the stock has fallen roughly 60% from its high and sits near a one-year low.

So it is a speculative bet: real upside if certification and the first commercial launches land (targets of \$18–\$25 in the bull case), a middle path around \$10–\$13, and downside to \$4–\$6 if approvals slip and dilution accelerates.
