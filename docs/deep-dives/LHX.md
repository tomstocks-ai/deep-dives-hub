# LHX — L3Harris Technologies, Inc.

L3Harris is the fourth-largest U.S. defense prime, reorganised in early 2026 into three segments — Space and Mission Systems, Communications and Spectrum Dominance, and Missile Solutions — and it is currently the cheapest large prime on the board for reasons that have almost nothing to do with the operating business. Q2 2026 produced 8% revenue growth, a 1.2x book-to-bill, a record \$42B backlog and a second guidance raise of the year. The stock nonetheless closed at \$237.70 on 2026-09-28, a fresh 52-week low and roughly 37% below its \$379.23 high, because three governance shocks landed in eight weeks: the Missile Solutions IPO slipped to 2027, CEO Chris Kubasik was removed for a code-of-conduct violation, and several law firms opened securities-fraud investigations. At ~20x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance and a ~6.8% free-cash-flow yield, the discount compensates for a real but bounded overhang. **BUY**, scaled in.

## Company Overview

L3Harris Technologies (NYSE: LHX) is a Melbourne, Florida-based aerospace and defense technology company formed by the 2019 merger of L3 Technologies and Harris Corporation, and expanded by the 2023 acquisition of Aerojet Rocketdyne. Market capitalisation is ~\$44.4B on ~186M shares outstanding. The company brands itself the "Trusted Disruptor" — a positioning built on commercial-style development speed rather than the traditional cost-plus prime model.

In early 2026 management collapsed four reporting segments into three, each with a distinct business model and margin profile. Full-year 2026 segment guidance issued in January was:

| Segment | 2026 revenue guide | 2026 margin guide | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space and Mission Systems (SMS) | ~\$11.5B | mid-10% | Space sensing payloads, missile-warning and hypersonic tracking, ISR aircraft missionisation |
| Communications and Spectrum Dominance (CSD) | ~\$8.0B | ~25% | Tactical radios, resilient communications, electronic warfare, spectrum operations |
| Missile Solutions (MSL) | ~\$4.4B | mid-12% | Solid rocket motors and propulsion — legacy Aerojet Rocketdyne, supporting PAC-3, THAAD, Tomahawk and Standard Missile |

CSD is the margin engine at roughly 25% segment operating margin, SMS is the revenue bulk, and MSL is the growth and value-crystallisation story. MSL was created in January 2026 by pulling missile capabilities from across the company into a single unit, and in April 2026 the U.S. Department of War closed a \$1B investment into Aerojet Rocketdyne Holdings in the form of a convertible preferred security plus warrants — an anchor-investor structure intended to secure the domestic solid-rocket-motor supply chain. L3Harris retains an 80% stake and planned to IPO the unit, converting the preferred into common equity on listing.

The moat is the usual prime combination — classified program access, qualified production capacity, and switching costs measured in years — sharpened in two places. Tactical communications is close to a franchise position, which is why CSD earns a 25% margin. Solid rocket motors is a duopoly-adjacent market where the U.S. government has now taken a direct equity position rather than simply writing contracts, an unusually explicit statement about supply-chain criticality.

Management changed abruptly. On 2026-08-17 the board removed Chairman and CEO Chris Kubasik, effective immediately, after an investigation found conduct inconsistent with the company's Code of Conduct. Insider Sam Mehta was installed as CEO and guidance was reaffirmed on the same day. Kubasik forfeited his 2026 incentive bonus and unvested equity — reported at ~\$45M — while retaining roughly \$80M in previously vested stock and options. The episode carries a specific resonance: Kubasik had earlier been forced out as President and COO of Lockheed Martin following an ethics investigation.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 (fiscal year ended 2026-01-02) was described by management as an inflection point, and the numbers support the direction if not the magnitude:

| FY2025 metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue | \$21.9B, +2.5% YoY, +5% organic |
| Operating margin | 9.7% |
| Adjusted segment operating margin | 15.8% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | \$8.53 |
| Non-GAAP diluted EPS | \$10.73, +11% YoY (from \$9.70) |
| Net income | \$1.61B, +6.9% YoY |
| Cash from operations | \$3.1B, +21% |
| Adjusted free cash flow | \$2.8B, +21% |
| Year-end backlog | \$38.7B (from \$34.2B) |

The 2026 trajectory has been stronger than the plan. Q1 2026 revenue was \$5.7B, up 12% and 15% organically, with Missile Solutions revenue of \$1.0B up 18% at a 12.5% margin. Q2 2026, reported 2026-07-29, delivered:

- Orders of \$7.3B, a 1.2x book-to-bill, lifting backlog to a record \$42B
- Revenue of \$5.88B, up 8.4% YoY, ahead of the ~\$5.81B consensus
- Operating margin of 11.1%, up 60 bps; segment operating margin of 16.0%
- Diluted EPS of \$3.13 versus \$2.80 consensus — up 12.6% against the \$2.78 prior-year comparable, which the company presented as up 28%
- Operating cash flow of \$879M and free cash flow of \$771M, both up 37%

Guidance was raised twice during 2026. The January initiation was \$23.0B–\$23.5B revenue with EPS of \$11.30–\$11.50 and ~\$3.0B free cash flow. April took EPS to \$11.40–\$11.60. July took revenue to \$23.2B–\$23.7B and EPS to \$11.80–\$12.00, with the \$3B free-cash-flow target intact. Sell-side consensus for the year sits at ~\$11.88, effectively the guide midpoint.

The balance sheet is leveraged but improving. As of 2026-07-03: cash and equivalents of \$1.52B (from \$1.07B at FY2025 year-end), total debt of \$11.0B, net debt of ~\$9.5B — down from \$10.8B a year earlier and \$13.4B at the peak of the Aerojet deal. Shareholders' equity is \$20.9B, which now includes \$968M of minority interest reflecting the Department of War's preferred position in Aerojet Rocketdyne Holdings. Debt-to-equity is 0.46, current ratio 1.18. The caveat is composition: goodwill of \$20.0B and other intangibles of \$6.5B against total assets of \$42.9B means most of the asset base is acquisition-derived, and book value per share of \$106.80 is not a meaningful floor.

Returns are adequate rather than exciting — net margin 8.17%, return on equity 10.96%. The dividend is \$1.25 per quarter, \$5.00 annualised, a ~2.1% yield at a ~50% payout ratio.

At the 2026 investor day in February, management laid out a 2028 financial framework: \$27B revenue on an 8% organic CAGR, \$4.4B segment operating income on a 9% CAGR, \$5B operating cash flow and \$3.5B free cash flow. On the current share count that last figure is roughly \$18.80 per share of free cash flow.

## Valuation

At \$237.70 the stock trades at:

- ~20.0x the \$11.90 midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance
- ~24.1x trailing GAAP earnings
- ~14.8x the \$3.0B 2026 free-cash-flow guide, a ~6.8% free-cash-flow yield on market capitalisation
- ~18x the trailing \$2.83B of free cash flow on an enterprise value of ~\$54.0B
- ~12.7x the 2028 framework's \$3.5B of free cash flow
- 2.1% dividend yield, ~50% payout

That is a discount to where large primes have historically cleared, and the gap is concentrated in the governance line rather than the earnings line. For reference, the sell-side average price target is \$355.92 with a Moderate Buy consensus — two Strong Buy, eight Buy, eight Hold.

**Bull case: \$380–\$420.** The 2028 framework lands, producing ~\$18.80 per share of free cash flow, and a 20–22x multiple on that cash is applied. The Missile Solutions IPO completes in 2027 at a premium propulsion multiple, crystallising the sum-of-the-parts discount while L3Harris keeps 80%. CSD defends its ~25% segment margin as allied tactical-radio demand scales. Investigations close without material findings, and the multiple returns to the 24x-plus band peers command. A 0.50 beta and a growing dividend make the name a natural destination in a risk-off rotation.

**Base case: \$285–\$305.** 2026 lands inside the raised \$23.2B–\$23.7B revenue and \$11.80–\$12.00 EPS guide with ~\$3B of free cash flow, and EPS compounds at high single digits to roughly \$13 in 2027. The MSL IPO is delayed rather than abandoned. The securities-fraud investigations resolve as disclosure-only matters or immaterial settlements. The stock recovers to ~22x forward EPS, about 17x free cash flow — still below the 2028 framework's implied value and well short of the \$355.92 street target. This is +20% to +28% from here.

**Bear case: \$190–\$210.** The investigations escalate into an SEC or DOJ action, or disclosure emerges that the conduct behind Kubasik's removal touched reported results. The MSL IPO is shelved rather than deferred, stranding the sum-of-the-parts thesis and complicating the Department of War's convertible preferred and warrants. Budget disruption slows conversion of the \$42B backlog. Capacity expansion consumes more capex and R&D than planned and free cash flow lands below \$3B. Unfavourable estimate-at-completion adjustments push segment margins back toward the mid-15% range. The multiple compresses to ~16x on continued downgrades. That is −12% to −18% from here.

The asymmetry is the argument: roughly 24% of upside to the base case midpoint against roughly 16% of downside to the bear case midpoint, with a 60%-plus bull tail if the 2028 framework proves real.

## Growth Catalysts

**Record backlog converting.** The \$42B backlog is up from \$38.7B at FY2025 year-end and \$21.1B in FY2021. Book-to-bill of 1.2x in Q2 2026 means the pipeline is still growing faster than revenue. This is the most mechanical part of the story: the orders exist, and the question is conversion pace rather than demand.

**Missile Solutions IPO in 2027.** Delayed, not cancelled. Management's stated reason for the July deferral was that market conditions would not have reflected the unit's value — a deferral for price, not viability. A listed pure-play solid-rocket-motor company with a government anchor investor would plausibly trade well above the ~12.5x segment multiple embedded in L3Harris today, and the parent retains 80%.

**Government as anchor investor.** The Department of War's \$1B convertible preferred plus warrants in Aerojet Rocketdyne Holdings is a structural signal, not just funding. It de-risks the capacity build for PAC-3, THAAD, Tomahawk and Standard Missile programs and aligns the customer with the equity.

**Space sensing and hypersonic tracking.** SMS is guided to ~\$11.5B in 2026 on growth in ISR aircraft missionisation and space solutions. Missile-warning and hypersonic-tracking constellations are among the least discretionary lines in the U.S. budget.

**2028 financial framework.** \$27B revenue, \$4.4B segment operating income, \$3.5B free cash flow. If delivered, free cash flow per share roughly doubles from the FY2025 level.

**Portfolio pruning.** The Space Technology Group was transferred to AE Industrial Partners on 2026-08-03. Management has shown willingness to divest sub-scale assets and reinvest in the three core segments, which supports the margin path.

**International demand.** Q2 strength was attributed in part to international sales, and allied budget increases across NATO and the Indo-Pacific favour the tactical-communications and munitions portfolios specifically.

## Risk Factors

**Governance and legal — the dominant risk.** A CEO removed for conduct inconsistent with the Code of Conduct, and open investigations by Pomerantz, SBS Law and Scott+Scott into whether the company or its officers issued false or misleading statements, failed to disclose material information, or breached fiduciary duties. Two points of precision matter here: these are plaintiff-side law firm investigations, not confirmed SEC or DOJ enforcement actions, and no restatement has been announced. The risk is that the scope widens or that the conduct is shown to have touched reported results. Until that is settled, the multiple stays suppressed.

**IPO execution.** The MSL listing has already slipped once. Another deferral, or abandonment, removes a catalyst that a meaningful share of the shareholder base underwrote — and the July reaction, an 11% single-day decline on an otherwise strong print, shows how much it is worth.

**Customer concentration.** Revenue is dominated by the U.S. government. Continuing resolutions, a shutdown, or reprioritisation of space-sensing and munitions lines would stall backlog conversion irrespective of demand.

**Balance sheet composition.** Net debt of ~\$9.5B is manageable against \$3B of free cash flow, but \$26.5B of goodwill and intangibles on \$42.9B of assets means an impairment is a live tail risk if any acquired business underperforms.

**Contract accounting.** Fixed-price development work carries estimate-at-completion exposure, and MSL has already reported unfavourable EAC adjustments. Margin guidance of mid-12% at MSL leaves limited cushion.

**Capital intensity.** Management is explicitly investing billions in production capacity and R&D. That is the right long-term call and the reason the 2028 framework is credible, but it is also the most likely source of a free-cash-flow miss in any given year.

**Momentum and sentiment.** The stock is below both its 50-day (~\$269) and 200-day (~\$312) moving averages, a death-cross configuration, and printed a fresh 52-week low on 2026-09-28. Four sell-side downgrades or target cuts have landed since 2026-07-30. Falling-knife risk is genuine even if the valuation case is sound.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Targets — Bull \$380–\$420 · Base \$285–\$305 · Bear \$190–\$210.

The operating business is doing what a well-run prime should do: 8% top-line growth, a 1.2x book-to-bill, record backlog, expanding margins, two guidance raises, and free cash flow up 37% in the most recent quarter. Every one of the three reasons the stock is at a 52-week low is a governance or timing matter, and two of the three are resolvable within twelve months. Paying ~20x earnings and ~15x free cash flow for that combination is a reasonable trade.

**Position sizing.** Treat this as a core defense holding rather than a trade — 2% to 4% of a diversified portfolio. The 0.50 beta and 2.1% dividend make it a genuine portfolio stabiliser, but the open legal matters mean it should not be the only defense position. Pair it with a prime that has no governance overhang if defense is a large theme allocation.

**Entry strategy.** Scale in rather than taking a full position. The stock is making new lows and there is no technical support established beneath \$234.59. A sensible approach is one third near current levels, one third on either a retest of the \$230–\$235 area or a reclaim of the \$269 50-day moving average, and the final third after the 2026-10-29 Q3 print confirms the guide and gives management a chance to address the IPO timetable and the investigations. RSI has been in oversold territory through the September decline, which argues for patience on the first tranche rather than urgency.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$215 — roughly 9% under the 52-week low — would indicate the market is pricing something worse than a governance embarrassment, most plausibly enforcement exposure or a restatement. That is a thesis break, not a drawdown, and warrants exit rather than averaging down. Investors sizing for volatility rather than using hard stops should cap the position so that a move to the \$190 bear case is tolerable.

**Catalyst calendar.**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2026-10-29 | Q3 2026 earnings — first full quarter under CEO Sam Mehta; book-to-bill, segment margins, IPO timetable |
| Q4 2026 | Resolution or escalation of the securities-fraud investigations |
| Q4 2026 | FY2027 defense budget request and NDAA appropriations |
| January 2027 | Q4 and FY2026 results plus initial 2027 guidance — first checkpoint against the 2028 framework |
| 2027 | Missile Solutions IPO; Department of War preferred converts to common on listing |

**What would change the rating.** Upgrade conviction on a confirmed 2027 IPO date at a credible valuation, or a formal close of the investigations. Downgrade to HOLD on an SEC or DOJ enforcement action, a restatement, abandonment of the MSL listing, or a free-cash-flow guide cut below \$2.7B.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Sell-side: cautiously constructive, and deteriorating.** Consensus is Moderate Buy with an average target of \$355.92 — two Strong Buy, eight Buy, eight Hold, no Sell. But the direction of travel is negative. Since the Q2 print: BNP Paribas Exane cut to \$310 from \$330 at neutral (2026-07-30), Susquehanna cut to \$350 from \$410 while staying positive (2026-07-31), Jefferies downgraded to Hold with a \$315 target (2026-08-26), Argus downgraded to Hold (2026-09-25), and Wall Street Zen downgraded to Hold (2026-09-28), which triggered the 52-week low. The one bright spot is Guggenheim initiating at Buy with a \$365 target on 2026-09-14 — a fresh look at the name concluding it is cheap.

**Institutional: accumulating into the weakness.** Institutional ownership is 84.76%. Second-quarter filings showed BlackRock establishing a ~\$5.53B position, Bank of New York Mellon a ~\$753M position, and Deutsche Bank ~\$174M, with Man Group adding aggressively. Artisan Partners raised its holding 174% in the prior quarter. Large, slow money was buying as the multiple compressed, which is a meaningfully different signal from retail capitulation.

**News tone: operationally positive, narratively negative.** The pattern of 2026 has been strong prints buried under governance headlines. The July quarter beat on both lines and raised guidance, and the stock fell 11%. The August CEO removal came with reaffirmed guidance, and the stock fell 4.6%. Headlines through September split between valuation-based bullishness — DCF-driven pieces arguing the stock is 31% to 45% undervalued, and commentary that the "record backlog makes its stock sell-off look overdone" — and steady investor-alert press releases from plaintiff firms. That second stream is a well-understood pattern after any abrupt executive exit, but it dominates search results and suppresses sentiment.

**Retail and social: dip-buyer framing.** Discussion is organised around whether this is a value opportunity or a falling knife, with headlines such as "After 15% L3Harris Price Drop, Is It Time to Buy or Time to Fly?" capturing the tone. There is no evident short-squeeze or meme dynamic; the debate is fundamental.

**Options flow.** No unusual call or put activity in LHX was identified in the sources reviewed for this note. Given a 0.50 beta and a 2.1% dividend, the shareholder base is not option-driven, and this is a genuine data gap rather than a neutral reading.

**Composite sentiment: 4 out of 10 — negative, with the tape at maximum pessimism and positioning quietly improving.** Sentiment is the reason the opportunity exists. Fundamentals score far higher than sentiment does, and the gap between the two is the trade.

## Readability Pass

L3Harris makes three things: satellite sensors and spy-plane systems, military radios, and rocket motors for missiles. All three are in demand. The company is growing about 8% a year, has a record \$42B of orders on the books, has raised its profit forecast twice this year, and generates about \$3B of cash annually.

The share price has nonetheless fallen 20% over the past year and just hit a 52-week low. Three things went wrong, none of them about whether the company can sell its products:

1. It postponed the stock-market listing of its rocket-motor division from 2026 to 2027, saying the market would not pay a fair price. Investors who had bought for that event sold — the shares dropped 11% in a day, on the same day the company reported better-than-expected results.
2. The board fired the CEO in August for breaking the company's own code of conduct. His replacement, an insider, immediately confirmed the financial targets.
3. Several law firms then opened investigations into whether the company misled investors. These are the plaintiff-lawyer investigations that routinely follow an abrupt CEO exit, not government enforcement actions, and nothing has been proven.

The result is a good business at a cheap price with an unresolved question mark over it. You pay about 20 times earnings — roughly a fifth less than similar defense companies — and collect a 2.1% dividend while you wait. If the legal cloud clears and the rocket-motor listing happens, the shares plausibly reach \$285–\$305. If the investigations turn into something serious, \$190–\$210 is realistic. Our view is that the first outcome is more likely, which is why this is a BUY — but buy it in pieces rather than all at once, because a falling stock can keep falling while the news is still bad.
