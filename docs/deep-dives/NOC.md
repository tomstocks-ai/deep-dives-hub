# NOC — Northrop Grumman Corporation

Northrop Grumman (NYSE: NOC) is one of the five largest U.S. defense prime contractors and the sole builder of the nation's next-generation strategic bomber, the B-21 Raider, as well as the Sentinel intercontinental ballistic missile that will replace the Minuteman III. Trading near \$549 with a market capitalization of roughly \$78B, the company closed FY2025 with \$42.0B of revenue, \$29.08 in diluted EPS, and a record backlog above \$95B. With strategic-deterrence modernization, the emerging Golden Dome homeland missile-defense initiative, and sustained NATO rearmament driving multi-year demand, I rate NOC a BUY, with margin recovery and the B-21 production ramp as the central upside levers.

## Company Overview

Northrop Grumman is a pure-play defense and aerospace prime contractor headquartered in Falls Church, Virginia, and led by Chair and CEO Kathy Warden. The business is organized into four reporting segments:

- **Aeronautics Systems** — manned and autonomous aircraft, most notably the B-21 Raider stealth bomber, the E-2D Advanced Hawkeye, the Global Hawk and Triton uncrewed systems, and major structures for the F-35.
- **Defense Systems** — weapons, armament, and mission readiness, including the Sentinel ICBM guided-missile work, ammunition, and sustainment services.
- **Mission Systems** — radars, sensors, electronic warfare, networked C4ISR, and advanced processing that ride on nearly every U.S. platform.
- **Space Systems** — the fastest-growing segment historically, spanning national-security satellites, the Space Development Agency's proliferated constellations, launch propulsion, and civil space (including the Artemis lunar program).

The company's moat rests on scale, security clearances, and irreplaceable franchise programs. Sole-source strategic-deterrence positions in the B-21 and Sentinel, decades of classified-program institutional knowledge, and multi-year production contracts create switching costs and revenue visibility that competitors cannot easily replicate. In FY2025 the U.S. government accounted for roughly 84% of sales, with international customers contributing about \$6.0B.

## Financial Analysis

Northrop Grumman's FY2025 results reflected a steady, cash-generative business rather than a high-growth one, with execution and cash conversion improving markedly off the depressed 2023 base.

- **Revenue:** \$42.0B, up 2.2% year over year (roughly 3% organically), split across four balanced segments.
- **Profitability:** Operating margin recovered to 10.8%, up from 6.5% in FY2023 when a large B-21 charge weighed on results. Net income was \$4.2B, a 10.0% net margin.
- **Earnings:** Diluted EPS reached \$29.08, up 2.6% year over year, aided by a shrinking share count (about 142M shares, down 2% on \$1.6B of buybacks).
- **Cash flow:** Free cash flow climbed 26% to \$3.3B — the third consecutive year of at least 25% growth — on \$4.8B of operating cash flow against \$1.4B of capex. Operating cash flow of \$4.8B exceeded net income of \$4.2B, giving the year an earnings-quality ratio of about 1.14x.
- **Balance sheet:** Cash of \$4.4B sits against \$15.2B of long-term debt, a debt-to-equity ratio near 0.91, and interest coverage of roughly 6.8x. Return on equity was a strong 25.1%.
- **Capital returns:** The dividend rose 11.7% to \$8.99 per share in FY2025, extending a long streak of annual increases, and management continues to buy back stock.

Management guided FY2026 to \$43.5B–\$44B of sales and \$3.1B–\$3.5B of free cash flow, and Q2 2026 results beat expectations with adjusted EPS of \$7.68 on \$10.9B of revenue, prompting a raised full-year outlook — though the quarter also carried charges on two guided-missile programs and a solid rocket booster that pressed segment margin below 10.6%.

## Valuation

At roughly \$549, NOC trades at about 19–20x forward earnings, broadly in line with the large-cap defense-prime peer group (Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics, RTX) and below faster-growing aerospace names such as GE Aerospace and TransDigm. The record \$95B-plus backlog equates to more than two years of revenue and anchors the earnings base, while the primary swing factor is segment margin: each point of operating-margin recovery is worth several dollars of EPS.

- **Bull case — target \$660–\$700.** B-21 transitions to full-rate production with improving fixed-price margins, Golden Dome drives multi-billion-dollar Space and Mission Systems awards, Sentinel re-baselines to profitable execution, allied rearmament sustains record bookings, and free cash flow compounds toward \$4B-plus to fund accelerated buybacks and dividend growth.
- **Base case — target \$560–\$600.** Low-to-mid single-digit organic revenue growth in line with 2026 guidance of \$43.5B–\$44B, segment operating margin recovering back toward 11%, backlog held above \$90B, and the stock trading at roughly 19–20x forward EPS consistent with defense-prime multiples.
- **Bear case — target \$470–\$500.** Additional fixed-price charges compress margins, deficit pressure or a continuing resolution slows outlays, Sentinel cost growth triggers further restructuring, Space Systems growth decelerates, and the multiple compresses toward 16–17x forward EPS.

## Growth Catalysts

- **B-21 Raider:** The flagship next-generation stealth bomber is Northrop's single most important program. As it moves from low-rate to full-rate production, volume scale and learning-curve efficiencies should lift Aeronautics margins and revenue for a decade or more.
- **Golden Dome homeland missile defense:** The administration's push for a layered homeland missile-defense architecture plays directly to Northrop's space-sensor, interceptor, and command-and-control strengths, and could seed a multi-billion-dollar new franchise across Space and Mission Systems.
- **Sentinel ICBM:** Despite cost growth and program restructuring, Sentinel remains a sole-source, multi-decade land-based strategic-deterrent modernization that provides durable long-cycle revenue once re-baselined.
- **Space Systems demand:** Proliferated low-Earth-orbit constellations for the Space Development Agency, national-security payloads, and civil space (Artemis) continue to expand the addressable market.
- **Allied rearmament:** NATO members lifting defense spending toward and beyond 2% of GDP sustains international demand for radars, munitions, electronic warfare, and autonomous systems.
- **Capital returns:** Growing free cash flow funds a rising dividend and continued buybacks that compound per-share value.

## Risk Factors

1. **Fixed-price program risk.** Northrop's most strategic programs — B-21, Sentinel, and several guided-missile efforts — carry fixed-price development elements where cost growth flows straight to margins. Recent charges on missile programs and a solid rocket booster underscore that this remains the dominant swing risk.
2. **Budget and political risk.** Roughly 84% of revenue comes from the U.S. government. Continuing resolutions, deficit-reduction pressure, or shifting administration priorities can delay awards and slow outlays.
3. **Customer concentration.** Heavy reliance on a single customer (the U.S. Department of Defense) leaves Northrop exposed to program-specific decisions, protests, and reprioritizations.
4. **Sentinel execution.** Cost growth on Sentinel has already drawn Nunn-McCurdy scrutiny; further overruns could trigger additional restructuring, schedule slips, or scope changes.
5. **Supply chain and labor.** Inflation in materials and constraints on cleared, skilled labor can pressure both margins and delivery schedules across long-cycle programs.

## Recommendation

I rate Northrop Grumman a **BUY**. The combination of sole-source strategic-deterrence franchises (B-21, Sentinel), a record \$95B-plus backlog, 26% free-cash-flow growth, and emerging Golden Dome demand offers an attractive multi-year risk/reward at roughly 19–20x forward earnings. The base-case target of \$560–\$600 implies modest upside from the current \$549, with the bull case reaching \$660–\$700 if margins recover and B-21 scales.

**Position sizing and entry.** For a diversified portfolio, a full defense-prime position is reasonable, ideally scaled in on weakness. The stock has been volatile in 2026 around quarterly charges, dipping toward the low \$500s and even into the \$510s intraday, so patient buyers can accumulate near or below \$520–\$540. On the chart, the low \$500s have acted as support while the mid-\$560s to \$580s mark near-term resistance; watch the 50- and 200-day moving averages and RSI for confirmation of trend, using the TradingView chart above for live levels.

**Risk management.** A stop near \$470 (the bear-case floor) limits downside on a thesis break such as a major new program charge or a budget shock. The catalyst calendar — Q3 2026 earnings in October, the FY2027 budget request and NDAA around December, Golden Dome award decisions, the B-21 full-rate production decision in 2027, and Q4/FY2026 results in January 2027 — provides regular checkpoints to reassess.

## Sentiment Analysis

- **News and analysts:** Coverage skews constructive. Northrop's Q2 2026 beat and raised outlook were well received, though analysts flagged the recurring margin charges on guided-missile and rocket-motor programs as the key watch item. The Golden Dome initiative has generated positive strategic commentary for space- and sensor-heavy primes like NOC.
- **Retail and social (X/Reddit):** Retail sentiment is moderately bullish and thesis-driven, centered on B-21 as a decade-long growth engine and on strategic-deterrence and missile-defense spending as secular tailwinds; bears focus on lumpy fixed-price charges and rich-versus-history valuation.
- **Options flow:** Activity tends to build into earnings and major budget milestones, consistent with an event-driven, headline-sensitive defense name rather than a momentum trade.
- **Composite score:** Net moderately positive (roughly 6.5–7 out of 10) — a high-quality franchise with strong secular demand, tempered by margin-execution noise and a full valuation.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: Northrop Grumman builds the hardware the United States relies on for deterrence — the new B-21 stealth bomber, the Sentinel nuclear missile that replaces aging 1970s-era rockets, military satellites, and the radars and sensors on most U.S. platforms. It is a steady, cash-rich business rather than a fast grower: sales rose about 2% to \$42B in 2025, but free cash flow jumped 26% to \$3.3B, and the company keeps raising its dividend and buying back stock. The bull thesis is that the B-21 scales into full production, the new Golden Dome missile-defense program becomes a big new revenue source, and profit margins recover. The main risk is that a few fixed-price programs keep taking cost hits that dent profits, and that most of the money comes from one customer — the U.S. government. At about \$549 the stock looks fairly priced with room to run, which is why I call it a BUY with a base-case target of \$560–\$600.
