# CRSP — CRISPR Therapeutics AG

CRISPR Therapeutics AG (NASDAQ: CRSP) is the gene-editing pioneer behind Casgevy (exa-cel), the world's first approved CRISPR/Cas9 medicine, commercialized with partner Vertex Pharmaceuticals. Trading at \$47.55 (as of 2026-07-29) with roughly a \$4.3B market cap and a fortress balance sheet of about \$2.4B in cash, CRSP pairs a first-in-class approved therapy with a broad, increasingly de-risked pipeline. The catch: Casgevy's launch is slow, the company burns close to \$400M a year, and its value hinges on binary clinical readouts. This is a high-conviction platform story that still belongs in the speculative bucket — rated **SPEC. BUY**.

## Company Overview

CRISPR Therapeutics, founded in 2013 and co-founded by CRISPR/Cas9 co-inventor Emmanuelle Charpentier, is headquartered in Zug, Switzerland with major operations in Boston. The company's mission is to translate the CRISPR/Cas9 gene-editing platform into transformative medicines for serious diseases.

**Business model.** CRSP is a clinical-and-commercial-stage biopharma. Its revenue today is negligible on the income statement — FY2025 total revenue was just \$3.5M (mostly grant income) — because its flagship product, Casgevy, is sold through a profit-share structure with Vertex rather than booked as direct product sales. The economics that matter are recognized as CRSP's share of the collaboration's profits and losses.

**Casgevy (exa-cel) — the crown jewel.** Casgevy is an autologous, ex-vivo CRISPR/Cas9 gene-edited cell therapy that reactivates fetal hemoglobin to treat sickle cell disease (SCD) and transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia (TDT). It won landmark approvals in the UK, US, EU, and additional markets beginning in late 2023 and 2024 — the first CRISPR-based medicine ever approved. Under the collaboration, **Vertex leads development and commercialization and books the revenue, while CRISPR Therapeutics is entitled to 40% of the program's profits and losses** (Vertex takes 60%). Casgevy is a functional one-time cure priced around \$2.2M per patient in the US, addressing an eligible population of roughly 35,000 patients in the US and Europe.

**Pipeline beyond Casgevy.** CRSP has deliberately built a diversified, wholly-owned pipeline:

- **In vivo cardiovascular editing:** CTX310 (targeting ANGPTL3, for LDL cholesterol and triglycerides) and CTX320 (targeting Lp(a), a genetically-driven cardiovascular risk factor). These are lipid-nanoparticle-delivered, in vivo one-time edits aimed at chronic cardiovascular disease — a far larger TAM than rare blood disorders.
- **Immuno-oncology and autoimmune CAR-T:** CTX112 (next-generation allogeneic CD19 CAR-T) for B-cell malignancies and autoimmune diseases, and CTX131 (CD70-directed) for solid and hematologic tumors.
- **Regenerative medicine:** CTX211 and the VCTX-series (with ViaCyte/Vertex heritage) for type 1 diabetes, aiming at insulin independence via gene-edited, immune-evasive islet cells.

**Moat.** CRSP's advantages are its foundational CRISPR/Cas9 intellectual property, a first-mover approved product, deep manufacturing and cell-therapy know-how, and the credibility of having taken a gene edit all the way to approval and commercialization — something almost no competitor has done.

**Management.** Samarth Kulkarni serves as Chairman and CEO. The leadership team is scientifically deep and has demonstrated execution through the regulatory finish line with Casgevy.

## Financial Analysis

CRSP's financials look unusual because its lead product's economics flow through a partner and a profit-share, not through reported revenue.

### Income statement
- **FY2025 revenue:** \$3.5M, down about 91% year over year. This is almost entirely grant revenue; collaboration revenue collapsed as the accounting shifted with Casgevy's commercialization. Reported revenue is not the right lens for this company.
- **Operating loss:** roughly -\$664.6M for FY2025, reflecting R&D across the pipeline plus CRSP's 40% share of Casgevy launch costs.
- **Net loss:** -\$581.6M in FY2025, or **-\$6.47 diluted EPS**.
- **Gross margin:** nominally ~100% given the tiny grant-based revenue base — not economically meaningful here.

### Balance sheet
- **Cash and investments:** about \$1.98B at the end of FY2025, rising to roughly \$2.4B by Q1 2026 after capital raises. This is the single most important number in the story — it funds years of pipeline development.
- **Total debt:** modest relative to cash (roughly \$0.2B at FY2025), leaving CRSP in a strong net-cash position (net cash of about \$18–20 per share).

### Cash flow
- **Operating cash flow:** -\$345.0M in FY2025.
- **Free cash flow:** -\$345.9M in FY2025, trending to roughly -\$400M on a trailing-twelve-month basis — the practical annual burn rate.
- **Runway:** at ~\$400M/year of burn against ~\$2.4B of cash, CRSP has roughly five to six years of runway before a raise is strictly necessary, giving management time to let catalysts play out.

The financial picture is that of a well-capitalized, pre-profit platform biotech: heavy losses funded by a large cash cushion, with the upside optionality carried entirely by the pipeline and Casgevy's eventual ramp.

## Valuation

Traditional earnings multiples are meaningless for CRSP — it has no earnings and negligible reported revenue. Valuation is a sum-of-the-parts, probability-weighted exercise across Casgevy's profit share and the pipeline.

- **Current price:** \$47.55 (2026-07-29)
- **Market cap:** ~\$4.3B, of which roughly \$2.4B is cash — implying the market assigns only about \$1.9B of enterprise value to Casgevy's 40% economics plus the entire pipeline.
- **Analyst consensus:** a "Moderate Buy" with an average price target near \$67.78, comfortably above the current price.

### Scenario analysis

| Scenario | Assumptions | 12–24 Month Target |
|----------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Bull** | Casgevy ramp accelerates and CRSP's 40% profit share turns positive; in vivo CTX310/CTX320 deliver best-in-class data; CTX112 validates the CAR-T franchise. | \$80–\$100 |
| **Base** | Gradual Casgevy uptake; encouraging but not definitive pipeline data; continued ~\$400M burn funded by cash; drift toward the ~\$67.78 consensus. | \$55–\$70 |
| **Bear** | Casgevy stays slow on ex-vivo logistics and payer friction; a pipeline setback resets expectations; eventual dilutive raise. | \$25–\$35 |

The enterprise value net of cash is small relative to the addressable markets in cardiovascular disease and autoimmune indications, which is the core of the SPEC. BUY thesis: you are paying a modest premium over cash for a validated platform with several shots on goal.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **Casgevy commercial inflection.** The key swing factor. As authorized treatment centers scale globally and ex-US reimbursement broadens, patient throughput should accelerate. Because Casgevy is a one-time \$2.2M therapy, even modest volume growth compounds meaningfully into CRSP's 40% profit share.
2. **In vivo cardiovascular pipeline.** CTX310 (ANGPTL3) and CTX320 (Lp(a)) target chronic cardiovascular disease with a single in vivo edit. Durable LDL and Lp(a) reductions would validate a franchise with a TAM orders of magnitude larger than rare blood disorders.
3. **CTX112 CAR-T in autoimmune disease.** The autoimmune CAR-T thesis (resetting the immune system in lupus and other conditions) is one of biotech's hottest areas. Positive CTX112 data would open a large new market for CRSP's allogeneic platform.
4. **Casgevy label and geographic expansion.** Additional treatment centers, new geographies, and potential label extensions with Vertex expand the eligible population beyond the initial ~35,000 SCD/TDT patients.
5. **Regenerative medicine optionality.** The type 1 diabetes programs (CTX211/VCTX-series) represent long-dated but potentially enormous upside if gene-edited islet cells can deliver insulin independence.
6. **Balance-sheet strength.** With ~\$2.4B in cash, CRSP can advance multiple programs simultaneously and is a plausible partner or acquisition target for large pharma seeking gene-editing exposure.

## Risk Factors

### Business risks
- **Slow Casgevy ramp.** Ex-vivo cell therapy is logistically complex: patients require stem-cell collection, myeloablative conditioning, and long vein-to-vein times. This inherently caps the near-term commercialization pace and has kept CRSP's profit share negative so far.
- **Binary clinical risk.** The pipeline's value rests on data readouts. A single safety signal or efficacy miss in CTX310, CTX320, or CTX112 can reset platform expectations sharply.
- **Partner dependence.** CRSP holds only 40% of Casgevy's profits and losses; commercialization decisions and revenue recognition sit with Vertex.

### Financial risks
- **Cash burn.** At roughly \$400M a year, the burn is substantial. Although the ~\$2.4B cash pile provides multi-year runway, a prolonged pipeline delay would eventually force a dilutive raise.
- **No near-term profitability.** There is no realistic path to GAAP profitability in the next few years absent a major Casgevy inflection.

### Macro and sector risks
- **Biotech rate sensitivity.** Pre-profit biotech is among the most rate-sensitive equity groups; a hawkish rate environment compresses valuations.
- **High short interest.** Roughly 22% of the float is sold short, which amplifies volatility in both directions.
- **Competition.** Intellia, Beam Therapeutics, and others are advancing gene, base, and prime editing; cardiovascular RNA and gene therapies (e.g., from larger pharma) compete for the same LDL/Lp(a) opportunity.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY**

CRISPR Therapeutics offers something rare in gene editing: an actually-approved product (Casgevy), a fortress balance sheet (~\$2.4B cash), and a broad, wholly-owned pipeline with multiple near-term catalysts. Stripping out cash, the market values Casgevy's economics plus the entire pipeline at only about \$1.9B — attractive for a validated platform. But Casgevy's launch is slow, the burn is heavy, and the upside is gated by binary readouts. That combination — strong thesis, high uncertainty — is the textbook definition of a speculative buy.

- **Position sizing:** speculative sizing (2–4% of a diversified portfolio). This is a volatile, pre-profit biotech with binary catalysts.
- **Entry strategy:** the stock trades at \$47.55, below the ~\$67.78 consensus target. Accumulate on weakness; scaling in around the low-\$40s improves the risk/reward given the ~22% short interest and choppy tape.
- **Stop / risk management:** for traders, a break below the \$35–\$38 support zone (near the bear-case ceiling) would signal the pipeline or Casgevy narrative is deteriorating. Longer-term holders should treat clinical readouts, not price, as the exit trigger.
- **Technical context:** watch the 50- and 200-day moving averages on the TradingView widget above and the 14-day RSI for overbought/oversold extremes around catalyst dates; the high short interest raises squeeze potential on positive news.
- **Catalyst calendar:** Q2 2026 earnings (August 2026) for Casgevy trends; H2 2026 in vivo CTX310/CTX320 data; 2026 CTX112 CAR-T readouts; ongoing Casgevy geographic and reimbursement expansion; 2027 diabetes program progress.

Price targets: **Bull \$80–\$100 · Base \$55–\$70 · Bear \$25–\$35**.

## Sentiment Analysis

- **Short interest:** elevated at about 22% of the float (~19.8M shares as of mid-July 2026), down slightly from the prior report. This signals meaningful skepticism about the Casgevy ramp — but also sets up squeeze potential on positive catalysts.
- **Retail and social (X/Reddit):** CRSP is a long-standing retail favorite in the gene-editing complex; sentiment swings hard around clinical data and skews optimistic on the "first CRISPR cure" narrative.
- **Institutional ownership:** high and stable, consistent with a widely-held, index-included mid-cap biotech.
- **Options flow:** activity clusters around earnings and data catalysts; implied volatility is structurally high, reflecting the binary nature of the readouts.
- **Net sentiment score:** moderately positive, tempered by the high short interest and slow-launch overhang — consistent with a SPEC. BUY.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: CRISPR Therapeutics made history by getting the first-ever CRISPR gene-editing medicine, Casgevy, approved. Casgevy is a one-time treatment (priced around \$2.2M) that essentially cures two inherited blood diseases — sickle cell and beta thalassemia. CRSP's partner Vertex sells it and hands CRSP 40% of the profits.

The problem is that the treatment is complicated to deliver, so it is rolling out slowly, and CRSP isn't making money yet — it loses about \$400M a year. The good news is CRSP has roughly \$2.4B in the bank, enough to fund itself for years while it develops its next wave of drugs. Those include one-time injections to lower cholesterol and heart-attack risk (CTX310 and CTX320) and cell therapies that could reset the immune system in autoimmune disease (CTX112).

Think of it this way: you are paying a small premium over the company's cash to own a proven gene-editing platform with several big experiments in progress. If a couple of them work, the stock could roughly double (bull case \$80–\$100). If the launch stays slow and an experiment fails, it could fall toward \$25–\$35. The middle path (\$55–\$70) still beats today's price. That balance of big upside and real risk is why this is a speculative buy, not a core holding.
