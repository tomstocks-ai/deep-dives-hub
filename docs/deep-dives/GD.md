# GD — General Dynamics Corporation

General Dynamics is a four-segment aerospace and defense prime: nuclear submarines and surface ships (Marine Systems), armoured vehicles, weapons and munitions (Combat Systems), government IT and mission systems (Technologies), and Gulfstream business jets plus Jet Aviation services (Aerospace). In July 2026 the US Navy awarded General Dynamics Electric Boat \$71.6B of the \$76.6B submarine package — \$29.5B for five Build II Columbia-class boats and \$42.1B for nine Block VI Virginia-class boats — lifting company backlog 32% year over year to a record \$136.5B. Management simultaneously raised FY2026 guidance to \$16.80–\$16.90 diluted EPS on roughly \$55.7B of revenue. Despite that, the stock closed at \$334.16 on 2026-09-28, near the \$306.77 low of its 52-week range and roughly 10% below its 50-day moving average of \$372.84, as a Middle East de-escalation narrative de-rated the entire prime-contractor group. That gap between contracted, decade-long production and a sentiment-driven multiple is the setup.

## Company Overview

GD reported \$52.55B of revenue in 2025 across four roughly comparable segments. Using Q2 2026 as the current mix:

| Segment | Q2 2026 revenue | YoY | Operating margin | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marine Systems | \$4.66B | +10.4% | 7.3% | Electric Boat and NASSCO — Columbia and Virginia class submarines, auxiliary ships, repair |
| Technologies | \$3.62B | +4.1% | 9.4% | GDIT and Mission Systems — federal IT, networks, cyber, C4ISR hardware |
| Aerospace | \$3.53B | +15.1% | 14.5% | Gulfstream large and mid-cabin jets, Jet Aviation completions and services |
| Combat Systems | \$2.29B | +0.3% | 13.9% | Land systems, Abrams and Stryker family, European vehicles, GD-OTS munitions |

The moat is structural rather than technological. Electric Boat is one of only two US yards capable of building nuclear submarines and the sole prime on Columbia-class, which makes it effectively unsubstitutable for the sea-based leg of the nuclear triad. Gulfstream holds a duopoly-plus position in large-cabin business jets against Bombardier and Dassault, with a service network that generates recurring aftermarket revenue. GDIT sits on long-cycle federal contract vehicles with high recompete win rates. Combat Systems is the most contestable of the four but benefits from installed-base sustainment and European rearmament.

Phebe Novakovic has been chairman and CEO since 2013 and has run the company on a consistent playbook: throughput and execution discipline over acquisition-led growth, steady margin accretion, and returning capital. The company has raised its dividend for 34 consecutive years; the current quarterly rate is \$1.59, or \$6.36 annualised, a ~1.9% yield at the current price. Employee count is roughly 117,000–120,000.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (latest complete fiscal year).** Revenue \$52.55B, up 10.1% from \$47.72B. Operating margin 10.2%, up 10 basis points. Net earnings \$4.21B, up 11.3%. Diluted EPS \$15.45, up 13.4% — the EPS growth running ahead of net income reflects buybacks. Free cash flow \$3.96B, or 94% of net earnings, up from 85% conversion in 2024. Year-end cash \$2.33B against \$8.01B total debt.

**Q2 2026 (quarter ended July 5, 2026).** Revenue \$14.09B, up 8.1%. Operating earnings \$1.46B, up 11.9%, on a 10.4% margin — 40 basis points of expansion. Net earnings \$1.16B, up 14.4%. Diluted EPS \$4.24, up 13.4% and ahead of the ~\$3.95 consensus. Interest expense fell to \$49M from \$88M as debt came down. Growth was broad: all four segments grew revenue, with double-digit gains and margin expansion in both Aerospace and Marine Systems.

**First half 2026.** Revenue \$27.58B, up 9.1%. Diluted EPS \$8.35, up 12.8%. Operating cash flow \$4.04B versus \$1.45B a year earlier, and free cash flow \$3.60B versus \$1.11B — a large swing driven mostly by \$1.17B of customer advances and deposits, i.e. cash collected up front on the new order book rather than a step change in underlying earnings quality. Worth watching: unbilled receivables rose \$846M in the half to \$9.26B, the usual tell for a defense business ramping production ahead of billing milestones.

**Balance sheet at July 5, 2026.** Cash and equivalents \$4.33B, total debt \$7.52B, net debt \$3.18B — down from \$5.68B at year-end 2025. Net debt is roughly 0.5x annualised EBITDA equivalent, a genuinely conservative position for a capital-intensive prime. Customer advances and deposits of \$11.03B on the liability side are the flip side of the record backlog. Goodwill of \$20.9B is the legacy of the CSRA and Jet Aviation era and is the main item that would matter in an impairment scenario.

**Capital deployment, Q2 2026.** \$429M of dividends, \$234M of capex, and \$498M of debt reduction. For the half: \$834M dividends, \$437M capex, \$319M of buybacks, \$500M of note repayment. Management has guided capex materially higher — above \$900M, roughly 3.5–4% of sales versus historical norms nearer 2% — to expand submarine and Gulfstream capacity. That is the right investment given the backlog, but it caps free cash flow conversion in the near term.

**Trailing twelve months.** Revenue ~\$54.86B, net income ~\$4.49B, diluted EPS ~\$16.40, on ~270.2M shares outstanding.

## Valuation

At \$334.16 with a \$90.3B market capitalisation:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailing P/E | ~20.4x |
| Forward P/E (FY2026 guide \$16.80–\$16.90) | ~19.8x |
| Forward P/E on ~\$18 FY2027 consensus | ~18.6x |
| EV / revenue (TTM, net debt \$3.2B) | ~1.7x |
| Dividend yield | ~1.9% |
| Backlog / TTM revenue | ~2.5x |
| Beta | ~0.32 |
| Analyst consensus | Buy, average target ~\$421 (24 analysts) |

Two things stand out. First, the backlog-to-revenue ratio of ~2.5x — and ~3.4x counting the \$186.9B total estimated contract value — is the highest in GD's modern history, and the Marine Systems component is contracted into the late 2030s. Second, the multiple is at the low end of the post-2022 range even though earnings guidance was raised twice this year. The market is not disputing the earnings; it is discounting the terminal demand assumption.

A simple earnings-based frame: 21–22x a ~\$18 FY2027 EPS puts fair value at \$378–\$396. Add the dividend and the base case lands at \$380–\$405. That is below the \$421 sell-side average, and deliberately so — the consensus targets were mostly set in late July and August before the September de-rating, and Bernstein has already trimmed to \$407.

**Bull: \$430–\$470.** Requires the submarine ramp executing cleanly, Gulfstream holding a 1.3x-plus book-to-bill with Aerospace margin above 14.5%, free cash flow conversion back at 100%, and the group re-rating toward 24–25x forward EPS as the peace-dividend story fades. Morgan Stanley (\$465) and Susquehanna (\$455) sit in this zone.

**Base: \$380–\$405.** FY2026 lands inside guidance at ~\$55.7B revenue, 10.5% operating margin and \$16.80–\$16.90 EPS; FY2027 EPS reaches ~\$18; the multiple holds at 21–22x; capex near 3.5–4% of sales caps FCF upside; dividend grows ~6% and buybacks more than cover dilution.

**Bear: \$280–\$310.** A durable de-escalation across both the Middle East and Ukraine drives a full de-rating to 16–17x forward EPS, or submarine cost growth and a business-jet demand rollover hit the two segments that have carried recent upside. Note the bear case is below the 52-week low — this is the scenario where the September drawdown extends rather than reverses.

## Growth Catalysts

**The submarine award is the dominant fact.** The \$76.6B Navy package announced July 29, 2026 ended a nearly three-year negotiation. Electric Boat took \$29.5B for five Build II Columbia-class SSBNs (SSBN 828 through 832) and \$42.1B for nine Block VI Virginia-class boats, plus shipyard productivity and infrastructure investment. This does three things: it fixes Marine Systems revenue visibility into the late 2030s, it includes government co-investment in shipbuilder productivity that de-risks the ramp, and it is the single reason backlog jumped 32% YoY.

**Order momentum is broad, not concentrated.** Q2 2026 orders totalled \$20B — \$14.7B defense, \$5.3B Aerospace — for a company-wide 1.4x book-to-bill with every segment above 1.0x. Aerospace alone booked 1.5x in the quarter and 1.3x for the half, versus 1.0x in the first half of 2025.

**Munitions and interceptors.** On August 31, 2026 the Pentagon signed seven-year framework agreements with General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems and with Lockheed Martin to scale interceptor missile component production. GD-OTS also took a \$229.7M modification for 188 additional Hammerhead units. Munitions replenishment is a lower-profile but higher-margin, faster-cycle growth line than shipbuilding.

**Gulfstream conversion.** 41 aircraft delivered in Q2 2026 (35 large-cabin, 6 mid-cabin) versus 38 a year earlier; 79 in the half versus 74. Aerospace margin expanded to 14.5% from 13.2%. The G700 and G800 ramp is what turns a large order book into the segment's operating leverage.

**Technologies awards keep landing.** GDIT won a \$1.3B enterprise network operations and cybersecurity contract for the Army National Guard in August 2026, plus a steady stream of Navy modifications in the \$150M–\$230M range through September. Technologies is the slowest-growing segment but also the least capital-intensive.

**Capital return.** 34 consecutive years of dividend growth, with a ~6.5% five-year average annual increase. The next raise decision typically lands with Q4 results. Buybacks resumed at \$319M in the first half, and a 100% FCF conversion year would expand capacity for both.

## Risk Factors

**The de-rating risk is the live one.** GD is down ~8% over the past month while the aerospace and defense group fell harder, and a Reuters report on an Iranian offer over the Strait of Hormuz knocked the primes 3% in a single session in September. The market is pricing a peace dividend. Contracted backlog protects revenue, not the multiple — and the multiple is most of the near-term share-price variance. This is the risk that a strong Q3 print will not fully resolve.

**Shipbuilding execution.** The Columbia-class award is an incentive-fee contract modification. Build-rate shortfalls, skilled-labour shortages at Groton and Quonset Point, or supplier-base constraints push cost growth onto GD. The industry has not yet demonstrated that it can hit the combined Columbia-plus-Virginia build rate the Navy wants, and Marine Systems already runs the thinnest segment margin at 7.3%.

**Business-jet cyclicality.** Aerospace is the highest-margin segment and the most macro-sensitive. Business aviation orders turn faster than any defense line. If corporate demand rolls over, the segment that has driven the recent earnings beats reverses first.

**Appropriations and political timing.** GD's revenue is overwhelmingly US government. Continuing resolutions, shutdowns and appropriations delays push obligations right and inflate unbilled receivables. Longer term, a post-reconciliation budget reset or a Pentagon acquisition overhaul — the "new Last Supper" consolidation scenario being discussed in Washington — could compress award flow in Technologies and Combat Systems.

**Combat Systems stagnation.** Revenue was up just 0.3% YoY in Q2 2026 with margin down 30 basis points. European rearmament should help, but the segment is the clearest evidence that GD's growth is currently concentrated in two of four businesses.

**Elevated capex and FCF conversion.** Capex rising to 3.5–4% of sales from ~2% historically is a ~79% step up. Necessary, but it means the 100% FCF conversion target has less cushion, and the first-half conversion was flattered by \$1.17B of customer advances that will not repeat at that magnitude every period.

**Goodwill.** \$20.9B of goodwill against \$26.8B of shareholders' equity. Not a near-term concern, but it is the item that would amplify any Technologies or Jet Aviation deterioration.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Position sizing: a core industrial holding, 3–5% of a diversified portfolio. This is a low-beta (~0.32), dividend-growing prime, not a trading vehicle.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is technically broken — \$334.16 sits below both the 50-day (\$372.84) and 200-day (\$355.39) moving averages, and it has been in oversold territory during the September drawdown. A staged entry makes more sense than a single fill: an initial tranche here, a second near the \$306.77 52-week low if the de-escalation trade extends, and a confirmation tranche on a reclaim of the 200-day at ~\$355. Waiting for the whole position until after the October 23 print risks paying up for a good quarter that is already well telegraphed by the Q2 order book.

**Stop loss.** For a core position, a fundamental rather than technical stop is more appropriate: exit on evidence of submarine cost growth flowing through Marine Systems margin, or on a guidance cut. For a tactical position, a close below \$300 — decisively through the 52-week low — invalidates the setup and puts the \$280–\$310 bear range in play.

**Technical context.** Both major moving averages are above price and the 50-day has crossed below the 200-day, which is a bearish configuration. RSI has been in or near oversold territory through September. Support is the \$306.77 52-week low; the first meaningful resistance is the 200-day at ~\$355, then the \$372 area where the 50-day sits. Constructively, the 1-year return is roughly flat (+0.6%) while EPS grew ~13% — the de-rating has done the work, not an earnings miss.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: mixed, deteriorating on macro rather than company news.** Company-specific headlines through Q3 have been uniformly positive — the \$76.6B submarine award, the seven-year interceptor framework agreement, the \$1.3B GDIT National Guard win, a raised guidance. Sector headlines have been the opposite: "defense stocks selloff deepens", coverage of primes falling on Hormuz reopening reports, and commentary that the defense-tech boom may be slowing. The company narrative and the sector narrative are pulling in opposite directions, and the sector narrative is winning.

**Sell side: constructive but trimming.** Consensus is Buy across 24 analysts with an average target near \$421, implying ~26% upside. The distribution matters more than the average: Morgan Stanley Overweight at \$465 and Susquehanna Positive at \$455 at the top, Deutsche Bank Hold at \$384 and Bernstein Market Perform at \$407 at the bottom, with a low target of \$313 in the mix. MarketBeat's tally is 12 buy, one strong buy, five hold, one sell — a "Moderate Buy" with genuine dispersion. Almost every target was raised after the July print, and the most recent action is a Bernstein cut explicitly attributed to the sector selloff. BofA added GD to its "US 1 List" in late August, removing Northrop.

**Retail and social: quiet, income-oriented.** GD does not generate the retail volume of defense-tech or drone names. What discussion exists frames it as a dividend-growth holding — the 34-year streak and the GD-versus-LMT retirement-income comparisons are the recurring theme. There is no meme dynamic here in either direction, which also means no retail bid to cushion the institutional de-risking.

**Insider activity: mildly negative.** A director sold 7,440 shares in late September. Routine in scale, but the absence of visible insider buying into a 52-week low is a small negative signal rather than a neutral one.

**Options flow.** No unusual activity was verifiable in this session; GD's options market is thin relative to its market cap and the ~0.32 beta reflects how it trades. Treat the absence of a signal as absence of information, not as confirmation.

**Composite: 6.5/10.** Fundamentals and order flow are close to a 9; sector positioning and price momentum are closer to a 3. The score reflects the tension rather than averaging it away — this is a case where the sentiment is the opportunity, provided the October print confirms execution.

## Readability Pass

General Dynamics builds nuclear submarines, tanks and ammunition, Gulfstream private jets, and runs government IT. In July 2026 the US Navy signed a \$76.6B submarine contract, of which General Dynamics got \$71.6B — five Columbia-class and nine Virginia-class boats. That single deal pushed the company's order book to a record \$136.5B, about two and a half years of revenue, with the submarine work stretching into the late 2030s. The company is also earning more: FY2025 revenue was \$52.55B with \$15.45 diluted EPS, and management raised this year's forecast to \$16.80–\$16.90 EPS on ~\$55.7B of revenue.

So why is the stock near its 52-week low? Because investors are betting on peace. Reports of Middle East de-escalation knocked every defense contractor in September, and GD fell with the group even though its own contracts are already signed. The result: the share price is flat over a year while earnings grew ~13%, and you can now buy it at roughly 19–20x earnings with a 1.9% dividend that has risen for 34 straight years.

The bet is that signed contracts eventually matter more than headlines. The main ways to lose: a lasting global de-escalation permanently lowers what investors will pay for defense earnings, or General Dynamics struggles to actually build submarines at the rate the Navy wants and eats the cost. Marine Systems runs a thin 7.3% margin, so there is not much room for error there. Rated BUY, sized as a core holding, with a staged entry given the broken chart.
