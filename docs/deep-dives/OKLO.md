# OKLO — Oklo Inc.

Oklo is an advanced-nuclear developer building compact, factory-built Aurora Powerhouse fast reactors (15–75 MW) on a "build, own, operate" model, selling electricity through long-term power purchase agreements. Trading at \$47.01 as of 2026-08-11 for a ~\$8.74B market cap (186.02M shares outstanding), the stock has round-tripped from a ~\$193.84 52-week high to near the \$36.61 low amid nuclear-sector profit-taking. Q2 2026 earnings on 2026-08-07 delivered Oklo's first reported revenue (\$1.2M vs ~\$72K estimate) and confirmed the Groves Isotope Test Reactor achieved first criticality following DOE startup authorization — a major near-term de-risking milestone now in the rearview. With ~\$3.0B in total liquidity (\$1.6B cash and equivalents + \$1.4B marketable securities) after raising \$1.9B via ATM equity in 2026, a Meta 1.2 GW Ohio campus partnership, and membership in a Trump-administration-backed ~\$200M advanced-nuclear-for-AI initiative, the risk/reward remains compelling but binary. Rating: **SPEC. BUY**.

---

## Company Overview

Oklo designs and intends to operate **Aurora Powerhouses** — small, factory-built nuclear power plants ranging from 15 MW to 75 MW. Rather than selling reactors as equipment, Oklo owns and operates them and sells the electricity under long-term PPAs. Customers such as data centers and industrial facilities get firm, carbon-free baseload power without needing any nuclear expertise, while Oklo captures 20+ years of predictable, recurring revenue per site.

- **Technology:** Liquid-metal-cooled, metal-fueled fast reactor with heat-pipe heat transport, based on the proven Experimental Breeder Reactor-II (EBR-II) that ran at Idaho National Laboratory from 1964–1994. The design can run on recycled fuel, supporting Oklo's longer-term fuel-recycling ambitions.
- **Business model:** "Build, own, operate" — Oklo finances against the PPA, not the customer's balance sheet.
- **Leadership:** Co-founder and CEO Jacob DeWitte has engaged with the NRC since roughly 2016; the company listed on the NYSE in May 2024 via a SPAC merger with AltC Acquisition Corp, which was backed by Sam Altman.
- **Key sites:** Santa Clara, California headquarters; Aurora-INL demonstration project and fuel work at Idaho National Laboratory; a Pike County, Ohio campus tied to the Meta partnership; and the Groves Isotope Test Reactor, which has now reached first criticality.

Two developments materially strengthen the story since the prior update. First, the **Groves Isotope Test Reactor achieved first criticality** following DOE startup authorization under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program, converting a major near-term milestone from paperwork into an operating reactor. Second, Oklo **remains part of a Trump-administration-backed initiative of roughly \$200M** alongside Microsoft, Nvidia, X-energy and others to accelerate advanced-nuclear deployment for AI data centers, reinforcing the AI-power-demand thesis that underpins its commercial pipeline.

Oklo remains pre-revenue at scale, but Q2 2026 marked its first reported revenue. The investment case rests on balance-sheet strength and burn discipline rather than an income statement.

The headline is a **fortress balance sheet: ~\$3.0B in total liquidity and essentially no debt**, giving Oklo a multi-year runway to reach commercialization without near-term dilution pressure — a rare position for a pre-revenue company. The YTD net loss of \$81.6M and operating cash used of \$65.5M reflect rising development activity, and losses will remain elevated as the company builds toward first commercial operation. The critical financial question is not solvency but execution: converting cash and milestones into operating reactors and, eventually, PPA revenue.

## Valuation

Oklo trades like a long-dated call option on advanced nuclear rather than on any current earnings. At \$47.01 and a ~\$8.74B market cap, the market is pricing meaningful eventual deployment success, discounted by execution and timing risk. Traditional DCF is of limited use pre-revenue, so the scenarios below frame outcomes by milestone achievement. Analyst consensus is **Buy across ~20 analysts with a mean 12-month target of ~\$79.88** — roughly 70% upside from the current price — with a high of \$130 and a low of \$14. MarketBeat shows a consensus target of ~\$85.03, while TipRanks and StockAnalysis both sit near ~\$79.88.

**Bull case — target \$90–120.** Groves first criticality is already achieved, Aurora-INL advances cleanly through NRC licensing, Meta Ohio proceeds, LOIs convert to binding PPAs, and the policy tailwind from the ~\$200M AI-nuclear initiative accelerates deployment. The stock re-rates back toward its prior highs.

**Base case — target \$65–80.** Groves criticality achieved and Aurora-INL advances with modest slippage; Meta Ohio Phase 1 and initial PPA conversions proceed; ~\$3.0B liquidity funds the plan without near-term dilution. The stock trades on execution milestones between its 52-week low and prior highs, consistent with the ~\$79.88 consensus target.

**Bear case — target \$25–35.** NRC delays push first commercial operation into 2029–2030, HALEU fuel constraints slow deployment, first-of-a-kind commissioning issues surface, and continued nuclear-sector profit-taking compounds the high-beta drawdown.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Groves Isotope Test Reactor first criticality** — already achieved following DOE startup authorization; the single most important near-term de-risking event is now complete.
- **Trump-administration-backed ~\$200M AI-nuclear initiative** with Microsoft, Nvidia, X-energy and others — validates the AI-power-demand thesis and strengthens policy support.
- **Meta 1.2 GW Ohio campus partnership** — anchor demand that provides a template for scaling the build-own-operate model.
- **LOI-to-PPA conversions** — turning the non-binding pipeline into financeable, revenue-generating contracts.
- **Aurora-INL and NRC licensing progress** — each licensing step compresses the path to first commercial operation and de-risks the regulatory pathway.
- **AI and data-center power demand** — structural tailwind driving urgency and pricing power for firm, carbon-free baseload power.
- **Fuel-recycling and isotope optionality** — longer-dated upside from recycled-fuel economics and medical-isotope production.

## Risk Factors

- **NRC licensing risk (high).** No non-water-cooled commercial reactor has yet been licensed in the US. Any delay pushes first revenue out by quarters or years; Oklo's earlier 2022 application for a smaller design was denied.
- **HALEU fuel supply (medium-high).** High-assay low-enriched uranium remains scarce after Russia was banned as a supplier; constrained fuel availability could throttle deployment speed.
- **First-of-a-kind execution (medium).** Commissioning Groves and Aurora-INL at commercial scale carries the usual first-mover technical risk despite EBR-II heritage.
- **Valuation and sentiment volatility (high).** The stock ran to a ~\$193.84 52-week high then pulled back to ~\$47, with a beta of 1.20 and a milestone-driven narrative, drawdowns can be sharp and sentiment-led.
- **Pre-revenue duration (medium).** First commercial operation is ~2 years out; a long, milestone-gated wait can test investor patience even with ample cash.
- **Competition (medium).** TerraPower, NuScale, X-energy, Kairos and others are pursuing the same advanced-reactor and AI-power opportunity.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: Oklo is trying to build small nuclear power plants in factories and sell the electricity — mostly to power-hungry AI data centers — instead of selling the reactors themselves. It just reported its first revenue (\$1.2M in Q2 2026) but is still losing money as it spends to finish its first plants. The big recent news is that its Groves test reactor reached "first criticality" after getting DOE approval, a real, tangible step toward proving the technology works. The catch: the stock is a rollercoaster — it went from about \$194 down to about \$47 — and it still needs regulators to approve its designs and enough special nuclear fuel (HALEU) to actually build. Analysts still mostly say buy, with an average target around \$80, roughly 70% above today's price. It's a high-risk, high-reward bet for patient investors.
