# FIGR — Figure Technology Solutions

Figure Technology Solutions (Nasdaq: FIGR) operates a blockchain-native capital marketplace for consumer credit. Partners originate home equity, small-business, auto and personal loans on Figure's loan origination system, then sell them to institutional buyers through Figure Connect, a tokenized marketplace that carried 65% of Q2 2026 volume. The operating numbers are unusually strong for a lender — Q2 2026 marketplace volume of \$4.3B (+132% year over year), adjusted net revenue of \$218M (+95%), GAAP net income of \$87M and a 54.6% adjusted EBITDA margin — yet at \$29.33 the stock sits ~62% below its January 2026 high of \$78.00 after a short-seller report attacked the technology story. This update replaces the stale 2026-06-27 version of this page in full.

## Company Overview

Figure describes itself as the leading blockchain-native capital marketplace for the origination, funding, sale and trading of tokenized assets. Collectively Figure and its partners have originated over \$30B of loans to date, making its ecosystem the largest non-bank provider of home equity financing in the U.S. CEO Michael Tannenbaum runs the company; founder Mike Cagney is no longer CEO, a correction versus the prior version of this page.

**How the business makes money.** Revenue arrives in six meaningful lines. In Q2 2026: ecosystem and technology fees \$72.9M (the largest line for the first time), gain on sale of loans \$57.6M, gain on servicing asset \$29.1M, origination fees \$26.3M, interest income \$22.8M and servicing fees \$11.3M. The mix is deliberately shifting from balance-sheet-heavy gain-on-sale toward capital-light recurring fees.

**The three channels.** Direct-to-consumer, "Figure as intermediary" (Figure warehouses the loan for three to four weeks before selling it), and Figure Connect (partner originates, Figure never owns the asset). Connect has the lowest take rate but the highest contribution margin and lowest capital intensity. It went from zero at launch in June 2024 to 65% of volume in Q2 2026, up from 56% in Q1 2026 and 42% a year earlier. Management has raised its medium-term Connect mix expectation from ~60% to ~70%.

**The blockchain layer.** Provenance underpins the stack. Around it sit DART (digital asset registry for custody and lien perfection), \$YLDS (an SEC-registered yield-bearing stablecoin that functions as a tokenized money market fund, \$556M in circulation at 2026-06-30, up from \$328M at year-end 2025), Democratized Prime (an on-chain lend/borrow marketplace acting as a modern warehouse line) and OPEN, the on-chain public equity network on which FIGR's Blockchain common stock trades as FGRS.

**Moat.** Standardisation and liquidity, not the chain itself. Figure's securitization shelf has priced 22 deals with AAA ratings from both S and P and Moody's; AAA spreads have tightened from ~255bps in 2023 to ~135bps year to date, and the note-buyer base has grown past 100 unique buyers with 70% active across multiple deals. Cheaper funding lets Figure pay partners better prices, which attracts more volume, which attracts more buyers. Management's "Figure factor" analysis of 185 partners with full 2025 HMDA data claims they originated 2.6x more HELOC volume than their pre-Figure trajectory would predict.

**Scale markers as of Q2 2026.** 489 active origination partners (+102 in the quarter, versus ~250 at IPO), partner-branded volume at 83% of the total, weekly application volume above \$1B for the first time in early July 2026, and a securitized collateral base of \$7.7B, nearly double a year earlier.

Diluted EPS of \$0.35 beat the \$0.24 consensus. Two caveats on quality of earnings: net income included a \$4.4M tax benefit from post-IPO option exercises (management still guides to a 26% normalised effective tax rate over the long run), and adjusted EBITDA included \$5.9M of realised profit from selling a minority interest — excluding it, margin was ~52% rather than 54.6%. Deliberately retaining ~\$360M of loans on balance sheet to seed Democratized Prime cost roughly another 1.7 points of margin.

**Full year 2025 (the last completed fiscal year).** Net revenue \$506.9M (+48.7%), adjusted net revenue \$514.8M (+51.8%), net income \$134.3M (+574%), diluted EPS \$0.44, adjusted EBITDA \$251.2M at a 48.8% margin, and marketplace volume of \$8.4B (+63%).

**Trailing twelve months.** Adding H1 2026 and removing H1 2025 gives TTM net revenue of ~\$709M and TTM net income of ~\$237M. MarketBeat reports trailing EPS of \$0.93 and a net margin of 33.45% with return on equity of 18.90%.

**Balance sheet at 2026-06-30.** Cash and equivalents \$1.44B excluding \$95.9M of restricted cash, up \$239M or 20% from year-end 2025. Loans held for sale \$597M (+47.7%), marketable securities \$354M, loan servicing asset \$155M, digital assets \$62M. Total assets \$3.02B against total liabilities \$1.60B and stockholders' equity \$1.41B. Accumulated deficit narrowed to \$54M from \$187M. Debt-to-equity is 0.22 and the current ratio 1.86. Subsequent to the quarter, on 2026-07-14, Figure closed a \$600M unsecured senior notes offering at 8.5% to fund Kiavi and broaden its funding base.

**Operating leverage is real.** Operations and processing costs fell to ~67bps of marketplace volume from ~79bps a year earlier while volume more than doubled, which management attributes to AI and automation in loan operations.

**Credit discipline.** Weighted average FICO at origination rose to 756 year to date from 737 in 2020; weighted average combined loan-to-value fell to 62.1%; average loan size grew to \$96,000 from \$52,000. Management says a meaningful share of delinquent loans cure or pay off in full within six months.

**The one soft spot is take rate.** At 3.6% it sat at the low end of the guided 3.5%–4.0% band, and management expects the low end again in Q3. The causes are structural rather than competitive: Connect carries the lowest take rate and is growing fastest; larger partners reach lower volume-tier pricing as they scale; rising rates compressed gain on sale; and first-lien volume, which carries a lower take rate, tripled year over year. Management insists there have been no price cuts and prices to contribution margin rather than take rate, and has promised a better unit-economics disclosure once Kiavi is fully absorbed.

The core tension: on reported numbers this is a ~100% revenue grower with 55% EBITDA margins at ~18x FY2027 earnings, which is cheap. The market is paying that price because it does not fully trust the numbers or their durability.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Kiavi.** Closed 2026-09-01. Kiavi is the largest U.S. residential transition loan (fix-and-flip) lender. Reported consideration differs by source — TipRanks headlined \$590M, CoinGape \$717M — and Figure funded it with the \$600M July notes. Management guided to ~\$100M of annual EBITDA, a ~40% volume uplift and an unlevered payback under four years, and intends to turn Kiavi's post-renovation valuation technology into a private-label marketplace product.
- **Figure Connect mix shift.** Each point of mix toward Connect reduces balance-sheet usage and raises fee-based economics. Management's medium-term expectation moved from ~60% to ~70% because the largest new partners now onboard straight onto Connect.
- **Democratized Prime as a warehouse replacement.** Third-party assets reached ~\$170M as of 2026-08-06, roughly 23x the year-end 2025 level, driven largely by the Agora auto partnership. Matched offers were \$392M and available lender supply \$522M at quarter end. The next phase adds permanent takeout via whole-loan sales and securitization of non-Figure assets — which would let Figure earn ecosystem fees on assets it finances short-term.
- **New verticals.** Small-business and home-improvement lending against home equity hit a \$470M run rate as of June 2026, with SMB volume up 57% quarter over quarter; SMB pools launched on Democratized Prime in July 2026. Banks and credit unions are a dedicated go-to-market motion, including depositories using Connect to source Community Reinvestment Act eligible assets.
- **The \$35 trillion home equity opportunity.** With rates elevated, consumers are using home equity to retire higher-rate consumer debt — a use case that added four percentage points of total volume year to date.
- **AI in the stack.** Figure's AI adapter standardised Agora's auto assets in five weeks instead of several months, and a 2026-09-10 partnership with Sierra deploys AI agents to recover abandoned home equity applications.
- **\$YLDS distribution.** EDX Markets integrated \$YLDS as collateral and a treasury asset on 2026-08-25. Figure retains a 35bps spread on outstanding balances, and partners paid in \$YLDS settle days faster than by wire.
- **Buyback.** A \$200M authorisation runs to 2027-02-27; execution at these prices would be meaningful against a ~\$6.6B cap.

## Risk Factors

- **The short-seller overhang is the defining risk.** A 2026 short report questioned Figure's technology positioning and business model. Granahan Investment Management, a holder, wrote in its Q2 2026 letter that "reported operating performance remained strong" but the "credibility and valuation overhang increased uncertainty," and it trimmed. Hedge fund holders fell from 51 to 32 across the quarter. Absent a resolution, the multiple stays compressed regardless of results.
- **HELOC concentration.** Figure's own risk factors lead with reliance on HELOCs and exposure to the HELOC market and housing values. Diversification into DSCR, RTL, auto and SMB is real but young.
- **Take-rate compression is structural, not cyclical.** The same Connect mix shift that drives margins also shrinks revenue per dollar of volume. If volume growth decelerates before contribution margin disclosure improves, the stock has a hard story to tell.
- **Credit cycle.** Delinquencies, AAA spreads and whole-loan demand move together in a downturn. A widening from ~135bps back toward 2023's ~255bps would hit gain-on-sale economics directly.
- **Leverage and integration.** \$600M of unsecured notes at 8.5% is expensive money, and Kiavi is by far the company's largest acquisition.
- **Governance.** A dual-class structure concentrates voting control, and the company has disclosed previously identified material weaknesses in internal controls that it is still remediating — an uncomfortable combination alongside a short report.
- **Digital-asset regulation.** \$YLDS, DART, Democratized Prime and the OPEN share class all depend on an evolving framework; a change in how such instruments are characterised would be disruptive.
- **Insider selling.** 67,650 shares worth ~\$2.16M were sold in the three months to late September 2026, including 47,650 shares by David Stevens at ~\$31.38 and 8,000 by the CFO at ~\$38.69. Both were under Rule 10b5-1 plans, but the direction is one-way. Insiders still own ~26.6% of the stock.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY**

The fundamental trajectory is among the best in fintech: third consecutive quarter of triple-digit volume growth, 95% adjusted revenue growth, 54.6% adjusted EBITDA margins, GAAP profitability, \$1.44B of cash and improving credit metrics. Management frames it as "Rule of 150." At ~18x consensus FY2027 EPS the price does not reflect that. The reason to size this as speculative rather than core is that the bear case is not about the numbers reported — it is about whether they can be trusted and whether the take-rate trend eventually caps revenue growth. Until the short-seller questions are clearly answered and the promised contribution-margin disclosure lands, that discount is rational.

**Position sizing:** 1.5–2.5% for growth portfolios; 1% or less for conservative ones. Treat it as a position that can halve.

**Entry strategy:** The stock is in a downtrend — \$29.33 sits below both the 50-day (\$32.84) and 200-day (\$36.73) moving averages, and the 52-week range is \$24.11 to \$78.00. Scale in thirds: an initial tranche at \$28–\$30, a second on a reclaim of the 50-day average, and the last only after Q3 results confirm the guide. Avoid a single full-size entry ahead of the November print.

**Stop loss:** \$23.50, just below the \$24.11 52-week low. A break there on volume would say the market has sided with the short thesis.

## Readability Pass

- **HELOC** — Home Equity Line of Credit. A revolving credit line secured against the equity in a home. Figure's core product.
- **Consumer Loan Marketplace volume** — the dollar value of loans originated on Figure's system plus third-party loans traded on Figure Connect. The company's headline scale metric, and the only line it guides.
- **Figure Connect** — a tokenized marketplace where partner lenders sell loans directly to institutional buyers. Figure takes a fee and never owns the loan, so it uses no balance sheet.
- **Net take rate** — marketplace revenue divided by marketplace volume. Falling because the cheapest, most profitable channel is growing fastest, not because Figure is discounting.
- **Democratized Prime** — an on-chain lending pool that lets originators borrow against loans they are aggregating. A blockchain-native replacement for a bank warehouse line.
- **\$YLDS** — an SEC-registered, yield-bearing stablecoin that behaves like a tokenized money market fund. Figure keeps a 35bps spread on balances outstanding and uses it to settle marketplace payouts faster than wires.
- **Residential transition loan (RTL)** — short-term financing for buying and renovating a house, the "fix-and-flip" loan. Kiavi is the U.S. market leader.
- **Gain on sale** — the profit booked when a loan is sold for more than its carrying value. Sensitive to interest rates, which is why rising rates hurt Figure's take rate.
- **Adjusted EBITDA margin** — operating profitability before interest, tax, depreciation, stock compensation and various non-cash marks, divided by adjusted net revenue. Figure targets 60%+ medium term versus 54.6% in Q2 2026.
- **In plain English** — Figure is a toll booth on home equity lending. Other lenders originate loans on its software and sell them through its marketplace to insurers and asset managers. Figure keeps a fee on both sides, and because loan records live on a blockchain, buyers do less diligence, funding costs less, and everyone makes more. Growth and margins are excellent. The debate is whether a short seller is right that the blockchain part matters less than advertised, and whether the fee per dollar of volume keeps shrinking.
