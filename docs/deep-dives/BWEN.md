# BWEN — Broadwind, Inc.

**Broadwind, Inc.** is a Cicero, Illinois–based diversified precision manufacturer serving wind power, gas turbines, oil and gas, mining, and broader industrial markets. Its three segments — Heavy Fabrications, Gearing, and Industrial Solutions — sit at the intersection of U.S. power-generation infrastructure and heavy industrial capex. With a micro-cap footprint of ~\$91M and thin but improving margins, the investment case rests on a double order tailwind: Industrial Solutions is anchored in the combined-cycle natural gas turbine supply chain, while Gearing and Heavy Fabrications benefit from grid-scale wind and industrial replacement demand. Analysts price the stock to ~\$6, and Q1 2026 order growth (+23% total, +66% Gearing, +44% Industrial Solutions) suggests the backlog inflection is real, but customer concentration, commodity volatility, and leverage keep the rating at **SPEC. BUY**.

## 1. Company Overview

Broadwind operates through three reportable segments:

- **Heavy Fabrications (formerly Towers and Heavy Fabrications)** — manufactures wind towers, large industrial fabrications, and weldments for energy and infrastructure customers. Revenue is cyclical with wind power capex and production tax credit (PTC) policy.
- **Gearing** — produces industrial gears, gearboxes, and drivetrain components for mining, oil and gas, power generation, and general industrial markets.
- **Industrial Solutions** — provides supply chain solutions, instrumentation and controls, valve assemblies, sensor devices, fuel-system components, electrical junction boxes, electromechanical devices, fabricated panels, kitting, packaging, and assembly primarily for the combined-cycle natural gas turbine market.

Headquartered in Cicero, Illinois, the company was formerly known as Broadwind Energy, Inc. and has steadily repositioned itself toward higher-value industrial and gas-turbine content, reducing pure wind-tower exposure over time. The Industrial Solutions backlog reached a record \$38.1M at the end of FY2025, reflecting a multi-year gas-turbine service and new-unit demand cycle.

## 2. Financial Analysis

Broadwind is a small-scale manufacturer with thin but positive margins, recently oscillating between profitability and small GAAP losses quarter-to-quarter.

**Annual Performance (FY2025):**

- Revenue: **\$158.1M**, up **10.4%** YoY.
- GAAP net income: **\$5.2M** (\$0.23 per diluted share) — a meaningful swing from prior losses.
- Non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA: **\$8.7M**.
- Net debt to trailing-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA: **1.6x**.
- Cash and availability under the credit facility: **~\$25M** at end of Q4 2025.
- Industrial Solutions backlog: **\$38.1M** (record).

**Quarterly Performance (Q1 2026):**

- Revenue: **\$34.1M**.
- GAAP net loss: **(\$0.5)M** or **(\$0.02)** per diluted share — a seasonally weaker quarter.
- Non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA: **\$2.2M** (6.5% of revenue).
- Total orders: **\$37.4M**, up **23% YoY**.
  - Gearing orders: **+66% YoY**.
  - Industrial Solutions orders: **+44% YoY**.
  - Heavy Fabrications orders: **-20% YoY** (wind capex timing remains uneven).

**Balance Sheet and Cash Flow:** The company is levered but not distressed. Net debt / TTM EBITDA of 1.6x is manageable for an industrial OEM supplier, and ~\$25M of cash plus credit availability provides liquidity. However, the margin of safety is narrow: a slowdown in wind or gas-turbine orders, or a spike in steel costs, could quickly pressure EBITDA and covenant headroom. Free cash flow is lumpy because of working capital swings tied to large project deliveries.

**Margins:** Gross margins are modest (typical for heavy fabrication and kitting/assembly) and EBITDA margins are improving but still single-digit to low-double-digit at best. The FY2025 EBITDA margin was roughly **5.5%** on \$158.1M revenue; Q1 2026 showed 6.5%, which is directionally encouraging. The bull case requires operating leverage to push EBITDA margins toward the high-single-digit range as revenue scales toward the \$140–150M guided band.

## 3. Growth Catalysts

1. **Natural Gas and AI Power Demand Cycle** — Industrial Solutions supplies the combined-cycle gas turbine (CCGT) supply chain. As data center load growth and grid reliability concerns drive new gas-turbine orders and service upgrades from OEMs like GE Vernova and Siemens Energy, Broadwind benefits from higher kitting, assembly, and controls demand. This is the most durable demand driver in the portfolio.
2. **Gearing Order Rebound** — Q1 2026 Gearing orders jumped 66% YoY, suggesting mining, oil and gas, and power-generation replacement cycles are re-accelerating. This segment is more cyclical but also more margin-accretive than heavy fabrications at low utilization.
3. **Heavy Fabrications / Wind Stabilization** — While Q1 2026 Heavy Fabrications orders fell 20% YoY, the segment is a leveraged play on wind PTC renewal and domestic content requirements. Any stabilization in the wind tower backlog would remove a drag on consolidated revenue and sentiment.
4. **Q2 2026 Earnings and Potential Guidance Raise** — Q2 2026 results are expected in August 2026. A revenue beat, margin improvement, or raised FY2026 guidance (currently ~\$140–150M) would be a near-term re-rating catalyst. The market is also looking for debt-paydown progress and confirmation that the Q1 order momentum is converting into H2 2026 shipments.
5. **Micro-Cap Asymmetric Sentiment** — With only ~\$91M market cap and limited sell-side coverage, a modest shift in institutional or retail interest can produce outsized price moves. The consensus price target of \$6.00 implies ~33% upside from current levels, and the low-float structure can amplify momentum if catalysts align.

## 4. Valuation and Scenarios

Broadwind is valued as a small-cap industrial turnaround with a leveraged cyclical profile. At ~\$4.51 and ~\$91M market cap, the stock trades at a low single-digit multiple of sales and a mid-single-digit EV/EBITDA on FY2025 results. The valuation is best framed around order momentum, margin execution, and the \$6.00 analyst consensus target.

- **Bull Case — \$6.50–\$7.00:** Industrial Solutions and Gearing orders sustain 20%+ YoY growth, Heavy Fabrications stabilizes, and EBITDA margins expand to ~7–8% on \$150M+ revenue. Debt paydown continues, and the micro-cap re-rates toward the upper end of sell-side targets. This implies a ~44–55% upside from current prices.
- **Base Case — \$5.00–\$6.00:** Revenue lands in the guided \$140–150M range, Adjusted EBITDA holds around \$8–10M, and the company stays mildly profitable. The \$6.00 analyst consensus acts as the base-case anchor, with the stock reaching that level as H2 2026 orders convert. This implies ~11–33% upside.
- **Bear Case — \$2.50–\$3.00:** Wind capex collapses further, steel costs spike, and a key gas-turbine OEM customer pushes out orders. EBITDA contracts back toward breakeven, net debt/EBITDA rises above 3x, and the company is forced to raise equity. The stock would re-test its 52-week low range.

## 5. Risk Factors

- **Customer Concentration / OEM Exposure:** A meaningful share of Industrial Solutions revenue is tied to a small number of gas-turbine OEM and service customers. A push-out or cancellation from one anchor customer would materially hurt backlog and EBITDA.
- **Commodity and Steel Cost Volatility:** Heavy Fabrications and Gearing are exposed to steel, aluminum, and energy input costs. Margin compression from input spikes is difficult to pass through quickly in fixed-price or semi-annual contracts.
- **Cyclical Wind Capex and PTC Uncertainty:** Heavy Fabrications remains tied to wind tower demand, which is sensitive to PTC policy, interest rates, and utility capex budgets. A slowdown here offsets gas-turbine strength.
- **Thin Margins and Small Scale:** A \$150M-revenue manufacturer with mid-single-digit EBITDA margins has little room for error. One bad project, warranty claim, or customer dispute can erase a quarter's profit.
- **Leverage and Liquidity:** Net debt at 1.6x EBITDA is not alarming, but it is still meaningful for a micro-cap with volatile cash flow. If EBITDA contracts, liquidity could tighten and an equity raise becomes more likely.
- **Micro-Cap Liquidity and Volatility:** Low float, limited analyst coverage, and retail-driven price action mean BWEN can swing 10–20% on thin news. Position sizing should reflect this.

After a rough period, orders are growing again — especially for gas-turbine and gearing products — and the company is expected to do roughly \$140–150M in sales this year.
