# ANET — Arista Networks, Inc.

Arista Networks is the leading merchant-silicon data-center Ethernet switching vendor and a core beneficiary of the AI back-end networking build-out. The company recently reported Q2 2026 revenue of approximately \$3.04B and guided Q3 2026 to ~\$3.3B, with FY2026 revenue guidance lifted to ~\$12.6B (~40% year-over-year). TTM EPS is roughly \$3.50, implying a ~54x P/E at the current price of \$188.67 — a full valuation near 52-week highs after a strong run. The long-term AI networking tailwind remains intact, but with the stock pricing in much of the near-term upside, we rate it **HOLD** and would wait for a better entry. Key risks include customer concentration, competition from Cisco/Nvidia/InfiniBand, and white-box alternatives.

## Company Overview

**Arista Networks, Inc.** (NYSE: ANET) designs and sells high-performance cloud networking solutions — primarily Ethernet switches and routers — for the world's largest data centers, cloud providers, AI clusters, and enterprise campuses. Founded in 2004 and headquartered in Santa Clara, California, Arista pioneered the use of merchant silicon paired with its own **Extensible Operating System (EOS)** to displace legacy, proprietary networking incumbents.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Arista's hardware switches are differentiated by software. Its single, unmodified EOS image runs across the entire portfolio, while **CloudVision** provides network-wide automation, telemetry, and management.

| Segment | Description | Revenue Driver |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **Cloud and AI data center switching** | 400G/800G leaf-spine and AI back-end fabrics | Largest and fastest-growing; hyperscaler AI clusters |
| **Campus and enterprise** | Wired/wireless campus, routing, cognitive Wi-Fi | Diversification beyond cloud titans |
| **Software and services** | EOS subscriptions, CloudVision, support | Recurring, high-margin, sticky |

The revenue mix is dominated by product sales to a concentrated set of cloud customers, with a growing software and services tail that improves retention and margin stability.

### Competitive Moat

Arista's moat is its **software architecture**. EOS uses a modular, self-healing state database (NetDB/Sysdb) that runs identically from top-of-rack switches to the largest spine, giving operators one automation model across the fleet. Combined with merchant-silicon agility — Arista is typically first to market on new Broadcom Tomahawk and Jericho generations — this delivers superior performance-per-watt and lower operating cost. Deep integration into hyperscaler and AI-cluster operations creates high switching costs once a fabric is deployed.

### Management

Co-founder **Jayshree Ullal** (Chair) and **Todd Nightingale** (CEO) lead the company; founder and CTO **Ken Duda** drives the EOS software vision. The founder-led technical culture and disciplined operating model have produced consistent market-share gains and margin expansion against larger incumbents.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **AI back-end Ethernet adoption.** Hyperscalers are increasingly standardizing AI training and inference clusters on Ethernet rather than InfiniBand. Arista's software-defined platform positions it to capture a disproportionate share of this spending.

2. **Raised FY2026 revenue guidance.** Management lifted the full-year outlook to ~\$12.6B (~40% YoY), a clear signal of backlog strength and AI-cluster demand visibility.

3. **800G and 1.6T Ethernet cycles.** The transition from 400G to 800G — and eventually 1.6T — drives higher port counts and average selling prices per AI deployment.

4. **Campus and enterprise diversification.** Growth in wired/wireless campus and routing reduces reliance on the largest cloud customers and adds higher-margin, stickier revenue.

5. **Software and CloudVision attach.** Rising EOS subscription and CloudVision attach rates build a recurring, high-margin base that improves revenue visibility.

6. **Capital returns.** Strong free cash flow funds buybacks that compound per-share value on top of organic growth.

## Risk Factors

### Business Risks

- **Customer concentration (critical):** Historically, Microsoft and Meta have accounted for a large share of revenue. Any budget cut, architectural shift, or supplier change at either would materially dent growth.
- **White-box and in-house switching:** Hyperscalers can build or buy commodity switches running open network operating systems, pressuring Arista's premium positioning over time.
- **Competition:** Nvidia (InfiniBand and Spectrum-X Ethernet), Cisco, Juniper, and Broadcom-based OEMs all compete for AI and cloud networking budgets.

### Financial Risks

- **Valuation risk:** At ~54x trailing earnings and near 52-week highs, any growth deceleration or margin miss could trigger a sharp multiple reset.
- **Gross-margin dilution:** Large concentrated cloud deals carry lower margins; a mix shift toward hyperscaler volume could pressure the gross-margin line.
- **Component and tariff costs:** Supply-chain dynamics and tariffs on networking components can weigh on margins and lead times.

### Macro and Sector Risks

- **AI capex cycle:** The thesis depends on continued hyperscaler AI spending; an ROI disappointment or macro tightening would hit orders and sentiment.
- **Cyclicality:** Networking demand has historically been lumpy; a digestion phase after heavy build-outs is a recurring pattern.
- **Regulatory and export controls:** U.S.-China technology restrictions could limit certain end-market opportunities.

## Readability Pass

**What Arista actually does:** When thousands of AI chips need to talk to each other inside a data center, they need extremely fast, reliable "traffic systems" — network switches. Arista builds some of the best of these for Ethernet, the most common networking standard, and runs them with one smart software system (EOS) that makes a giant network easy to manage. As AI data centers explode in size, more Arista switches are needed to connect the chips.

**What the numbers say:** Arista is expected to sell roughly \$12.6B of gear and software in 2026, up about 40% from 2025. It keeps roughly 64 cents of gross profit on every dollar of sales and has no debt. It also generates several billion dollars of free cash flow each year.

**What the ~54x P/E means:** Investors pay about \$54 today for every \$1 Arista earned over the last year. That is expensive and only pays off if the company keeps growing fast. The good news is that growth is still accelerating; the risk is that the stock could fall hard if growth slows or the market re-rates AI stocks lower.

**What could go wrong:** Two big customers — Microsoft and Meta — have historically bought a large share of Arista's products. If either builds its own switches or cuts spending, Arista's growth would take a serious hit. The stock is also expensive, so any disappointment could lead to a sharp pullback.

**Key terms explained:**

- **Ethernet:** The standard way computers talk to each other over wired networks.
- **Leaf-spine fabric:** A modern data-center network design where every switch connects to many others, so no single point slows things down.
- **EOS (Extensible Operating System):** Arista's software that runs on all of its switches, making them easier to automate and manage.
- **CloudVision:** Arista's network management platform that watches and controls the entire network from one place.
- **Merchant silicon:** Off-the-shelf networking chips from vendors like Broadcom, rather than chips designed in-house.
