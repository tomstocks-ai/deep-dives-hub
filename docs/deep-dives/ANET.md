# ANET — Arista Networks, Inc.

Arista Networks is the merchant-silicon leader in high-speed data center Ethernet and one of the clearest beneficiaries of the AI infrastructure build-out. FY2025 revenue grew 28.6% to \$9.0B with 64% gross margins, ~43% operating margins, and roughly \$4B of free cash flow on a debt-free balance sheet. As hyperscalers increasingly standardize AI back-end fabrics on Ethernet rather than InfiniBand, Arista's software-defined switching platform is positioned to keep compounding. At ~\$169.71 (2026-07-29) the ~58x trailing multiple is full but underpinned by durable growth and elite profitability. Rated **BUY**, with the main risk being customer concentration in Microsoft and Meta.

## Company Overview

**Arista Networks, Inc.** (NYSE: ANET) designs and sells cloud networking solutions — primarily high-performance Ethernet switches and routers — for the world's largest data centers, cloud providers, AI clusters, and enterprise campuses. Founded in 2004 and headquartered in Santa Clara, California, Arista pioneered the use of merchant silicon (chips from Broadcom and others) paired with its own **Extensible Operating System (EOS)** to displace legacy, proprietary networking incumbents.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Arista sells hardware switches whose value is unlocked by software. Its differentiation is a single, unmodified EOS image running across the entire portfolio, plus **CloudVision** for network-wide automation, telemetry, and management.

| Segment | Description | Revenue Driver |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **Cloud and AI data center switching** | 400G/800G leaf-spine and AI back-end fabrics | Largest and fastest-growing; hyperscaler AI clusters |
| **Campus and enterprise** | Wired/wireless campus, routing, cognitive Wi-Fi | Diversification beyond cloud titans |
| **Software and services** | EOS subscriptions, CloudVision, support | Recurring, high-margin, sticky |

The revenue mix is dominated by product sales to a concentrated set of cloud customers, with a growing high-margin software and services tail that improves retention.

### Competitive Moat

Arista's moat is its **software architecture**. EOS is a single binary with a modular, self-healing state database (NetDB/Sysdb) that runs identically from top-of-rack switches to the largest spine, giving operators one automation model across the fleet. Combined with merchant-silicon agility — Arista is often first to market on new Broadcom Tomahawk and Jericho generations — this delivers superior performance-per-watt and lower operating cost. Deep integration into hyperscaler and AI-cluster operations creates high switching costs once a fabric is deployed.

### Management

Co-founder **Jayshree Ullal** (Chair, and long-time CEO) built Arista into a networking powerhouse; **Todd Nightingale** serves as CEO, with founder and CTO **Ken Duda** driving the EOS software vision. The founder-led technical culture and disciplined operating model have produced consistent margin expansion and market-share gains against larger incumbents.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

FY2025 was a record year, capped by a strong Q4 and further acceleration into 2026.

| Metric | FY2025 | YoY Change |
|--------|--------|------------|
| **Revenue** | \$9.01B | **+28.6%** |
| **GAAP Gross Margin** | 64.1% | Flat vs 64.1% FY2024 |
| **GAAP Net Income** | \$3.51B | +23% vs \$2.85B |
| **GAAP EPS (diluted)** | \$2.75 | vs \$2.23 FY2024 |
| **Q4 2025 Revenue** | \$2.49B | +28.9% YoY |

Momentum strengthened in **Q1 2026**, with revenue of \$2.71B, up 35.1% year over year and above the \$2.6B guide. Operating margin expanded from 34.9% in FY2022 to roughly 42.8% in FY2025 — evidence of strong operating leverage as revenue scales. Q4 2025 marked the first quarter in company history with non-GAAP net income above \$1.0B.

**Quality of earnings:** The gap between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings is modest and driven mainly by stock-based compensation. Gross margins in the low-to-mid 60s reflect the mix between high-volume cloud deals (lower margin) and software/enterprise (higher margin); management guides margins down slightly during large AI ramps, which is a healthy sign of winning volume business.

| **Total Debt** | None (debt-free) |

The balance sheet is fortress-like: no debt, roughly \$8.3B in cash and marketable securities, and consistent multi-billion-dollar free cash flow. This funds R&D, buybacks, and the working capital needed to secure long-lead components for AI deployments.

### Cash Flow

Arista converts earnings to cash efficiently — the fabless, software-led model keeps capital intensity low. Annual free cash flow of roughly \$4B provides ample capacity for share repurchases, which compound EPS on top of organic growth. Inventory and purchase commitments rise ahead of AI ramps, a lead indicator of forward demand rather than a red flag.

## Growth Catalysts

1. **Ethernet displacing InfiniBand in AI back ends.** The industry is coalescing around Ethernet (and the Ultra Ethernet Consortium) for scale-out AI fabrics. Arista's leadership positions it to capture a disproportionate share of AI networking spend, targeted at \$3.5B in 2026.

2. **800G ramp and 1.6T on the horizon.** The transition from 400G to 800G — and eventually 1.6T — drives higher port counts and ASPs per cluster, expanding revenue per AI deployment.

3. **Campus and enterprise diversification.** Growth in wired/wireless campus and routing reduces reliance on the largest cloud customers and adds higher-margin, stickier revenue.

4. **Software and CloudVision attach.** Rising EOS subscription and CloudVision attach rates build a recurring, high-margin base that improves visibility and retention.

5. **Capital returns.** Roughly \$4B of annual free cash flow funds buybacks that compound per-share value on top of organic growth.

## Risk Factors

### Business Risks

- **Customer concentration (critical):** Microsoft was ~26% of FY2025 revenue and Meta ~16% — roughly 42% combined. A budget cut, architectural shift, or supplier change at either would materially dent growth.
- **White-box and in-house switching:** Hyperscalers can build or buy commodity switches running open network operating systems, pressuring Arista's premium positioning over time.
- **Competition:** Nvidia (InfiniBand and Spectrum-X Ethernet), Cisco, Juniper, and Broadcom-based OEMs all compete for AI and cloud networking budgets.

### Financial Risks

- **Valuation risk:** At ~58x trailing earnings, any growth deceleration or margin miss could trigger a sharp multiple reset.
- **Gross-margin dilution:** Large concentrated cloud deals carry lower margins; a mix shift toward hyperscaler volume could pressure the gross-margin line.
- **Tariffs and component costs:** Supply-chain and tariff dynamics on networking components can weigh on margins and lead times.

### Macro and Sector Risks

- **AI capex cycle:** The thesis depends on continued hyperscaler AI spending; an ROI disappointment or macro tightening would hit orders and sentiment.
- **Cyclicality:** Networking demand has historically been lumpy; a digestion phase after heavy build-outs is a recurring pattern.
- **Regulatory and export controls:** U.S.-China technology restrictions could limit certain end-market opportunities.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY**

Arista is a high-quality compounder at the center of the AI networking build-out. FY2025 revenue of \$9.0B (+28.6%), ~43% operating margins, roughly \$4B in free cash flow, and a debt-free balance sheet combine with a genuine software moat (EOS/CloudVision) and a leadership position in AI-grade Ethernet. Q1 2026 acceleration to +35% and management's \$11.5B revenue and \$3.5B AI-networking targets support continued growth.

**Position sizing:** 3–5% core position in a growth-oriented portfolio. Higher quality and lower binary risk than most AI-infrastructure names, but concentration and valuation warrant discipline.

**Entry strategy:** Near \$169.71 the stock trades in the middle of the base-case range. Accumulate on weakness toward \$150–160; scale in rather than chasing sharp rallies into earnings.

**Stop loss:** A close below ~\$135 (near the top of the bear range) would signal the AI-networking narrative is breaking and warrant reassessment.

## Readability Pass

**What Arista actually does:** When thousands of AI chips need to talk to each other inside a data center, they need extremely fast, reliable "traffic systems" — network switches. Arista builds the best of these for Ethernet, the most common networking standard, and runs them with one smart software system (EOS) that makes a giant network easy to manage. As AI data centers explode in size, more Arista switches are needed to connect all the chips.

**What the numbers say:** Arista sold \$9.0B of gear and software in 2025, up nearly 29% in a year, and keeps about 43 cents of operating profit on every revenue dollar — unusually high. It has no debt, roughly \$8.3B in cash, and generates about \$4B of spare cash annually.

**What the ~58x P/E means:** Investors pay about \$58 today for every \$1 Arista earned over the last year. That is expensive and only pays off if the company keeps growing fast. The good news is growth is still accelerating (+35% early in 2026); the risk is that if growth slows, the stock could fall hard.

**The main risk in plain terms:** Two customers — Microsoft and Meta — buy roughly 42% of Arista's products. If either builds its own switches or cuts spending, Arista's growth would take a serious hit.
