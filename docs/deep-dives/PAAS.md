# PAAS — Pan American Silver Corp.

Pan American Silver is the largest primary silver producer in the Americas and a mid-tier gold producer, with mines in Mexico, Peru, Brazil, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina and Canada, plus a 44% joint-venture interest in the high-grade Juanicipio mine acquired with MAG Silver in September 2025. The business is throwing off cash: H1 2026 attributable free cash flow was \$832M, the balance sheet carries \$1.8B of cash and short-term investments against \$841M of total debt, and management is on track to return up to \$1B to shareholders in 2026. The problem is the tape. Silver peaked near \$121.62/oz on 29 January 2026 and has since fallen roughly 49% to ~\$62/oz, dragging PAAS from a \$69.99 52-week high to \$45.77 (2026-09-28) — a stock that is cheap on consensus earnings but whose direction is still set by the metal.

## Company Overview

**What it does.** Pan American operates a diversified portfolio of silver and gold mines across seven countries in the Americas. The Silver Segment includes La Colorada and Cerro Moro (Mexico, Argentina), Huaron (Peru), San Vicente (Bolivia, 95% owned) and the 44%-owned Juanicipio JV with Fresnillo in Mexico. The Gold Segment includes Jacobina (Brazil), El Peñón and Minera Florida (Chile), Timmins (Canada), Shahuindo (Peru) and Dolores (Mexico). The company also holds a 100% interest in the Escobal mine in Guatemala, which has been suspended since 2017, and the undeveloped Navidad silver project in Argentina.

**Revenue mix.** Q2 2026 attributable revenue of \$1.27B came from silver, gold, zinc, lead and copper. Gold is the larger revenue line by dollar value — 165.9 thousand attributable gold ounces at a realized \$4,402/oz versus 6.47 million silver ounces at a realized \$70.97/oz — which is why the stock is best understood as a levered silver *thesis* funded by a gold *cash flow* base.

**Moat.**

- **Scale and reserve life:** proven and probable reserves of ~511.1 million ounces of silver and ~6.3 million ounces of gold as at 30 June 2026 — one of the largest primary silver reserve bases in the sector.
- **Juanicipio:** the MAG Silver acquisition (closed September 2025) added 44% of one of the world's highest-grade, lowest-cost silver mines, which is the main reason Silver Segment AISC fell to \$12.64/oz in H1 2026 against guidance of \$15.75–\$18.25/oz for the full year.
- **Diversification:** ten-plus operating mines across seven jurisdictions limits single-asset risk, though it raises the aggregate political-risk count.
- **Growth pipeline owned outright:** the La Colorada Skarn project, the Timmins Camp Project and Escobal are all company-controlled optionality that does not require acquisitions.

**Management.** President and CEO Michael Steinmann has pivoted the company from acquisition-led growth (Tahoe, Yamana, MAG) to capital returns and organic project development. In May 2026 the board adopted an enhanced shareholder return framework targeting 35–40% of annual attributable free cash flow, with roughly \$305M of dividends and up to ~\$700M of buybacks in 2026. Roughly 7.3 million shares had been repurchased through 11 August 2026 at an average of \$49.22.

### Balance sheet and cash flow

- **Cash and short-term investments:** \$1.8B at 30 June 2026, including \$97M attributable to the 44% Juanicipio interest.
- **Total debt:** \$841M, primarily senior notes and lease obligations. The revolving credit facility is undrawn.
- **Liquidity:** \$3.2B total, after the July 2026 renewal that doubled the five-year senior unsecured revolver to \$1.5B with a \$750M accordion.
- **Net cash:** roughly \$1.0B — unusual for a precious-metals producer of this size and the key reason the La Colorada Skarn build can be self-funded.
- **Attributable free cash flow:** \$488M in Q1 2026 and \$344M in Q2 2026, for \$832M in H1, net of \$205M of taxes paid in Q2 alone.
- **Shares outstanding:** 417.07 million at 30 June 2026, down from 421.85 million at year-end 2025 on buybacks.

## Growth Catalysts

- **La Colorada Skarn.** The revised PEA (24 March 2026) contemplates a 15,000 tpd plant, a 37-year mine life, average silver production of 15.8 Moz per year over 2034–2038 incremental to the existing vein mine, and Silver Segment AISC of *negative* \$22.67/oz over that period thanks to zinc and lead by-product credits. After-tax NPV(5%) is \$2.6B with a 17% IRR at \$45/oz silver, rising to \$5.2B and 25% at \$75/oz. Initial capital is \$1.9B over six years, expected to be funded from operating cash flow. The 588 Decline first cut was completed in early August 2026, and engineering for the material handling system and ventilation shafts goes to the board in H2 2026.
- **Juanicipio at full contribution.** 2026 is the first full year of the 44% JV interest. It is the principal reason H1 Silver Segment AISC of \$12.64/oz sits below the full-year guidance range, and it converts directly into dividends and attributable free cash flow.
- **Timmins Camp Project.** The board approved a \$146M first phase in May 2026 to extend the Bell Creek shaft and drive two exploration drifts to the Vogel and Samson deposits. A PEA is expected in H1 2027.
- **Capital returns compounding.** Up to \$1B of 2026 returns against a ~\$19.1B market cap, with ~7.3 million shares already retired in 2026 at an average of \$49.22 — below the current price, so the buyback has been accretive to date.
- **Escobal optionality.** The ILO 169 consultation with the Xinka continues under Guatemala's Ministry of Energy and Mines; a bilateral meeting was held on 7 July 2026. There is no timeline and no restart date, so this is a free option rather than a forecastable catalyst.
- **Silver's structural deficit.** The Silver Institute expects 2026 to mark a sixth consecutive annual market deficit, with retail investment offsetting softness in jewellery, silverware and industrial demand. UBS holds a path of \$70/oz by December 2026 rising to \$80/oz by September 2027; J.P. Morgan models a 2027 average around \$63.90/oz.

## Risk Factors

- **Silver price, first and last.** Silver fell from \$121.62/oz in late January 2026 to ~\$62/oz by late September — roughly 49%. At PAAS's cost structure the business stays profitable well below current spot, but the equity multiple and sentiment do not.
- **Gold execution.** Gold is the bigger revenue line, and it is the segment missing. Jacobina seismicity mitigation and El Peñón grade continuity have pushed FY2026 gold to the low end of guidance with AISC at the high end. Continued slippage would break the base case regardless of what silver does.
- **Cost and tax leakage from higher prices.** Royalties, labour and the cash tax bill all scale with metal prices; the 2026 taxes-paid guide was raised to \$585–\$635M. Upside in the metal converts to free cash flow less efficiently than headline revenue suggests.
- **Jurisdiction concentration in Latin America.** Mexico, Peru, Bolivia, Argentina, Chile, Brazil and Guatemala all carry permitting, tax-reassessment, social-licence and expropriation risk. Bolivia (San Vicente) and Argentina (Cerro Moro, Navidad) are the sharpest.
- **Escobal may never restart.** Nine years suspended, a court-mandated consultation with no deadline, and no restart date. Any valuation that leans on Escobal is leaning on a maybe.
- **La Colorada Skarn execution.** A \$1.9B, six-year underground build with shaft sinking, a new plant and new tailings facilities, on a PEA that includes inferred resources and is preliminary by definition. Permits for the shafts, plant and tailings expansion are still to be sought. Capital overruns or a lower price deck at commissioning would compress the returns.
- **Weather and operational disruption.** El Niño rainstorms affected site access in Chile and Argentina in July and August 2026 and may continue through the year.
- **Currency.** Stronger local currencies against the U.S. dollar raise in-country operating costs; guidance assumes specific rates for MXN, PEN, ARS, BOB, CAD, CLP and BRL.

## Readability Pass

The plain-English version: Pan American digs silver and gold out of the ground in seven countries. It is the biggest silver miner in the Americas and, since buying MAG Silver in late 2025, owns 44% of Juanicipio, an exceptionally rich Mexican silver mine that has pulled the company's silver production costs down to about \$12.64 per ounce in the first half of 2026 — against silver selling for \$70.97 per ounce in the second quarter. That gap is why the company generated \$832M of free cash in six months and is handing up to \$1B back to shareholders this year through dividends and buybacks.

The catch is that silver went almost vertical into January 2026, touching \$121.62 an ounce, and has since fallen by about half to roughly \$62. The stock followed it down, from \$69.99 to \$45.77. On next year's expected earnings that is about nine times profit for a company holding a billion dollars of net cash — cheap, as long as silver does not fall much further. Gold, which is actually the larger source of revenue, is also running below plan at two mines, so the company is leaning on silver to carry the year.

Longer term there is one big project: La Colorada Skarn in Mexico, a \$1.9B, six-year build that studies suggest could produce almost 16 million ounces of silver a year at negative net cost once by-product zinc and lead are credited. First production is in the 2030s, so it is a reason to hold, not a reason this year's stock price moves.

Glossary:

- **AISC (all-in sustaining cost):** the full cost of producing one ounce, including mining, processing, royalties and the capital needed to keep production flat. A *negative* AISC means by-product metals more than pay for the primary metal.
- **Attributable:** the company's own share of production or cash flow after accounting for partners — for example 44% of Juanicipio and 95% of San Vicente.
- **By-product credit:** revenue from secondary metals (gold, zinc, lead, copper) subtracted from the cost of the primary metal.
- **PEA (preliminary economic assessment):** an early-stage study that may include inferred resources. It is not a reserve statement and carries no guarantee of being realised.
- **P and P reserves:** proven and probable reserves — the portion of the deposit demonstrated to be economically mineable. PAAS reports ~511.1 Moz silver and ~6.3 Moz gold as at 30 June 2026.
- **ILO 169 consultation:** the indigenous-consultation process a Guatemalan court requires before the Escobal mine can restart.
- **NPV(5%):** the value today of a project's future after-tax cash flows, discounted at 5% a year.
