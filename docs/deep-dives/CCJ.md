# CCJ — Cameco Corporation

Cameco is the largest Western-listed uranium producer and the only company that spans the full non-Russian nuclear fuel cycle at scale: tier-one mines in northern Saskatchewan and Kazakhstan, the world's largest commercial uranium refinery and conversion complex, and a 49% stake in Westinghouse Electric, whose AP1000 is the only fully designed, licensed and deployed Generation III+ reactor. At \$87.04 (2026-09-28) the stock has round-tripped a year of gains — up just 3.1% over twelve months, 36% below its \$135.24 52-week high, and trading beneath both its 50-day (\$94.79) and 200-day (\$105.79) moving averages — even as the uranium long-term contract price set a nominal record of \$96.50/lb in August 2026. That gap between a deteriorating reported earnings line and improving commodity fundamentals is the entire investment debate.

## Company Overview

Cameco operates three reporting segments.

**Uranium** (C\$2.87B of FY2025 revenue, 83% of the total) covers exploration, mining, milling and the purchase and resale of uranium concentrate. The core assets are McArthur River/Key Lake and Cigar Lake in northern Saskatchewan — among the highest-grade uranium deposits on earth — plus a 40% interest in JV Inkai, the Kazakh in-situ recovery operation. In Q2 2026 Cameco closed an agreement to increase its ownership interest in Cigar Lake, consolidating control of a proven tier-one asset. Guidance for 2026 is 19.5–21.5 million pounds of U3O8 on Cameco's share, with JV Inkai on track for 10.4 million pounds on a 100% basis and a 4.2 million pound purchase allocation to Cameco.

**Fuel Services** (C\$562M of FY2025 revenue) refines, converts and fabricates uranium: UF6 conversion, UO2 conversion and CANDU fuel bundles. The Blind River refinery is the largest commercial uranium refinery in the world and Port Hope is the only UF6 conversion facility in Canada. Annual production guidance is 13–14 million kgU. This segment is the least-discussed part of the story and arguably the most strategically scarce — Western conversion capacity is a genuine bottleneck independent of the uranium price.

**Westinghouse** is held as a 49% equity-accounted investment alongside Brookfield, acquired in 2023 for roughly US\$2.1 billion. Westinghouse technology underpins 57% of the global operating fleet of 417 reactors, generating a large recurring Operating Plants business (fuel, services, outages) plus a New Plants pipeline management sizes at up to 91 potential AP1000 deployments. Because the stake is equity-accounted, Westinghouse revenue (C\$3.46B in FY2025) is eliminated from consolidated revenue and only Cameco's share of net earnings flows through the income statement — which is why the reported P/E is a poor guide to the business.

The moat is jurisdictional and physical rather than technological: grade, licensed and permitted mills, the only meaningful non-Russian conversion chain, and long-duration utility relationships. Tim Gitzel remains CEO with Grant Isaac as President and COO; the management team's defining choice over the last cycle has been contracting discipline — deliberately holding back uncommitted supply rather than locking in volume at low prices.

## Financial Analysis

All company figures below are in Canadian dollars, as reported under IFRS. Cameco's share price and market capitalisation are in US dollars, and the FX rate has been running near 1.42 CAD per USD.

FY2025 was the strongest year in the company's modern history:

| Gross profit | 1,263M | 1,063M | +18.8% |
| Gross margin | 36.3% | 33.9% | +240bp |

Adjusted net earnings reached C\$627M, up 114%. Year-end cash was C\$1,214M against C\$1,013M total debt — a net cash position of C\$202M.

**2026 has been a step backwards on reported numbers, by design and by accident.** Q2 2026 revenue fell 7% year over year to C\$814M (US\$588M), which still beat the consensus revenue estimate by roughly 10%. Net earnings were C\$25M, adjusted net earnings C\$77M and adjusted EBITDA C\$391M. Adjusted EPS of C\$0.18 came in at less than half the C\$0.38 consensus — a severe miss. Uranium sales volumes fell 18% to 7.1 million pounds, consistent with management's deliberately lower 2026 delivery plan; average realized prices in both uranium and fuel services rose.

The bulk of the earnings shortfall was Westinghouse, not uranium. Cameco's share of Westinghouse swung to a C\$10M net loss in Q2 2026 from C\$126M of net earnings a year earlier, and its share of Westinghouse adjusted EBITDA fell to C\$163M from C\$352M. The comparison is distorted: Westinghouse's participation in the Dukovany two-reactor construction project in the Czech Republic contributed roughly US\$170M to Cameco's share of Q2 2025 revenue and adjusted EBITDA — a lumpy project recognition that does not repeat. Strip that out and the underlying Westinghouse trajectory is far less alarming than the headline, but it is also not visibly growing.

The core uranium segment held up. First-half uranium earnings before taxes were C\$528M versus C\$509M in 2025, and first-half uranium adjusted EBITDA was C\$676M versus C\$641M — modest growth despite lower volumes, because realized prices from market-related contracts improved. Fuel services was the soft spot, with Q1 earnings before taxes of C\$44M against C\$68M a year earlier on a lower average realized price.

Trailing twelve months to 30 June 2026: revenue C\$3,475M (-2.7%), gross margin 35.1%, net income C\$355M, diluted EPS C\$0.81 (-33.6%), free cash flow C\$556M (-38.3%). Cash was C\$1,113M at quarter end against C\$1,222M of debt plus an undrawn C\$1.0B revolver, so the balance sheet has flipped to a small net debt position of about C\$109M — still negligible against a US\$38B market capitalisation. Cameco also received US\$124M net of withholdings from JV Inkai as a dividend on 2025 performance.

Uranium inventory stood at 8.7 million pounds on 30 June 2026 at an average cost of C\$58.05/lb, against second-quarter purchases made at C\$91.40/lb (US\$66.60/lb). The dividend is a rounding error: C\$0.24 per share for FY2025, a 0.2% yield. Cameco is not a yield story.

Operationally, 2026 has been messy. Spring flooding along the northern Saskatchewan supply routes disrupted haulage, there were temporary unplanned disruptions at Key Lake and McArthur River during Q2 and at Cigar Lake after quarter-end, and annual production guidance was nonetheless reaffirmed — which implies the second half must carry more of the load.

## Valuation

On reported earnings Cameco looks indefensible: roughly 152x trailing P/E, 97x price to free cash flow, 15.5x price to sales, and a forward P/E near 61x. TIKR data shows the next-twelve-month P/E at 97.5x at the end of March 2026, a 12-month peak of 110.8x, a post-Q2 low of 59.5x and 65.5x on 25 September. GuruFocus puts its GF Value at \$68.24 and flags the stock as overvalued.

Earnings multiples are the wrong lens here for two reasons. First, 2026 deliveries were deliberately held low under the contracting strategy, so the denominator is a trough the company chose. Second, the single largest asset — 49% of Westinghouse — contributes almost nothing to reported EPS because equity-method income absorbs depreciation, financing costs, taxes and purchase-accounting effects.

A sum-of-the-parts frame is more honest, and it is where the range of outcomes gets wide. On 18 September 2026 Bloomberg reported that Westinghouse is targeting a US listing at a valuation above US\$50B, with a public filing possible as soon as October. If that is equity value and Cameco retains 49%, the look-through stake is worth more than US\$24.5B — roughly 64% of Cameco's entire market capitalisation, before a single pound of uranium. Against that, Reuters citing the Korea Economic Daily reported a 15% Westinghouse stake at US\$2.25–3.0B, implying a US\$15–20B enterprise and a 49% stake worth only US\$7.4–9.8B. That is a 2.5x spread on the most important number in the story, and the market is clearly not paying the high mark — the IPO report produced a one-day 3.6% bump and no sustained re-rating.

The uranium and fuel services businesses on their own generate first-half adjusted EBITDA of roughly C\$730M, annualising near C\$1.5B (about US\$1.05B). At 12–15x that is US\$12–16B of enterprise value, consistent with a mid-cycle producer holding scarce Western assets and a book of over 28 million pounds of average annual deliveries contracted for the next five years.

Scenario targets:

- **Bull case: \$150–180.** Westinghouse prices its IPO at or above the reported US\$50B mark and firm AP1000 orders convert from the DOE, Korean and Saudi pipelines. Long-term uranium pushes through \$100/lb, 2029–2030 deliveries reprice into market-related mechanisms, and uranium plus fuel services adjusted EBITDA scales toward C\$2.5B while the Westinghouse stake is marked near US\$25B.
- **Base case: \$105–125.** Long-term uranium holds \$95/lb or better, 2026 production guidance is met, uranium adjusted EBITDA grows at a high-single-digit pace as realized prices catch up to contract terms, and Westinghouse lists at a US\$30–40B valuation — a real step up from the US\$2.1B cost basis without validating the headline. This range straddles the S&P Global consensus target of \$127.77 from 21 analysts and sits well below MarketBeat's \$148.68 average across 17 analysts.
- **Bear case: \$62–75.** Westinghouse prices near the Korean-press-implied US\$15–20B, or the IPO is shelved; AP1000 opportunities stay a pipeline rather than a backlog; Saskatchewan operational disruptions force a guidance cut; and uranium term prices stall as utilities step back. On trough numbers Cameco is a 30x-plus EBITDA producer with an equity stake nobody will underwrite, and the GF Value of \$68.24 becomes the reference point.

## Growth Catalysts

**The Westinghouse IPO is the near-term event that matters.** A public registration statement — possible as soon as October 2026 — would deliver standalone Westinghouse financial statements and, depending on disclosure, contracted backlog and order detail. Cameco currently gets almost no credit for this asset. A filing that substantiates the pipeline is the fastest available path to a re-rating; a filing that reveals thin firm orders removes the option value.

**AP1000 order conversion.** The US Department of Energy has issued a US\$17.5B conditional loan commitment to finance long-lead equipment for up to 10 AP1000 reactors in the United States. That is financing, not a signed reactor order. South Korea is discussing six AP1000s inside its US investment package. The US and Saudi Arabia signed a 123 civil-nuclear cooperation agreement in July 2026. Management sizes the addressable pipeline at up to 91 AP1000 opportunities and estimates Westinghouse could capture 40–45% of project value at roughly 20% EBITDA margins — management estimates, explicitly not contracted revenue. Any one definitive agreement would change the market's willingness to underwrite the rest.

**Record long-term uranium pricing feeding through contracts.** The long-term price closed August 2026 at \$96.50/lb, the highest in the series and above the previous nominal record of \$95/lb from 2007, and it has not declined in a single month since July 2025. Spot traded near \$89–90/lb in September. Because Cameco's realized price lags the market through its contract book, today's term prices show up in 2028–2030 revenue rather than 2026. Deliveries are contracted above the five-year average in 2026–2028 and below it in 2029–2030 — precisely the years with the most upside exposure left uncommitted.

**Data centre and sovereign demand.** Hyperscaler power agreements, the Canadian federal Nuclear Energy Strategy published in June 2026, and the security-of-supply motive driving utilities to contract off-market are all durable rather than cyclical demand drivers. Cameco's own commentary points to increased on- and off-market contracting activity in the first half.

**Cigar Lake consolidation and the eVinci microreactor.** The increased Cigar Lake interest adds pounds from an asset Cameco already operates. Westinghouse achieved initial criticality on its eVinci microreactor in the third quarter of 2026, an early but real technical milestone in the small-modular segment, alongside the AP300.

**Q3 2026 results on 30 October 2026** are the next scheduled test of whether second-half production can recover the ground lost to flooding and unplanned outages.

## Risk Factors

**Valuation is the dominant risk.** At 61x forward earnings and 97x free cash flow, Cameco requires the growth story to deliver. A commodity producer priced for structural scarcity has no margin of safety if either the commodity or the equity-stake narrative slips.

**The Westinghouse valuation is a genuine binary.** The public marks span US\$15–50B. If the IPO prices at the low end, roughly a quarter of Cameco's market capitalisation rests on an asset worth a third of what bulls assume. An IPO priced well below the reported headline would reset the math on the company's most debated asset, and it could happen within weeks.

**Equity-method earnings are noisy and currently negative.** Westinghouse posted a C\$56M net loss on Cameco's share over the first half of 2026 against C\$64M of earnings a year earlier. Investors cannot easily distinguish operating deterioration from project timing and purchase accounting, and that opacity depresses the multiple the market will pay.

**Operational risk in northern Saskatchewan is recurring, not exceptional.** Spring flooding, road closures, and unplanned disruptions at Key Lake, McArthur River and Cigar Lake all occurred in 2026. Guidance was maintained, which loads the second half. A miss on the 19.5–21.5 million pound range would land on a stock already 36% off its high.

**Contracting discipline cuts both ways.** Holding back uncommitted supply preserves upside but depresses near-term deliveries and earnings — exactly what 2026 demonstrates. If the uranium price mean-reverts before those pounds are committed, the patience is uncompensated.

**Commodity and currency exposure.** Uranium is a thin, opaque, contract-negotiated market. Spot moved little over the past month and is up only about 7% year over year despite the record term price, and miner equities have de-rated far more than the commodity. Cameco reports in Canadian dollars while its shares trade in US dollars, adding a translation layer to every comparison.

**Concentration and geopolitics.** JV Inkai exposes Cameco to Kazakh jurisdiction and export logistics. Russian enrichment displacement is a tailwind, but any normalisation of Russian supply or a policy reversal on nuclear support in a major market would compress the scarcity premium.

**Technical posture is weak.** The stock sits below both its 50-day and 200-day moving averages, with the 50-day beneath the 200-day — a configuration that has persisted since the Q2 report and offers no trend support.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Bull \$150–180 / Base \$105–125 / Bear \$62–75.

The case rests on three things a reported P/E cannot capture: a record and still-rising long-term uranium price that has not yet reached Cameco's realized revenue; a contracted book of over 28 million pounds of average annual deliveries with the least-committed years being the ones furthest out; and a Westinghouse stake bought for US\$2.1B that the market is presently valuing at a fraction of even conservative independent marks. A 36% drawdown from the high, on a quarter whose miss was driven overwhelmingly by a non-recurring prior-year project comparison, is where this kind of asset should be accumulated rather than sold.

**Position sizing.** Treat this as a core holding within a nuclear or critical-minerals sleeve, not a trade — but size for the binary. A 2–4% portfolio position is appropriate for most allocations, and building it in thirds rather than at once is the correct response to an IPO filing that could land in October and cut either way.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is below its 50-day (\$94.79) and 200-day (\$105.79) averages with the 52-week low at \$77.70. Initiate in the mid-\$80s, add on any retest of \$78–80, and reserve the final tranche for after the Westinghouse registration statement is public and readable. Chasing a post-filing gap above \$100 gives up most of the risk-adjusted edge.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$74 — decisively through the 52-week low — invalidates the thesis that the Q2 miss was timing rather than deterioration. For a thesis-based exit rather than a price-based one, the trigger is a Westinghouse IPO priced at or below a US\$20B valuation, or a cut to 2026 production guidance.

**Catalyst calendar.** Westinghouse public registration statement, possibly October 2026. Q3 2026 results on 30 October 2026. Monthly UxC and TradeTech long-term price prints. FY2026 results and 2027 guidance in February 2027. Any definitive AP1000 order tied to the DOE programme, the Korean package or the Saudi 123 agreement — unscheduled and the highest-impact of the set.

**On technicals.** Momentum is unambiguously negative: price under both major moving averages, 50-day below 200-day, and no reclaim of the pre-Q2 level since July. That argues for patience on entry rather than against the position. Support is the \$77.70 low; resistance is the 50-day near \$95 and then the pre-Q2 shelf around \$105.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Analyst sentiment is constructively bullish with an unusually wide dispersion.** S&P Global polls 21 analysts at a consensus "Buy" and an average target of \$127.77, about 47% above the current price, with a low of \$83.99 and a high of \$170.86. MarketBeat's 17-analyst panel averages \$148.68 with a \$97 low and a \$202 high and reads the consensus as "Moderate Buy". TipRanks shows a "Strong Buy" consensus on a tighter 17% upside. Recent actions skew positive: Jefferies initiated at Buy with a \$138 target in early September 2026, RBC set C\$184 on the Toronto line on 24 September, Scotiabank raised to \$175 from \$150 in May, and UBS upgraded to Buy explicitly arguing that the 18% one-month and 27% six-month pullback reflected broader market and AI-related sentiment rather than any fundamental change. One initiation came in at \$108. GuruFocus is the notable dissenter, scoring the company 83/100 while calling the price overvalued against a GF Value of \$68.24.

**News tone is positive on the story and negative on the tape.** The September flow is dominated by Westinghouse IPO coverage — Bloomberg's US\$50B report, a WSJ feature on Westinghouse riding the nuclear revival to a listing, and multiple pieces quantifying Cameco's stake at US\$24.5B. Alongside that sits a steady stream of comparison articles positioning Cameco as the quality, cash-generating option against pre-revenue or cash-burning alternatives such as Oklo, USA Rare Earth, MP Materials and TMC. The counterweight is a cluster of "why did Cameco fall again" pieces attributing weakness to risk-off trading and AI-adjacent sentiment rather than fundamentals.

**Retail and social sentiment is patient-bullish but fatigued.** The framing circulating most widely is "a stock can lose one-third of its value without losing one-third of its business" — the dip-buyer's case. Against it, a name that is flat over a year while the underlying commodity sets records tests conviction, and the uranium equity complex has visibly de-rated relative to the commodity, with URA down 18% in a month at one point in July while spot held near \$85.

**Positioning.** Institutional activity is mixed, with visible trimming (Engineers Gate Manager reduced its position in late September) rather than wholesale exit. Beta is 1.01, so the stock is not structurally more volatile than the market despite the drawdown. Options flow data was not available in this session, so no inference is drawn from it.

**Composite sentiment score: 6.5/10** — bullish institutional research, credible structural narrative, weak price action and an unresolved binary event inside the next month. The score is capped by the fact that the most bullish argument depends on a number, the Westinghouse valuation, that ranges by a factor of 2.5 across published sources.

## Readability Pass

Cameco digs up uranium, turns it into reactor fuel, and owns nearly half of Westinghouse, the company whose reactor design most of the world's nuclear plants are built on. The uranium business is doing fine. The price utilities pay under long-term contracts just hit an all-time high of \$96.50 a pound, and Cameco has already sold more than 28 million pounds a year for the next five years — with the most uncommitted volume in 2029 and 2030, the years where high prices matter most.

Two things went wrong this year. Cameco deliberately chose to deliver fewer pounds in 2026, so revenue and profit fell on purpose. And Westinghouse earned a lot less than last year, because last year included a one-off chunk of revenue from a Czech reactor construction project that does not repeat. The second-quarter profit was less than half what analysts expected, and the stock is 36% below its high even though the business is not 36% worse.

The big unresolved question is what Westinghouse is actually worth. Cameco paid about \$2.1 billion for its 49% in 2023. Bloomberg reports Westinghouse wants to go public at more than \$50 billion, which would make Cameco's slice worth over \$24 billion — about two-thirds of the whole company's market value. But a Korean report implies a valuation closer to \$15–20 billion, which would make the slice worth \$7–10 billion. That filing could arrive in October, and it will settle the argument one way or the other.

So: a real business with scarce assets, record-high pricing that has not reached the income statement yet, a very expensive-looking share price on today's earnings, and a coin-flip event in the next few weeks. That is why we rate it BUY but size it modestly and buy it in pieces — bull \$150–180, base \$105–125, bear \$62–75.
