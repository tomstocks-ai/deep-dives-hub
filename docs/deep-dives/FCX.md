# FCX — Freeport-McMoRan Inc.

Freeport-McMoRan (NYSE: FCX) is the world's largest publicly traded copper producer, with Tier 1 assets across Indonesia (Grasberg), North America (Arizona/New Mexico), and South America (Peru/Chile). At \$62.72 (2026-07-28), FCX offers direct leverage to copper prices sitting at record highs on a structural supply deficit driven by electrification, grid modernization, and AI data-center power demand. A strong Q2 2026 beat, an on-schedule Grasberg Block Cave ramp, and a new Indonesia agreement that extends key asset lifespans underpin a **BUY** rating with a **\$70–74** base case.

**Report Date:** July 28, 2026  
**Ticker:** NYSE: FCX  
**Current Price:** \$62.72  
**Market Cap:** ~\$90.2B  
**Recommendation:** BUY (Long-Term Core Holding)

---

## Company Overview

**Business model.** Freeport-McMoRan is a vertically integrated miner whose economics are dominated by copper, with meaningful gold and molybdenum by-product credits. It is the #1 publicly traded copper producer globally and operates world-class, long-life assets on three continents — a scale and diversification unmatched among listed pure-play copper names.

| Segment | Key Assets | Role |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **Indonesia** | Grasberg minerals district (Block Cave, smelting) | High-grade copper + gold |
| **North America** | Morenci, Bagdad, Safford, Sierrita (AZ); Chino, Tyrone (NM) | Copper cathode + leach upside |
| **South America** | Cerro Verde (Peru), El Abra (Chile) | Copper concentrate |

**Revenue mix (approx.):** copper concentrate and cathode ~75%, gold ~15%, molybdenum ~7%, rod and refining ~3%.

**Moat.** FCX's edge is asset quality and cost position. Grasberg's rich gold content generates by-product credits that keep it among the lowest-cost copper mines in the world, while the U.S. leach initiative unlocks copper from existing stockpiles at low capital intensity. New copper supply globally is constrained by decade-long development cycles and declining ore grades, so FCX's installed base of Tier 1 assets is difficult to replicate.

**Management.** CEO Kathleen L. Quirk, a long-tenured FCX executive and architect of the Grasberg underground transition, has framed the current phase as one where "the best lies ahead" and the company is "entering a period of growth" in its Americas business. Strategic priorities: safely restoring and ramping Grasberg mining and smelting, scaling the North America leach initiative toward a ~300M lb/yr run-rate, advancing Safford/Lone Star and Kucing Liar studies, and maintaining a disciplined investment-grade balance sheet alongside shareholder returns.

## Growth Catalysts

**1. Copper structural deficit.** Copper prices are at record highs, driven by electrification (EVs, renewables), grid modernization, and rapidly growing AI data-center power demand — against constrained new mine supply. As the world's largest publicly traded copper producer, FCX has the most direct listed leverage to this deficit.

**2. Grasberg Block Cave ramp.** The Block Cave ramp-up is progressing on schedule and has met expectations following the earlier operational disruption. Management is focused on safely restoring full Grasberg mining and smelting operations; continued milestone delivery is a direct volume and earnings catalyst.

**3. Volume growth trajectory.** Guidance points to copper sales of ~3.1B lbs in FY2026, rising to 3.8B lbs (2027) and 4.1B lbs (2028); gold sales of ~0.65M oz in FY2026, scaling to 1.0M oz (2027) and 1.2M oz (2028). This is a clear multi-year growth ramp, not a flat commodity producer.

**4. North America leach initiative.** The low-capital-intensity leach program targets a ~300M lb/yr copper run-rate from existing stockpiles, with Safford/Lone Star studies and the Kucing Liar project adding further Americas growth optionality.

**5. Indonesia asset-life extension.** A new agreement/MoU in Indonesia extends key asset lifespans, reducing the prior regulatory-risk overhang and improving long-term visibility on Grasberg economics.

**6. Balance sheet and returns.** An investment-grade balance sheet with no significant 2026 maturities supports the \$4.3B capex plan, the base dividend, and performance-based capital returns.

## Risk Factors

**Indonesia political/regulatory risk.** The government is a majority stakeholder through Inalum, and export/permitting risk remains material even after the new asset-life-extension agreement. Terms can shift with the fiscal and political climate.

**Grasberg operational execution.** The Block Cave ramp is complex, and the recovery from the prior operational disruption is still in progress. A fresh disruption — or a stalled ramp — would hit both volumes and sentiment. This is simultaneously a key risk and, as it de-risks on schedule, an improving catalyst.

**Copper price volatility.** FCX's earnings are highly sensitive to copper. A global recession, a deeper China property slowdown (China is ~40% of global copper demand), or faster-than-expected new supply could pressure prices and the stock toward the bear case.

**Capex and cost inflation.** FY2026 capex of ~\$4.3B is substantial; unit-cost inflation or project overruns would compress free cash flow.

**ESG and jurisdictional.** Tailings and water management, community relations in Papua, and currency exposure (IDR, PEN, CLP) remain ongoing risks inherent to large-scale mining.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: FCX digs copper (plus some gold and molybdenum) out of huge, high-quality mines in Indonesia, the U.S., and South America. Copper is the metal that wires up electric cars, power grids, renewables, and AI data centers — and the world is short of it, so prices are at record highs. FCX makes more money when copper is expensive, and right now it is.

The company just had a strong quarter (it earned more than Wall Street expected), its big Indonesian mine (Grasberg) is coming back on track after past problems, and a new deal with Indonesia's government makes the future there more predictable. It plans to dig up a lot more copper over the next few years (about 3.1B pounds this year rising toward 4.1B pounds by 2028), pays a small dividend, and has a healthy balance sheet with no big debts due this year.

The bet: if copper stays strong and the mines ramp as planned, the stock is worth roughly \$70–74 (base case), with upside to \$82–90 if everything goes right. The main risks are a copper price crash (recession or China) and any renewed trouble at Grasberg or with Indonesia's rules — which is why the downside case is \$48–52. On balance, the risk/reward favors owning it: **BUY**.
