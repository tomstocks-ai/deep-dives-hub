# SMR — NuScale Power Corporation

NuScale Power is the developer of the NuScale Power Module (NPM), a 77 MWe light-water small modular reactor and the **only SMR design to receive U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) design certification** — a multi-year regulatory head start over every rival. Trading at \$9.01 (2026-08-04) for a ~\$3.29B market cap, the stock has fallen hard from a \$57.42 52-week high toward its \$7.21 low amid nuclear-sector profit-taking and slipping commercialization timelines. Deployment now runs through ENTRA1 Energy — NuScale's exclusive global partner to develop, own and operate its plants — with the RoPower six-module project in Romania and a growing U.S. data-center pipeline as the near-term test. NuScale ended Q1 2026 with roughly \$1.0B in cash and essentially no debt, but revenue is tiny and declining (~\$31.5M in FY2025) and losses are large. The result is an asymmetric, binary bet. Rating: **SPEC. BUY**.

---

## Company Overview

NuScale Power designs and licenses the **NuScale Power Module (NPM)** — a factory-fabricated, light-water small modular reactor that generates 77 MWe per module. Modules are combined into a **VOYGR** power plant (typically 4, 6, or 12 modules), giving utilities and industrial off-takers scalable, firm, carbon-free baseload power with a smaller footprint and lower up-front capital than a conventional gigawatt-scale reactor.

- **Technology:** A passively-cooled light-water reactor housed with its own steam generator inside a single pressure vessel, submerged in a below-grade water pool. The passive-safety design needs no external power or operator action to shut down and cool — a central regulatory and marketing advantage.
- **Regulatory moat:** NuScale is the **only company with an NRC-certified SMR design** (the 50 MWe standard design was certified in 2023), and it has pursued NRC standard design approval for the uprated 77 MWe module. This certification lead is the company's core competitive advantage.
- **Go-to-market:** Deployment is channeled through **ENTRA1 Energy**, NuScale's exclusive global partner to develop, own and operate NuScale-powered plants. NuScale supplies the technology, modules and services; ENTRA1 carries the project-development and ownership role. This shifts NuScale toward an equipment-and-services supplier model rather than a plant owner.
- **Ownership and leadership:** Founded in 2007 as a spinout from Oregon State University, headquartered in Corvallis/Portland, Oregon. Fluor Corporation remains the majority economic owner, which is why NuScale reports large non-controlling-interest amounts and a dual-class structure. The company listed on the NYSE in 2022 via a SPAC merger.

The 2023 cancellation of the Carbon Free Power Project (the UAMPS/Utah first-of-a-kind plant) was a major setback that reset the commercialization narrative. Since then, NuScale has rebuilt its pipeline around ENTRA1, RoPower in Romania, and rising AI-driven power demand.

## Financial Analysis

NuScale is pre-commercial: its revenue comes from engineering, design and licensing-support work, not from operating reactors. The investment case rests on the balance sheet, the NRC lead, and pipeline conversion rather than the income statement.

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Revenue (FY2025) | \$31.5M | Down ~15% year over year; project-timing-driven, not commercial-scale |
| Revenue growth | -15% | Reflects lumpy design/licensing work between milestones |
| Net income (FY2025) | -\$355.8M | Large development-stage loss (inflated by the Fluor/NCI structure) |
| EPS (diluted) | -\$1.82 | Losses expected until plants are ordered and built |
| Cash and investments | ~\$1.0B | Q1 2026 balance; multi-year runway, essentially debt-free |
| Free cash flow | negative | Ongoing development burn |

The headline is **liquidity: roughly \$1.0B in cash, cash equivalents and short- and long-term investments at Q1 2026, with essentially no debt** — a strong runway that lets NuScale fund development for years without near-term forced dilution. Shares outstanding are ~365M, and beta is a high 2.25. FY2025 revenue of ~\$31.5M was *down* ~15% year over year, a reminder that revenue is milestone-lumpy and not yet a commercial signal. The FY2025 net loss of ~\$355.8M is large and partly reflects the consolidated Fluor-majority (non-controlling-interest) structure rather than pure operating cash burn. The critical financial question is not solvency today but whether cash converts into firm plant orders before the runway shortens.

## Valuation

NuScale trades as a long-dated option on SMR commercialization, not on current earnings. At \$9.01 and a ~\$3.29B market cap, the market is pricing meaningful eventual deployment, discounted heavily for timing and execution risk. Traditional DCF is of limited use pre-commercially, so the scenarios below frame outcomes by milestone achievement. Analyst consensus is **Hold across ~18 analysts with an average 12-month target near \$14.57** (roughly 60% above the current price), spanning a wide \$7 low to \$25 high — Citi at Sell \$7.50, Barclays at Equal Weight \$11, and Truist at Hold \$10.

**Bull case — target \$20–25.** RoPower reaches a positive final investment decision and moves toward construction; ENTRA1 converts its pipeline into firm, financeable U.S. and international orders including AI data-center off-takers; NRC standard design approval for the 77 MWe module clears; and structural AI power demand accelerates SMR procurement. The stock re-rates toward the ~\$25 street high.

**Base case — target \$12–15.** RoPower advances toward FID with modest slippage and ENTRA1 signs initial firm orders while ~\$1.0B cash funds operations without forced dilution. Revenue stays small near-term but the NRC-certified design underpins a multi-year ramp. The stock trades toward the ~\$14.57 consensus.

**Bear case — target \$5–7.** RoPower FID slips again and no binding orders materialize, heavy burn forces dilutive raises, and nuclear-sector profit-taking with a 2.25 beta drives the stock below its \$7.21 52-week low toward the \$7 street low.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Q2 2026 earnings (August 5, 2026)** — the next read on revenue, cash runway, ENTRA1 pipeline progress and RoPower FID timing.
- **RoPower (Romania) final investment decision** — a positive FID on the six-module VOYGR plant would be the first concrete commercial-scale commitment and the single most important de-risking event; it has slipped to late 2026.
- **ENTRA1 order conversion** — turning the development pipeline into firm, financeable orders, particularly with U.S. data-center and industrial off-takers riding the AI power-demand wave.
- **NRC 77 MWe design approval** — standard design approval for the uprated module cements the regulatory lead and improves per-plant economics.
- **U.S. Department of Energy support** — federal financing and policy tailwinds for nuclear, including long-lead-component financing proposals, that lower deployment cost and risk.
- **AI and data-center power demand** — a structural driver creating urgency for firm, carbon-free baseload capacity that SMRs can address.

## Risk Factors

- **Commercialization risk (high).** NuScale has an NRC-certified design but no operating plant and no binding commercial plant order yet. RoPower's FID has already slipped to late 2026; further delays push first revenue out by years.
- **Tiny, declining revenue (high).** FY2025 revenue of ~\$31.5M fell ~15% year over year — the top line is milestone-lumpy and far from commercial scale, so losses persist.
- **Dilution risk (medium-high).** Despite ~\$1.0B liquidity, continued burn could require dilutive equity raises, especially if orders and FIDs slip.
- **Valuation and sentiment volatility (high).** With a beta of 2.25, the stock has round-tripped from a \$57.42 52-week high toward its \$7.21 low; the advanced-nuclear complex (OKLO, SMR, XE) frequently moves 8–10% together on sentiment and profit-taking.
- **Competition (medium).** BWXT, X-energy, Oklo, TerraPower, Kairos and others are pursuing the same SMR and advanced-reactor opportunity, which could erode NuScale's first-mover edge.
- **Structural complexity (medium).** The Fluor-majority, dual-class, ENTRA1-partner structure complicates the financials and the economics that flow to public Class A holders.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** NuScale offers asymmetric, milestone-driven upside for risk-tolerant investors with a multi-year horizon. Its NRC design certification is a genuine, hard-won moat that no competitor shares, its ~\$1.0B cash balance funds the plan for years, and it trades ~60% below the ~\$14.57 consensus target. But the outcome is binary: the thesis depends on converting the pipeline — starting with RoPower's FID — into firm orders, and the stock's 2.25 beta means drawdowns can be severe.

- **Position sizing:** Treat as speculative. Conservative 1–2%, moderate 3–5%, aggressive up to ~5–8% for high-conviction holders.
- **Entry strategy:** With shares near the low end of the range after a steep decline, scale in rather than chase. Consider a partial position at current levels (~\$9) and add on confirmation of a positive RoPower FID or firm ENTRA1 orders. Dollar-cost averaging suits the milestone-gated timeline.
- **Technical context:** Shares sit toward the bottom of the \$7.21–\$57.42 52-week range, below the 50-day (~\$9.96) and 200-day (~\$12.23) moving averages, with the \$7.21 low as key support and the moving averages as overhead resistance. Given the high beta and headline sensitivity, expect wide swings around catalysts; treat RSI extremes on the chart above as context, not signals.
- **Risk management:** A sustained break below the \$7.21 52-week low on a failed FID or dilutive raise would challenge the thesis; size positions so a drawdown into the bear-case \$5–7 range is survivable.

**Catalyst calendar:** Q2 2026 earnings on 2026-08-05; RoPower FID (late 2026); NRC 77 MWe design approval (2026); ENTRA1 order conversions (ongoing); DOE nuclear financing and policy support (ongoing).

## Readability Pass

In plain English: NuScale is trying to build small, factory-made nuclear reactors and sell the technology and services to power plants — increasingly aimed at AI data centers that need lots of clean, always-on electricity. Its big advantage is that it's the only company whose small-reactor design the U.S. regulator (the NRC) has already approved, which is hard to replicate. It doesn't make real money yet — revenue is tiny (about \$31.5M last year) and actually shrank — and it loses a lot, but it has around \$1.0B in the bank and almost no debt, so it can keep going for years. The key question is whether it can turn its pipeline into actual signed orders. The most important near-term event is a "final investment decision" on its Romania project (RoPower), which has been pushed to late 2026. The stock is a rollercoaster: it fell from about \$57 down to about \$9, near its yearly low. Analysts mostly say "hold" with an average target around \$14.57, roughly 60% above today's price, but views range from a \$7 sell to a \$25 bull case. It's a high-risk, high-reward bet for patient investors.
