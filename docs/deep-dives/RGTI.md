# RGTI — Rigetti Computing, Inc.

Rigetti Computing is a full-stack superconducting quantum computing pure-play that builds its own quantum processing units (QPUs) and sells access through quantum-computing-as-a-service (QCaaS) and on-premises Novera systems. At \$14.52 (2026-07-29), the stock sits near the low end of a wild \$12.53–\$58.15 52-week range, having round-tripped a massive 2025-26 quantum rally. The bull case is anchored not by current revenue — a tiny \$7.1M in FY2025, down 34% year over year — but by an exceptionally strong balance sheet (~\$590M cash, no debt) that funds a multi-year roadmap, plus government tailwinds including a U.S. Department of Commerce letter of intent for an award of up to \$100M and a Trump executive-order push for quantum. This is a high-beta, binary, milestone-driven speculation where a Buy analyst consensus (average target near \$30) collides with a valuation that discounts years of flawless execution.

## Company Overview

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Rigetti Computing, Inc. (Nasdaq: RGTI), founded in 2013 and headquartered in Berkeley, California, designs and builds superconducting quantum computers and the quantum processors inside them. It is one of the few quantum firms that controls its full stack — from chip fabrication at its own Fab-1 facility to the control systems and software that run programs — and it monetizes that stack three ways:

| Segment | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **QCaaS (cloud)** | Cloud access to Rigetti QPUs on a pay-per-use / subscription basis |
| **Novera on-premises systems** | The 9-qubit Novera QPU sold as an on-site quantum computer to labs, supercomputing centers, and governments |
| **Development contracts and grants** | Government and research programs (DARPA, DoD, DOE, U.S. Department of Commerce, plus international partners) |

Revenue is small and lumpy — FY2025 total revenue was only \$7.1M — because the business is still at the research-and-early-deployment stage. The strategic bet is that superconducting QPUs, built on a **modular chiplet architecture**, can scale qubit counts and fidelity fast enough to reach commercially useful, error-corrected computing within roughly three years.

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

- **Superconducting modality:** Rigetti competes in the same gate-based, superconducting camp as IBM and Google, but as a nimble pure-play. Rivals pursue different modalities — trapped ion (IonQ), neutral atom (Infleqtion), and quantum annealing (D-Wave).
- **Vertical integration:** owning chip fabrication (Fab-1) is a genuine differentiator — it controls its own iteration cycle rather than depending on a foundry.
- **Modular chiplet roadmap:** tiling smaller chips (e.g. 9-qubit Novera tiles) into larger systems is Rigetti's path to 100+ and eventually 1,000+ qubits without a single monolithic chip.
- **Government anchor:** a Department of Commerce LOI for an award of up to \$100M, DARPA/DoD relationships, and national-lab deployments provide a credibility and revenue base that most pure-plays lack.

**Moat Assessment:** MODERATE — real IP and vertical integration, but the market is nascent, revenue is minuscule, and the technology lead over IBM/Google and other modalities is not yet decisive.

### Management Team

Rigetti is led by a management team focused on the modular chiplet roadmap and the transition from research contracts toward commercial system sales. The company maintains a lean headcount of roughly 164 employees, reflecting its pre-commercial stage. Leadership has emphasized error-correction progress, on-premises deployments, and disciplined use of the post-rally cash balance to fund the roadmap rather than chase revenue at any cost.

## Readability Pass

**Plain-English thesis:** Rigetti builds quantum computers using superconducting chips that it makes in its own factory. It sells time on those computers over the cloud and ships a small on-site machine called Novera to labs and governments. The technology is real and improving, but sales are tiny — only about \$7 million last year, and actually *down* from the year before.

What makes Rigetti interesting is its bank account: it raised so much money during the 2025-26 quantum stock boom that it now sits on roughly \$590 million with no debt. The catch is the price tag. The company is worth about \$4.8 billion while selling only ~\$7 million a year and losing money, and the stock has already crashed from \$58 to about \$14.52. So it is a speculative bet: real upside if the chip milestones and contracts land (bull case \$30–\$45), a middle path around \$18–\$25, and downside to \$6–\$10 if the quantum hype cools, milestones slip, or the company keeps issuing new shares.
