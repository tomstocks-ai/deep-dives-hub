# BWXT — BWX Technologies, Inc.

BWX Technologies is the only company licensed to manufacture nuclear reactors for the U.S. Navy and the largest Western supplier of heavy components to the commercial nuclear industry. FY2025 revenue was \$3.20B (+18.3 percent) with \$4.01 of non-GAAP EPS, and management has raised FY2026 guidance twice — to roughly \$3.80B of revenue, \$662M–\$672M of adjusted EBITDA, \$4.70–\$4.80 of non-GAAP EPS and \$345M–\$360M of free cash flow. Backlog is a record \$8.40B, up 40 percent year over year. Yet the stock closed at \$134.35 on 28 September 2026, a fresh 52-week low, down 26 percent over one year and 44 percent from its \$241.82 high. This deep dive argues the gap is a sector-wide de-rating rather than a company-specific break, and rates BWXT **BUY** with a base case of \$155–\$185.

## Company Overview

BWXT operates two reporting segments.

**Government Operations (FY2025 revenue \$2,350.1M, 73 percent of total, +8 percent YoY)** designs and manufactures the nuclear reactors and steam-generating equipment that power U.S. Navy submarines and aircraft carriers. BWXT has held this franchise for more than six decades and is effectively sole-source: the Navy's reactor designs, the qualified supply chain, the nuclear-grade welding and machining capability, and the security clearances required are not replicable on a commercial timetable. The segment also processes special nuclear materials for the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), manages and operates government sites through joint ventures, supplies research-reactor fuel, and develops the BWXT Advanced Nuclear Reactor (BANR) microreactor and space-nuclear propulsion concepts.

**Commercial Operations (FY2025 revenue \$853.1M, +63 percent YoY)** supplies steam generators, reactor pressure vessel components, fuel handling systems and field services to the global commercial nuclear fleet, principally CANDU and Western PWR operators. This is the segment doing the heavy lifting right now: Q2 2026 revenue rose 72 percent to \$302.5M and segment adjusted EBITDA rose 123 percent to \$36.1M. Two acquisitions drive much of it — Kinectrics (nuclear engineering and testing services, acquired January 2025 for \$525M) and Precision Components Group (PCG, closed 1 July 2026), which gives BWXT a U.S. manufacturing footprint for commercial nuclear components for the first time.

The moat is regulatory and industrial rather than technological. Nuclear-grade manufacturing requires ASME N-stamp certification, decades-long qualification cycles and customer relationships measured in programs rather than purchase orders. That is why Government Operations revenue is highly predictable and why new entrants have not appeared despite the nuclear revival.

Management is led by CEO Rex Geveden (in the role since 2020, previously NASA and Teledyne). The company has ~11,000 employees, owns 19 manufacturing facilities and supports U.S. and Canadian government customers at more than two dozen additional sites. Capital allocation has been consistent: a growing dividend (now \$0.27 per quarter, \$1.08 annualised, ~28 percent payout), modest buybacks (\$30.0M in FY2025) and heavy reinvestment.

In August 2026 BWXT agreed to sell its medical business — BWXT Medical plus the Kinectrics stable medical isotopes unit — to Nordic Capital in a transaction valued at up to \$800M, retaining a minority stake. Management framed this as a focusing move: out of radiopharmaceuticals, deeper into nuclear national security and commercial nuclear power.

**Q2 2026 (reported 3 August 2026)**

Revenue of \$901.6M was up 18 percent year over year (9 percent organic), a shade under the ~\$902M consensus. Government Operations grew 2 percent to \$601.3M on naval propulsion and special materials, partly offset by lower advanced-reactor volumes. Commercial Operations grew 72 percent to \$302.5M. GAAP diluted EPS was \$0.97 and non-GAAP EPS \$1.07, ahead of the \$1.04–\$1.05 consensus. Adjusted EBITDA reached \$155.5M and free cash flow \$115.0M.

The mix shift matters. Government Operations segment operating income actually *fell* 3 percent to \$105.7M, because the prior-year quarter contained more favourable contract adjustments and this year's mix was less rich. All of the consolidated growth came from Commercial Operations, where operating income rose 254 percent to \$24.3M off a small base. Investors comfortable with BWXT as a defensive government contractor need to recognise that the growth engine has moved to the more cyclical, more competitive commercial side.

**Balance sheet and cash (30 June 2026)**

Cash and equivalents of \$608.2M against long-term debt of \$2,019.9M gives net debt of roughly \$1.41B, about 2.1x FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA. Equity is \$1,334.1M, so debt-to-equity is 1.51. Current ratio 2.40, quick ratio 2.33. Return on equity is a strong 30.8 percent and net margin 10.1 percent. H1 2026 operating cash flow was \$249.0M against \$83.9M of capex, so free cash flow conversion remains healthy even while the company funds the Cambridge plant expansion and PCG integration.

**Backlog and bookings**

Total backlog at 30 June 2026 was \$8,398.1M (Government \$6,797.6M, Commercial \$1,600.5M) versus \$6,015.2M a year earlier, up 40 percent. That is roughly 2.2 years of revenue at the FY2026 guided run rate and is the single most important number in the story. Bookings, however, are lumpy: Q2 2026 bookings of \$648.9M compared with \$1,640.5M in Q2 2025, which flatters the year-over-year backlog figure and will make quarter-to-quarter book-to-bill look erratic.

### Scenarios

**Bull case: \$225–\$250.** Backlog converts on schedule, Commercial Operations keeps compounding at 40 percent-plus, Janus becomes a repeatable installed-base business rather than a one-off award, and up to \$800M of medical proceeds is redeployed into capacity or buybacks. The multiple returns toward where it traded in early 2026 — roughly 29–32x EV/FY2026E adjusted EBITDA, or 27–30x FY2027E — which still leaves it below the January peak.

**Base case: \$155–\$185.** FY2026 lands inside guidance and FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches the ~\$5.26 consensus. Government Operations grows high single digits on naval propulsion and special materials; Commercial Operations grows 25–35 percent with a full year of PCG. Apply 29–35x FY2027E EPS and the shares are worth \$155–\$185. That is 15–38 percent above the current price and still a 15–28 percent discount to the sell-side consensus target.

**Bear case: \$100–\$120.** The nuclear complex keeps unwinding and the market decides BWXT is a defense prime with a nuclear label, valuing it at 20–23x earnings. Government Operations margins stay flat-to-down as favourable contract adjustments normalise, federal budget disruption pushes bookings right, and growth capex plus \$2.02B of debt squeeze free cash flow. That is 11–26 percent below the current price.

The skew is favourable: roughly 26 percent downside to the middle of the bear range against 27 percent upside to the middle of the base range and 77 percent to the middle of the bull range.

## Growth Catalysts

**Naval propulsion is in a structural up-cycle.** Government Operations backlog of \$6.80B reflects multi-year naval propulsion and special-materials awards, including the large Navy contracts booked in early 2026. Submarine build rates and the Columbia-class programme underpin demand regardless of the equity market's view of nuclear power.

**Army Janus programme (26 August 2026).** The U.S. Army, with the Defense Innovation Unit, selected BWXT's BANR microreactor for deployment at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, as one of five vendors sharing awards worth up to \$2.2B in total. BWXT executes under a phased contracting approach: Phase 1 covers final site selection within Fort Campbell, initiating nuclear regulatory processes and starting TRISO fuel fabrication at existing BWXT facilities. BWXT's individual share was not disclosed, and near-term revenue will be modest — but this is the first credible path from microreactor R and D to a fielded, repeatable product, and BWXT already owns the TRISO fuel capability that peers must build.

**NNSA lithium processing facility.** Over summer 2026 BWXT secured an NNSA contract to develop a new lithium processing facility, extending the special-materials franchise that was one of the two drivers of Q2 Government Operations growth.

**PCG gives BWXT a U.S. commercial nuclear manufacturing base.** Closed 1 July 2026, PCG establishes domestic component manufacturing for the commercial nuclear market and also supports existing Navy programmes. It contributes to the raised FY2026 guidance and provides capacity for SMR component orders that previously would have gone elsewhere.

**Kinectrics expands the services annuity.** The engineering and testing arm keeps winning independent work — in September 2026 Clean Core Thorium Energy selected Kinectrics to perform the independent technical review of its ANEEL fuel qualification programme ahead of a potential commercial reactor demonstration. These are small contracts individually but they compound into a recurring, capital-light revenue stream and position BWXT as the neutral technical authority across competing reactor designs.

**Medical divestiture as a capital-allocation event.** The up-to-\$800M Nordic Capital transaction, once closed, converts a business BWXT was subscale in into cash. Management has signalled reinvestment in nuclear capacity and modernisation. A buyback at current prices would be materially accretive.

## Risk Factors

**Government concentration.** Roughly 70 percent of revenue comes from U.S. government customers, dominated by the Navy's nuclear propulsion programme and NNSA. BWXT's own risk disclosures lead with federal budget uncertainty, continuing-resolution funding mechanisms, the debt ceiling, government shutdowns and shifting acquisition priorities. A single-customer franchise is a moat and a concentration risk simultaneously.

**Government Operations margin erosion.** Segment operating income fell 3 percent year over year in Q2 2026 and was down 8 percent in Q4 2025, in both cases because prior periods benefited from favourable contract adjustments. If this is normalisation rather than noise, consolidated margin expansion depends entirely on Commercial Operations — a segment with lower margins and more competition.

**Valuation still assumes success.** After a 44 percent drawdown BWXT trades at ~28x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS and ~21x EV/EBITDA. That is not cheap for a company guiding to high-teens revenue growth with mid-single-digit growth in its largest segment. A further de-rating does not require anything to go wrong operationally.

**Sector flows are hostile.** The nuclear and AI-power complex has de-rated hard in 2026: NuScale is down ~48 percent year to date, Oklo ~51 percent, and uranium and nuclear ETFs fell roughly a third from their January peak. BWXT was also removed from several Russell 1000 Defensive-style indexes in June 2026, adding mechanical selling. Shares sit below both the 50-day (\$159.46) and 200-day (\$190.54) moving averages.

**Advanced reactors are a 2030s revenue story.** Microreactor revenue declined in 2025. Janus is phased, regulatory timelines for military reactors are unproven, and BWXT's disclosures explicitly flag the timing of technology development, regulatory approvals and production automation as risks.

**Leverage and capex.** \$2,019.9M of long-term debt against \$1,334.1M of equity, with capex rising from \$153.6M (FY2024) to \$184.6M (FY2025) to \$83.9M in H1 2026 alone. The Cambridge expansion and PCG integration consume cash before they generate it.

**Bookings volatility.** Q2 2026 bookings of \$648.9M versus \$1,640.5M a year earlier. Investors who anchor on book-to-bill will get noisy signals.

**Divestiture execution.** The medical sale removes a growing unit, requires regulatory approval, and leaves BWXT with a minority stake in a business it no longer controls. Proceeds redeployed poorly would be a real setback.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY. Base case \$155–\$185. Bull case \$225–\$250. Bear case \$100–\$120.**

The case is straightforward: fundamentals accelerated through 2026 while the multiple collapsed. Guidance has been raised twice, backlog is up 40 percent to a record \$8.40B, free cash flow guidance went from \$305M–\$320M to \$345M–\$360M, and the company won a place in the Army's microreactor programme — all while the share price fell 44 percent. Nothing in the reported numbers explains a 52-week low; the nuclear theme unwinding does.

**Position sizing.** Treat this as a core industrial position rather than a thematic trade: 2–4 percent of a diversified portfolio. BWXT's beta of 0.75 and 94.4 percent institutional ownership make it the lowest-volatility way to own nuclear, but the 2026 drawdown shows that theme correlation overwhelms beta when flows reverse.

**Entry strategy.** Scale in rather than committing at once. The stock is in an established downtrend and has just printed a new 52-week low at \$133.90, so momentum offers no support. A reasonable approach is one-third near \$130–\$135, one-third on a retest of \$115–\$125 if the sector unwind continues, and the final third only after the price reclaims the 50-day average near \$159. Buyers who need confirmation should wait for the Q3 print in early November.

**Stop loss / invalidation.** The thesis is fundamental, not technical, so the invalidation triggers are: FY2026 guidance cut, Government Operations backlog declining sequentially, or a federal appropriations disruption that delays naval propulsion awards. Traders wanting a price stop should use a close below \$115 — roughly 14 percent below current levels and beneath the bear-case midpoint — as the signal that the market is pricing a different business than the one described here.

**Technical context.** Support is the \$133.90 52-week low; the next reference below that is the \$115–\$120 area implied by a 20–23x FY2027 earnings multiple. Resistance is the 50-day average at \$159.46 and then the 200-day at \$190.54. With the stock 30 percent below its 200-day average and printing new lows, RSI has been in or near oversold territory — useful for timing entries, not for judging the thesis.

**Catalyst calendar.** FY2027 defense appropriations flow from October 2026; Q3 2026 earnings in early November; the Nordic Capital medical sale closing, pending regulatory approvals; FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance in February 2027; Janus Phase 1 milestones through 2027.

**News tone: mixed-to-positive on fundamentals, negative on price action.** Coverage since the August print splits cleanly. Operational headlines have been good — guidance raise, Janus selection, the NNSA lithium award, the Kinectrics ANEEL mandate. Price-driven headlines have been uniformly bad, culminating in MarketBeat's 28 September "sets new 52-week low" alert. Investing.com noted shares slipped after hours on 3 August on the small revenue miss despite the guidance raise, which captures the regime: good news is not being paid for.

**Analyst sentiment: deteriorating at the margin, still net positive.** Consensus is "Moderate Buy" with a \$213.75 average target (2 Strong Buy, 8 Buy, 5 Hold). But the recent direction of travel is down: Wall Street Zen cut to hold on 8 August, Weiss Ratings cut from buy to hold on 26 August, and BNP Paribas Exane cut its target from \$190 to \$165 on 24 September. The bulls are older calls — Seaport's \$245 upgrade on 22 June, JPMorgan's \$230 initiation on 27 July — both struck well above the current price. Expect further target trims before upgrades.

**Retail and community sentiment: constructive, contrarian.** Simply Wall St reports 94 investors backing a narrative that values BWXT at \$232.31, roughly 40 percent above the current price, citing backlog growth and multi-year defense contracts. The same platform simultaneously hosts pieces arguing the stock "may be fully priced" and "could be 21 percent overvalued on nuclear cash flow hopes." Retail sentiment is engaged and split, which is what you would expect mid-de-rating.

**Insider activity: mildly negative.** CEO Rex Geveden sold 10,000 shares at an average \$172.50 on 12 August 2026 (a \$1.73M transaction) under a pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plan, leaving him with 192,491 shares directly held. **Institutional positioning: stable.** Institutions and hedge funds own 94.4 percent of the shares. Recent 13F activity visible in filings shows small incremental adds from wealth managers and RIAs rather than large position changes in either direction. No evidence of forced institutional liquidation, though the June 2026 removal from several Russell 1000 Defensive-style indexes was a mechanical outflow event.

**Options flow: not assessed.** No reliable options positioning or unusual-activity data was available for BWXT in this research session. Treating the absence as neutral rather than inferring a signal.

**Sentiment score: 4 out of 10 on price and flow, 7 out of 10 on fundamentals.** The divergence between those two numbers is the investment case.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: BWXT builds the nuclear reactors that power U.S. Navy submarines and aircraft carriers. It is the only company allowed to do this, and has been doing it for sixty years. That business — about 70 percent of revenue — is as close to a guaranteed order book as exists in industrials, and its order backlog just hit a record \$8.4 billion, up 40 percent in a year.

The company also makes big components for commercial nuclear power plants, and that part is growing fast — up 72 percent last quarter — helped by two acquisitions. Management has raised its profit forecast for this year twice. It is selling its medical-isotope division for up to \$800 million to focus on nuclear power and defense, and it just won a spot in the U.S. Army's programme to put small reactors on military bases.

So why is the stock at a one-year low, down 44 percent from its high? Because in 2026 investors fell out of love with anything connected to nuclear power and AI electricity demand. Peers like NuScale and Oklo are down around 50 percent this year. BWXT got sold with them, even though its earnings went up rather than down.

The stock is not obviously cheap — about 28 times next year's expected earnings — and its largest segment's profit margins have stopped expanding. So this is not a bargain-bin situation. But you are buying a business with a genuine monopoly, a record backlog, rising cash flow and a dividend, at a 37 percent discount to what analysts on average think it is worth. If it simply trades back to a sensible multiple of next year's earnings you get \$155–\$185. If the nuclear enthusiasm returns, \$225–\$250. If the de-rating continues, \$100–\$120. Those odds are worth taking, but scale in slowly — a stock making new lows usually makes a few more.
