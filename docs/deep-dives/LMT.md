# LMT — Lockheed Martin Corporation

Lockheed Martin (NYSE: LMT) is the world's largest pure-play defense prime contractor, built around the F-35 Lightning II — the largest weapons program in history — and a deep portfolio of missiles, missile defense, military rotorcraft, and national-security space systems. FY2025 delivered record demand: sales of \$75.0B (up 6% YoY), a record \$194B backlog, and \$6.9B of free cash flow, even as GAAP EPS of \$21.49 absorbed a \$479M pension settlement charge and program losses. With surging global munitions replenishment, the U.S. "Golden Dome" homeland missile-defense initiative, and elevated allied budgets, LMT offers durable, government-backed cash flows and a growing shareholder return. At ~\$581 the stock trades around 20x forward earnings — a reasonable entry for a franchise with multi-year revenue visibility — earning a BUY rating.

---

## 1. Company Overview

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

Lockheed Martin designs, develops, manufactures, and sustains advanced technology systems and products, with roughly three-quarters of revenue coming from the U.S. government (including the Department of Defense and other federal agencies) and the balance from international governments and commercial customers. The business is organized into four reporting segments:

| Segment | % of Revenue | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Aeronautics** | ~40% | F-35 Lightning II, F-16, F-22 sustainment, and classified advanced development programs (Skunk Works). |
| **Rotary and Mission Systems (RMS)** | ~25% | Sikorsky helicopters (Black Hawk, CH-53K), naval combat systems (Aegis), radar, and C6ISR. |
| **Missiles and Fire Control (MFC)** | ~20% | PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, HIMARS-launched munitions, and energy/sensor systems. |
| **Space** | ~15% | Strategic and hypersonic missiles, satellites, missile-warning, and human/deep-space systems. |

The model is anchored by long-cycle, multi-year production and sustainment contracts, which convert the record \$194B backlog into highly visible future revenue. Sustainment and aftermarket services on an installed base of fielded aircraft, missiles, and radars generate recurring, higher-margin revenue that compounds over program lifetimes measured in decades.

### Industry Position and Competitive Moat

**Competitive Advantages:**

- **F-35 franchise:** As prime on the largest weapons program in history, LMT holds decades of procurement, upgrade (Block 4, TR-3), and sustainment revenue spanning a global fleet of thousands of aircraft.
- **Scale and program breadth:** Few competitors span fighters, rotorcraft, hypersonics, missile defense, and national-security space under one roof.
- **Backlog depth:** A record \$194B backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility and pricing leverage.
- **Barriers to entry:** Security clearances, classified know-how, certification cycles, and entrenched platform positions create very high switching costs and near-impenetrable moats on strategic programs.

**Competitive Moat Assessment:** STRONG.

### Management Team Track Record

Chairman, President and CEO **James Taiclet** has led a capital-disciplined strategy emphasizing "21st Century Security," digital engineering, and consistent cash returns to shareholders. CFO **Evan Scott** oversees a balance sheet managed to investment-grade ratings while funding a growing dividend and steady buybacks. Management returned substantial cash to shareholders in 2025 and reaffirmed a shareholder-friendly capital allocation framework alongside the record backlog.

## 2. Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Revenue** | \$71.0B | \$75.0B | **+6%** |
| **Segment Operating Profit** | ~\$6.0B | \$6.7B | **+11%** |
| **Net Earnings** | \$5.3B | \$5.0B | −6% |
| **EPS (Diluted)** | \$22.31 | \$21.49 | −4% |
| **Free Cash Flow** | \$5.3B | \$6.9B | **+31%** |

FY2025 sales rose 6% to a record \$75.0B, with growth across every segment driven by ramped production in aircraft and missile programs. Segment operating profit grew 11% to \$6.7B (a 9.0% margin). GAAP EPS of \$21.49 was held back by a \$479M pension settlement charge (\$1.63 per share after-tax) and prior program losses, masking underlying operating strength. Consolidated gross margin sits near 11%, typical for a prime-contractor cost structure dominated by cost-of-sales on large production programs.

### Cash Flow

| Metric | FY2025 | Analysis |

Cash generation is the core of the LMT story: \$6.9B of FY2025 free cash flow comfortably funds a dividend that yields roughly 2.3% and consistent share repurchases, with net debt held at manageable, investment-grade levels.

## 4. Growth Catalysts

- **Golden Dome:** The U.S. homeland missile-defense initiative is a potentially transformational, multi-year opportunity for LMT's Missiles and Fire Control and Space segments, spanning interceptors, sensors, and command-and-control.
- **Munitions replenishment:** Sustained NATO and allied restocking of PAC-3, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, and THAAD drives multi-year MFC production growth at attractive margins.
- **F-35 lifecycle:** Block 4 modernization, TR-3 hardware/software upgrades, and a growing global sustainment base extend the franchise's revenue for decades.
- **International demand:** Elevated European and Indo-Pacific budgets expand foreign military sales across fighters, rotorcraft, and integrated air and missile defense.
- **Hypersonics and space:** Strategic deterrent modernization, hypersonic strike programs, and missile-warning constellations add long-cycle growth and technology leadership.

---

## 5. Risk Factors

1. **Government funding dependence:** With roughly three-quarters of revenue from the U.S. government, continuing resolutions, budget caps, or shifting political priorities can delay procurement, new starts, and awards.

2. **F-35 concentration:** The program is LMT's single largest revenue driver. Funding reductions, delivery pauses tied to TR-3 software, or engine-upgrade disputes can materially affect results and sentiment.

3. **Next-generation air dominance:** The U.S. Air Force awarded the F-47 NGAD fighter to Boeing, ceding a marquee future franchise and pressuring Aeronautics' long-term growth narrative.

4. **Fixed-price contract risk:** Development programs on fixed-price terms carry cost-overrun exposure; further program losses or pension charges can compress margins and distort GAAP earnings.

5. **Export and geopolitical risk:** ITAR/EAR export controls and shifting alliances can restrict, delay, or reshape lucrative international sales.

## 8. Readability Pass

In plain English: Lockheed Martin makes the world's most important fighter jet (the F-35) plus a huge range of missiles, missile-defense systems, helicopters, and military satellites, and it sells mostly to the U.S. government and its allies. In 2025 it booked record orders (a \$194B backlog), grew sales 6% to \$75B, and generated \$6.9B of cash — even though a one-time pension charge dented reported profit. Demand for missiles is booming, and a new U.S. homeland missile-shield program ("Golden Dome") could add years of high-quality work. The stock pays a solid, growing dividend and trades at a fair ~20x earnings. The main worries are that budgets are political, the F-35 dominates revenue, and Lockheed lost the next big fighter contract to Boeing. On balance, dependable cash flows and record demand make this a **BUY**, best bought on dips toward the \$540–\$560 area with targets of \$610–\$650 (base), \$680–\$720 (bull), and \$470–\$510 (bear).
