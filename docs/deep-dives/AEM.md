# AEM — Agnico Eagle Mines Limited

Agnico Eagle is the world's second-largest gold producer and Canada's largest mining company, with roughly 70% of production from Canada and Australia. At \$184.15 (September 28, 2026) the stock trades ~28% below its 52-week high of \$255.24, caught between two opposing forces: a business that is generating record free cash flow with a \$3.3 billion net cash position and an A- credit rating, and a gold price that has corrected from \$4,696/oz in late August 2026 to roughly \$4,150–\$4,300/oz as long-term Treasury yields push above 5.5%. Layered on top is a company-specific setback — a pit wall failure at Canadian Malartic's Barnat pit in July 2026 that trims output through 2028. Rating: BUY, on the view that the balance sheet and the 2030 growth pipeline are worth more than the near-term gold drawdown costs.

## Company Overview

Agnico Eagle operates eleven mines across four countries: LaRonde, Canadian Malartic (including the Odyssey underground mine) and Goldex in Quebec; Detour Lake and Macassa in Ontario; Meliadine and Meadowbank in Nunavut; Fosterville in Australia; Kittila in Finland; and Pinos Altos in Mexico. The company produced 3.45 million ounces of gold in 2025 and guides 3.3–3.5 million ounces for 2026, now expected near the lower end of that range.

**Business model.** Revenue is almost entirely gold sales, with silver, zinc and copper recovered as by-products (Q2 2026 by-products credited \$75/oz against cash costs). Gold is refined in Canada, Australia or Europe, which management believes leaves the revenue structure largely unaffected by US tariffs.

**Competitive position.**

- **Jurisdiction quality.** Quebec, Ontario, Nunavut, Australia and Finland — no exposure to West Africa, Latin American resource nationalism or Russia. This is the structural reason AEM trades at a premium to most senior peers.
- **Balance sheet.** Total debt of just \$197 million against \$3,464 million of cash at June 30, 2026 — a net cash position of \$3,267 million. Debt-to-equity is 0.01. Fitch upgraded the issuer default rating to A- in April 2026; Moody's reaffirmed A3 in July.
- **Cost position.** Q2 2026 total cash costs of \$1,054/oz and AISC of \$1,459/oz against a realized price of \$4,483/oz. Detour Lake, the flagship, ran at \$825/oz total cash costs.
- **Reinvestment inside the fence.** Every major growth project — Odyssey, Detour Lake underground, Upper Beaver, Hope Bay, Ikkari — sits in a region where the company already operates, holds permits and has community agreements.

**Management.** CEO Ammar Al-Joundi has held the line on capital discipline while funding a large build-out. In the second quarter of 2026 the company returned a record \$625 million to shareholders: a \$0.45 quarterly dividend plus \$400 million of buybacks at an average of \$178.86 per share. The NCIB was renewed in May 2026 with the internal purchase limit raised to \$2 billion. Agnico Eagle has declared a dividend every year since 1983.

**Recent portfolio activity.** In Q2 2026 the company consolidated the Central Lapland Greenstone Belt in Finland — a 2,492 km² package containing Kittila and the advanced Ikkari project — by acquiring Rupert Resources (\$1,687 million in shares plus contingent value rights worth up to C\$3.00 each; 207.7 million CVRs outstanding), Aurion Resources (\$339 million cash) and B2Gold's 70% of the FinGold JV (\$325 million cash).

## Valuation

At \$184.15 the market capitalisation is ~\$93.9 billion against a trailing P/E of ~15.9. Consensus FY2026 EPS is approximately \$11.46, putting the forward P/E near 16x. Annualising H1 free cash flow of \$2.07 billion gives a free cash flow yield of roughly 4.4% — well below 2025's level because capex is up ~25% and cash taxes of \$3.4–\$3.6 billion are being paid on the 2025 windfall. The dividend of \$1.80 annualised yields ~1.0%.

Analyst consensus sits above the market: MarketBeat reports a Moderate Buy from 17 analysts with an average target of \$227.15 (September 26, 2026); stockanalysis.com reports a Buy from 22 analysts with an average of \$214.89. Recent target revisions have gone both ways — Citigroup cut from \$256 to \$200 while keeping a buy rating (July 27), Scotiabank trimmed \$278 to \$260 (July 14), and RBC raised \$210 to \$225 on a sector perform rating (September 16).

## Growth Catalysts

1. **Hope Bay construction.** A positive investment decision on May 19, 2026 backed an underground operation with a 6,000 tonne-per-day plant producing 400,000–435,000 ounces a year over an initial 11-year life, with an after-tax IRR above 20% at \$4,000/oz gold. Only 55% of measured and indicated resources and 48% of inferred resources are in the mine plan, across an 80-kilometre greenstone belt. Detailed engineering was ~67% complete at June 30 and the first sealift arrived in August 2026.

2. **Odyssey at Canadian Malartic.** Phase one of shaft sinking finished in July 2026 at 1,586 metres. Shaft-hoisted production from East Gouldie remains on schedule for Q2 2027. Odyssey set a quarterly production record of 28,800 ounces in Q2 2026. Together with a possible second shaft, the Marban satellite pit and the Wasamac underground project, management sees Canadian Malartic moving toward one million ounces a year from as early as 2033.

3. **Detour Lake underground.** 1,263 metres of lateral development completed to date, with the exploration ramp at 180 metres. Drilling into the West Extension returned 20.8 g/t gold over 4.8 metres some 2.3 kilometres west of the resource pit outline. Potential for 300,000–350,000 incremental ounces a year, with first underground production possibly as early as 2028.

4. **Upper Beaver.** Exploration ramp at 165 metres and exploration shaft at 478 metres. A high-intensity drilling programme completed in Q2 2026 could pull initial production forward to early 2030 — 200,000–225,000 ounces of gold plus 3,600 tonnes of copper annually.

5. **Ikkari and Central Lapland.** The consolidated 2,492 km² Finnish package adds a large advanced project next to an existing mill and a well-understood permitting regime. An internal evaluation for the optimised mine design is targeted by the end of 2027.

6. **San Nicolás.** The 50/50 copper-zinc joint venture with Teck received its land use change and environmental impact assessment permits in July 2026, moving toward a potential sanction decision. Anticipated contribution is 50,000–60,000 tonnes of copper a year (AEM share) from 2030.

7. **Aggregate profile.** Management targets a 20–30% increase in annual gold production over the next decade, with a first step-up in 2030 and the potential to exceed 4.0 million ounces in the early 2030s. These figures rest on internal evaluations that include inferred resources and are preliminary.

8. **Capital returns.** A \$2 billion internal NCIB limit and a net cash balance sheet mean buybacks can be leaned on while the share price is depressed — \$550 million was repurchased in H1 2026 at an average of \$185.89.

## Risk Factors

**Gold price — the dominant risk.** Gold peaked at \$4,696/oz on August 25, 2026 and had fallen to roughly \$4,150–\$4,300/oz by late September, with December futures opening at \$4,150.10 on September 29. The 30-year US Treasury yield reached 5.53%, its highest since 2004, and market-implied odds of an October Fed hike rose from about 7% in mid-August to roughly 73% by late September. Sell-side forecasts span a wide range — several large banks publish \$5,000–\$6,300 targets while ICICI Bank sees \$4,200–\$4,600 for the rest of 2026. AEM is a levered play on which of those views proves right.

**Barnat pit wall failure.** On July 1, 2026 approximately one million tonnes of rock moved along the north wall of the Barnat open pit at Canadian Malartic. No one was injured. Mining had already been suspended as a precaution. The company attributes it to weaker altered rock associated with the Cadillac Fault, aggravated by freshet and heavy rainfall. The consequences are material: 60,000–80,000 fewer ounces in H2 2026 and up to 150,000 fewer ounces in each of 2027 and 2028, with Canadian Malartic total cash costs rising to approximately \$1,260/oz for 2026 against prior guidance of \$1,187. Remediation was expected to finish in Q3 2026 with mining resuming in Q4. Management states the Odyssey development outlook is unaffected.

**Safety.** Fatal accidents occurred at Canadian Malartic in April 2026 and at Upper Beaver in May 2026, prompting a global safety reset across all operations. Beyond the human cost, this is a social licence and operational continuity risk.

**Cost inflation.** Diesel for the 2026 Nunavut sealift — about 130 million litres, roughly 70% of requirements through to the 2027 sealift — was purchased at prices approximately 30% above budget net of hedges. Diesel is ~10% of operating costs. Labour and royalty costs also rose in H1. AISC guidance of \$1,400–\$1,550/oz has limited headroom.

**Capital intensity and taxes.** Capex guidance rose to \$2.6–\$2.8 billion excluding capitalized exploration, and cash taxes of \$3.4–\$3.6 billion are due in 2026. Together they consume most of the operating cash flow at current gold prices, which is why free cash flow will not repeat the 2025 record.

**FX.** Roughly 30% of production sits outside North America. A stronger US dollar helps reported costs but the Canadian dollar drove H1 production cost increases; hedges cover only ~60% of remaining 2026 CAD exposure.

**Rupert dilution and CVRs.** The Rupert acquisition was share-funded (\$1,687 million) and carries 207.7 million contingent value rights worth up to C\$3.00 each if reserve and production milestones are met — a real, if contingent, future liability. Management has flagged using investment sale proceeds to offset the dilution via buybacks.

**Permitting and community.** San Nicolás still needs additional Mexican authorisations. Wasamac and Marban require permitting. Indigenous and community engagement is well managed — C\$346 million in direct contributions to Indigenous Nations in Canada across 2024–2025 — but remains a gating factor.

## Recommendation

**Rating:** <span class="rating-buy">BUY</span>

Agnico Eagle is the highest-quality large-cap gold producer available, and the current price reflects a macro drawdown in gold plus a discrete operational setback rather than any deterioration in the franchise. Net cash of \$3.3 billion, an A- rating, Tier-1 jurisdictions, peer-leading costs and a fully funded pipeline toward 4.0 million ounces are intact. The risk/reward skews favourably: roughly +9% to +19% in the base case, +33% to +44% if gold recovers, against –16% to –27% if gold breaks to \$3,400–\$3,800/oz.

The honest caveat is that this is a bet on gold not falling much further. Gold is in a five-week downtrend against rising real yields and a repricing Fed, and technically the stock trades at its 50-day moving average (\$184.50) and below its 200-day (\$188.77) — neutral-to-weak. Buy it in tranches, not in one move.

**Position sizing.** Core precious metals holding, 4–7% of a diversified portfolio. AEM should be the anchor of any gold allocation before adding higher-beta silver or junior names.

**Entry strategy.**

- Starter position at current levels around \$184
- Add on weakness toward \$165 and \$150 — the latter being roughly where the stock traded when gold was last near \$3,800/oz
- Full position if gold stabilises above \$4,200/oz and the Q3 print on October 28 confirms Barnat remediation is on schedule

**Stop loss.** \$132 on a closing basis, just below the 52-week low of \$134.38. A break there would signal the gold bull market has structurally ended rather than merely corrected, which invalidates the thesis rather than testing it.

The pattern is analysts marking gold price decks lower while retaining positive views on the company — target compression, not thesis abandonment.

**Price action: weak.** The stock gapped down on Monday September 28, opening at \$183.44 against a prior close of \$194.48, and closed the session at \$184.15 — a 5.3% single-day decline tracking gold's slump. **Institutional ownership: 68.3%** of the float, per MarketBeat. Q2 2026 filings showed net accumulation among the smaller managers disclosed — PensionDanmark added 12%, Annex Advisory added 70.5%, with new positions from K.J. Harrison and BIP Wealth. Nothing in that sample suggests institutional capitulation.

**News tone: mixed, with a negative skew since July.** The Q2 beat, record free cash flow, the Fitch upgrade to A- and the Hope Bay sanction are all constructive. Against that, the Barnat wall failure and two fatalities are the kind of headlines that cause generalist investors to step back, and the sell-side commentary through September has framed gold miners as a "comeback story" or "buy the dip" candidate — language that implies the market currently sees them as broken.

**Beta 0.67.** Low correlation to the broad equity market, which is the portfolio argument for owning it. In practice AEM's beta to gold matters far more than its beta to the S&P.

**Composite read: cautiously positive, 6/10.** Fundamentals and analyst views support the stock; momentum and the macro backdrop do not. That divergence is exactly what creates an entry point for someone with a multi-year horizon — and exactly what punishes someone with a three-month one.

## Readability Pass

- **AISC (all-in sustaining cost)** is the full cost of producing an ounce of gold including mining, processing, royalties, sustaining capital and overhead. AEM's Q2 2026 AISC was \$1,459/oz against a realized gold price of \$4,483/oz — roughly \$3,000 of margin per ounce. That is why the company can absorb a lot of gold price decline before it stops making money.
- **Net cash** means the company holds more cash (\$3,464M) than debt (\$197M). Most miners are the other way round. It lets AEM fund a \$2.6–\$2.8 billion capital programme, pay a dividend and buy back stock at the same time.
- **Free cash flow** is what is left after operating costs and capital spending. AEM generated a record \$1,335 million in Q2 2026 alone. It will be lower in 2026 than 2025 because the company is building Hope Bay and paying \$3.4–\$3.6 billion of tax on last year's profits — a good problem, not a deteriorating one.
- **The Barnat problem** in plain terms: a wall of an open pit mine in Quebec gave way. Nobody was hurt. The affected rock will be left in place and the pit redesigned around it. The cost is about 60,000–80,000 ounces of lost gold this year and up to 150,000 ounces in each of 2027 and 2028 — on a base of roughly 3.4 million ounces a year, so a 2–4% production hit, not an existential one.
- **Why the stock fell** is mostly not about the company. Gold dropped from \$4,696/oz in August to about \$4,150–\$4,300/oz by late September because long-term US interest rates rose sharply and traders started expecting the Federal Reserve to raise rates in October. Gold miners amplify gold moves in both directions.
- **What to watch** is the October 28 results for confirmation that Barnat remediation is on track, and the gold price itself. Everything else in the thesis is already known.
