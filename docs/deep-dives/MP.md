# MP — MP Materials Corp.

MP Materials (NYSE: MP) owns Mountain Pass in California — the only integrated rare earth mine and processing facility in the Western Hemisphere — and is racing to build a domestic magnet supply chain to challenge China's grip on rare earths. At \$41.28 (2026-07-29), the stock sits at a fresh 52-week low, having given back most of its 2025 surge. A July 2025 Department of Defense partnership made the Pentagon its largest shareholder, set a \$110/kg NdPr price floor, and locked in a 10-year magnet offtake with an EBITDA guarantee, while a \$500M Apple agreement anchors commercial demand. The catch is that MP is still deeply unprofitable (FY2025 net loss \$85.9M, free cash flow -\$328M) and priced on a magnet ramp that does not commission until 2028. That combination — a powerful strategic thesis with binary execution risk — earns a **SPEC. BUY** with a **\$52–60** base case.

## Company Overview

**Business model.** MP Materials is a rare earth producer transitioning into an integrated "mine-to-magnet" company. It operates in two segments:

| Segment | What it does | FY2025 revenue |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Materials** | Mines and processes rare earth ore at Mountain Pass; sells rare earth concentrate and refined NdPr oxide/metal | ~\$203M |
| **Magnetics** | Manufactures NdFeB magnets and precursors (Independence facility, Fort Worth, TX); building the "10X" campus | ~\$21M |

Mountain Pass, in San Bernardino County, California, is the only operational rare earth mine and processing site in the United States. Neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) — the key input for the permanent magnets in EV motors, wind turbines, robotics, defense systems, and AI-era hardware — is MP's core product.

**Moat.** MP's edge is strategic scarcity, not cost. China controls the overwhelming majority of global rare earth mining and nearly all magnet production, so a vertically integrated Western producer with a Tier 1 orebody, permits, and government backing is extraordinarily difficult to replicate. That position is now reinforced by U.S. policy: the Pentagon is the largest shareholder, and the thesis is explicitly a national-security reshoring play.

**Government and commercial anchors.** The July 2025 DoD public-private partnership includes a \$400M convertible preferred equity investment, a \$150M low-interest loan for heavy rare earth separation, a \$110/kg price floor on NdPr products, a \$140M minimum-EBITDA guarantee on a new 10,000 metric ton magnet facility, and a 10-year magnet offtake. Separately, Apple committed \$500M for U.S.-made recycled magnets.

**Management.** Founder and CEO James Litinsky has steered MP from a distressed mine acquisition into the centerpiece of U.S. rare earth policy, prioritizing downstream magnet capacity over near-term margins. Strategy: scale NdPr refining, execute the 10X magnet campus, and convert government and commercial contracts into durable, high-value magnet revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was \$224.4M, up 10.1% YoY, driven by higher NdPr oxide and metal sales as MP shifted from selling raw concentrate to refined products. Gross margin was thin at 14.1%, and the company posted an operating loss of ~\$200M and a net loss of \$85.9M (diluted EPS -\$0.50) as it absorbed a cost base built for far greater throughput than current volumes carry.

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | \$224.4M (+10.1% YoY) | \$203.9M |
| Gross margin | 14.1% | 5.5% |
| Operating income | -\$200.4M | -\$169.4M |
| Net income | -\$85.9M | -\$65.4M |
| Diluted EPS | -\$0.50 | -\$0.57 |

**Recent momentum.** Q1 2026 (reported May 7, 2026) revenue rose 49.1% YoY to \$90.6M — Materials \$72.2M (with \$42.3M of PPA income and \$36.7M adjusted EBITDA) and Magnetics \$21.1M (\$9.6M adjusted EBITDA) — with record NdPr oxide production and sales, and MP broke ground on the 10X facility. Q2 2026 results are due August 6, 2026.

**Balance sheet.** This is the crucial offset to the losses. MP held ~\$1.83B in cash and investments at year-end 2025 against ~\$1.0B of debt, for a net cash position of ~\$832M and a current ratio above 7. That war chest — bolstered by the DoD capital — funds the magnet build-out through the ramp.

**Cash flow.** Free cash flow was deeply negative at -\$328M in FY2025 (operating cash flow -\$156M, capex -\$172M) as MP invested ahead of revenue. Cash burn is the core near-term risk and the reason a strong balance sheet matters so much.

## Valuation

MP cannot be valued on current earnings — it is loss-making, with a forward P/E near 150x and a price-to-sales ratio around 21x. The equity is a forward option on Western magnet capacity, so the scenarios below map execution and rare earth pricing to a 12-month value range.

| Scenario | Setup | Target |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **Bull** | 10X commissions on schedule; NdPr recovers well above the \$110/kg floor; DoD/Apple offtake pulls profitability forward; policy tailwinds reward the sole U.S. producer | **\$70–90** |
| **Base** | Revenue keeps compounding ~40-50% off a small base; floor and contracts stabilize demand; 10X stays on track for 2028; stock re-rates toward its \$56–59 moving averages | **\$52–60** |
| **Bear** | 10X slips or overruns; NdPr stays weak amid Chinese oversupply; heavy burn forces dilution; growth multiple compresses | **\$28–36** |

**Analyst view.** Consensus is Buy with an average 12-month target around \$79–80 — well above the current \$41.28 and above even my bull case, reflecting Wall Street's confidence in the magnet ramp. Recent notes: Canaccord \$82, Needham Buy \$73 (cut from \$81), Morgan Stanley Overweight \$71.50, Citigroup positive, and Deutsche Bank Buy \$61 (cut from \$70). Not all are bullish — Wall Street Zen downgraded to Sell — underscoring the wide dispersion of views. My base case of \$52–60 is deliberately more conservative than consensus given the cash burn and 2028 timeline.

## Growth Catalysts

**1. The 10X magnet campus.** In February 2026 MP selected a 120-acre site in Northlake, Texas for "10X," a large-scale rare earth magnet manufacturing campus. MP expects to invest more than \$1.25B (supported by ~\$200M of state and local incentives), create 1,500+ jobs, and begin commissioning in 2028. This is the single biggest value driver — it turns MP from a materials seller into a magnet producer.

**2. DoD partnership.** The Pentagon's \$400M equity stake, \$150M heavy-rare-earth loan, \$110/kg NdPr price floor, \$140M minimum-EBITDA guarantee, and 10-year offtake collectively de-risk both financing and demand — a structural advantage no competitor enjoys.

**3. Apple and commercial offtake.** A \$500M Apple agreement for U.S.-made recycled magnets anchors blue-chip commercial demand and validates the magnet business beyond defense.

**4. Rare earth pricing and reshoring policy.** Tightening ex-China supply for EVs, robotics, defense, and AI hardware — plus tariffs, export controls, and reshoring incentives — favor the only integrated U.S. producer. NdPr prices above the \$110/kg floor are direct upside.

**5. Downstream integration.** Moving up the value chain from concentrate to NdPr oxide to finished magnets steadily lifts revenue per ton and margins as volumes scale.

## Risk Factors

**Unprofitability and cash burn.** MP lost \$85.9M in FY2025 with -\$328M free cash flow. The profitability inflection depends on a magnet ramp that does not commission until 2028; until then, the company burns cash and leans on its ~\$1.8B balance sheet.

**Valuation.** At a forward P/E near 150x and P/S around 21x, expectations are steep. Any execution stumble — or a broad de-rating of growth names — could compress the multiple sharply, which is exactly what the bear case (\$28–36) captures.

**Execution risk on 10X.** A greenfield magnet campus costing more than \$1.25B carries real budget, schedule, and ramp risk. Magnet manufacturing at scale is new territory for MP.

**Rare earth prices and China.** China dominates rare earth supply, pricing, and processing and can flood the market. Weak NdPr prices would leave MP dependent on the DoD floor and undercut the growth narrative.

**Single-site concentration.** Mountain Pass is the whole upstream business. Any operational, geological, or permitting setback there hits the entire thesis.

**Policy dependence.** The investment case leans heavily on continued government support. Shifts in defense priorities, tariffs, or incentives would change the risk/reward.

**Dilution.** The DoD convertible preferred and future capital needs create ongoing dilution risk.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY** — a high-conviction thesis paired with genuinely binary execution risk, appropriate only for investors who can tolerate volatility and a multi-year horizon.

**Thesis in one line.** The only integrated U.S. rare earth producer, backed by the Pentagon and Apple, is building the Western magnet supply chain — but the payoff waits on a 2028 ramp, and the stock is priced for it.

**Scenario targets:** Bull \$70–90 · Base \$52–60 · Bear \$28–36.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: MP Materials runs the only rare earth mine in America (Mountain Pass, California) and is building factories to turn those minerals into the powerful magnets that go inside electric-car motors, robots, wind turbines, weapons, and AI hardware. Right now almost all of these magnets come from China, and the U.S. wants its own supply — so the Pentagon invested in MP, became its biggest shareholder, guaranteed a minimum price for MP's key material, and promised to buy magnets for 10 years. Apple also signed up to buy \$500M of magnets.

The problem: MP is losing money (a \$85.9M loss last year) and spending heavily to build a giant new magnet plant in Texas that will not open until 2028. It has a big pile of cash (~\$1.8B) to get there, but the stock has fallen to a one-year low around \$41 because investors are impatient for profits.

The bet: if MP builds the plant on time and rare earth prices hold up, the stock could be worth \$52–60 (base case) and \$70–90 if things go really well. If the plant is delayed or prices stay weak, it could drop to \$28–36. Because the outcome is genuinely uncertain but the upside is large and the government has its back, this is a **SPEC. BUY** — a small, high-risk position, not a core holding.
