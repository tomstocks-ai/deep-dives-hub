# RDW — Redwire Corporation

Redwire is a \$2.8B space and defense technology company whose financial profile was rebuilt by the 2025 Edge Autonomy acquisition. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of \$117.1M (+89.6% YoY), a record 27.8% gross margin, and record contracted backlog of \$542.1M (+64.5% YoY), while a large 2026 equity raise left \$557M of cash against just \$50M of term loans. The catch: the legacy Space segment is shrinking, consolidated Adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow are still negative, and at \$11.20 the stock trades ~58% below its May 2026 record high after the SpaceX IPO derated the entire space complex. Rated **SPEC. BUY** — a backlog-conversion story with a funded balance sheet and real execution risk.

## Company Overview

Redwire Corporation (NYSE: RDW) was assembled in 2020 through a roll-up of space heritage businesses, went public via SPAC on 3 September 2021, and now runs two reportable segments with roughly 1,410 employees across North America and Europe.

**Space (\$55.2M of Q2 2026 revenue, 47% of the total).** This is the original franchise: star trackers, sun sensors and other sensors and avionics; camera systems and infrared, space-situational-awareness and position-navigation-timing payloads; deployable structures and solar arrays; digital engineering software; and spacecraft platforms including the SabreSat very-low-Earth-orbit (VLEO) vehicle selected for DARPA's Otter programme (a \$44M phase 2 award in Q4 2025). It also owns the most commercially interesting in-space manufacturing franchise among small caps: eleven active payload facilities on the ISS, more than 50 PIL-BOX pharmaceutical crystallisation flights since November 2023, and a wholly owned venture, SpaceMD, commercialising microgravity drug development with partners including Bristol Myers Squibb, Aspera Biomedicines, Purdue and Rowan. Lunar and deep-space exposure comes through regolith-based construction work (Mason), berthing and docking hardware — two International Berthing and Docking Mechanisms for The Exploration Company's Nyx spacecraft — and European Space Agency programme content.

**Defense Tech (\$61.9M of Q2 2026 revenue, 53%).** Created by the Edge Autonomy acquisition that closed in Q2 2025, this segment sells combat-proven uncrewed aerial systems — Stalker and Penguin — plus Octopus electro-optical/infrared ISR payloads. Q2 2026 brought follow-on Stalker Block 30 awards from the Marine Corps Portfolio Acquisition Executive Robotic Autonomous Systems and the US Army Aviation Center of Excellence, a high-eight-figure multi-year Penguin contract from an undisclosed NATO country, a Penguin Mk2.5 award for the Taiwan Coast Guard, and nearly 200 Octopus payload deliveries year to date (up more than 15% YoY).

The moat is heritage and qualification, not scale. Flight-proven components, ISS payload facilities and cleared facility status are difficult and slow to replicate, and Redwire sits in the supplier layer rather than competing head-on with SpaceX. That is also the weakness: as a payload and component supplier, Redwire absorbs margin pressure when launch capacity is scarce and pricing power sits with the launch provider. Management is Chairman, CEO and President Peter Cannito with CFO Chris Edmunds; both have run the business through the Edge Autonomy integration and the 2026 recapitalisation.

## Financial Analysis

**Latest quarter (Q2 2026, reported 5 August 2026).** Revenue \$117.1M, +89.6% YoY and +20.7% sequentially, beating the \$105M consensus by 11.4%. Gross profit \$32.5M for a 27.8% margin, versus -30.9% in Q2 2025 when fixed-price programme charges gutted the P and L. Net loss improved \$56.0M YoY to -\$41.0M (-\$0.19 per share); adjusted EPS was -\$0.09 versus -\$0.31. Adjusted EBITDA improved \$24.2M YoY to -\$3.2M, and that figure absorbed \$12.5M of R and D spend in the quarter.

**Segments tell the real story.** Defense Tech revenue grew from \$5.1M to \$61.9M YoY with segment adjusted EBITDA of +\$14.1M (versus a -\$15.0M loss). Space revenue fell 3% YoY to \$55.2M and swung to a -\$4.2M segment adjusted EBITDA loss as R and D rose. Consolidated profitability is therefore a bet on Defense Tech scaling faster than Space deteriorates.

**Bookings and backlog.** Contracted backlog reached a record \$542.1M at 30 June 2026, up 64.5% YoY and up from \$411.2M at year-end 2025, split \$322.0M Space / \$220.2M Defense Tech. Q2 book-to-bill was 1.42 and the last-twelve-month ratio 1.52. Management said visibility to the FY2026 revenue midpoint was in the 90% range.

**Full year and guidance.** FY2025 revenue was \$335.4M (+10.3%), with a net loss of about -\$272.3M, and Edge Autonomy contributed \$107.1M post-acquisition. H1 2026 revenue was \$214.0M with a -\$117.5M net loss. Management reaffirmed FY2026 revenue of \$450M–\$500M, a midpoint of \$475M implying ~41.6% growth. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through Q2 2026 was \$426.3M (+63.1%) with TTM EPS of -\$1.41.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The transformation here is dramatic. H1 2026 financing brought \$566.2M of common stock proceeds; the company repaid debt aggressively, cutting term loans from \$90.0M to \$50.0M during Q2, and ended June with \$557.0M of cash and \$607.8M of total liquidity, up 366.9% from year-end 2025. Total assets were \$1.94B against \$313.5M of total liabilities and \$1.63B of equity; all convertible preferred stock has been retired. The cost was dilution: shares outstanding went from 191.9M to 249.2M in six months.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was -\$31.6M and free cash flow -\$48.0M, a large improvement on -\$142.7M a year earlier but still a burn, driven by working capital: contract assets +\$28.4M and inventory +\$30.2M as UAS deliveries accelerate. Capex was \$16.4M in H1. Goodwill of \$772.2M and intangibles of \$319.1M mean roughly two-thirds of the asset base is acquisition-related.

## Valuation

At \$11.20 with ~249.0M shares, market cap is ~\$2.79B. Netting \$557M of cash against ~\$48M of debt gives an enterprise value near \$2.28B, or ~4.8x the \$475M FY2026 revenue midpoint. Zacks put the stock at 4.88x forward twelve-month sales on 3 September 2026 against 2.38x for the aerospace and defense sub-industry and a five-year median of 1.38x — so the 58% drawdown from the high has not made RDW statistically cheap. There is no P/E: the company is loss-making, and EV/EBITDA is meaningless until Adjusted EBITDA crosses zero.

The honest framing is that RDW is priced on 2027–2028 outcomes. Analyst consensus is a ~\$14.69 twelve-month target across 12 analysts, with a range of \$8.00 (Bank of America, underperform, raised from \$7.00 on 31 August 2026) to \$22.00; Guggenheim initiated at Neutral on 14 September 2026. Rating tallies differ by provider — MarketBeat shows a consensus Hold (1 strong buy, 5 buy, 4 hold, 2 sell) while S and P Global data shows Buy — which is itself a fair description of the debate.

**Scenarios.**

- **Bull: \$19–25.** FY2026 lands at or above the \$500M top end, Defense Tech holds ~20%+ segment EBITDA margins, Space returns to growth on VLEO and docking hardware, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA turns positive in 2027. On ~\$650M of 2027 revenue that implies roughly 7–9x EV/sales — a premium the market has paid for space names during risk-on phases, and roughly a return to the May 2026 record high of \$25.90.
- **Base: \$13–16.** FY2026 near the \$475M midpoint, gross margin in the mid-to-high 20s, Adjusted EBITDA modestly negative for the year, free cash flow negative but improving, no further large equity raise. Roughly 4–5x EV to forward sales, in line with the ~\$14.69 consensus target.
- **Bear: \$6–8.** Backlog conversion slips and FY2026 misses the \$450M low end, Space stays loss-making, the at-the-market programme dilutes further, or an estimate-at-completion charge revives the 2025 margin problem. The multiple compresses toward the ~2.4x peer average, which maps to the Bank of America \$8 target and below — the 52-week low is \$4.87.

## Growth Catalysts

- **H2 2026 backlog conversion.** A \$542.1M backlog against a \$450M–\$500M annual guide and \$214.0M already booked in H1 means the second half is arithmetically covered if programme timing holds; management put visibility at roughly 90% of the midpoint.
- **NITE-STAR IDIQ.** On 24 September 2026 Redwire was selected as one of 15 vendors on Space Systems Command's NITE-STAR indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity vehicle with a ceiling above \$980M. Nothing is funded until task orders land, but it is a seat at the table for tactical space awards.
- **Uncrewed systems demand.** European rearmament, NATO-country Penguin orders, Taiwan Coast Guard deliveries and repeat US Marine Corps and Army Stalker awards are funded, recurring-consumable-like demand. The Huntsville, Alabama expansion and the new Georgetown, Indiana facility add the capacity to deliver it.
- **VLEO franchise.** The DARPA Otter phase 2 award (\$44M) leverages SabreSat and positions Redwire in very low Earth orbit, a sub-orbit-regime niche with defence ISR applications and few credible suppliers.
- **In-space pharmaceuticals.** SpaceMD's first commercial mission on SpaceX's Starfall spacecraft, more than 50 PIL-BOX flights to date, and partnerships with Bristol Myers Squibb and academic groups make this the closest thing to a real commercial microgravity manufacturing business. It is an R and D cost today; any contracted pharma revenue would be a genuine re-rating event.
- **Balance sheet optionality.** With \$557M of cash and only \$50M of term debt, Redwire can fund capacity, R and D and bolt-on M and A — it has already made a strategic investment in next-generation phased-array antenna technology and signed a teaming agreement with Kanematsu for Japan — without returning to the equity market on bad terms.

## Risk Factors

- **Still unprofitable and cash-consumptive.** -\$117.5M net loss and -\$48.0M free cash flow in H1 2026. Working capital builds with every UAS delivery ramp, so growth consumes cash before it produces it.
- **Dilution.** The share count rose 30% in six months, from 191.9M to 249.2M, and a \$500M at-the-market programme announced in June 2026 remains a standing overhang. The June 2026 announcement itself coincided with a 50% monthly drawdown.
- **Space segment stagnation.** Revenue down 3% YoY with a negative segment EBITDA means the "space" half of a space company is currently a drag. If that persists, the story becomes a mid-cap drone supplier trading on a space multiple.
- **Contract accounting.** Fixed-price programmes produced a -30.9% gross margin in Q2 2025; H1 2026 still carried \$1.1M of net unfavourable estimate-at-completion adjustments. This line item has burned shareholders before.
- **Internal controls.** The company continues to disclose material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, with advisory spend directed at remediation. That is a real reporting-risk discount, not a technicality.
- **Government and launch dependency.** US and European budget cycles, continuing resolutions and shutdown risk drive award timing, and orbital payload revenue depends on third-party launch schedules that Redwire does not control.
- **Sentiment and supply.** Beta is ~3.1, institutional ownership only ~8.1%, and in September 2026 director entity AE Red Holdings sold 929,435 shares at ~\$11.34, cutting its stake ~86%, with a 10% owner exercising warrants and signalling further sales. Thin institutional support plus insider supply makes the tape fragile.
- **Goodwill.** \$772.2M of goodwill on \$1.63B of equity leaves impairment risk if the Edge Autonomy growth case falters.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** Targets: Bull \$19–25 · Base \$13–16 · Bear \$6–8.

The asymmetry has genuinely improved. A year ago RDW was a leveraged, loss-making roll-up with negative gross margin and \$90M of term loans; today it has record revenue, record gross margin, record backlog, \$557M of cash, \$50M of debt and a profitable Defense Tech segment — and the share price is roughly where it was before any of that was true. What has not improved is profitability and free cash flow, which is why this is speculative rather than a core holding.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a high-volatility speculative position: 1–2% of a diversified portfolio, and size it as one of several space names rather than the only one. Beta near 3.1 means a 2% position behaves like a 6% market position.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is pinned to its moving averages — \$11.20 against a 50-day of \$10.97 and a 200-day of \$10.99 — with the \$10–11 zone having acted as support through July, August and September. Accumulate in thirds into the \$10–11.50 band; add on confirmation, meaning a Q3 print that shows H2 backlog conversion and Adjusted EBITDA closer to breakeven. Chasing strength above \$14 forfeits most of the base-case upside.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$9.50 breaks the multi-month base and the 200-day average; below \$8 the bear case is in control and the Bank of America target becomes the reference point. Because the equity is now cash-rich, a fall to the \$4.87 52-week low would require an operational failure, not a funding scare.

**Catalyst calendar.** Q3 2026 earnings, estimated 4 November 2026 (the decisive near-term event); first NITE-STAR task orders in Q4 2026; FY2026 results and first FY2027 guidance around February 2027; DARPA Otter/SabreSat VLEO mission in 2027; SpaceMD commercial missions ongoing.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Overall: mixed, leaning cautious — score 5.5/10.**

News tone is constructive on operations and sceptical on the stock. Coverage of Q2 2026 emphasised record revenue, record margin and record backlog, but the dominant headline frame through September was drawdown and insider selling: "RDW After a 30.5% 3-Month Drop", "Redwire slides as space stocks pull back and no fresh company catalyst emerges", "Stock Price Down 3.6% Following Insider Selling".

**Sell-side.** Consensus target ~\$14.69 across 12 analysts (range \$8–\$22), with MarketBeat showing a Hold consensus and S and P Global data showing Buy. Zacks carries a Rank 3 (Hold) with an explicit "premium valuation raises the rebound hurdle" argument. Guggenheim initiated at Neutral on 14 September 2026; Bank of America remains at underperform with a \$8 target, nudged up from \$7 on 31 August 2026. No one is euphoric.

**Retail and social.** RDW is a well-known retail space name and trades heavy volume (11.9M shares on 26 September 2026). Retail commentary flipped hard after the SpaceX IPO on 12 June 2026: the stock set a record \$25.90 on 28 May 2026, fell 50.2% during June following the \$500M at-the-market announcement and the SpaceX listing, dropped a further 43% between 12 and 20 July, and has since built a base around \$10–12. Motley Fool and 247 Wall St coverage frames the sector as "sitting in neutral waiting on one rocket" — Starship cadence — with Redwire described as the strongest performer among space peers over the past month (RDW -6% versus RKLB -8% and LUNR -16%).

**Positioning and flow.** Institutional ownership is only ~8.1%, though Bank of America initiated a 1.07M-share (~\$13.1M) position in Q2 2026, which is a notable contradiction of its own underperform rating on the research side. Options flow was skewed bullish earlier in 2026 (call-to-put activity reported at roughly 3.6-to-1 in June), but the more actionable signal now is insider supply: AE Red Holdings sold 929,435 shares at ~\$11.34 on 23 September 2026, cutting its holding ~86%, and a 10% holder has signalled sales after exercising warrants.

**Read-through.** Sentiment is neither capitulation nor enthusiasm — it is a wait-for-the-print stance. That is usually the right environment to build a position in a company whose fundamentals are improving faster than its narrative, provided position size respects the volatility.

## Readability Pass

Redwire makes the parts and platforms other people need in space — star trackers, solar arrays, docking mechanisms, ISS research lockers — and, since buying Edge Autonomy in 2025, it also makes battlefield drones (Stalker, Penguin) and the camera payloads that fly on them. The drone half is now bigger and profitable; the space half is flat and losing money at the segment level.

The numbers moved a long way in a year. Last quarter revenue nearly doubled to \$117M, gross margin flipped from deeply negative to a record 27.8%, and signed-but-unbuilt work hit a record \$542M. A large share sale in 2026 raised over \$560M, which paid down debt and left \$557M of cash against just \$50M of loans. The company still loses money and still burns cash, but it is no longer at risk of running out of it.

The share price tells a different story: \$25.90 in late May 2026, \$11.20 now. The fall was not caused by bad results — it followed the SpaceX IPO, which reset how investors value everything in space, plus a \$500M share-sale programme that diluted existing holders.

So the question is simple. If Redwire converts its backlog, keeps drone margins where they are and stops losing money in space, the stock is worth \$19–25. If it just does what it says it will do this year, \$13–16 is fair, which is about where analysts sit. If the backlog slips or it has to sell more shares, \$6–8. Given a funded balance sheet and improving operations, that is a reasonable speculative bet at a small position size — but the emphasis stays on speculative until free cash flow turns.
