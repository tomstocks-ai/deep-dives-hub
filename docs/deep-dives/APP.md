# APP — AppLovin Corporation

AppLovin is a pure-play AI advertising platform that matches app and e-commerce advertisers with mobile inventory through its AXON recommendation engine and MAX mediation stack. The business is extraordinarily profitable — 84% adjusted EBITDA margins, \$3.95B of FY2025 free cash flow on \$5.48B of revenue — yet the stock has lost 57% over the past year and trades at \$308.24, barely above its \$297.50 52-week low. The de-rating came from a single narrow Q2 2026 revenue miss (\$1.924B vs ~\$1.94B consensus) that broke a long beat-and-raise streak, followed by analyst downgrades, channel checks suggesting MAX market share has plateaued, and a securities-fraud class action filed in September 2026. Growth is still 53% year over year and the stock now trades near 19x estimated FY2026 earnings, which is the crux of the debate: a mispriced compounder, or a decelerating ad network whose spread is being competed away.

## Company Overview

**Business model.** AppLovin operates a single reportable advertising segment following the 2025 divestiture of its Apps (mobile gaming studio) portfolio, which generated ~\$407M of net proceeds and a \$106M gain and is reported as discontinued operations. The remaining business is software: advertisers bid to acquire users, AXON — the machine-learning bidding and creative-matching engine — decides which ad to show to which user, and AppLovin keeps a spread between what the advertiser pays and what it pays the publisher for the impression. MAX, the in-app bidding and mediation layer, aggregates supply from thousands of mobile games and gives AXON a privileged view of auction dynamics across the ecosystem.

**Revenue drivers.** Three vectors matter. Core mobile gaming user acquisition is the mature base and still the majority of revenue. E-commerce and direct-to-consumer web advertising is the growth vector management has leaned into hardest since 2024, extending AXON beyond app installs into retail and brand spend. Self-service tooling — AppLovin's Ads Manager — is the mechanism intended to scale the long tail of advertisers without adding headcount, and management flagged a product launch and "explicit momentum in the consumer vertical" on the Q2 2026 call.

**Moat.** The moat is a data-and-scale flywheel rather than intellectual property. More advertiser spend means more conversion signal, which improves AXON's targeting, which raises return on ad spend, which attracts more spend. MAX's mediation position means AppLovin sees bid-level data its competitors do not. The counter-argument — and it is the bear case — is that this flywheel has a natural ceiling: once MAX has aggregated most available mobile gaming supply, incremental share gains require either taking wallet from Meta and Google in categories where they are stronger, or accepting a thinner spread.

**Management.** Co-founder Adam Foroughi remains CEO and is the architect of both the Apps divestiture and the AXON pivot. CFO Matt Stumpf confirmed on the Q2 2026 call that the SEC inquiry opened in October 2025 into AXON's data practices was closed with no action. Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-friendly: \$2.58B of stock repurchased and withheld in FY2025 and \$1.53B in the first half of 2026, shrinking the share count from 340M at the end of 2024 to 335M at 30 June 2026 despite heavy equity compensation.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was \$5.481B, up 70% from \$3.224B, with cost of revenue of \$665M implying a gross margin near 88%. Operating income was \$4.152B, net income \$3.334B (\$3.433B from continuing operations), and diluted EPS \$9.75. Adjusted EBITDA reached \$4.512B at an 82% margin. Operating leverage is the standout: total costs and expenses were essentially flat year over year at \$1.329B versus \$1.313B, so nearly all incremental revenue dropped to operating income.

**Most recent quarter (Q2 2026, reported 5 August 2026).** Revenue \$1.924B, up 53% year over year and 4% sequentially. Net income \$1.267B (66% net margin), diluted EPS \$3.76. Adjusted EBITDA \$1.614B at an 84% margin. Gross margin was ~88%. First-half 2026 revenue of \$3.766B was up 56% with diluted EPS of \$7.32. The miss was small — roughly \$16M against consensus — and management attributed it to the timing of model improvements: CEO Foroughi said "our pace of meaningful model improvement was lighter than normal during the quarter and the next step-up in model performance landed just after quarter end." Notably, research and development more than doubled year over year to \$99.9M in the quarter, and the company cited higher compute spend to train improved models, which is the first visible crack in the flat-cost story.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and equivalents of \$3.053B at 30 June 2026 against \$3.515B of long-term debt, so net debt is roughly \$460M — negligible against \$4B+ of annual cash generation. Accounts receivable of \$2.171B is large relative to quarterly revenue and has been a persistent working-capital drag (\$353M outflow in the first half). Goodwill of \$1.519B and intangibles of \$356M are modest, though FY2025 did include a \$189M goodwill impairment and \$50M of investment impairment.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was \$3.971B and free cash flow \$3.952B, up 91%. In Q2 2026 alone, operating cash flow was \$869M and free cash flow \$863M. Capital intensity is almost nil — \$473K of property and equipment purchases in all of FY2025 — because compute is rented, not owned. First-half 2026 operating cash flow was \$2.160B.

**Guidance.** Q3 2026 revenue is guided to \$2.055–\$2.085B with adjusted EBITDA of \$1.710–\$1.740B at an 83% margin. That 83% is a step down from 84% and the market read it as the beginning of margin normalisation as compute costs rise.

## Valuation

At \$308.24 with 335.3M shares outstanding, market capitalisation is approximately \$103B and enterprise value roughly \$104B after net debt.

On reported trailing figures the stock is ~31x FY2025 diluted EPS of \$9.75 and ~26x FY2025 free cash flow. On forward figures it is far cheaper. Annualising first-half EPS of \$7.32 and applying Q3 guidance plus a normal seasonal Q4, consensus FY2026 EPS sits around \$15.50–\$16.00 (consensus estimate, not company-guided — AppLovin guides revenue and adjusted EBITDA only), putting the multiple near 19–20x. EV to annualised free cash flow is roughly 23x. For a business compounding revenue above 50% with 84% EBITDA margins, that is a deep-value multiple by software standards — and it reflects genuine market scepticism that the growth rate is durable rather than an obvious inefficiency.

Sell-side consensus is "Moderate Buy" with an average price target near \$529 as of late September 2026, implying ~72% upside. That consensus is stale relative to the post-Q2 downgrade wave: Piper Sandler cut to Neutral on 6 August 2026 and slashed its target from \$665 to \$385, citing "more questions than answers on beat/raise cadence from here," and Bank of America cut from Buy to Neutral on 11 August 2026. Treat the \$529 average as an upper anchor rather than a central estimate.

**Bull case — \$520–\$600.** The Q2 stumble proves to have been genuine timing. The model step-up that landed just after quarter end shows up in Q3 and Q4 prints, e-commerce and self-service scale the advertiser base beyond gaming, and revenue growth holds above 35% into 2027 with EBITDA margin stabilising in the 83–84% band. FY2027 EPS reaches roughly \$20 and the market pays 27–30x for a capital-light compounder with a shrinking share count. The class action settles or is dismissed without material damages, removing the governance discount.

**Base case — \$390–\$450.** Growth decelerates as Edgewater's channel checks suggest — MAX supply aggregation has hit a functional ceiling and competition compresses net revenue spreads — but the absolute business remains exceptional: revenue growth settles in the 25–35% range through 2027, margins hold near 82–83%, and FY2027 EPS lands near \$19–\$20. The market applies a 20–23x multiple, reflecting durable profitability but no re-acceleration premium. Buybacks at depressed prices add a few percent a year to per-share figures.

**Bear case — \$210–\$260.** The spread compression thesis is right and structural. Growth falls toward 15% as MAX share plateaus, Meta and Google defend e-commerce budgets, and rising compute costs push EBITDA margin below 80%. FY2027 EPS stalls near \$17 and the multiple compresses to 12–15x as AppLovin is re-rated from AI platform to cyclical ad network. The class action produces damaging discovery about how AXON's performance claims were made, adding a governance discount on top.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 results (expected early-to-mid November 2026).** The single most important event. Guidance is \$2.055–\$2.085B; a beat with a Q4 raise would validate the "timing, not demand" explanation and likely trigger a violent re-rating from a heavily de-rated base. A second consecutive miss confirms the bear thesis.
- **Self-service Ads Manager scaling.** Moving the long tail of advertisers onto self-service is the mechanism to grow the advertiser count without proportional cost, and it is the clearest path to breaking out of the mobile gaming supply ceiling.
- **E-commerce and web advertising expansion.** The consumer vertical is where AppLovin competes for budgets currently held by Meta, Google and Amazon. Management called out momentum here on the Q2 call; sustained triple-digit growth in this vertical would change the total addressable market conversation entirely.
- **SEC inquiry closure (confirmed August 2026).** Already banked but underappreciated — the October 2025 inquiry into AXON's data practices was closed with no action, removing the most serious regulatory tail risk from the short thesis.
- **Buyback at depressed prices.** With \$4B of annual free cash flow, negligible net debt and the stock down 57%, repurchases are materially more accretive than they were at \$700. First-half 2026 repurchases were already \$1.53B.
- **Resolution of the securities class action.** Lead plaintiff deadline is 16 November 2026. Dismissal or an immaterial settlement removes a discrete overhang.

## Risk Factors

- **Spread compression is the core structural risk.** Edgewater Research's September 2026 channel checks found AppLovin's share-of-wallet and share-of-voice metrics no longer rising, attributing it to MAX reaching "a functional ceiling" and to competition compressing net revenue spreads. If the take rate is being competed away rather than temporarily flat, the entire margin structure is at risk, not just the growth rate.
- **Growth deceleration from a high base.** Revenue growth has gone from 70% in FY2025 to 59% in Q1 2026 to 53% in Q2 2026, with Q3 guidance implying 7–8.6% sequential growth. Edgewater models Q4 sequential growth of only 8–9%. Deceleration is visible and the market is now pricing the trajectory, not the level.
- **Rising compute costs compress margins.** R&D more than doubled to \$99.9M in Q2 2026 and management cited higher compute spend to train models. Q3 EBITDA margin guidance of 83% versus 84% delivered is the first step down. The flat-cost-base operating leverage that powered FY2025 may be over.
- **Securities-fraud class action.** Filed mid-September 2026, alleging AppLovin misrepresented the strength, viability and development of its AI-based business model and products, with a 16 November 2026 lead plaintiff deadline. The allegations are unproven, but discovery risk and management distraction are real.
- **Concentration in mobile gaming advertisers.** The majority of revenue still comes from mobile game user acquisition, a market tied to consumer discretionary spend and to Apple and Google platform policy. Any further tightening of mobile identifier or attribution rules degrades AXON's signal.
- **Competitive asymmetry.** Meta, Google and Amazon have far larger first-party datasets and can subsidise performance advertising to defend share. AppLovin's edge depends on continuing to out-innovate them inside a narrow niche.
- **Model opacity.** AXON's performance is not independently verifiable by outsiders, so a single quarter of underperformance forces investors to take management's explanation on faith. That is precisely what happened in Q2 2026 and it is why the multiple contracted so violently.
- **Momentum and technical damage.** The stock is below both its 50-day (\$341.09) and 200-day (\$464.32) moving averages, with the 50-day well under the 200-day. Broken high-multiple momentum names often need multiple quarters of proof before institutional buyers return.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** The fundamentals are genuinely excellent — 53% revenue growth, 84% EBITDA margins, \$4B of annual free cash flow, negligible net debt, a shrinking share count and a closed SEC inquiry — and the valuation at ~19x estimated FY2026 earnings is anomalously low for that profile. But the reason it is cheap is a legitimate unresolved question about whether the take rate is structurally compressing. Until Q3 answers that, this is a speculative position, not a core holding.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a 1.5–3% position for a growth-oriented portfolio. The business quality justifies exposure; the binary nature of the Q3 print argues against sizing it like a compounder. Investors who cannot tolerate a further 30% drawdown on a bad print should wait for the November report.

**Entry strategy.** The stock is \$308.24, roughly 3.5% above the \$297.50 52-week low. Scale in rather than committing at once: a first tranche near current levels, a second on a retest of the \$290–\$300 low if it holds, and a third only after a Q3 beat confirms the thesis. A break below \$290 on volume invalidates the "base is forming" read and suggests waiting.

**Stop loss.** Position-level stop below \$270, roughly 12% under current price and decisively through the 52-week low. On a fundamental basis, the thesis is broken if Q3 revenue lands below the \$2.055B guidance floor or if Q4 guidance implies year-over-year growth below 30%.

**Technical context.** Price is below the 50-day moving average of \$341.09 and far below the 200-day of \$464.32, with the 50-day beneath the 200-day — a confirmed downtrend. The \$297.50 low is the reference support; \$341 (the 50-day) is first resistance, then the \$385–\$400 zone where several post-downgrade targets cluster. Late-September price action showed dip-buying and a 4% up day on 28 September 2026, which is encouraging but not yet a trend change.

## Sentiment Analysis

**News tone: negative, with the first signs of stabilisation.** Coverage through August and early September 2026 was dominated by the post-earnings collapse, the downgrade wave and law-firm litigation notices. Late September headlines shifted toward "is the selloff overdone" framing — a 26 September Motley Fool piece titled "Down 54%, Is AppLovin Still a Millionaire-Maker Stock?", a 28 September note on buyers returning, and a valuation piece arguing the shares could be 50% undervalued. That mix of capitulation and contrarian interest is characteristic of a sentiment trough, though it is not evidence of a bottom.

**Sell-side: bifurcated.** Consensus remains "Moderate Buy" with an average target near \$529, but the most recent actions are downgrades: Piper Sandler to Neutral with a \$385 target on 6 August, Bank of America to Neutral on 11 August, and Edgewater's mid-September channel-check warning on stalled market share. The gap between the \$529 average and the \$385 freshest target indicates the consensus number has not fully reset and is likely to drift lower.

**Retail and social: divided and vocal.** AppLovin has been one of the most-discussed retail momentum names of the past three years — up 280% in 2023 and roughly 700% in 2024 — so the 2026 drawdown has produced an unusually polarised holder base. Bulls anchor on the 19x forward multiple and the three-year 7x total return; bears anchor on the \$40B of market value erased by a \$16M revenue miss, which they read as evidence the prior valuation was narrative-driven. Litigation notices circulating on retail forums amplify the negative side.

**Options and positioning.** Detailed options flow was not available in the sources reviewed, so no claim is made about skew or gamma positioning. What is observable: the stock gapped up ~4% on 28 September 2026 while ad-tech peers were roughly flat, which suggests stock-specific dip-buying rather than sector rotation. Realised volatility is extreme — a 20% single-day decline on 6 August and a 28% after-hours move — so implied volatility into the November print should be assumed elevated.

**Composite sentiment score: 3.5/10** — bearish, improving from a trough. The score reflects hostile recent news flow and an unresolved litigation overhang, offset by contrarian valuation interest, the closed SEC inquiry and early signs of price stabilisation above the 52-week low.

## Readability Pass

AppLovin sells AI-driven advertising. Companies pay it to find users; its AXON system decides which ad to show to whom, and AppLovin keeps the difference between what advertisers pay and what it pays app publishers for the ad slot. It is astonishingly profitable — 84 cents of every revenue dollar reaches adjusted EBITDA, and it generated nearly \$4B of free cash in 2025 with almost no capital spending.

The stock has nonetheless halved. In August 2026 AppLovin reported revenue of \$1.92B — up 53% — but about \$16M short of what Wall Street expected. After years of beating expectations, one small miss cost roughly \$40B of market value. Management said the shortfall was timing: an improvement to its AI model landed a few days after the quarter closed. Sceptics say something more durable is happening — that AppLovin has signed up most of the mobile game inventory available to it, and that competitors are forcing it to accept a thinner cut on each ad.

So the question is simple. At \$308 the stock trades at roughly 19 times this year's estimated earnings, which is cheap for a company growing above 50%. That is only cheap if the growth lasts. The November quarterly report is the test: hit or beat the \$2.055–\$2.085B target and the shares are probably too low; miss again and the bears' structural argument wins. A securities class action filed in September adds a separate, unresolved risk. That combination — excellent business, real unanswered question — is why this is rated SPEC. BUY with targets of \$520–\$600 (bull), \$390–\$450 (base) and \$210–\$260 (bear), and why position size should stay modest until November.
