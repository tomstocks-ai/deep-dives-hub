# NEM — Newmont Corporation

Newmont is the world's largest gold producer and the only gold miner in the S&P 500. It guides 5.26 million attributable gold ounces for 2026 at by-product all-in sustaining costs (AISC) of \$1,680/oz, alongside meaningful copper, silver, lead and zinc co-product revenue. The first half of 2026 delivered sales of \$13.4B (+30% year over year), net income of \$5.46B and record free cash flow of \$5.35B at a realised gold price of \$4,661/oz. At \$116.05 (28 September 2026) the shares trade on roughly 12.8x the ~\$9.09 FY2026 consensus EPS with a net cash balance sheet — a low multiple that exists because bullion has fallen ~26% from its January 2026 peak of ~\$5,608/oz while the Federal Reserve resumed hiking. Rating: **BUY**, with targets of Bull \$150–\$170, Base \$125–\$140, Bear \$85–\$100.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1921 and listed since 1925, Newmont operates a deliberately slimmed-down "core portfolio" after divesting six non-core mines (CC&V, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine, Akyem and others) during 2025 for \$2.8B of proceeds. What remains is 13 managed and non-managed Tier-1 style assets:

- **Australia and Papua New Guinea:** Boddington, Cadia, Tanami, Lihir
- **Africa:** Ahafo South and Ahafo North (Ghana), which reached commercial production in Q4 2025
- **The Americas:** Brucejack and Red Chris (Canada), Peñasquito (Mexico), Yanacocha (Peru), Merian (Suriname, 75%), Cerro Negro (Argentina)
- **Joint ventures:** Nevada Gold Mines (38.5%, proportionately consolidated), Pueblo Viejo (40%, equity method), Lundin Gold's Fruta del Norte (32%, equity method, reported on a one-quarter lag)

The business model is straightforward scale plus by-product credits. Peñasquito is effectively a polymetallic mine whose silver, lead and zinc revenue drives gold by-product costs deeply negative (minus \$6,201/oz in Q2 2026), and Cadia and Red Chris do the same with copper. That mix is why group by-product AISC of \$1,321/oz year to date sits far below the co-product figure of \$1,822/oz, and why copper and silver prices matter to the gold margin.

The moat is reserve scale, jurisdictional spread and cost of capital rather than any single low-cost asset. Newmont carries a net cash balance sheet, an investment-grade profile and the deepest large-scale project pipeline in gold — Cadia panel caves, Tanami Expansion 2, Red Chris Block Cave, Cerro Negro Expansion 1 and the Lihir Nearshore Barrier. Natascha Viljoen became President and CEO after serving as COO, and the company announced further internal executive appointments alongside Q2 results; Peter Beaven joined the board effective 1 September 2026.

Capital allocation is formulaic and, so far, honoured. The framework prioritises \$1.95B of sustaining capital, a through-cycle cash dividend of ~\$1.1B per year (\$0.26 per quarter, \$1.04 annualised, recalculated each February off a shrinking share count), \$1.4B of development capital, a \$1B net cash target with a \$2B band, and then ratable buybacks. Since February 2024 the share count is down more than 100 million shares, roughly 9%.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was \$22.67B (+21%) with net income attributable to shareholders of \$7.09B and diluted EPS of \$6.39. The step up in 2026 is dramatic: H1 2026 sales reached \$13.43B (+30%), net income \$5.46B and diluted EPS \$5.07 (adjusted \$5.01). Q2 2026 alone produced sales of \$6.12B (+15%), net income of \$2.20B (\$2.06 diluted), adjusted net income of \$2.25B (\$2.10 per diluted share) and adjusted EBITDA of \$3.76B. Trailing-twelve-month figures are revenue of \$25.77B, net income of \$8.60B and EPS of \$7.90.

Quarterly detail matters here because Q2 was optically weaker than Q1. Realised gold fell from \$4,900/oz in Q1 to \$4,414/oz in Q2, and by-product AISC rose from \$1,029/oz to \$1,621/oz. Three things drove the cost jump: an April seismic event at Cadia that halted operations until mid-June (Cadia attributable output collapsed from 94koz to 34koz and copper production fell 43% to 17kt), a full quarter of higher Ghanaian royalties, and higher diesel. Management flagged that Q3 unit costs should rise again on heavier sustaining capital, partly offset by more Peñasquito silver volume. Sales of 1,195koz also lagged production of 1,293koz, and the revenue line missed consensus (\$6.12B versus ~\$6.35B) even as EPS beat (\$2.10 versus \$2.05).

**Balance sheet.** At 30 June 2026 Newmont held \$9.0B of cash against \$5.08B of debt and \$0.52B of leases, a net cash position of \$3.41B and total liquidity of \$13.0B including an undrawn \$4.0B revolver. Net debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA of \$16.77B is minus 0.2x. Shareholders' equity is \$35.2B on \$57.6B of assets. The main non-debt liability is \$6.18B of reclamation and remediation provisions.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was \$6.71B against \$1.36B of capital expenditure, giving record free cash flow of \$5.35B — Q2's \$2.21B was a second-quarter record. Uses were \$3.46B of buybacks and \$0.56B of dividends in the half. Two cash drags deserve attention. Reclamation spending was \$458M in H1 (including \$351M on the Yanacocha water treatment plants) and is guided to ~\$850M for 2026 before normalising to \$300–400M from 2028. And in August 2026 Newmont agreed to pay Barrick \$1.95B in cash within 30 days to settle all Nevada Gold Mines disputes, with Newmont contributing its Mike and Fiberline developments and Barrick contributing Fourmile to create a nearly 100Moz Nevada complex. That payment lands in Q3 and reduces the net cash cushion.

**Guidance.** 2026 guidance (plus or minus 5%, set in February) is 5,260koz attributable gold, by-product costs applicable to sales of \$1,055/oz, by-product AISC of \$1,680/oz, \$1.95B sustaining capital, \$1.4B development capital, \$2.82B of depreciation and a 33% adjusted tax rate — all assuming gold at \$4,500/oz, copper at \$5.00/lb and silver at \$60.00/oz. Production and spending are weighted 51% and 58–63% respectively to the second half. Sensitivity is linear and large: a \$100/oz move in gold is worth ~\$505M pre-tax, less ~\$6/oz of royalty and participation offset.

Free cash flow yield is the most compelling line. H1 FCF was \$5.35B; with heavier H2 capital and a lower gold price, a full-year figure around \$8.5–9.0B is a reasonable estimate (this is my estimate, not company-guided), implying roughly a 7% FCF yield before the one-off Barrick payment. Sell-side consensus is a Buy with an average target of \$133.78 across 24 brokers (MarketBeat) or \$136.60 across 23 (S&P Global via stockanalysis.com), a spread of \$102 to \$175. Recent target revisions run in both directions: CIBC \$170 (from \$168), Scotiabank \$149, Bernstein \$144 (from \$147), BofA \$135, Barclays \$124.

**Bull case: \$150–\$170.** Gold recovers toward \$5,000–\$5,500/oz, 2026 guidance is met with AISC below plan, and the buyback keeps shrinking the share count. On ~\$11–12 of normalised EPS at a 14x multiple this range is arithmetically reachable, and it brackets the most bullish published targets.

**Base case: \$125–\$140.** Gold averages \$4,200–\$4,600/oz through 2027, production lands near 5.26Moz, FY2026 EPS approximates consensus, and the multiple holds in a 13–15x band. This overlaps the \$133.78–\$136.60 consensus and implies 8% to 21% upside.

**Bear case: \$85–\$100.** Gold retraces to \$3,500–\$3,800/oz on continued Fed tightening, costs run above guidance, and the multiple compresses at the same time earnings fall. Note the stock traded near \$94–\$97 as recently as late June and early July 2026, so this range is not hypothetical.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 results on 22 October.** Management has already guided to flat sequential production and higher unit costs and development capital, so the interest is in cost control, Cadia's recovery and the cash impact of the Barrick payment.
- **Red Chris Block Cave FID.** British Columbia granted an amended Environmental Assessment Certificate — achieved through a consent-based process with the Tahltan Nation — plus an amended Mines Act permit. A positive final investment decision would add a long-life, low-cost copper-gold block cave.
- **Nevada consolidation.** Folding Fourmile, Mike and Fiberline into Nevada Gold Mines creates a nearly 100Moz gold complex with modernised JV governance. Newmont's 38.5% share of a larger, better-sequenced Nevada is the offset to the \$1.95B cheque.
- **Project pipeline in execution.** Cadia PC1-2 (deferred from H1 after the seismic event), the Lihir Nearshore Barrier starting in H2 2026, Cerro Negro Expansion 1, Tanami Expansion 2 and the Pueblo Viejo expansion.
- **Ahafo North ramp.** Commercial production was achieved in Q4 2025 and output is guided to rise sequentially through 2026, at an AISC of \$1,448/oz year to date — below group average.
- **Per-share compounding.** \$4.3B remains under the \$6B repurchase authorisation and management intends to seek a further authorisation as it approaches completion. Because the dividend pool is fixed in dollars and divided by a falling share count, buybacks mechanically raise dividend per share each February.
- **Reclamation cliff edge.** Yanacocha water treatment spend of ~\$550M in 2026 falls away once the \$1.8B programme completes, with total reclamation spending guided back to \$300–400M from 2028 — a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual free cash flow tailwind.

## Risk Factors

**The gold price dominates everything else.** Bullion peaked at ~\$5,608/oz in January 2026 and closed near \$4,149/oz on 28 September, a 26% drawdown, with a secondary peak of ~\$4,696/oz on 25 August. Spot is now below the \$4,500/oz guidance assumption; at \$505M of pre-tax revenue per \$100/oz, a sustained \$4,100/oz costs roughly \$2B annualised versus plan.

**Macro regime shift.** The Federal Reserve raised its target range in September 2026 and the 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.53%, the highest since 2004. Rising real yields are the classic headwind for a non-yielding asset, and gold equities carry that beta with operating leverage on top.

**Cost inflation.** Q2 by-product AISC of \$1,621/oz was 58% above Q1 on Ghanaian royalty increases, diesel and Cadia downtime. Royalties also scale with the gold price, so cost relief from lower bullion is partial.

**Operational and geotechnical risk.** The April 2026 Cadia seismic event is the second significant disruption in two years (Cerro Negro was suspended in Q1 2025 after safety events). Block caving and refractory ore at Lihir carry inherent execution risk, and the highest-cost assets — Ahafo South at \$2,604/oz, Cerro Negro at \$2,338/oz, Brucejack at \$2,156/oz, Tanami at \$2,033/oz in Q2 — have thin margins if gold falls to the bear case.

**Jurisdiction and currency.** Operations span PNG, Peru, Argentina, Ghana, Suriname, Mexico, Canada, Australia and the US. Guidance assumes AUD/USD 0.70, CAD/USD 0.75 and USD/MXN 17.00; a \$0.05 move in the Australian dollar is worth ~\$100M.

**Closure liabilities.** \$6.18B of reclamation and remediation provisions, with \$1.1B of \$1.8B spent on the Yanacocha water treatment plants. These are cash costs that do not generate ounces.

**Capital allocation.** The \$1.95B Barrick payment and continued large buybacks consume the balance sheet buffer that makes the dividend credible below \$4,000/oz gold. Management targets a \$5B minimum cash balance through the cycle, which leaves room, but a deeper gold drawdown would force a choice between buybacks and the net cash target.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** Position sizing: a core holding for precious-metals exposure, sized as the lowest-volatility way to own gold equity beta — 3–5% of a diversified portfolio, or the anchor position within a 5–10% precious-metals sleeve alongside royalty names and bullion.

The case is that you are paying ~12.8x forward earnings and ~7x EBITDA for the largest, most liquid gold producer, with net cash, a ~7% estimated free cash flow yield, a shrinking share count and a cost base running below guidance year to date. That is not an expensive entry for an asset generating \$2.2B of quarterly free cash flow. The reason it is cheap is visible and legitimate: gold is in a sharp correction and the Fed is tightening, so near-term earnings revisions are likely to go the wrong way.

**Entry strategy.** Stage entries rather than buying in one clip. The stock is sitting almost exactly on its 50-day moving average (\$116.03) and 4% above its 200-day (\$111.15), so the medium-term trend is intact but the short-term momentum is negative — it fell 4.4% on 28 September alone as gold futures dropped more than 3%. A first tranche here, a second on a retest of the 200-day near \$111, and a third only on confirmation that gold has stabilised above \$4,000/oz is the disciplined sequence. Buying strength above \$125 is also defensible for momentum-oriented accounts, since that would signal the gold correction has ended.

**Stop loss / invalidation.** A weekly close below \$100 would break the 200-day and the 2026 base structure; that is the level at which the bear case is being priced rather than feared. Fundamentally, the thesis is invalidated if gold sustains below \$3,800/oz or if AISC guidance for 2027 is set above \$1,900/oz, either of which would compress the free cash flow yield to a level that no longer compensates for the operating risk.

**Catalyst calendar.** Q3 2026 results on 22 October 2026; Red Chris Block Cave final investment decision expected around Q4 2026; FY2026 results, 2027 guidance and the annual dividend reset in February 2027; continuous buyback execution against the \$4.3B remaining authorisation; and, overriding all of it, the gold price and Fed path.

**Targets.** Bull \$150–\$170 · Base \$125–\$140 · Bear \$85–\$100.

## Sentiment Analysis

**Sell-side: constructive but trimming.** 24 brokers give a Moderate Buy consensus — 2 strong buy, 18 buy, 4 hold, no sells — with an average target of \$133.78 (MarketBeat) and \$136.60 from a 23-analyst S&P Global panel. The pattern in recent revisions is telling: targets are being nudged down from high levels rather than cut aggressively (Bernstein \$147 to \$144, CIBC \$175 to \$168 then back up to \$170, Barclays \$125 to \$124), while Scotiabank raised to \$149 and BofA to \$135. Nobody has capitulated on the thesis; they are marking gold-price decks.

**News tone: mixed, macro-driven.** Coverage in September was dominated by headlines such as "Newmont Stock Leads Gold Miners Lower as Gold Prices Plummet" and notes on the 16 September Fed hike. Company-specific news was positive — record Q2 free cash flow, the Nevada settlement removing a multi-year overhang, a board appointment, a Nevada earn-in with Headwater Gold, and a Barron's piece arguing the stock is still worth buying at around 10x earnings. The tension between good company news and bad commodity news is the whole story.

**Positioning and flow.** Short interest was 18.89M shares as of 14 August 2026, just 1.79% of float — no crowded short. Beta of 0.54 reflects gold's low correlation with equities rather than low volatility in the name itself. Institutional flow has been two-way, with visible trimming (Envestnet reduced its stake, cited in coverage of the 28 September decline) against earlier institutional inflow signals from October 2025. GF Value's model reads the shares as still overvalued, a useful dissent to note.

**Retail and social.** Gold miners drew heavy retail attention through the bullion melt-up into January 2026 and interest has cooled with the correction. Newmont keeps appearing in defensive "protect your portfolio" lists, which historically marks sentiment closer to a floor than a top. I did not find a reliable quantitative read on Reddit or X mention volumes or on options skew for this session, so treat the retail and options read as qualitative.

**Sentiment score: 6/10.** Institutional and analyst support is solid and valuation-driven; price momentum and macro sentiment are negative. That combination is typically where accumulation, not chasing, is rewarded.

## Readability Pass

Newmont digs up more gold than anyone else — about 5.3 million ounces this year — and also sells copper, silver, lead and zinc as by-products, which keeps its net cost of producing gold low.

It costs Newmont roughly \$1,300 to \$1,700 to produce an ounce. Gold currently sells for about \$4,150. That gap is enormous, which is why the company earned \$5.5B in six months and generated \$5.35B in free cash — real cash left over after all spending.

Management sends that cash back in a disciplined way: a fixed dividend pool of about \$1.1B a year, and heavy share buybacks that have retired 9% of the shares since early 2024. Fewer shares means each remaining share gets a bigger slice, including a bigger dividend.

So why is the stock down 14% from its high? Gold peaked near \$5,600 an ounce in January and has since fallen to around \$4,150, and the Federal Reserve raised interest rates in September. Higher rates make holding gold less attractive. Gold miners fall harder than gold itself because their costs stay fixed while revenue drops.

The investment question is simple. You are paying about 12.8 times this year's expected earnings for the biggest, safest operator in the sector, with more cash than debt. That is cheap — provided gold does not fall much further. If gold holds above \$4,000, the shares should work toward \$125–\$140. If gold recovers toward \$5,000, \$150–\$170 is in play. If gold slides to \$3,500–\$3,800, expect \$85–\$100. Buy in tranches rather than all at once, and watch the gold price more closely than any company announcement.
