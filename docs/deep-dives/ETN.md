# ETN — Eaton Corporation plc

Eaton is the highest-quality large-cap way to own data center electrification. It sells essentially everything between the utility line and the chip: medium-voltage switchgear, busway, breakers, UPS and power quality systems, modular power skids, grid equipment, and — since the Boyd Thermal acquisition — the cold plates and coolant distribution units that liquid-cool AI racks. Q2 2026 set records on revenue (\$8.53B, +21% total and +14% organic) and adjusted EPS (\$3.15), data center organic revenue grew ~65% against an underlying market growing 23%, and total Electrical backlog is up 43% year over year. At \$431.41 (2026-09-28) the stock trades at ~32x the guided FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of \$13.50 and ~44x trailing GAAP earnings — a premium that the order book currently supports but that leaves nothing for a stumble.

## Company Overview

Eaton Corporation plc is an Irish-domiciled, Cleveland-operated intelligent power management company founded in 1916, with roughly 97,000 employees and FY2025 revenue of \$27.45B. It reports four operating groups:

| Segment | Q2 2026 revenue | Q2 2026 organic growth | Q2 2026 operating margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical Americas | \$4.0B | +18% | 27.5% |
| Electrical Global | \$2.5B | +18% (+44% total incl. Boyd) | 19.8% |
| Aerospace | \$1.2B | +7% (+13% total incl. Ultra PCS) | 22.8% |
| Mobility (Vehicle plus eMobility) | ~\$0.8B | -2% | margin up 90 bp YoY |

On a trailing twelve-month basis through 2026-06-30, Electrical Americas was \$14.5B of revenue, Electrical Global \$7.9B and Aerospace \$4.6B, against total company revenue of \$30.0B. Electrical is therefore ~75% of the business today and will be closer to 80% once Mobility exits.

**The moat.** Eaton's advantage is not any single product — it is the combination of an installed base, a hybrid distribution-plus-direct go-to-market model, lead-time credibility, and a service network. In data centers specifically management estimates content of ~\$3.4M per megawatt across gray space power, and the 2025–2026 acquisition run has been explicitly aimed at widening that: Fibrebond (modular power buildings), Resilient Power (medium-voltage solid-state transformers for 800-volt DC architectures), Boyd Thermal (liquid cooling), Ultra PCS (aerospace and defense electronics), and — announced 2026-09-24 — COL Group from Oaktree for EUR810M, adding EMEA medium-voltage distribution capacity. Management calls the result a "grid to chip" portfolio and argues a credible 800-volt DC supplier needs four blocks: medium-voltage solid-state transformers, DC breaker technology, power electronics and power quality, and cooling. Eaton now claims all four plus the service footprint.

**Management.** CEO Paulo Ruiz Sternadt took over in 2025 and has run a "lead, invest, execute for growth" framework since: a leadership and culture reset, aggressive portfolio reshaping, and an operational push to convert backlog into shipments. CFO David Foster gives unusually granular margin bridges on calls. The signature portfolio decision is the June 2026 agreement to separate Mobility and combine it with Dana Incorporated in a Reverse Morris Trust, creating a company valued above \$10B with Eaton shareholders holding at least 50.1%, expected to close in Q1 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (last completed fiscal year).** Revenue of \$27.45B, up 10.3%, with organic growth of 8%. Gross margin 37.8%. GAAP EPS of \$10.45 (+10%) and adjusted EPS of \$12.07 (+12%), both records. Segment margin of 24.5% was a record. Operating cash flow \$4.47B and free cash flow \$3.55B, also records. Net income was \$4.09B.

**Trailing twelve months through 2026-06-30.** Revenue \$30.03B (+15.5%), gross margin 36.0%, operating income \$5.46B, net income \$3.83B, GAAP EPS \$9.82. Operating cash flow \$4.95B and free cash flow \$3.93B (+20%). The gap between accelerating revenue and flat-to-down GAAP earnings is the acquisition accounting: Q2 2026 GAAP EPS was \$2.11 versus adjusted \$3.15, with \$0.50 of intangible amortization, \$0.49 of acquisition and divestiture charges, and \$0.05 of restructuring.

**Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-31).** Revenue \$8.53B, +21.4% total and +14% organic (+16% excluding Mobility), beating consensus of roughly \$8.0–8.2B. Adjusted EPS \$3.15, +6.8% year over year and \$0.10 above the guidance midpoint. Segment margin 23.1%, above the high end of guidance but down 80 bp year over year. Operating cash flow \$1.1B (+23%) and free cash flow \$874M (+22%), both Q2 records. Management characterised the \$0.25 segment profit beat as fully operational, partially offset by \$0.15 from a higher tax rate, and noted the IEEPA tariff refund contributed under \$3M, below one cent of EPS.

**Orders and backlog — the forward indicator.** Rolling twelve-month orders were up 41% in Electrical Americas and 33% in Electrical Global, with Aerospace up 17%. Total Electrical backlog rose 43% year over year; Electrical Global backlog was up 103% (up 54% excluding Boyd) and Aerospace up 28%. Total company book-to-bill was 1.2, with Electrical Americas at 1.3 and Aerospace at 1.2. Electrical Americas added roughly \$700M of backlog sequentially and \$5B since the start of 2025 despite 18% organic revenue growth. The Americas negotiations pipeline was up 60% year to date.

**Balance sheet — the weak point.** Cash and short-term investments were \$695M at 2026-06-30 against total debt of \$21.3B, for net debt of about \$20.6B. That is roughly double the \$10.7B of total debt at 2025-12-31, the consequence of closing about \$11B of acquisitions in Q1 2026 including Boyd Thermal and Ultra PCS. Against ~\$3.9B of trailing free cash flow, the leverage is serviceable but no longer trivial, and the COL Group deal adds more. The dividend is \$4.40 annualised, a 1.02% yield and a ~42% payout on GAAP EPS.

**Guidance.** FY2026 organic growth of 11–13% (raised twice, from 8% at the start of the year to 10% after Q1 to 12% at the midpoint now), driven by Electrical Americas at a 15% midpoint and Electrical Global at 12%. FY2026 adjusted EPS of \$13.40–\$13.60 and GAAP EPS of \$10.36–\$10.56. Q3 2026 guidance is adjusted EPS of \$3.46–\$3.56 with segment margin of 24.6–25.0%. Cash flow expectations were reaffirmed.

**The margin bridge worth tracking.** Foster laid out the Electrical Americas sequential path explicitly: Q1 to Q2 was +190 bp (100 bp price/cost, 90 bp output); Q2 to Q3 is +250 bp (150 bp price/cost, 100 bp output and productivity); Q3 to Q4 is +200–250 bp (150 bp price/cost, 50–100 bp output). First half to second half is +450–500 bp. All pricing actions were implemented in Q2 or early August. The 2030 commitment is a 32% Electrical Americas margin and a 28% company segment margin.

This is a full multiple for an industrial, and it is worth being clear about why: Eaton is no longer valued as a 6–8% grower. Organic growth guidance is 12% and the CEO's framing is that between the start of 2024 and the end of 2026 the company will add \$10B of revenue — ten times what it added in the entire prior decade. The bridge from a 44x trailing GAAP multiple to a 29x forward multiple is real earnings growth plus the roll-off of acquisition charges, not financial engineering.

Sell-side consensus is a Buy. S&P Global's 27-analyst average target is ~\$480 (11% above spot); MarketBeat's 18-analyst average is ~\$457 with a \$295–\$520 range. September 2026 brought a cluster of bullish actions: UBS upgraded to Buy with a \$515 target, Baird initiated at Outperform with \$500, Morgan Stanley raised to \$520, and Wells Fargo initiated Overweight at \$503.

**Scenarios**

- **Bull: \$560–\$620.** Data center orders keep compounding — the 307 GW of announced US data center backlog is ~15 years of build at 2025 rates, and only ~20% converts near term with the bulk landing in 2028 and beyond. Electrical Americas margins reach the 32% 2030 target on schedule as price/cost turns neutral and the two dozen capacity projects hit full productivity. Boyd scales past the raised \$1.8B FY2026 plan and joins the organic base in 2027. The 800-volt DC transition lifts content per megawatt above \$3.4M, with Resilient Power's solid-state transformers as the differentiator. Mobility exits in Q1 2027 and the remaining Electrical-plus-Aerospace company re-rates on mix. Implies roughly \$15.5–16.0 of 2027 adjusted EPS at 36–39x.
- **Base: \$470–\$510.** FY2026 lands near the guided midpoint of 12% organic growth and \$13.50 adjusted EPS, with Q3 adjusted EPS of \$3.46–\$3.56 and segment margins of 24.6–25.0%. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly \$15 on low-double-digit organic growth plus Boyd and COL, and the stock holds ~31–34x forward adjusted earnings. This brackets the ~\$480 consensus target.
- **Bear: \$310–\$350.** Hyperscaler capex digestion pulls orders down from a 1.3 book-to-bill, the 43% backlog growth reverses, and the multiple compresses toward 22–24x on flat earnings. A relapse in the Electrical Americas margin recovery, a re-opened negative price/cost gap from tariffs or copper and steel inflation, or a Dana RMT complication would each get you there. Note the 52-week low is \$311.92, so the bear case is a revisit of levels seen within the past year, not a novel valuation.

## Growth Catalysts

**Data centers, and the shape of the backlog.** This is the dominant driver. Data center organic revenue grew ~65% in Q2 2026 against a market growing 23%, and management counted eight consecutive quarters of data center growth above 35%. The number that matters for duration is 307 GW of announced US data center backlog, up from a figure implying 12 years at the prior update — roughly 15 years of building at 2025 rates, against the ~50 GW built over decades that will be online by end-2026. Only about 20% converts near term; the majority translates into 2028-and-beyond deliveries. Eaton's 2030 commitments assumed only 17% data center growth, so the current trajectory runs well ahead of the plan the 2030 targets were built on, and management now describes the 2030 revenue target as a floor.

**Liquid cooling via Boyd.** Boyd delivered \$432M of Q2 revenue, 20% above its commitment, and full-year guidance was raised to \$1.8B (of which \$1.5B lands on Eaton's books) from \$1.1B in the prior year. Strategically it puts Eaton on chip vendors' roadmaps, which management argues gives early visibility into power architecture decisions and a first look at bids.

**The 800-volt DC transition.** Moving data center distribution to 800-volt DC improves efficiency, and management believes the transition happens because the economics are too large to ignore at gigawatt scale. Eaton claims a technology lead in medium-voltage solid-state transformers through Resilient Power and holds the other three blocks — DC breakers, power electronics and power quality, cooling — plus the service network.

**Capacity as the near-term constraint being removed.** More than \$1B is committed across two dozen Electrical Americas expansion projects, including a \$242M North Little Rock, Arkansas facility announced in September 2026. Revenue per day is up ~25% since the start of 2025, including 8% in Q2 alone, and management says the hardest sequential ramp is behind them.

**Portfolio reshaping.** The Dana RMT removes a low-growth, lower-margin automotive business and is accretive to both organic growth and margins on close in Q1 2027. COL Group adds EMEA medium-voltage capacity. A Trane Technologies collaboration on an AI data center reference design and Workbench 360 integration with Autodesk Revit both widen the design-in funnel.

**Breadth beyond AI.** Q2 orders grew mid-to-high teens across commercial, institutional, utility, industrial and even residential, with machine OEM orders up in the mid-30s. Aerospace backlog is up 28% with a 1.2 book-to-bill. The thesis does not rest on data centers alone, which matters for the bear case.

## Risk Factors

**Valuation.** 32x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 44x trailing GAAP is priced for execution. Eaton has raised guidance twice this year; the market now expects it.

**AI capex concentration and cyclicality.** Data center is the growth engine, and hyperscaler spending is discretionary, lumpy and currently at a historic peak relative to history. The 307 GW backlog is announcements, not contracts — announced capacity can be deferred or cancelled. A single quarter of order deceleration would hit the multiple before it hits the P&L.

**Margin recovery is guided, not delivered.** Electrical Americas operating margin was down year over year in Q2 on negative price/cost, and the capacity ramp already disrupted Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. The second-half bridge depends on 300 bp of price/cost that has been announced but not yet fully realised in reported results.

**Leverage.** \$21.3B of total debt against \$695M of cash is a different balance sheet than the one Eaton had nine months ago. If rates stay elevated and the acquisition cadence continues, interest expense and refinancing become a live variable rather than a footnote.

**Integration load.** Fibrebond, Resilient Power, Boyd, Ultra PCS and COL Group are being absorbed roughly simultaneously, alongside two dozen greenfield and brownfield capacity projects. Historically this is where industrials disappoint.

**Transaction risk.** The Dana RMT has not closed. Delay, renegotiation, regulatory conditions, or dis-synergies in the separation would complicate the 2027 growth and margin framing that a meaningful part of the bull case rests on.

**Input costs and trade policy.** Tariffs, copper and steel pricing, and tariff-refund mechanics such as IEEPA all move the price/cost line. Residential remains the one end market management describes as soft.

**Competition.** Schneider Electric, ABB, Siemens, Vertiv, GE Vernova and Hubbell are all expanding data center electrical capacity. Lead times are the competitive currency, and management concedes Eaton's lead times are extended on some product lines.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY.** The setup is a company growing organic revenue at a guided 12% with a backlog up 43%, a book-to-bill of 1.3 in its largest segment, a clearly articulated margin recovery path, and an imminent portfolio simplification — trading 10% below its 52-week high and 11% below the consensus target, with four separate sell-side upgrades or initiations in September 2026 all at \$500 or above. The premium multiple is the cost of admission to the cleanest large-cap AI power franchise, not an anomaly to be arbitraged.

**Position sizing.** This is a core industrial holding, not a trade. A 3–5% position is reasonable for a diversified portfolio. Recognise that ETN is correlated with the rest of the AI infrastructure complex — if you already own PWR, MTZ, GEV or VRT, size ETN as part of one combined power-infrastructure sleeve rather than a diversifier.

**Entry strategy.** Spot is \$431.41, above the 50-day moving average of \$419.73 and well above the 200-day of \$387.28, so the trend is intact but the stock is mid-range rather than cheap. The 52-week range is \$311.92 to \$478.00. Scale in: a first tranche here, a second on any pullback toward the 50-day near \$420, and a third only if the 200-day near \$387 is tested. Buying the full position into a 44x trailing multiple ahead of a quarter is the avoidable mistake.

**Stop loss.** For trading positions, a close below the 200-day moving average (~\$387) on order or margin news is the signal to exit — that level has been support all year. For long-term holders, the thesis breaks on evidence rather than price: Electrical Americas book-to-bill falling below 1.0, or the second-half margin bridge missing by more than 100 bp.

**What would change the rating.** A downgrade to HOLD if Q3 segment margins come in below the guided 24.6% or Americas orders decelerate to single digits. An upgrade in conviction if Q3 delivers the full 250 bp sequential Americas margin step and FY2027 guidance is framed above \$15.50 adjusted EPS.

## Readability Pass

In plain English: Eaton makes the electrical guts of a data center. When a hyperscaler builds an AI campus, somebody has to get utility-scale power into the building, step it down safely, distribute it to thousands of racks, keep it clean and uninterrupted, and — increasingly — pump liquid coolant over the chips. Eaton sells almost all of that, roughly \$3.4M worth for every megawatt of capacity.

Business is booming. Last quarter revenue hit a record \$8.5 billion, up 21%, and the data center piece grew about 65% — nearly three times as fast as the market. More importantly, orders are growing faster than revenue: the order book is up 43% from a year ago, which means the next two years of shipments are already substantially visible.

Two things to watch. First, profit margins in the big US electrical business actually fell year over year because Eaton was raising prices more slowly than its costs rose, and because opening two dozen new factories is disruptive. Management has now put the price increases through and says margins climb every quarter from here — that claim gets its first real test on November 3rd. Second, Eaton borrowed heavily this year, about \$11 billion of acquisitions in one quarter, leaving \$21 billion of debt and under \$1 billion of cash.

The stock at \$431 is not cheap: about 32 times this year's expected adjusted earnings. Wall Street's average target is around \$480. Our base case is \$470–\$510, our bull case \$560–\$620 if the data center buildout runs as long as the announced pipeline implies, and our bear case \$310–\$350 if AI spending pauses and the premium multiple deflates. We rate it a BUY, but a scaled-in one — the upside comes from years of backlog conversion, not from the next few weeks.
