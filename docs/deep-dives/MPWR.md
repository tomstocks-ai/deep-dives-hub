# MPWR — Monolithic Power Systems, Inc.

Monolithic Power Systems (MPS) designs the analog and power-management silicon that converts and delivers electricity inside AI servers, GPUs, memory, optical modules, switches, cars and appliances. After a year in which growth accelerated from 26% to 48% year over year and Q3 2026 guidance landed 16% above consensus, MPS is no longer a quietly compounding analog name — it is one of the most direct power-delivery plays on AI rack density, priced accordingly at ~\$66B market cap and ~82x trailing GAAP earnings. The thesis is content growth per socket as MPS sells encapsulated power modules instead of chips; the risk is that the multiple, not the business, sets the return.

## Company Overview

MPS is a fabless-plus analog semiconductor company headquartered in Kirkland, Washington with corporate registration in Schaffhausen, Switzerland. It owns its proprietary BCD process technology and licenses it into partner fabs rather than running its own wafer plants, which is why capacity — not design — has been the governing constraint on growth.

**What it sells.** Voltage regulators, power modules, motor drivers, battery-management and e-fuse devices, and increasingly complete power stages and vertical power-delivery solutions. Management describes the company as transforming "from a chip-only semiconductor supplier to a full service, silicon-based solutions provider." The economic significance is content: a module that integrates silicon, inductors and capacitors into one encapsulated part carries several times the dollar value of the discrete controller it replaces.

**FY2025 revenue by end market** (total \$2.79B):

|---|---|---|---|

| Enterprise Data | \$701.8M | 25.2% | -2.0% |
| Automotive | \$592.5M | 21.2% | +43.1% |
| Communications | \$309.1M | 11.1% | +36.8% |
| Consumer | \$255.2M | 9.1% | +26.3% |
| Industrial | \$199.4M | 7.1% | +35.3% |

The FY2025 mix understates where the business is today. Enterprise Data was flat in 2025 on a platform transition, then grew 45% sequentially in Q2 2026 as AI and server power ramped; Communications — optical modules, switches and NIC cards — jumped 33% sequentially in Q1 2026.

**Moat.** Three reinforcing pieces: proprietary process technology that MPS controls and ports across foundry partners; a very large catalogue of analog parts with long design-win lifecycles (automotive sockets, once designed in, persist for the vehicle program's life); and module packaging know-how that is genuinely hard to copy. CEO Michael Hsing on module assembly: it "is more difficult. It's a 3D effect. It's more complicated than you assemble a phone even."

**Management.** Michael Hsing is founder and CEO and remains the technical driver of the roadmap. Tony Balow runs finance. Capital allocation is conservative: no meaningful debt, a dividend raised for eight consecutive years, and modest buybacks. The governance blemish is insider selling — roughly \$723M of stock sold over the trailing year with no reported open-market buying, per GuruFocus data cited by TIKR, against insider ownership of only 3.53%.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Cash, equivalents and short-term investments were \$1.26B at year-end 2025 and \$1.41B as of Q2 2026, against \$18.9M of total debt — net cash of \$1.39B, or \$28.38 per share. Current ratio 4.98. FY2025 operating cash flow was \$838.2M with \$172.0M of capex, giving \$666.2M of free cash flow (23.9% FCF margin).

On a trailing-twelve-month basis through June 2026: revenue \$3.27B, GAAP net income \$802.0M, GAAP diluted EPS \$16.39, operating cash flow \$822.3M, capex \$236.8M, free cash flow \$585.5M. Note the FCF direction — capex is rising for capacity and working capital is absorbing cash as revenue accelerates (change in working capital of -\$257M TTM), so FCF fell 17% even as earnings rose. That is the signature of a supply-constrained company scaling, not of deteriorating quality: ROE is 22.0% and ROIC 31.7%.

**Margin structure.** Gross margin has been deliberately held near 55% rather than pushed higher; Hsing frames this as prioritising revenue and profit dollars over an optically higher percentage. Operating leverage is coming from opex discipline instead — non-GAAP operating margin moved from 35.2% in FY2025 to 37.5% in Q2 2026.

Forward P/E of 42.2x implies next-twelve-month EPS near \$32 — roughly double FY2025 non-GAAP EPS of \$17.77, which is what a Q3 revenue run-rate of \$1.15B at a 37–38% operating margin produces. The peer comparison is unflattering on the surface: TIKR pegs the semiconductor peer median near 17x EV/EBITDA against MPWR around 32x on a forward basis. The premium is defensible only while revenue compounds above 30% and the module mix lifts margins; a deceleration to 15% growth would likely reset the multiple faster than earnings could offset it.

**Scenarios (12–18 months):**

**Bull — \$2,150–\$2,500.** AI rack power density keeps climbing, the GlobalFoundries Singapore capacity lands on schedule in early 2027, and 800V architectures plus DDR5 memory-interface controllers open new content. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of \$40–45 at 50–58x. The upper end matches TIKR's advanced-model mid target of ~\$2,463; the lower end brackets the Street high of \$2,100.

**Base — \$1,700–\$1,900.** Revenue grows ~45% in 2026 and ~25% in 2027, gross margin holds near 55%, operating margin settles at 37–38%, and the multiple compresses modestly to 50–55x forward earnings. This band straddles the two published consensus targets: \$1,839.80 average from 17 analysts polled by S&P Global, and \$1,731.42 from the 15 brokerages tracked by MarketBeat.

**Bear — \$850–\$1,050.** An AI digestion cycle arrives before the new capacity ships, Enterprise Data plateaus, and the multiple resets toward 30x on flat-to-down earnings. The lower bound is close to the 52-week low of \$833.18 and the lowest published Street target of \$1,200 sits above this range — meaning the bear case requires the Street to be wrong, not merely conservative.

## Growth Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 results on 29 October 2026.** Guided to ~\$1.15B at the midpoint versus \$986.8M consensus at the time of guidance — roughly 56% year-over-year growth. This is the single most informative event: Enterprise Data either holds the raised pace or the premium becomes the story.
- **GlobalFoundries manufacturing partnership (announced 9 September 2026).** MPS proprietary process technology deployed at GF's 300mm Singapore facility, with capacity coming online in early 2027, targeting next-generation automotive power, industrial robotics and smart power stages for AI and cloud infrastructure. Financial terms were not disclosed, so treat it as an announced capacity agreement rather than a quantified revenue commitment.
- **Capacity ceiling raised beyond \$6B.** MPS secured more than \$4B of geographically balanced capacity during 2025 and has since extended its goal "significantly beyond \$6 billion." Given that supply has repeatedly been the binding constraint, capacity headroom converts fairly directly into revenue headroom.
- **800V data center power.** MPS is sampling high-voltage AC-to-DC products for 800V rack architectures — the direction hyperscalers are moving to cut copper and conversion losses at 1MW-plus racks. First revenue is a 2027 event.
- **DDR5 memory-interface controllers.** A genuine adjacency beyond power conversion, attaching MPS content to memory-module volume rather than only to board power.
- **Automotive diversification.** More than 1,500 new sockets won, expanding from ADAS into zonal architectures, 48V systems, battery management and LiDAR, including a fully integrated 48V e-fuse and a kilowatt-class zonal controller. Production ramp is weighted to the second half of 2026 and 2027.
- **Capital returns.** The quarterly dividend was raised 28% to \$2.00 per share for 2026 (\$8.00 annualised, 0.59% yield, 46% payout), the eighth consecutive annual increase, with the Q3 2026 dividend declared 10 September 2026.

## Risk Factors

**Valuation is the dominant risk.** At 82x trailing GAAP earnings, 65x EV/EBITDA and 113x trailing free cash flow, the shares discount multi-year 30%-plus compounding. Beta is 1.66 and the 52-week range spans \$833.18 to \$1,714.09 — a 106% peak-to-trough spread. Even a single quarter of in-line rather than beat-and-raise results can cost 20%.

**AI capex cyclicality.** Enterprise Data and Communications are the growth engines and both ride hyperscaler and AI-infrastructure spending. An inventory digestion cycle in 2027 would hit MPS after it has already committed capex and capacity. Management's mitigation is real but partial: Hsing states the segment has "no concentrated customers," and the non-Enterprise-Data end markets grew over 40% in 2025.

**Supply chain and China exposure.** A significant portion of MPS manufacturing capacity sits in China. Export controls, tariffs, retaliatory measures or cross-strait disruption would hit both cost and availability. The GF Singapore deal and multi-partner strategy reduce this over time, but not before 2027.

**Back-end scaling execution.** The module thesis depends on assembly and test capacity that is harder to scale than wafers, by management's own description. Missing back-end ramp is the most plausible way a demand-rich year still disappoints.

**Working capital and inventory.** Inventory days were 153 at end-2025 before improving to 140 in Q2 2026 — still high in absolute terms. Inventory turnover of 2.51x means a demand air pocket writes down slowly.

**Competition.** Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, Infineon, Renesas, onsemi and specialists such as Vicor and Navitas all target AI power delivery. Vertical power delivery and 800V conversion are contested greenfield, not MPS-owned territory.

**Governance and gross-margin ceiling.** Roughly \$723M of insider selling over the trailing year with no reported buying, and an explicit management choice to cap gross margin near 55%, both limit how much investors should pay for the franchise. A plaintiff-firm press release in August 2026 questioning insider fiduciary duties is investor-solicitation material rather than filed litigation, but it reflects the optics.

## Recommendation

**Rating: BUY** — with position sizing that respects a 1.66 beta and a 42x forward multiple.

The case for owning it: growth is accelerating, not decelerating (26% → 48% year over year, with Q3 guided near 56%); the beat was on revenue and margin together; the balance sheet holds \$1.39B net cash and essentially no debt; ROIC is 31.7%; and both published consensus targets (\$1,731 and \$1,840) sit 28–36% above the current price. The stock is 21% below its 52-week high while fundamentals are at a record — the de-rating has already happened once this year.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a core-satellite AI-infrastructure holding at 2–3% of an equity sleeve, not a concentrated bet. Because of the dollar price per share, use dollar-based rather than round-lot sizing.

**Entry strategy.** Price is \$1,351.20 against a 50-day moving average of \$1,304.61 and a 200-day of \$1,275.07 — above both, with RSI at 58.3, so the trend is intact without being stretched. Scale in thirds: one tranche now, one on a pullback into the \$1,275–\$1,305 moving-average confluence, and the third only after Q3 results on 29 October confirm the Enterprise Data trajectory. Buying the full position ahead of a print that carries a 16%-above-consensus guide is asymmetric the wrong way.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$1,150 (roughly 10% under the 200-day average) invalidates the trend thesis. A close below \$1,050 breaks the base case entirely and puts the bear scenario in play.

**Catalyst calendar.** Ex-dividend 30 September 2026 → Q3 2026 results 29 October 2026 → Q4 and FY2026 results with FY2027 framing in early February 2027 → GlobalFoundries Singapore capacity online early 2027 → 800V and DDR5 product revenue through 2027.

**Scenario summary:** Bull \$2,150–\$2,500 · Base \$1,700–\$1,900 · Bear \$850–\$1,050.

MarketBeat's 15-brokerage panel reads "Moderate Buy" — 1 strong buy, 12 buy, 2 hold, zero sell — with a \$1,731.42 average, a \$2,100 high and a \$1,200 low. StoneX carries Buy with an \$1,800 target. Notably the lowest published target is above the current price.

**News tone — constructive and event-driven.** Coverage since August has been dominated by the Q2 beat-and-raise, the GlobalFoundries capacity agreement, and repeated "stock outperforms competitors" data notes. Zacks ran MPWR as Bull of the Day on 14 August 2026. The 8.06% single-day gain on 22 September was sector beta from a memory-complex rally rather than company news, which argues the stock is now traded as an AI-infrastructure proxy.

**Retail and social sentiment — bullish but thinner than mega-cap AI names.** The \$1,351 share price and lack of any stock split in the company's history keep round-lot retail participation low; institutions hold 98.94% of shares and float is 47.36M of 49.14M shares. Discussion skews toward the "quality compounder at a high multiple" framing rather than momentum chasing.

**Positioning and options flow — mildly constructive, no crowded short.** Short interest is 1.91M shares, 3.88% of shares outstanding and 4.03% of float, down from 2.00M the prior month, with a 3.1-day cover ratio. Average 20-day volume is 725,355 shares. Detailed options-flow data was not available in this session, so no claim is made about skew or unusual activity.

**Bear voices.** The consistent criticism is valuation (82x trailing, 65x EV/EBITDA against a ~17x peer median) coupled with insider selling of ~\$723M over twelve months and no insider buying. An August 2026 plaintiff-firm release probing insider conduct adds noise without a filed claim.

**Composite sentiment score: 7.5/10** — bullish. Deducted for valuation-driven fragility, the insider-selling optics, and the fact that the next print must clear a guide already 16% above where consensus sat.

## Readability Pass

MPS makes the chips and modules that turn wall power into the precise voltages an AI server, a GPU, a memory stick, an optical transceiver or a car needs. When AI racks get denser, more of the electricity problem lands on parts like these, and MPS has been selling bigger, more integrated versions of them — modules instead of single chips — which means more dollars per socket.

The numbers show that working. Revenue grew 26% in 2025 to \$2.79B, then accelerated: \$804M in Q1 2026, a record \$981M in Q2, and management guided Q3 to about \$1.15B, roughly 56% above last year and far above what analysts expected. Profit margins improved at the same time. There is \$1.39B of net cash, essentially no debt, and a dividend raised eight years running.

The catch is the price. At \$1,351 the stock trades at 82 times last year's earnings and 42 times next year's — a large premium to other chip companies. That premium is the whole debate: it holds if growth stays above 30%, and it does not if AI spending pauses. A new manufacturing partnership with GlobalFoundries in Singapore should relieve the supply ceiling that has limited growth, but that capacity does not arrive until early 2027, so 2026 has to be delivered with what exists today.

Bottom line: a high-quality business in the right place at the right time, at a price that requires it to keep executing. Buy, but in tranches, with a stop, and with the 29 October earnings date circled.
