# LUNR — Intuitive Machines, Inc.

**Intuitive Machines, Inc.** (NASDAQ: LUNR) is a Houston-based space technology, infrastructure, and services company and the leading commercial pure-play on the emerging cislunar economy. Best known for landing the first commercial spacecraft on the Moon (IM-1 Odysseus, February 2024), the company is building a diversified business around NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program, the Near Space Network communications contract, and lunar data-relay infrastructure. At \$13.10 (as of 2026-08-04), down sharply from its \$46.75 52-week high, LUNR is a high-risk, high-reward speculative call on the buildout of a permanent commercial presence around and on the Moon.

---

## 1. Company Overview

Founded in 2013 by Steve Altemus, Tim Crain, and Kris Kimel, Intuitive Machines went public on the Nasdaq in February 2023 via a SPAC merger with Inflection Point Acquisition Corp. The company designs and operates end-to-end robotic lunar missions — spacecraft design, mission management, navigation, communications, and data services — under NASA's CLPS framework, and has since expanded into space communications and orbital services.

### Business Model and Revenue Segments

| Segment | Description | Revenue Driver |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **Lunar Access Services** | Nova-C class landers delivering NASA and commercial payloads to the lunar surface under fixed-price CLPS task orders | Milestone-based mission revenue (IM-1, IM-2, IM-3, and late-2028 tranche) |
| **Data Transmission / Space Network** | Lunar data-relay satellites and the NASA Near Space Network (NSN) services contract for communications and navigation | Recurring, higher-margin communications revenue as the network scales |
| **Space Infrastructure and Services** | Engineering services (OMES III), orbital services, propulsion, and — post-August 2026 — the Goonhilly Earth Station and COMSAT ground-station assets | Contracted engineering and ground-segment services |

**Key customers** are dominated by NASA (CLPS, OMES III, Near Space Network) with a growing set of commercial and allied-government payload customers. The company reported TTM revenue of roughly \$334.3M (+53.8%), reflecting mission cadence and services growth even as FY2025 annual revenue of \$210.1M dipped versus the prior year.

### Competitive Moat

Intuitive Machines' moat is narrow but real: it is the **only commercial company to have soft-landed on the Moon**, giving it flight heritage that competitors Firefly Aerospace and Astrobotic are still building. Its vertically integrated Nova-C platform, in-house navigation and propulsion, and multi-award positions on both CLPS and the Near Space Network create switching costs for NASA and a diversified contract base. The moat is undercut, however, by the fact that both landings to date tipped over on touchdown — flight heritage that is as much cautionary as it is proof of capability.

**Competitive Moat Assessment:** MODERATE.

### Management

CEO **Steve Altemus** (a former NASA deputy center director at Johnson Space Center) leads a founder team with deep human-spaceflight engineering credentials. CTO **Tim Crain** drives mission and landing systems. Management has demonstrated an ability to win recurring NASA business, but the repeated landing anomalies keep execution scrutiny high.

**Quality of earnings:** Revenue is real and contracted, but fixed-price CLPS missions carry thin-to-negative gross margins, and losses have widened on a TTM basis as the company invests in future missions, the Near Space Network, and acquisitions. ### Cash Flow

Free cash flow is negative as the company funds mission hardware, the lunar data-relay network, and the Goonhilly Earth Station and COMSAT acquisition (closed August 2026). Milestone-based CLPS payments create lumpy operating cash flow. A durable turn to positive free cash flow depends on services revenue scaling and lander margins improving.

## 4. Growth Catalysts

### Investment Thesis

Intuitive Machines is the most direct listed way to invest in the commercial cislunar economy. The bet is that NASA's Artemis-era demand for lunar delivery, communications, and services is durable, and that IM's first-mover CLPS position plus its Near Space Network role convert into a diversified, partly recurring revenue base that eventually earns acceptable margins.

### Key Catalysts

| Timeline | Catalyst | Impact |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **August 13, 2026** | Q2 2026 earnings | Revenue trajectory, margin progress, cash runway, and IM-3 / NSN commentary |
| **Q3 2026** | Goonhilly Earth Station and COMSAT integration | Expands ground-station and space data-services footprint |
| **2027** | IM-3 lunar mission | The next Nova-C landing attempt — the key test of a fully upright, operational landing |
| **Ongoing 2026–2027** | Near Space Network task orders and lunar data-relay (Khon) deployments | Builds recurring communications revenue away from binary lander missions |
| **Late 2028** | NASA CLPS tranche mission (~\$148M IM share of the \$590M award) plus sixth CLPS lunar-utility award | Multi-mission backlog and revenue visibility |

The **Near Space Network** contract — a NASA multi-award Indefinite-Delivery/Indefinite-Quantity vehicle worth up to \$4.82B across providers — is the strategic linchpin: it positions IM to sell communications and navigation services for missions in the near-space and lunar region, a recurring business fundamentally different from one-shot landers. Combined with the lunar data-relay satellites, it is the path to a services-led revenue mix.

| **Profitability / Margins** | Thin-to-negative fixed-price CLPS margins; -\$109.9M TTM net loss | Higher-margin NSN and data-relay mix over time |
| **Dilution** | Repeated equity raises and acquisition spend pressure share count | Larger contract base could reduce future raise needs |
| **Government Dependency** | Heavy reliance on NASA CLPS, OMES III, and NSN — exposed to budget and Artemis risk | Multi-award positions and allied/commercial customers diversify demand |
| **Competition** | Firefly Aerospace and Astrobotic compete directly for CLPS share | First-mover flight heritage and NSN role |
| **Concentration / Macro** | Few large contracts drive revenue; high 1.83 beta amplifies sector risk-off | Growing contract count; strong secular Artemis tailwind |

## 7. Sentiment Analysis

**Bullish factors:**

- First and only commercial company to soft-land on the Moon — genuine flight heritage.
- Sixth CLPS award (June 2026) and a ~\$148M share of NASA's \$590M late-2028 Moon-lander tranche extend the backlog.
- Position on the Near Space Network services contract (up to \$4.82B multi-award) opens a recurring communications business.
- Goonhilly Earth Station and COMSAT acquisition (August 2026) broadens ground-segment and data services.
- ARK Investment has been an active buyer; consensus rating is Buy with a ~\$39 average target.

**Bearish / cautious factors:**

- Both IM-1 and IM-2 tipped over on landing, undermining confidence in the core lander product.
- Persistent net losses (-\$109.9M TTM) and thin fixed-price margins.
- Repeated equity raises dilute shareholders; the stock is down ~72% from its high.
- Deutsche Bank cut its target to \$20 from \$34 (while keeping a Buy) — a reminder of downward estimate revisions.
- Heavy dependence on NASA budgets and the Artemis program.

**Overall sentiment:** Cautiously constructive but skeptical. The market is pricing in real execution doubt, leaving upside if IM-3 lands cleanly and the services mix scales — and meaningful downside if it does not.

## 8. Readability Pass

| Term | Plain English |
|------|---------------|
| **CLPS** | Commercial Lunar Payload Services — NASA's program that pays private companies to deliver cargo to the Moon |
| **Nova-C** | Intuitive Machines' lunar lander spacecraft (Odysseus, Athena are Nova-C landers) |
| **IM-1 / IM-2 / IM-3** | The company's numbered lunar missions; IM-1 and IM-2 landed but tipped over, IM-3 is the next attempt |
| **Near Space Network (NSN)** | A NASA program buying communications and navigation services for missions near Earth and the Moon |
| **Lunar data-relay** | Satellites that relay signals between the Moon's surface/orbit and Earth — like cell towers for the Moon |
| **OMES III** | A NASA engineering-services contract Intuitive Machines holds |
| **Cislunar** | The region of space between Earth and the Moon (and around the Moon) |
| **Fixed-price contract** | A contract for a set price — the company eats any cost overruns, squeezing margins |
