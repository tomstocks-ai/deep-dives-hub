# VIAV — Viavi Solutions Inc.

Viavi sells the instruments that prove an optical network works. Every 400G, 800G and 1.6T transceiver, switch ASIC and AI fabric has to be characterised in the lab, screened in production and monitored in the field, and Viavi is the broadest independent supplier across all three stages. That position turned into a step-change in FY2026: revenue rose 40.0% to \$1.518B, non-GAAP operating margin expanded 630bps to 20.6%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS more than doubled to \$1.00. The data center ecosystem now accounts for roughly half of the larger of Viavi's two segments, and aerospace and defense for another ~17%. At \$40.87 (2026-09-28) the shares carry a ~\$10.1B market cap, ~25x guided forward non-GAAP earnings, and a balance sheet where \$656.7M of cash roughly matches \$650M of principal debt. The catch is that the stock is up 226% in a year, sits 32% below its 52-week high of \$60.43, and is now a leveraged read on hyperscaler optical capex.

## Company Overview

Viavi (Chandler, Arizona; ~4,100 employees; NASDAQ-listed since the JDS Uniphase split in 2015) runs two reportable segments.

**Network and Service Enablement (NSE)** — \$1,182.9M of FY2026 revenue, up 52.3% year over year, 64.4% gross margin. This is the test-and-measurement business: lab and production instruments for optical components and transceivers, high-speed Ethernet and network-security test (the acquired Spirent product lines), fiber inspection and monitoring, field instruments for service providers, radio and avionics test, and resilient position, navigation and timing (PNT). Management disclosed on the Q4 FY2026 call that the data center ecosystem is now running at roughly 50% of NSE revenue, aerospace and defense at ~17%, with the remainder the legacy service provider business. Excluding Spirent, the data center business more than doubled year over year.

**Optical Security and Performance Products (OSP)** — \$335.4M, up 9.0%, 52.2% gross margin but a 36.6% operating margin. OSP makes the light-interference pigments used in banknote anti-counterfeiting and the optical filters used in 3D sensing, automotive and industrial applications. It is a slow-growing, high-margin annuity that funds NSE's R&D.

The moat is threefold. First, Viavi's instruments are designed into customers' qualification flows — once a transceiver vendor validates on a Viavi platform, switching costs are high and recurring. Second, the portfolio spans layers 0 through 3 plus the AI fabric, so Viavi can sell into silicon photonics, co-packaged optics (CPO), pluggables, switches and installed fiber from one relationship; competitors such as Keysight, Anritsu and EXFO are stronger in some layers and absent in others. Third, complexity is the product: CPO and optical circuit switching (OCS) are harder to yield than pluggables, and harder to yield means more test.

Capital allocation has been deliberate. Viavi bought the Spirent Communications high-speed Ethernet, network security and channel emulation lines from Keysight for \$425M all-cash, closing in October 2025; it contributed \$145M to FY2026 revenue (\$47.7M in Q4 alone) and management declared integration complete and ahead of schedule as of the June 2026 quarter. Viavi also acquired Inertial Labs in 2025 to deepen PNT. Oleg Khaykin has been CEO since 2016; Ilan Daskal is CFO.

The fourth quarter was the inflection: \$443.1M of revenue (+52.5% year over year, +8.9% sequentially), above the \$427–437M guide; 59.1% GAAP and 62.3% non-GAAP gross margin; 24.0% non-GAAP operating margin against a 22.2–23.2% guide; and \$0.34 non-GAAP EPS against \$0.29–0.31 guided and \$0.30 consensus. NSE delivered \$353.9M at a 20.0% GAAP operating margin versus 4.6% a year earlier — that 1,540bps swing is the whole story of the year.

The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap deserves attention, because it is large: \$207.8M of FY2026 charges were excluded from operating income, comprising \$55.4M stock-based compensation, \$67.9M acquisition-intangible amortisation, \$33.0M of contingent-consideration remeasurement, \$15.9M restructuring, \$12.6M acquisition and integration costs and \$6.1M inventory step-up. Below the line, \$56.7M of losses on extinguishing the 1.625% convertibles and the Term Loan B drove a \$47.5M tax provision on just \$16.3M of pre-tax income, producing the GAAP net loss. Most of these are genuinely transitional — the debt was refinanced, Spirent is integrated — but amortisation of roughly \$68M a year will suppress GAAP earnings for several more years.

Balance sheet and cash. Cash, short-term investments and restricted cash finished at \$656.7M, up from \$508M at the end of Q3, against \$250M of 0.625% senior convertible notes and \$400M of 3.75% senior notes — \$641.9M net carrying value. On a principal basis Viavi is approximately net-cash-neutral, a meaningful improvement after paying down \$49M of converts and prepaying \$150M on the Term Loan B during the year. Interest expense was \$47.4M. Operating cash flow was \$113.9M for the year (\$66.7M in Q4 alone, versus \$23.8M in the prior-year quarter) against \$31.1M of capex, so free cash flow was ~\$83M. That is thin relative to \$243.8M of non-GAAP net income; the gap sits in working capital, with receivables up \$90M and inventories up \$37M to support the revenue ramp, plus cash taxes and restructuring payments. Cash conversion should improve as growth normalises, but it has not yet.

Geographic mix for FY2026 was Americas 45.0%, Asia-Pacific 30.9%, EMEA 24.1%. Share count is drifting up: 246.75M shares outstanding after an 11.1M-share equity offering, and only ~2.7M shares were repurchased for ~\$30M during FY2026, with none in Q4 as management prioritised debt.

Guidance for Q1 FY2027 (13 weeks plus one, ending 3 October 2026) is \$450–460M of revenue — NSE \$360–368M, OSP \$90–92M — with 27.1% plus or minus 40bps non-GAAP operating margin and \$0.40–0.42 non-GAAP EPS. Two caveats management flagged: the quarter is 14 weeks, which adds variable operating expense without materially adding revenue, and an \$11M tariff refund received in July flows mostly through Q1 cost of goods sold, worth about 100bps of the margin guide. Strip that out and the underlying margin is closer to 26%.

The forward figure is the one that matters. Annualising the Q1 guide alone gives ~\$1.64 of non-GAAP EPS, and Viavi grows sequentially through its fiscal year, so consensus of roughly \$1.65–1.80 for FY2027 is reasonable — note this is a consensus estimate, not company-guided; the company has guided only Q1. A high-20s-percent revenue grower with expanding margins and a clean balance sheet at 23–25x forward earnings is not demanding by 2026 AI-infrastructure standards, where optical peers trade materially higher.

Sell-side targets bracket wide. The S&P Global poll of 8 analysts averages \$61.43 with a \$44 low and \$70 high and a Strong Buy consensus; MarketBeat's 9-analyst set averages \$55.43 with a \$23 low, rated Moderate Buy; TipRanks shows \$62.00. Individual marks after the Q4 print: Stifel \$65 (Buy, raised from \$60), Susquehanna \$66 (Positive), B. Riley \$60 (Buy, trimmed from \$63), UBS \$44 (Neutral, cut from \$60), and Rosenblatt initiating Buy on 23 September 2026. The dispersion is the honest signal — the bulls are underwriting a multi-year 1.6T-plus-CPO cycle, the bears are underwriting a capex digestion.

**Bull case: \$62–\$72.** 1.6T reaches volume parity with 800G in calendar 2027 while the 800G installed base keeps upgrading; CPO and OCS broaden beyond the first hyperscaler; quarterly revenue crosses \$500M inside calendar 2027; operating margin reaches the mid-to-high 20s; FY2028 non-GAAP EPS approaches \$2.00 and the market pays 33–36x.

**Base case: \$48–\$56.** FY2027 revenue of ~\$1.85–1.95B, non-GAAP operating margin of 24–27%, non-GAAP EPS of \$1.65–1.80, and a 28–30x forward multiple. This lands on the current 9-analyst consensus of ~\$55 and implies ~17–37% upside from spot.

**Bear case: \$26–\$32.** Hyperscaler optical orders pause, 1.6T proves substitutive rather than additive, the service provider base stays flat, and the multiple compresses to 16–18x forward earnings. That takes the shares back to the \$30-ish level they traded at in early September 2026 — a level this stock held three weeks ago, which is why the bear case is not remote.

## Growth Catalysts

**1.6T Ethernet ramp.** Management's framing on the Q4 call was specific: 800G is still the biggest volume driver, 1.6T is ramping quickly into production, and the two reach parity in calendar 2027, after which 1.6T grows and 800G pulls back — but slowly, because the installed base keeps upgrading. Viavi launched a TestCenter D2 1.6T appliance in late 2025 and the industry's first validation solution for Ultra Ethernet transport in 2026.

**Co-packaged optics and optical circuit switching.** CPO lets 3nm silicon perform like 2nm by eliminating electrical reach penalties, which is why hyperscalers want it despite the assembly complexity. That complexity is Viavi's revenue: yields have to be managed with more test, not less. Management said CPO revenue started in the September 2026 quarter with acceleration expected in December, and that OCS revenue exists today with the bulk arriving as adoption moves past the initial hyperscaler.

**Aerospace and defense.** Roughly 17% of NSE. On 24 September 2026 Viavi completed CMMC Level 2 certification for its radio test, avionics, synthetic and modular, and PNT product lines — mandatory for Department of Defense contractors handling controlled unclassified information, and the direct cause of the ~6–9% share-price jump that week. Extending the certification to the Inertial Labs lines acquired in 2025 is the next step and would widen the addressable defense pipeline.

**Spirent operating leverage.** The \$425M acquisition contributed \$145M in FY2026 and is fully integrated with restructuring savings realised. Spirent's revenue is seasonally back-half weighted (45/55 first half to second half of the calendar year), so December should be its strongest quarter, and management guided it up ~10% sequentially in September.

**Margin structure.** NSE gross margins run from the low 60s for field instruments to the high 70s for lab products. As the lab and production mix grows, blended gross margin trends up, and management expects overall operating margin in the mid-to-high 20s "in the not-too-distant future" with R&D not materially higher. That is the clearest articulated path to earnings leverage.

**Adjacent programmes.** Viavi joined the EU-funded SHIELD-6G consortium (\$1.1M award, July 2026) for AI-driven 6G security testing, introduced the Vertex 6.0 channel emulator for 6G and Wi-Fi 7/8 in August 2026, and on 29 September 2026 launched the CyberFlood CF50, a 100G platform for application performance, security and AI-inference/LLM testing with native post-quantum cryptography support. None are individually material; collectively they show the portfolio extending into AI-inference and security test.

## Risk Factors

**Business risks.** The data center ecosystem is now ~50% of NSE, which means roughly 39% of total company revenue depends on a capex cycle Viavi does not control. Management itself flagged that the 1.6T transition is partly substitution for 800G, so headline port-speed growth overstates the revenue opportunity. The service provider business — field instruments and wireless — grows only 1–2%, and management describes wireless demand as anemic though stable; that is roughly a third of NSE acting as ballast. OSP grew 9.0% and is tied to banknote replacement cycles and consumer 3D-sensing designs, neither of which Viavi can accelerate.

**Financial risks.** Viavi lost \$0.13 per diluted share on a GAAP basis in FY2026, and the bridge to \$1.00 of non-GAAP EPS runs through \$207.8M of excluded operating charges plus \$57M of non-cash interest and extinguishment losses. Investors are being asked to look through a lot. Free cash flow of ~\$83M against \$243.8M of non-GAAP net income shows working capital absorbing the growth. Interest expense was \$47.4M against \$650M of principal debt, and no shares were bought back in Q4 as debt took priority. Share count has risen to 246.75M after an equity offering.

**Macro and sector risks.** Asia-Pacific is 30.9% of revenue, so China trade measures, export controls and retaliation are live exposures; the company also flagged semiconductor cost inflation, which it has so far passed through but may not always. The \$11M tariff refund benefiting Q1 FY2027 is a reminder that tariff policy cuts both ways. Viavi's own risk factors call out limited demand visibility, significant in-quarter book-and-ship business, and broad gross-margin ranges across the portfolio that make mix shifts swing profitability — all of which make quarterly results harder to forecast than the growth rate suggests.

**Positioning risk.** A 226% one-year gain, a 1.23 beta, a 52-week range of \$12.23–\$60.43, a 32% drawdown from the high already this year, and a 21% gain over nine sessions into 28 September 2026. Momentum of that shape unwinds quickly when the AI-infrastructure trade rotates.

## Recommendation

**Rating: SPEC. BUY.** The fundamentals justify a buy — 40% revenue growth, 630bps of margin expansion, a doubled non-GAAP EPS, a roughly net-cash balance sheet, and 23–25x forward earnings. The "SPEC." qualifier reflects that the earnings stream is levered to hyperscaler optical capex, that GAAP profitability is still absent, and that the shares have already demonstrated a 32% peak-to-trough drawdown within the past twelve months.

**Position sizing.** Treat as a satellite AI-infrastructure holding, not a core position: 1–2% of portfolio. Viavi is correlated with the optical complex (LITE, CIEN, FN, AAOI, CRDO), so size it as part of a combined optical allocation rather than independently.

**Entry strategy.** Spot is \$40.87 after a nine-session, 21% run — the shares are extended against a 50-day moving average of \$38.12 and a 200-day of \$36.28, and the RSI on the widget above will show it. Scale in rather than chase: a third here, a third on a pullback into the \$36–38 area where the 50-day sits, and a third reserved for either a confirmed Q1 beat on 28 October or a washout toward the 200-day near \$36. Above \$46 the risk/reward compresses toward the base-case range and new money is better deployed elsewhere.

**Stop loss.** A close below \$32 breaks the September breakout structure and argues the data center order narrative has cracked; that is the exit level for a thesis-based holder. A trader's stop sits tighter, below the \$36 200-day average.

## Readability Pass

Viavi makes the test equipment that proves fibre-optic networks and the gear inside AI data centers actually work. When a company builds an 800-gigabit or 1.6-terabit optical transceiver, it uses Viavi instruments to design it, to check every unit coming off the line, and to monitor the fibre once it is installed. AI data centers are being wired at speeds that were laboratory curiosities three years ago, which means a lot more testing — and Viavi is the broadest independent supplier of it.

That showed up in the numbers for the year ended June 2026: sales rose 40% to \$1.52 billion, and adjusted profit per share doubled from 47 cents to \$1.00. Data centers now make up about half of Viavi's main business segment, and defense and aerospace testing another sixth. The company bought a high-speed Ethernet testing business from Spirent for \$425 million, finished integrating it early, and has roughly as much cash as debt.

The bet is simple: the more complicated optical networking gets — 1.6-terabit links, co-packaged optics, optical switching — the more testing is required, and the more Viavi sells. The risk is equally simple: about 40% of company revenue now depends on how much the big cloud companies choose to spend on optical hardware next year. The rest of the business, selling instruments to phone carriers, is barely growing.

The shares have tripled in a year and sit a third below their high. At roughly 24 times next year's expected adjusted earnings, that is not expensive for this kind of growth, but it is not cheap enough to absorb a spending pause either. We rate it a speculative buy: worth owning in modest size, worth scaling into rather than chasing, and worth exiting below \$32.
